## Why this matters

Circle theorems are a marks goldmine in Cambridge 4024 Paper 1 and Paper 2. The questions are short, and each theorem you spot gives you a quick angle. But there is a catch: you must **state the reason** when you use a theorem. Cambridge often awards a separate B mark for the reason on top of the A mark for the value. Saying "$x = 90°$" with no reason can cost you a mark even when the number is right.

In this part we cover the two simplest circle facts:

- Angle in a semicircle is $90°$.
- Tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

Each theorem has its own draggable manipulative below. Move the points and watch the angles update live: the rule holds for every position.

## Theorem 1: angle in a semicircle is $90°$

If $AB$ is a **diameter** and $C$ is any point on the circle, then angle $ACB = 90°$.

**Reason to write:** "angle in semicircle is $90°$".

The chord $AB$ must go through the centre - that is what makes it a diameter. If $AB$ does not pass through the centre, this theorem does not apply.

It does not matter where $C$ sits on the arc. Move it left or right, up or down, and the right angle stays.

## Theorem 2: tangent is perpendicular to the radius

A **tangent** is a straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point. At that point of contact, the tangent and the radius meet at $90°$.

**Reason to write:** "tangent perpendicular to radius at point of contact" (or "radius is perpendicular to tangent").

## Worked example

In the diagram, $AB$ is a diameter of the circle, $C$ lies on the circle, and angle $CAB = 28°$. The tangent to the circle at $B$ meets line $CB$ extended at point $D$. Find angle $CBD$.

**Step 1: find angle $ACB$.**

$AB$ is a diameter, so by the angle-in-semicircle theorem, angle $ACB = 90°$.

**Step 2: find angle $ABC$ from the triangle.**

Angles in triangle $ABC$ sum to $180°$.

$$\text{angle } ABC = 180° - 90° - 28° = 62°$$

**Step 3: use tangent-radius at $B$.**

The radius $OB$ lies along the diameter $AB$, and the tangent at $B$ is perpendicular to it. So the angle between $AB$ and the tangent is $90°$.

**Step 4: subtract to get angle $CBD$.**

Angle $ABD$ (the full angle from $A$ to the tangent direction $D$) is $90°$. Angle $ABC$ is $62°$. So:

$$\text{angle } CBD = 90° - 62° = 28°$$

**Answer:**

angle $ACB = 90°$ (angle in semicircle is $90°$). **[B1 value; B1 reason]**

angle $ABC = 62°$ (angle sum of triangle is $180°$). **[M1 method; A1 value]**

angle $CBD = 28°$ (tangent perpendicular to radius at point of contact). **[A1 value; B1 reason]**

Full marks $= 6$.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to write the reason.** "$x = 90°$" alone does not earn the B mark for reason. Always write "angle in semicircle is $90°$" or "tangent perpendicular to radius at point of contact".
- **Assuming $AB$ is a diameter when it is just a chord.** The semicircle rule only applies when the chord goes through the centre.
- **Mixing up "tangent perpendicular to radius" with "tangent perpendicular to chord".** It is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the contact point, not to any chord.
- **Forgetting the right-angle mark on the diagram.** As soon as you spot a diameter or a tangent, mark the $90°$ before doing anything else.

## Quick summary

- Angle in a semicircle (chord on a diameter) is $90°$.
- Tangent meets the radius at the contact point at $90°$.
- Always state the reason. Cambridge awards a B mark for the reason on top of the A mark for the value.
- Mark right angles on the diagram as soon as you spot a diameter or a tangent.
