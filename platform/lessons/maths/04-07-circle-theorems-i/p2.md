## Why this matters

These two theorems show up almost every paper. They link points on the circumference to each other and to the centre, and they often combine with the angle-sum rule for triangles to give multi-step answers.

In this part:

- Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference (subtended by the same arc).
- Angles in the same segment are equal.

## Theorem: angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference

If an arc subtends an angle at the centre and an angle at any point on the major arc (on the other side of the chord), the centre angle is **twice** the circumference angle.

**Reason to write:** "angle at centre is twice angle at circumference".

Watch the diagram. The chord $AB$ creates two angles: angle $AOB$ at the centre, and angle $APB$ at the point $P$ on the far arc. Whatever angle $P$ is, $O$ is double.

A useful special case: when the chord is a diameter, the centre angle is $180°$ and the circumference angle is $90°$. That is the semicircle theorem you saw in p1 - it is just this same rule with the centre angle stretched flat.

## Theorem: angles in the same segment are equal

If two points $P$ and $Q$ both sit on the same arc and both look at the same chord $AB$, then angle $APB$ equals angle $AQB$.

**Reason to write:** "angles in the same segment are equal".

The chord $AB$ splits the circle into two segments. As long as both vertex points sit on the same side of the chord (same segment), they see $AB$ under the same angle. Move the vertex anywhere along that arc; the angle stays the same.

## Worked example

In the circle, $O$ is the centre. Points $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ lie on the circle, with angle $AOC = 110°$ (reflex angle not used). Point $D$ is on the major arc. Find angle $ADC$ and angle $ABC$ (where $B$ is also on the major arc).

**Step 1: angle at centre to angle at circumference.**

Angle $AOC$ at the centre is $110°$. Angle $ADC$ at the circumference (on the major arc) is half of this.

$$\text{angle } ADC = \dfrac{110°}{2} = 55°$$

**Step 2: angle $ABC$ by same segment.**

$B$ and $D$ both sit on the major arc, both looking at chord $AC$. They are in the **same segment**.

$$\text{angle } ABC = \text{angle } ADC = 55°$$

**Answer:**

angle $ADC = 55°$ (angle at centre is twice angle at circumference). **[M1 halve; A1 value; B1 reason]**

angle $ABC = 55°$ (angles in the same segment are equal). **[A1 value; B1 reason]**

Full marks $= 5$.

## Common mistakes

- **Halving when you should double, or vice versa.** Read the question carefully. Centre angle = 2 $\times$ circumference angle. If you are given the centre angle, divide by $2$. If you are given the circumference angle, multiply by $2$.
- **Using the rule across the chord (opposite segment).** The "twice" rule needs the circumference angle on the major arc when the centre angle is the obtuse/reflex one. If the circumference vertex sits on the minor arc, you actually get the **reflex** centre angle as twice the circumference angle. Sketch carefully.
- **Calling angles equal when they are in opposite segments.** Two points on opposite sides of the chord see it under angles that **sum to $180°$**, not equal.
- **Writing a vague reason like "circle theorem".** Use the proper wording: "angle at centre is twice angle at circumference" and "angles in the same segment are equal".

## Quick summary

- Angle at centre = $2 \times$ angle at circumference (same arc).
- Angles in the same segment are equal (same chord, same side).
- Always write the full theorem name as the reason for the B mark.
- Special case of the centre rule: a diameter gives a $180°$ centre angle, hence a $90°$ semicircle angle.
