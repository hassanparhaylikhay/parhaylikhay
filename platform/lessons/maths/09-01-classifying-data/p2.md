## Why this matters

A two-way table groups items by TWO characteristics at once. The exam loves them: questions about two-way tables show up on most papers, worth $2$ to $4$ marks. The skills tested are:

- Reading a cell, row total, or column total.
- Completing a partly-filled table from given row/column totals.
- Computing probabilities from the cells.

Three key facts:

1. **Each cell** is the count of items matching BOTH the row label and the column label (year AND sport).
2. **Margins** (right column and bottom row) give counts for a SINGLE category alone.
3. **Grand total** (bottom-right corner) is the total of everything; it equals the sum of either margin.

## Filling in a partly-given table

A common exam question: most cells are blank, the question gives you some cells AND some totals. Use subtraction to fill in the rest.

**Rule of thumb**: each row of a two-row table has $3$ cells (two values + a total). If you know any TWO, you can subtract to find the third. Same for columns. Work through the rows and columns, picking the easiest unknown to solve next.

## Reading probabilities from a two-way table

A student is chosen at random. Three flavours of probability question:

- **Whole row or column**: $P(\text{plays cricket}) = \dfrac{\text{cricket column total}}{\text{grand total}}$.
- **Single cell**: $P(\text{Year 10 AND cricket}) = \dfrac{\text{the cell}}{\text{grand total}}$.
- **Conditional**: $P(\text{cricket} \mid \text{Year 10}) = \dfrac{\text{the cell}}{\text{row total}}$. This is the row total in the denominator, NOT the grand total.

## Worked example: complete the table

A school has $40$ students. $22$ play cricket. $18$ are in Year 10. Among Year 10, $12$ play cricket. Complete the table.

**Step 1: Set up.**

| | Cricket | Football | Total |
|-|---------|----------|-------|
| Year 10 | $12$ | ? | $18$ |
| Year 11 | ? | ? | ? |
| Total | $22$ | ? | $40$ |

**Step 2: Fill row 1.** Year 10 cricket = $12$, Year 10 total = $18$, so Year 10 football = $18 - 12 = 6$.

**Step 3: Fill the column totals.** Cricket total = $22$, Grand total = $40$, so Football total = $40 - 22 = 18$.

**Step 4: Fill row 2.** Year 11 total = $40 - 18 = 22$. Year 11 cricket = $22 - 12 = 10$. Year 11 football = $18 - 6 = 12$ (or $22 - 10 = 12$, check).

**Step 5: Verify.** Every row and column total matches. $\checkmark$

**Answer:** $\boxed{\text{Year 11 football} = 12}$. **[B1 for completing the table; B1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

The chain of subtractions is the entire skill. Always start with the cell whose row AND column you both know.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing the row total with the grand total.** $P(\text{cricket})$ uses the GRAND total, not a row total.
- **Wrong cell read** for $P(\text{both A and B})$. Find the cell at the intersection of A's row/column and B's column/row.
- **Forgetting the grand total** sanity check. The two margins must each sum to the grand total.
- **Missing the "given" condition.** $P(A \mid B)$ uses the row total for $B$ as the denominator.

## Quick summary

- **Two-way table** = rows for one characteristic, columns for another.
- **Cells** are intersections; **margins** are single-category totals; **corner** is the grand total.
- To fill blanks: each row and column has $3$ cells; knowing $2$ gives the third by subtraction.
- $P(\text{A}) = \dfrac{\text{A's column total}}{\text{grand total}}$; $P(\text{A and B}) = \dfrac{\text{cell}}{\text{grand total}}$.
