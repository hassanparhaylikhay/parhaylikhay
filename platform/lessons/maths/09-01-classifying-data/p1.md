## Why this matters

Almost every statistics question on the paper starts with a TABLE. The exam might give you a list of measurements that you need to ORGANISE into a frequency table, or it might give you a finished table and ask questions from it. Either way, the first habit is the same: count carefully, lay the data out cleanly, check the total.

This page covers two related tools:

- **Tally tables**, quick counting tool while you collect data.
- **Frequency tables**, the formal version with a count column, used everywhere afterwards.

Both are worth $1$ to $2$ marks each on the easier statistics questions, and they unlock everything that follows in this unit.

## Tally tables: groups of five

When you are counting many things, you mark each one as a vertical stroke. Every fifth stroke is drawn DIAGONALLY across the previous four, making a "five group". Then to total the count, count the five-groups and the leftover strokes.

## Frequency tables: count, total, check

A frequency table has two essential columns: the VALUE or CATEGORY and the FREQUENCY (count). The bottom-most row is always the TOTAL, which is the sum of all frequencies. The total tells you the sample size $n$.

## Grouped frequency tables

When values are continuous (heights, weights, times) or there are MANY possible values, you group them into CLASSES (intervals) before counting. Each class has a lower bound and an upper bound, written like $20 \leq x < 30$ (read: "$x$ is at least $20$ and less than $30$").

The midpoint of a class is the average of its bounds: midpoint of $20 \leq x < 30$ is $25$. You will use midpoints heavily in 9.3 when you estimate the mean from grouped data.

## Worked example: build a frequency table from a list

Twelve students recorded the number of pets they own:

$$0,\, 1,\, 2,\, 0,\, 3,\, 1,\, 1,\, 0,\, 2,\, 4,\, 1,\, 2$$

Build a frequency table.

**Step 1: List the distinct values.** From smallest to largest: $0, 1, 2, 3, 4$.

**Step 2: Count each.**

- $0$ pets: appears $3$ times ($0, 0, 0$).
- $1$ pet: appears $4$ times.
- $2$ pets: appears $3$ times.
- $3$ pets: appears $1$ time.
- $4$ pets: appears $1$ time.

**Step 3: Check the total.** $3 + 4 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 12$ $\checkmark$.

**Answer:**

| Pets | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| 0    | 3         |
| 1    | 4         |
| 2    | 3         |
| 3    | 1         |
| 4    | 1         |
| Total | 12       |

**[B1 for all frequencies correct; B1 for the total matching the sample size]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping a value** when copying tallies into the frequency table.
- **Mis-counting** when the tallies stop mid-group. Always check the total against the original list.
- **Mixing up boundaries** in grouped tables. $20 \leq x < 30$ INCLUDES $20$ but EXCLUDES $30$.
- **Using the value as the count.** The frequency is the COUNT, not the value being counted.

## Quick summary

- **Tally marks** group counts in fives for fast counting.
- **Frequency tables** show categories and their counts. The total = sample size $n$.
- **Grouped tables** use class intervals when values are continuous or numerous.
- Always **check the total** against the original data.
