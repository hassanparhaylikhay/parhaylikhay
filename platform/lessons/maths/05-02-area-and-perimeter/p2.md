## Why this matters

Triangle area is the ONLY formula on this part of the syllabus that Cambridge gives you on the formula sheet:

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$$

But the mark scheme still expects you to write the formula before substituting numbers, because the M mark is for the method, not the answer. The thing that catches students out: $b$ and $h$ must be **perpendicular** to each other. Pick the wrong "height" and your answer is wrong even with the right formula.

In this part:

- $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ (height MUST be perpendicular to the base).
- Perimeter is just the sum of the three sides.
- For right-angled triangles, the two legs ARE the base and height, and you can find the hypotenuse with Pythagoras.

## The formula in one line

$$\boxed{\;A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h\;}$$

with $h$ measured **perpendicular** to $b$.

For perimeter, just add the three sides:

$$P = a + b + c$$

## What counts as the height?

A common Cambridge trap: the "height" the question wants is not always vertical on the page. The $h$ in the formula is whatever line is **perpendicular to the chosen base** and reaches the opposite vertex. If the triangle is drawn with the base slanted, the height is also slanted but still perpendicular to the base.

For an obtuse triangle, the height can fall **outside** the triangle. The formula still works, but you may need to extend the base to drop the perpendicular onto.

## Worked example

In triangle $ABC$, the base $BC$ has length $12$ cm. The perpendicular distance from vertex $A$ to side $BC$ is $7$ cm.

Find the area of the triangle.

**Step 1: identify $b$ and $h$.** Base $b = 12$ cm. Perpendicular height $h = 7$ cm (the dashed pink line in the diagram).

**Step 2: apply the formula.**

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 7$$

**Step 3: compute.**

$$A = \boxed{42}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for the formula; A1 for $42$ with cm² units]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Right-angled triangles are easier

When the triangle is right-angled, the two **legs** (the sides next to the right angle) are already perpendicular. You don't need to drop a perpendicular: just use the two legs as $b$ and $h$ directly.

For a right triangle with legs $5$ and $12$:

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 12 = 30\;\text{cm}^2$$

The hypotenuse comes from Pythagoras: $\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ cm. So the perimeter is $5 + 12 + 13 = 30$ cm.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a slant side as the height.** A scalene triangle has three different sides; only ONE of them might be perpendicular to the base, and usually none of them are. Drop a perpendicular from the opposite vertex to the base; that's $h$.
- **Forgetting the $\tfrac{1}{2}$.** The triangle is HALF a parallelogram of base $b$ and height $h$. Without the half, you get the parallelogram's area, which is double.
- **Wrong unit on perimeter.** Area is in cm² (squared), perimeter is in cm (linear). Mixing them up costs the A1.
- **Skipping the formula on a "show your working" question.** Even though triangle area is given, write $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ first. The M mark is for the method.

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Area | $\tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ | $h$ must be perpendicular to $b$ |
| Perimeter | sum of three sides | use Pythagoras for the hypotenuse if right-angled |

The "perpendicular height" is the most-marked, most-missed concept in the chapter. When in doubt, draw the dashed line and a tiny right-angle marker on your sketch.
