## Why this matters

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides. Imagine a rectangle that has been pushed sideways at the top. That image is the key to its area formula:

If you snip a triangle off the slanted end and slide it across to the other side, you get a rectangle with the **same base** and the **same perpendicular height**. So:

$$A_{\text{parallelogram}} = A_{\text{rectangle}} = b \times h$$

The parallelogram **area is the same as a rectangle of equal base and equal perpendicular height**. The slant doesn't matter for area.

In this part:

- $A = b \times h$ where $h$ is the perpendicular height (NOT the slant side).
- $P = 2(b + s)$ where $s$ is the slant side.

## The formulas in one line

$$\boxed{\;A = b \times h \quad\text{and}\quad P = 2(b + s)\;}$$

$h$ is the **perpendicular** distance between the two parallel bases. $s$ is the **slant** side.

If a question gives you the slant side $s$ and the slant angle $\theta$, the perpendicular height is $h = s \sin\theta$. (You'll meet this again in trigonometry.)

## Worked example

A parallelogram has base $14$ cm, perpendicular height $9$ cm, and slant side $11$ cm.

(a) Find its area.

(b) Find its perimeter.

**Step a: area.**

$$A = b \times h = 14 \times 9 = \boxed{126}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for $b \times h$; A1 for $126$ with cm² units]**

Note we did NOT use the slant side. It is a distractor here.

**Step b: perimeter.**

$$P = 2(b + s) = 2(14 + 11) = 2 \times 25 = \boxed{50}\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for $2(b + s)$; A1 for $50$ with cm units]**

Full marks $= 4$.

## Why the slant doesn't change area

Picture two parallelograms with the same base $b = 10$ cm and same height $h = 4$ cm. The first is barely slanted; the second is leaned over hard. Both have area $40$ cm², because $A = bh = 10 \times 4 = 40$ in either case.

The leaned-over one **has a longer slant side**, though. So its perimeter is bigger. That is the only difference.

A reliable mental check: a parallelogram with $h$ equal to its slant side IS a rectangle (slant angle $= 90°$). So $A = b \times h$ is a generalisation of the rectangle formula.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the slant side as the height.** This is the single most-marked error on parallelogram questions. The slant is longer than the perpendicular height (unless the figure is a rectangle).
- **Doubling only one side for perimeter.** A parallelogram has TWO pairs of equal opposite sides. So $P = 2(b + s)$, not $b + s$.
- **Forgetting that $h$ might not be drawn.** Some Cambridge diagrams give you base and slant, and you have to find $h$ from context (often using trigonometry or Pythagoras with a given right triangle).
- **Mixing units.** Area cm² vs perimeter cm. Always check.

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Area | $b \times h$ (perpendicular height) |
| Perimeter | $2(b + s)$ (slant side $s$) |

If you remember one fact: **the slant is for perimeter, the perpendicular height is for area**. They are different lengths.
