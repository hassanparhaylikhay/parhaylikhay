## Why this matters

Rectangles are the easiest shape on the syllabus, but Cambridge still puts area and perimeter questions on every paper. The marks are easy if you remember three things:

- the formulas (they are NOT given to you in the exam),
- the difference between area and perimeter,
- the correct units.

In this part:

- $A = l \times w$ for a rectangle.
- $P = 2(l + w)$.
- A square is the special case where $l = w$, so $A = s^2$ and $P = 4s$.

Slide the length up and watch the area grow much faster than the perimeter. That is a clue: **area scales with the product of two dimensions**, while **perimeter scales with the sum**. Doubling both sides multiplies area by $4$ and perimeter by only $2$.

## The formulas in one line

$$\boxed{\;A = l \times w \quad\text{and}\quad P = 2(l + w)\;}$$

For a square (side $s$):

$$A = s^2, \qquad P = 4s$$

## Worked example

A rectangular swimming pool is $25$ m long and $12$ m wide.

(a) Find the area of the pool.

(b) Find the perimeter (the length of fence needed around it).

**Step a: area.** Apply the formula:

$$A = l \times w = 25 \times 12 = \boxed{300}\;\text{m}^2$$

**[M1 for $l \times w$; A1 for $300$ with m² units]**

**Step b: perimeter.** Apply the formula:

$$P = 2(l + w) = 2(25 + 12) = 2 \times 37 = \boxed{74}\;\text{m}$$

**[M1 for $2(l+w)$; A1 for $74$ with m units]**

Full marks $= 4$.

The most common slip on a 4-mark question like this is mixing up the units (writing $300$ m for the area, or $74$ m² for the perimeter). The unit is half the answer.

## When the unknown is a side

Cambridge sometimes flips the question: "a rectangle has area $48$ cm² and length $8$ cm; find its width". You rearrange:

$$w = \dfrac{A}{l} = \dfrac{48}{8} = 6\;\text{cm}$$

For perimeter the rearrangement is similar:

$$P = 2(l + w) \;\Rightarrow\; w = \dfrac{P}{2} - l$$

If $P = 30$ and $l = 9$, then $w = 15 - 9 = 6$ cm.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing area and perimeter units.** Area is in cm² (or m²); perimeter is in cm (or m). The wrong unit costs you the A1.
- **Using $l + w$ instead of $2(l + w)$ for perimeter.** That gives only HALF the perimeter (the path along two adjacent sides only).
- **Squaring the wrong number for a square.** $A = s^2$, so a square of side $7$ has area $49$, not $14$ (which would be just $7 \times 2$).
- **Forgetting to rearrange when a side is unknown.** If you are given the area and one side, divide. If you are given the perimeter and one side, halve it and subtract.

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Area of rectangle | $l \times w$ | cm², m², etc. |
| Perimeter of rectangle | $2(l + w)$ | cm, m, etc. |
| Area of square | $s^2$ | cm², m², etc. |
| Perimeter of square | $4s$ | cm, m, etc. |

Every $A$ answer ends in a squared unit. Every $P$ answer ends in a plain length unit. Always check.
