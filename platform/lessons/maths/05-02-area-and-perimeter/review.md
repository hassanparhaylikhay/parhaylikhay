## What you should know by now

If you've worked through P1 to P4 you can:

- Apply $A = lw$ and $P = 2(l + w)$ for rectangles (and $A = s^2$, $P = 4s$ for squares).
- Apply $A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$ for triangles, with $h$ measured perpendicular to $b$.
- Apply $A = bh$ for parallelograms, knowing the slant side does NOT enter the area formula.
- Apply $A = \tfrac{1}{2}(a+b) \times h$ for trapeziums, with $a$ and $b$ the parallel sides.
- Use Pythagoras to find missing sides in right-angled triangles before computing perimeter.

## Marking patterns at a glance

For a typical 2-3 mark area or perimeter question:

- **B1** for stating the correct formula (often combined with the M mark).
- **M1** for substituting the correct values into the formula.
- **A1** for the final value, **with the correct unit**.

If the question is a "show" question or compound (volume of a prism, surface area, etc.), the area formula is one line of working that earns its own M1 within a longer chain. Always state the formula explicitly.

## Comprehensive worked example

A garden has the shape of a trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB$ parallel to $DC$. The dimensions are $AB = 8$ m, $DC = 14$ m, and the perpendicular distance between $AB$ and $DC$ is $5$ m. The slant sides are $AD = 6$ m and $BC = 7$ m.

(a) Calculate the area of the garden.

(b) Calculate the perimeter of the garden.

(c) Topsoil is laid on the garden at a rate of $4$ kg per m². How much topsoil is needed?

**Step a: area.** Trapezium formula:

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(AB + DC) \times h = \tfrac{1}{2}(8 + 14) \times 5 = \tfrac{1}{2}(22)(5) = \boxed{55}\;\text{m}^2$$

**[M1 for $\tfrac{1}{2}(a+b)h$; A1 for $55$ with m² units]**

**Step b: perimeter.** Sum all four sides:

$$P = AB + BC + CD + DA = 8 + 7 + 14 + 6 = \boxed{35}\;\text{m}$$

**[A1 for $35$ with m units]**

**Step c: topsoil.** Rate $\times$ area:

$$\text{topsoil} = 4 \times 55 = \boxed{220}\;\text{kg}$$

**[M1 for rate $\times$ area; A1 for $220$ kg]**

Full marks $= 5$.

A typical 5-mark Paper 2 question packs together the area formula, perimeter, and a rate. The first stumbling block is reading off which sides are parallel. The next is keeping units consistent throughout.

## Quick reference (memorise these)

- $A_{\text{rectangle}} = lw$, $\;P_{\text{rectangle}} = 2(l + w)$
- $A_{\text{square}} = s^2$, $\;P_{\text{square}} = 4s$
- $A_{\text{triangle}} = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$ (formula given on the paper)
- $A_{\text{parallelogram}} = bh$ (perpendicular height), $\;P = 2(b + s)$
- $A_{\text{trapezium}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, $\;P = $ sum of all four sides

The biggest habit that earns marks: every time you finish an area calculation, **check the unit is squared** (cm², m²). Every time you finish a perimeter calculation, **check the unit is linear** (cm, m). Two small habits that recover a lot of dropped marks.
