## Why this matters

Once you can simplify single surds, the next step is doing arithmetic with them. The rules look like algebra: **add or subtract surds with the same "type"** (just like collecting like terms), and **multiply by combining what's under the root**. The key is to **simplify first**, before adding or subtracting.

## Adding and subtracting surds

You can only add or subtract surds with **the same number under the root**. Treat them like algebraic terms with $\sqrt{n}$ as the variable.

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{a}\sqrt{n} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{b}\sqrt{n} \;=\; (\textcolor{#fff067}{a} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{b})\sqrt{n}$$

**Example.** $3\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{2} = (3 + 4)\sqrt{2} = 7\sqrt{2}$.

If the surds are different, you cannot combine them: $3\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{5}$ is already in simplest form.

But sometimes surds **look** different but become the same after simplifying. Always simplify first:

**Example.** $\sqrt{200} - \sqrt{32}$:

- $\sqrt{200} = 10\sqrt{2}$ (since $200 = 100 \times 2$).
- $\sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}$ (since $32 = 16 \times 2$).
- Same surd $\sqrt{2}$: $10\sqrt{2} - 4\sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$.

## Multiplying surds

To multiply two surds, **multiply the numbers under the roots together**, then simplify if possible.

$$\sqrt{\textcolor{#fff067}{a}} \times \sqrt{\textcolor{#00abfa}{b}} \;=\; \sqrt{\textcolor{#fff067}{a} \times \textcolor{#00abfa}{b}}$$

**Example.** $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12} = \sqrt{36} = 6$.

**Example.** $\sqrt{6} \times \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{48} = 4\sqrt{3}$.

For surds with coefficients, multiply the coefficients and the surds separately:

$$(\textcolor{#fff067}{a}\sqrt{m})(\textcolor{#00abfa}{b}\sqrt{n}) \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{a} \cdot \textcolor{#00abfa}{b} \cdot \sqrt{m \cdot n}$$

**Example.** $2\sqrt{3} \times 5\sqrt{2} = (2 \times 5)\sqrt{3 \times 2} = 10\sqrt{6}$.

**Example.** $(\sqrt{2})^2 = \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.

The last one is worth remembering: **squaring a surd cancels the root**. $(\sqrt{n})^2 = n$.

## Expanding brackets with surds

Use the same expansion rules as algebra (FOIL or grid method).

**Example.** $(\sqrt{2} + 3)(\sqrt{2} - 1)$.

$$\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{2} \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot \sqrt{2} - 3 \cdot 1 \;=\; 2 - \sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} - 3 \;=\; -1 + 2\sqrt{2}$$

**Example.** $(\sqrt{5} + 2)(\sqrt{5} - 2)$.

This is the **difference of two squares** with surds:

$$(\sqrt{5})^2 - 2^2 \;=\; 5 - 4 \;=\; 1$$

The pattern $(\sqrt{a} + b)(\sqrt{a} - b) = a - b^2$ is huge: it kills the surd entirely. We use this trick in p3 for rationalising denominators.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})^2 \neq a + b$.** Expanding properly: $(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})^2 = a + 2\sqrt{ab} + b$. The middle term $2\sqrt{ab}$ is NOT zero unless $a$ or $b$ is zero. Don't drop it.

</div>

## Worked example

Simplify $2\sqrt{8} + 3\sqrt{18}$.

**Step 1: Simplify each surd.**

- $\sqrt{8} = \sqrt{4 \times 2} = 2\sqrt{2}$, so $2\sqrt{8} = 2 \times 2\sqrt{2} = 4\sqrt{2}$.
- $\sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \times 2} = 3\sqrt{2}$, so $3\sqrt{18} = 3 \times 3\sqrt{2} = 9\sqrt{2}$.

**Step 2: Add the like terms.**

$$4\sqrt{2} + 9\sqrt{2} \;=\; 13\sqrt{2}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{13\sqrt{2}}$. **[M1 for simplifying both surds; A1 for the final form]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Adding surds with different roots. $3\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{5}$ does NOT combine. Only same-surd terms collect.
- Forgetting to simplify before adding. $\sqrt{200} - \sqrt{32}$ looks unrelated, but both reduce to $\sqrt{2}$ form.
- Squaring two surds and forgetting the cross term. $(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})^2 = a + 2\sqrt{ab} + b$, not $a + b$.
- Multiplying coefficients into the root: $2\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{6}$ is WRONG. $2\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{4 \times 3} = \sqrt{12}$; note we square the coefficient when bringing it inside.

## Quick summary

- <b style="color:#fff067">Add/subtract</b>: only same-surd terms combine. Simplify each first.
- <b style="color:#0fee89">Multiply</b>: combine under the root. Coefficients multiply separately.
- $(\sqrt{n})^2 = n$. Squaring removes the root.
- $(\sqrt{a} + b)(\sqrt{a} - b) = a - b^2$. The "conjugate" trick that kills the surd.
