You've now seen the three skills in 1.18: simplifying surds, doing arithmetic with them, and rationalising the denominator. Cambridge tests these directly and inside other topics (Pythagoras, trig, quadratics). The single biggest lesson: **always simplify first**, both when adding/subtracting and when looking at a final answer.

## The skills you should have

- **Simplify** $\sqrt{n}$ by pulling out the largest perfect-square factor.
- **Add/subtract** surds with the same root after simplifying each.
- **Multiply** by combining under the root; coefficients separate.
- **$(\sqrt{n})^2 = n$**: squaring removes the root.
- **Rationalise** single-surd denominators by multiplying by $\sqrt{n}$.
- **Rationalise** binomial denominators by multiplying by the conjugate (opposite sign on the surd term).

## The single biggest mistake

**Splitting $\sqrt{a + b}$ as $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$.** The product rule splits multiplication ($\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$), NOT addition. $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, never $3 + 4 = 7$. This catches students every year.
