## Why this matters

Cambridge gives mixed-operation expressions all the time, and the answer depends entirely on **what order you evaluate**. Get the order wrong and you can be off by 100% on a 1-mark calculation. The rule is universal: **BIDMAS**.

Two things students miss:

1. **DM share a level.** $\div$ and $\times$ have *equal* priority, so when both appear you go **left to right**. Same for $+$ and $-$ at the bottom level.
2. **Indices come before multiplication.** $3 \times 2^2$ is $3 \times 4 = 12$, not $(3 \times 2)^2 = 36$.

## Brackets override everything

Whatever sits inside brackets gets evaluated first, no matter how complex. Then the outside resolves using the normal order.

The brackets in row 2 force the addition first; the answer changes from 23 to 35. Brackets are how you tell the order to do something other than the default.

**Trap: $\div$ and $\times$ at the same level go left to right.** $24 \div 4 \times 2$ is $6 \times 2 = 12$, NOT $24 \div 8 = 3$. Same for $20 - 8 + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17$, NOT $20 - 13 = 7$.

## Worked example

Compute $\dfrac{(7 - 3)^2 + 8}{6}$.

The horizontal fraction bar acts like a giant pair of brackets: evaluate the top fully and the bottom fully, **then** divide.

**Step 1: Top, brackets.** $(7 - 3) = 4$.

**Step 2: Top, indices.** $4^2 = 16$.

**Step 3: Top, addition.** $16 + 8 = 24$.

**Step 4: Divide by the bottom.** $24 \div 6 = 4$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{4}$. **[M1 for the brackets-first method; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Going strictly left to right ignoring BIDMAS. $3 + 4 \times 5 = 35$ is wrong; multiplication first gives $23$.
- Treating × as higher than ÷ (or + as higher than −). They share a level: go left to right.
- Forgetting that the fraction bar groups the top and the bottom. $\tfrac{7+3}{2}$ is $\tfrac{10}{2} = 5$, not $7 + \tfrac{3}{2}$.
- Squaring before dealing with brackets. $-3^2 = -9$ (the minus is outside the square), but $(-3)^2 = 9$. The brackets matter.

## Quick summary

- BIDMAS: <b style="color:#00abfa">B</b>rackets, <b style="color:#ff822c">I</b>ndices, <b style="color:#0fee89">D</b>ivision/<b style="color:#0fee89">M</b>ultiplication, <b style="color:#fff067">A</b>ddition/<b style="color:#fff067">S</b>ubtraction.
- D and M share a level; A and S share a level. Within a level, work left to right.
- A horizontal fraction bar is implicit brackets on both top and bottom.
- Replace one sub-expression at a time and write the result down. Don't try to solve in your head.
