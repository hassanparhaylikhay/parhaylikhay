## Why this matters

Picture a winter morning in Murree at $-4$°C. By midday the sun pushes it up by $7$°C, and by night it drops $9$°C below midday. Working out the night-time temperature is exactly the kind of negative-number arithmetic Cambridge loves to slip into a 1-mark question.

Negative numbers are where most O-Level arithmetic mistakes happen. Cambridge often slips a negative into otherwise easy questions to catch students who rush. Get the sign rules automatic and you stop losing marks here forever.

## Adding and subtracting on the number line

Think of the number line as a road. **Adding** moves you right, **subtracting** moves you left. The starting point is the first number; the second number is how far you move.

So $-7 + 4 = -3$. Start at $-7$, step 4 right, end at $-3$.

The same picture explains $-5 - 3$: start at $-5$, step 3 **left**, land at $-8$. Subtracting moves left.

## Two negatives become a plus

When you see a minus sign immediately followed by a negative number, the two negatives **cancel**:

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{-} \textcolor{#fff067}{(-)} \;=\; \textcolor{#0fee89}{+}$$

So:

- $7 - (-3) = 7 + 3 = 10$
- $-6 - (-4) = -6 + 4 = -2$
- $-2 - (-9) = -2 + 9 = 7$

Read it as: "minus a negative is the same as plus a positive". The two minus signs cancel out.

Multiplying or dividing two numbers: count the negatives.

The rule is short: **same signs → positive, different signs → negative**. Two negatives give a positive; a positive and a negative give a negative.

The same rule works for division: $\dfrac{-12}{3} = -4$ (different signs), $\dfrac{-12}{-3} = +4$ (same signs).

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: addition rules are different from multiplication rules.** $-3 + (-5) = -8$ (adding two negatives gives a more-negative number). $-3 \times -5 = +15$ (multiplying two negatives gives a positive). Don't mix the two up.

</div>

## Worked example: temperature change

A cold morning starts at $-4$°C. By midday the temperature has risen by $7$°C. By night it falls $9$°C below midday. What is the night-time temperature?

**Step 1: Morning to midday.** Rising means adding.

$$-4 + 7 = 3 \text{°C}$$

**Step 2: Midday to night.** Falling means subtracting.

$$3 - 9 = -6 \text{°C}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{-6\text{°C}}$. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Writing $-3 + (-5) = -2$ instead of $-8$. Both numbers are negative, the answer is *more* negative, not less.
- Writing $-3 \times -5 = -15$. Same signs multiply to give a POSITIVE.
- Confusing "subtract a negative" with "add a negative". $7 - (-3) = 10$, not $4$.
- Forgetting brackets when computing things like $-3^2$. With no brackets, $-3^2 = -9$ (square first, then negate); $(-3)^2 = +9$.

## Quick summary

- Adding moves <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">right</span>; subtracting moves <span style="color:#ff4670;font-weight:600">left</span> on the number line.
- Two minus signs in a row cancel: $a - (-b) = a + b$.
- For × and ÷: <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">same signs → positive</span>, <span style="color:#ff4670;font-weight:600">different signs → negative</span>.
- Bracket your negatives in powers: $(-3)^2 = 9$, but $-3^2 = -9$.
