## Why this matters

Decimal arithmetic on Paper 1 is non-calculator, so Cambridge expects you to handle it cleanly by hand. The key is that addition/subtraction need decimal points lined up, while multiplication needs you to count decimal places. Two different mental moves.

## Adding and subtracting: line up the decimal points

The decimal point is the anchor. Line points up vertically so the same place values sit in the same column. Pad with trailing zeros if one number has fewer decimal places.

The padded zero after $1.5$ (making it $1.50$) just makes the column structure obvious. It doesn't change the value.

For subtraction, same alignment. Borrow from the column on the left when needed: $4.20 - 1.85 = 2.35$.

## Multiplying: count the decimal places

Multiplication doesn't care about lining up the points. Instead:

1. **Multiply as if both numbers were whole numbers** (ignore the decimal points).
2. **Count the total number of decimal places** in the original two numbers.
3. **Place the decimal point** in the answer that many places from the right.

Quick sanity check: $0.4 \approx 0.5$ and $0.3 \approx 0.3$, so the answer should be roughly $0.5 \times 0.3 = 0.15$. We got $0.12$, close. Good.

A second example: $1.2 \times 0.05$. Integers: $12 \times 5 = 60$. Total places: $1 + 2 = 3$. Place the decimal $3$ from the right of $60$ (which is $060$): $0.060 = 0.06$.

## Dividing: make the divisor a whole number

Division by a decimal is awkward, so the trick is to **shift the decimal point in BOTH numbers** until the divisor is a whole number. Shifting by the same amount keeps the answer the same.

<div class="diagram">
<div class="diagram-caption">$1.6 \div 0.4$ · SHIFT BOTH</div>
<div style="font-family:var(--font-geist-mono),monospace;font-size:16px;color:#f0eeea;line-height:1.7;text-align:center">
$\dfrac{1.6}{0.4} \;\xrightarrow{\text{shift both by 1 place}}\; \dfrac{16}{4} \;=\; 4$
</div>
</div>

You shifted $0.4 \to 4$ (one place right) and $1.6 \to 16$ (one place right): same shift, value unchanged. Now it's plain integer division.

For $0.84 \div 0.4$: shift by 1 to get $\tfrac{8.4}{4} = 2.1$. (You can shift by less if it's already enough to clear the divisor.)

For $0.84 \div 0.04$: shift by 2 to get $\tfrac{84}{4} = 21$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: the answer can be larger than what you started with.** $1.6 \div 0.4 = 4$, which is bigger than $1.6$. That's correct: dividing by something less than 1 makes the answer bigger. Don't be surprised when the result jumps up.

</div>

## Worked example

A pen costs $\$0.85$. How much do $12$ pens cost?

**Step 1: Multiply as integers.** $85 \times 12 = 1020$.

(Mental: $85 \times 12 = 85 \times 10 + 85 \times 2 = 850 + 170 = 1020$.)

**Step 2: Count decimal places.** $0.85$ has 2 places, $12$ has 0 places, total = $2$.

**Step 3: Place the decimal.** $1020$ with the decimal $2$ from the right is $10.20$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\$10.20}$. **[M1 for the integer multiplication; A1 for the correct decimal placement]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Lining numbers up by their right edges instead of by the decimal point. $1.5 + 0.27$ is NOT $1.5\overline{2.7}$.
- Forgetting to count zeros in decimal places. $0.05$ has 2 decimal places, not 1.
- For division, shifting only one of the two numbers. Both have to shift the same amount.
- Treating $0.4 \times 0.3 = 1.2$. Don't lose the decimal places; count them.

## Quick summary

- <b style="color:#00abfa">+ / −</b>: align decimal points vertically, pad with zeros, then add/subtract column by column.
- <b style="color:#0fee89">×</b>: multiply as integers, then count total decimal places and place the point.
- <b style="color:#fff067">÷</b>: shift both numbers right until the divisor is a whole number, then divide normally.
