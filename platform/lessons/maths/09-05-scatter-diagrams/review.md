## What you learned in 9.5

- **Scatter diagram**: plot $(x, y)$ pairs for two numeric variables.
- **Correlation TYPE**: positive (up-right), negative (down-right), none.
- **Correlation STRENGTH**: strong (tight) vs weak (loose).
- **Line of best fit**: a single straight line, drawn with a ruler, through the cloud, points balanced.
- **Predict** $y$ from $x$: read up to the line, then across to the y-axis.
- **Interpolation** (within data range) is safer than **extrapolation** (outside).
- Correlation $\neq$ causation.

## Comprehensive worked example

A scatter diagram shows hours studied ($x$) vs test score ($y$) for $10$ students. The cloud of points climbs steeply from bottom-left to top-right with tight clustering.

(a) Describe the correlation.

(b) Interpret the correlation in context.

(c) Use the line of best fit to estimate the test score for a student who studied for $5$ hours.

**Part (a).** Strong POSITIVE correlation. **[B1 for type; B1 for strength]**

**Part (b).** As hours studied increases, test scores TEND to increase. **[B1 for an in-context statement using "tend to" or equivalent]**

**Part (c).** Reading the line at $x = 5$: $y \approx 60$. **[B1, allowing tolerance]**

Full marks $= 4$.

## Quick summary

- Type + strength describes correlation completely.
- Line of best fit is your prediction tool, but only when the correlation is reasonable.
- "Tend to" beats "always" and "causes".
