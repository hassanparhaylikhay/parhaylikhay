## Why this matters

When the correlation is at least moderate, you can draw a LINE OF BEST FIT through the scatter and use it to ESTIMATE one variable from the other. This is a common $2$- to $3$-mark question on the paper, often phrased as:

- "Draw a line of best fit on the diagram."
- "Use your line of best fit to estimate $y$ when $x = 5$."

## How to draw a line of best fit

By eye, with a ruler. The rules:

1. **One straight line.** Not curved.
2. **Through the cloud**, not above or below.
3. **Roughly even points on each side.** As many points above the line as below it, with their distances from the line balanced.
4. **The line need not pass through any specific point.** Some textbooks suggest passing through the centroid ($\bar{x}, \bar{y}$), but the eye-fit method works fine for O-Level.

A pencil with a ruler is the standard tool. Sketch lightly, check the balance, then darken.

## Using the line to predict

Once the line is drawn, you can ESTIMATE $y$ for a chosen $x$:

1. Find the chosen $x$ on the horizontal axis.
2. Go VERTICALLY up to the line.
3. Go HORIZONTALLY across to the y-axis.
4. Read the $y$ value.

This works for any $x$ that lies WITHIN the data range (the smallest and largest observed $x$ values). Estimates BEYOND that range, called extrapolation, are riskier because the trend may not continue.

If the exam asks you to estimate at $x = 50$ but the data only ranges from $0$ to $30$, you would write: "I''d use my line of best fit, but the estimate is unreliable because $x = 50$ is OUTSIDE the data range."

## Worked example: predict from a line of best fit

A scatter diagram shows hours studied vs test scores for $10$ students. The line of best fit has been drawn. Use it to estimate the test score for a student who studied for $5$ hours.

**Step 1:** Locate $x = 5$ on the horizontal axis.

**Step 2:** Move vertically up to the line of best fit.

**Step 3:** Move horizontally across to the y-axis.

**Step 4:** Read off the predicted $y$ value: approximately $60$.

**Answer:** estimated score $\approx \boxed{60}$. **[B1 for the read-off, accepting reasonable tolerance]**

Full marks $= 1$.

The mark scheme allows a small tolerance band, usually $\pm 2$ from the "exact" line position. So $58$ to $62$ would all be accepted.

## Common mistakes

- **Drawing a curve instead of a straight line.**
- **Reading off the wrong axis** (going across first instead of up).
- **Connecting points** instead of drawing a single line through them.
- **Extrapolating wildly** without noting the unreliability.
- **Forgetting a ruler.** A sketch line by hand wavers and is hard to read.

## Quick summary

- **Line of best fit** = a single straight line, drawn by eye with a ruler, through the cloud of points.
- Use it to **predict** $y$ from $x$ (or vice versa) when the correlation is at least moderate.
- **Interpolation** (within data range) is safer than **extrapolation** (outside).
- Use a ruler.
