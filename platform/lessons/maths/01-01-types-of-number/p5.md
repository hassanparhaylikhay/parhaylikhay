## What this part is about

**HCF** and **LCM** are the two questions that build directly on the prime factorisations you learned in Part 4. Get the factorisations right, and HCF/LCM become a 30-second exercise. The exam question almost always dresses them up as a word problem, "flashing lights", "boxes packed every N minutes", "the smallest number where …", but the maths underneath is exactly the same.

## The definitions

**HCF** = **Highest Common Factor**. The **biggest** number that divides both (or all) of the given numbers exactly. *("Highest" and "Greatest" mean the same here. HCF and GCD are the same thing.)*

**LCM** = **Lowest Common Multiple**. The **smallest** number that is a multiple of both (or all) of the given numbers. *("Lowest" and "Least" mean the same. LCM and LCD are the same thing.)*

In plain language:

- HCF is the biggest shared **chunk** of the numbers, what both of them have in common.
- LCM is the smallest number you would have to count up to before **both** numbers divide into it evenly.

## The prime-factorisation method

Once you have each number written as a product of primes, HCF and LCM follow a simple rule:

- **HCF:** for each prime that appears in **both** numbers, take the **lowest** power. Ignore primes that only appear in one.
- **LCM:** for each prime that appears in **either** number, take the **highest** power.

That is the whole trick. Write them side by side as a table and it becomes a picture you can read off.

## Worked example 2: with algebra

$x = 2^2 \times 3^n \times 7$ and $y = 2^3 \times 3^{n+1} \times 7$. Find the HCF of $x$ and $y$ in terms of $n$.

*(This is w25_21 Q15, two marks for picking the right powers.)*

**Working.** HCF takes the **lowest power** of each shared prime.

- For $2$: powers are $2$ and $3$. Lower is $2$, so $2^2$. **[B1 for this component]**
- For $3$: powers are $n$ and $n+1$. Lower is $n$ (since $n < n+1$ always), so $3^n$.
- For $7$: both are $7^1$, so $7^1 = 7$. **[B1 for this component]**

$$\text{HCF} = \boxed{2^2 \times 3^n \times 7}$$

Full marks $= 2$.

## Memory trick: HC**F**ew vs LC**M**ore

HC**F** → take **F**ewer (the **lowest** powers; primes only if in **both**).

LC**M** → take **M**ore (the **highest** powers; primes if in **either**).

If you're ever stuck mid-question, write the table out like above. The visual layout stops you picking the wrong powers.

## Word-problem version

Most exam HCF/LCM questions are phrased as real-world scenarios. Translating them is the skill:

| Phrasing | Maths underneath |
|---|---|
| "When do two events **next happen at the same time**?" | **LCM** of their periods |
| "What is the **largest square tile** that fits both floor dimensions?" | **HCF** of the dimensions |
| "The **smallest number of boxes** needed so each type is packed whole" | **LCM** of the item counts |
| "The **largest group** that divides two piles evenly" | **HCF** of the pile sizes |

The keyword **"next together"** or **"smallest"** → LCM. The keyword **"largest"** or **"biggest piece"** → HCF.

## Worked example 3: word problem

Two lights flash: a green light every $12$ minutes, a red light every $45$ minutes. They flash together at $11{:}00$. At what time do they next flash together?

*(This is s25_13 Q11, a standard LCM problem.)*

**Working.** "Next flash together" → LCM.

$$12 = 2^2 \times 3, \qquad 45 = 3^2 \times 5$$

**[M1 for a valid method, factor tree or listing multiples both accepted]**

$$\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180 \text{ minutes}$$

**[B2 for LCM $= 180$ seen]**

$180$ minutes $= 3$ hours. Next time together: $11{:}00 + 3\text{h} = \boxed{14{:}00}$. **[A1]**

Full marks $= 3$.

> **Examiner tip:** Cambridge rewards the **method**, showing the factor trees or listing multiples. Writing down "$180$ minutes = $3$ hours" with no method, even if the answer is right, can lose the method mark.
