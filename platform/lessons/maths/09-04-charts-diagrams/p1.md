## Why this matters

Bar charts and pictograms are the simplest visual summaries on the syllabus. Almost every paper has at least one bar-chart or pictogram question worth $1$ to $3$ marks. The skills:

- READ bars accurately, including the y-axis scale.
- COMPLETE a bar chart from a frequency table.
- USE a pictogram key correctly (halves and quarters count proportionally).

## Bar charts

Categories on the horizontal axis. Frequencies (or another value) on the vertical axis. Each bar has the same WIDTH; only the HEIGHT varies.

Key rules:

- Equal bar widths (the value is in the height, not the width).
- Gaps between bars (because categories are separate, not continuous).
- Y-axis starts at $0$ unless the chart explicitly shows a break.
- Label the axes and give the chart a title.

To READ a bar: trace from the top of the bar HORIZONTALLY to the y-axis. The y-axis value is the bar''s frequency.

## Dual bar charts (for comparing two groups)

If you want to compare two groups (boys vs girls, weekdays vs weekends), use a **dual bar chart**, two bars per category, side by side, in different colours, with a legend.

This makes comparison easy: are boys'' bars consistently taller than girls'' bars? Or is there one category where the gap reverses?

## Pictograms

A pictogram uses icons instead of bars. The KEY (legend) says how many items each icon represents. Halves and quarters count proportionally.

Always check the key first. If "🍎 = $5$" and a row shows "🍎🍎🍎🍓" (two and a half apples), the count is $2.5 \times 5 = 12.5$. (A row with a fractional answer would be unusual on the paper, so the key is usually a nice number like $4$ or $10$.)

## Worked example: read and complete

A bar chart shows weekly book sales:

| Day | Books sold |
|-|-|
| Mon | $30$ |
| Tue | $25$ |
| Wed | $40$ |
| Thu | $35$ |
| Fri | $50$ |
| Sat | $60$ |
| Sun | $20$ |

Find (a) the total books sold for the week, (b) the day with the most sales.

**Part (a).** $30 + 25 + 40 + 35 + 50 + 60 + 20 = 260$. **[B1]**

**Part (b).** The largest number is $60$ → Saturday. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- **Misreading the y-axis scale** (e.g. each gridline = $5$, not $1$).
- **Ignoring the pictogram key** and just counting symbols.
- **Forgetting to count a half-symbol** (or counting it as a full one).
- **Comparing bars from different charts with different scales**.

## Quick summary

- Bar charts: bar HEIGHT = value. Equal widths, gaps between bars, y-axis starts at $0$.
- Dual bar charts: compare two groups side by side.
- Pictograms: count icons $\times$ key value, with halves = half value.
