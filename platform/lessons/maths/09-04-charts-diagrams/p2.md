Each slice shows its angle in degrees. As you change a category''s count, every slice''s angle reshuffles so they always add up to $360°$.

## Why this matters

A pie chart shows EACH CATEGORY''S SHARE of the whole. It is the right tool when "fraction of the total" matters more than "absolute count". Common exam question types ($1$ to $3$ marks each):

- Find the angle of a given sector.
- Find the count for a sector given its angle.
- Read the largest/smallest sector.

## The two formulas

Every pie-chart question is one of these two:

$$\boxed{\text{angle} = \dfrac{\text{count}}{\text{total}} \times 360°}$$

$$\boxed{\text{count} = \dfrac{\text{angle}}{360°} \times \text{total}}$$

They are the same relationship, rearranged. The two unknowns are the ANGLE and the COUNT; everything else is given.

## Worked example: angle from count

A survey of $60$ students about favourite sport. $25$ chose cricket. Find the angle of the cricket sector.

<iframe src="/widgets/probability-step-explorer.html?preset=stats-pie-angle" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:540px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1:** Fraction of cricket students: $\dfrac{25}{60} = \dfrac{5}{12}$.

**Step 2:** Multiply by $360°$:

$$\text{angle} = \dfrac{25}{60} \times 360° = 150°$$

**Answer:** Cricket angle $= \boxed{150°}$. **[M1 for $\tfrac{25}{60} \times 360$; A1 for $150°$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example: count from angle

A pie chart for $90$ students shows the chai sector with angle $80°$. How many students chose chai?

$$\text{count} = \dfrac{80°}{360°} \times 90 = \dfrac{2}{9} \times 90 = 20$$

$\boxed{20}$ students. **[M1 for $\tfrac{80}{360} \times 90$; A1 for $20$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- **Using $100$ instead of $360°$.** $100$ is for percentages, $360°$ is for angles in a pie chart.
- **Forgetting to multiply by the total** when going from angle to count.
- **Not checking that angles sum to $360°$.** If they don''t, something has been mis-counted.
- **Confusing "biggest sector" with "biggest angle"**. They are the same thing.

## Quick summary

- Pie chart: each sector represents a category, with angle proportional to count.
- Sum of all angles $= 360°$.
- $\text{angle} = \dfrac{\text{count}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$ (and its rearrangement).
- Best for showing FRACTIONS of the whole; worse than bar charts for direct comparison.
