## What you learned in 9.4

- **Bar charts** show frequencies as bar heights. Equal widths, gaps between bars, y-axis starts at $0$.
- **Dual bar charts** compare two groups; use a legend.
- **Pictograms** use icons with a key; halves count as half the key value.
- **Pie charts** show fractions of a whole; angle $= \dfrac{\text{count}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$.

## Comprehensive worked example

$120$ students were surveyed about favourite drink: $30$ chose chai, $40$ chose coffee, $20$ chose juice, $15$ chose milk, $15$ chose water.

(a) Find the angle for each sector on a pie chart.

(b) Which drink is most popular?

(c) On a bar chart, which bar would be the tallest?

**Part (a).** Sample size $= 120$. Each angle = $\dfrac{\text{count}}{120} \times 360° = \text{count} \times 3°$.

- Chai: $30 \times 3 = 90°$
- Coffee: $40 \times 3 = 120°$
- Juice: $20 \times 3 = 60°$
- Milk: $15 \times 3 = 45°$
- Water: $15 \times 3 = 45°$

Check: $90 + 120 + 60 + 45 + 45 = 360°$ $\checkmark$. **[B2 for all five angles correct; B1 for partial]**

**Part (b).** Coffee, with $40$ students. **[B1]**

**Part (c).** Coffee, same answer, the bar would be tallest because it has the largest frequency. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 4$.

The "$\text{count} \times 3°$" shortcut works any time the sample size divides evenly into $360$. Spot it and the arithmetic is fast.

## Quick summary

- Bar chart: heights compare.
- Pie chart: angles show fractions of total.
- Pictogram: count icons $\times$ key.
- Always SUM-CHECK pie chart angles to $360°$.
