## Why this matters

A job in Pakistan pays between Rs. $30\,000$ and Rs. $60\,000$ per month depending on experience. Algebraically: $30\,000 \leq S \leq 60\,000$. That's a **compound inequality**: a range with both a lower and an upper bound on the same variable.

Cambridge asks you to (1) solve compound inequalities to express the range cleanly, (2) draw the result on a number line, and (3) list integer values inside the range. Each task is one mark, and they appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2 routinely.

## What a compound inequality looks like

The standard form has the unknown sandwiched between two bounds:

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{a} \leq \textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{expression in } x} < \textcolor{#fff067}{b}$$

where $\textcolor{#fff067}{a}$ is the **lower bound** and $\textcolor{#fff067}{b}$ is the **upper bound**. Either bound can be strict ($<$ / $>$) or inclusive ($\leq$ / $\geq$); they don't have to match.

Examples:

- $-3 \leq 2x - 1 < 5$ : the expression $2x - 1$ is at least $-3$ and strictly less than $5$.
- $1 < x + 2 < 7$ : straightforward range on $x + 2$.
- $-1 \leq y \leq 1$ : the simplest form, $y$ alone in the middle.

## Solving by working on all three parts

The technique: **whatever you do, do it to ALL THREE parts** : the lower bound, the middle expression, and the upper bound. Like a regular equation, but with three pieces instead of two.

**Walk.** Solve $-3 \leq 2x - 1 < 5$.

**Step 1: Add $1$ to all three parts.**

$$-3 + 1 \leq 2x - 1 + 1 < 5 + 1$$

$$-2 \leq 2x < 6$$

**Step 2: Divide all three parts by $+2$.**

$$-1 \leq x < 3$$

The inequalities stay the same direction (we divided by a positive). Final answer: $-1 \leq x < 3$.

If you ever need to divide by a NEGATIVE, both inequality signs flip. (Cambridge avoids this in compound questions but it's the same rule from p1.)

## Listing integer solutions

Cambridge often asks: "List the integer values of $x$ such that ..." Walk through every integer in the range, **including or excluding boundaries** based on whether they're $\leq$ or $<$.

**Example.** List integers satisfying $-3 \leq x \leq 2$.

The $\leq$ on both sides means both boundaries are INCLUDED. Integers in the range: $-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$. **Six values.**

**Example.** List integers satisfying $-1 < x \leq 4$.

The $<$ on the left means $-1$ is EXCLUDED; the $\leq$ on the right means $4$ is INCLUDED. Integers in the range: $0, 1, 2, 3, 4$. **Five values.**

The bookkeeping is small but the marks are exact: miss a boundary value and you lose a mark.

## Compound inequalities with no solution

If the upper bound is **less than** the lower bound, the compound inequality has **no solution** (the interval is empty).

**Example.** $3 < x < 1$ requires $x$ to be greater than $3$ AND less than $1$ at the same time. No such $x$ exists.

Always check: the upper bound should be **greater than or equal to** the lower bound for solutions to exist.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: every operation hits all three parts.** If you only modify the middle and one end, the inequality breaks. $-3 \leq 2x - 1 < 5$ becomes $-3 \leq 2x < 6$ (add $1$ to all THREE), not $-3 \leq 2x < 5$ or $-2 \leq 2x < 5$.

## Worked example

Solve $-7 < 3 - 2x \leq 5$.

**Step 1: Subtract $3$ from all three parts.**

$$-10 < -2x \leq 2$$

**Step 2: Divide all three parts by $-2$.** Negative divisor, both inequalities FLIP.

$$5 > x \geq -1$$

**Step 3: Rewrite in standard "increasing" form** (lower bound on the left).

$$-1 \leq x < 5$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{-1 \leq x < 5}$. **[M1 for the subtraction; M1 for the division-and-flip; A1 for the final form]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Modifying only one or two parts of the three. Every operation must hit all three.
- Forgetting to flip BOTH inequality signs when dividing by a negative. Both signs flip together.
- Confusing strict vs inclusive on the number line. Closed = inclusive, open = strict.
- Including or excluding boundary integers wrongly when listing. $-3 \leq x < 4$ includes $-3$ but NOT $4$.

## Quick summary

- Compound inequality: $a \leq \text{expression} < b$ (or any combination of $<$ and $\leq$ on the two ends).
- Solve by doing the same operation to **all three parts**.
- Multiplying / dividing by a negative flips both inequality signs.
- On a number line: closed circle for $\leq$ / $\geq$, open for $<$ / $>$, line segment between.
- Listing integers: walk through every integer in the range, respecting boundaries.
