## What you should know by now

If you've worked through P1 to P3 you can:

- Solve linear inequalities using the four basic moves and the **flip rule** (negative multiplier flips the sign).
- Show the solution on a number line: closed for $\leq$ / $\geq$, open for $<$ / $>$, arrow in the direction of solutions.
- Solve compound inequalities by working on **all three parts** at once.
- List integer values inside a range, respecting boundary inclusion.
- Sketch the region defined by a linear inequality in two variables: solid vs broken boundary, half-plane choice via test point.
- Combine multiple inequalities to define a **feasible region** (the intersection of all half-planes).

## Marking patterns at a glance

This sub-topic is a regular Paper 1/2 component:

- **B1** for solving a simple linear inequality.
- **M1 + A1** for "solve and show on a number line": M for solving, A for the correct number-line drawing.
- **M1, M1, A1** for compound inequalities (each step preserves the structure).
- **M1, M1, A1** for region sketching: M for boundary line, M for solid/broken classification, A for the shaded region.

The trap markers Cambridge uses repeatedly:

- Failing to flip when dividing by a negative.
- Wrong circle on the number line.
- Solid line where broken is needed (or vice versa).
- Counting boundary integers as included when they're excluded by a strict $<$.

## Worked example: combine

Solve $\dfrac{2x - 1}{3} \leq 5$ and show the solution on a number line.

**Step 1: Multiply both sides by $+3$.**

$$2x - 1 \leq 15$$

**Step 2: Add $1$.**

$$2x \leq 16$$

**Step 3: Divide by $+2$.**

$$x \leq 8$$

**Step 4: Number line.** Closed circle at $8$ (because $\leq$); arrow pointing left.

**Answer:** $\boxed{x \leq 8}$. **[M1 for clearing the fraction; A1 for the value; B1 for the number line]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Quick reference

- Flip rule: ÷ or × by negative → flip the sign.
- Number line: closed for inclusive, open for strict.
- Compound: act on all three parts together.
- 2D region: solid boundary for inclusive, broken for strict; test a point to decide the half-plane.
- Multiple constraints: intersect them.
