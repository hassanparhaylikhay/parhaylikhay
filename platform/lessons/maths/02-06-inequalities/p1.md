## Why this matters

To pass a Cambridge subject you need **at least** $80$ marks. Written algebraically: $\text{marks} \geq 80$. The "at least" turned into a $\geq$ symbol, with a single threshold value. That is an **inequality**: a maths sentence with a $<$, $>$, $\leq$, or $\geq$ instead of an $=$.

Cambridge tests inequalities directly on Paper 1 (algebra) and Paper 2 (graph regions). The mechanics are nearly identical to solving equations from 2.5 p1, with **one extra rule**: multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number FLIPS the inequality. Watch that single rule and the rest is familiar territory.

## The four moves

A linear inequality is solved by the same four moves as a linear equation:

- **Add** the same thing to both sides
- **Subtract** the same thing from both sides
- **Multiply** both sides by the same non-zero number
- **Divide** both sides by the same non-zero number

The added rule:

<div class="trap-callout">

**The flip rule: multiplying or dividing by a NEGATIVE flips the inequality.**

If you do $-1 \times \text{both sides}$ to $-2 < 5$, you get $2 > -5$ (the original $<$ became $>$). The same applies when dividing by a negative. If the multiplier is positive, the inequality direction stays the same.

</div>

Whenever you have to divide by a negative, **note it explicitly** in your working: "Dividing by $-2$, sign flips."

## Walk: simple solve

Solve $3x + 5 > 11$.

**Step 1: Subtract $5$.**

$$3x > 6$$

**Step 2: Divide by $+3$** (positive, sign stays).

$$x > 2$$

The solution is every value of $x$ greater than $2$. On a number line: an **open** circle at $2$ (because the $>$ is strict), with an arrow pointing right.

## Walk: divide by a negative

Solve $4 - 2x \geq 10$.

**Step 1: Subtract $4$.**

$$-2x \geq 6$$

**Step 2: Divide by $-2$.** Negative divisor, the inequality FLIPS.

$$x \leq -3$$

Solution: $x$ is at most $-3$. Number line: closed circle at $-3$, arrow pointing left.

## Walk: $x$ on both sides

Solve $5x - 3 < 2x + 9$.

**Step 1: Subtract $2x$** to collect $x$-terms on the left.

$$3x - 3 < 9$$

**Step 2: Add $3$.**

$$3x < 12$$

**Step 3: Divide by $+3$.**

$$x < 4$$

Just like solving an equation, but with the inequality preserved.

## Showing the solution on a number line

Cambridge often asks you to **show** the solution on a number line. Two visual cues:

- **Open circle** ($\circ$) at the boundary if the inequality is strict ($<$ or $>$).
- **Closed circle** ($\bullet$) at the boundary if the inequality is inclusive ($\leq$ or $\geq$).
- **Arrow** points in the direction of solutions.

| Inequality | Boundary | Arrow |
|---|---|---|
| $x > 2$ | open at $\textcolor{#fff067}{2}$ | right |
| $x \geq 2$ | closed at $\textcolor{#fff067}{2}$ | right |
| $x < -1$ | open at $\textcolor{#fff067}{-1}$ | left |
| $x \leq -1$ | closed at $\textcolor{#fff067}{-1}$ | left |

The closed/open distinction is **always tested** when Cambridge wants the number line. Don't lose the easy mark by drawing the wrong circle.

## Worked example

Solve $7 - 3x \leq 4 - x$.

**Step 1: Add $3x$ to both sides** (so $x$-terms collect on the right with a positive coefficient).

$$7 \leq 4 + 2x$$

**Step 2: Subtract $4$.**

$$3 \leq 2x$$

**Step 3: Divide by $+2$** (positive, sign stays).

$$\dfrac{3}{2} \leq x$$

Same as $x \geq \dfrac{3}{2}$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{x \geq \dfrac{3}{2}}$. **[M1 for collecting; A1 for the final form]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to flip the inequality when dividing by a negative. $-2x > 6$ gives $x < -3$, NOT $x > -3$.
- Drawing the wrong circle on the number line. $\leq$ / $\geq$ → closed (filled). $<$ / $>$ → open (empty).
- Reversing the arrow direction. $x > 5$ has the arrow going RIGHT (toward larger values), not left.
- Solving like an equation but writing $=$ in the answer. The answer to an inequality is an inequality, not an equation.

## Quick summary

- Inequalities use $<$, $>$, $\leq$, $\geq$ instead of $=$.
- Solve with the same four moves as a linear equation.
- **Multiplying or dividing by a negative FLIPS** the inequality direction.
- Show the solution on a number line: open vs closed circle at the boundary, arrow in the direction of solutions.
