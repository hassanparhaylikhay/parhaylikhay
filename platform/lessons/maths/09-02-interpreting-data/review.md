## What you learned in 9.2

- **Reading values** off frequency tables, bar charts, pictograms, pie charts.
- **Pie chart angle** $= \dfrac{\text{count}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$.
- **Pictogram counts**: number of symbols $\times$ key value, halves count as half.
- **Comparing data sets**: ALWAYS compare BOTH centre AND spread.
- **Centre + spread pairings**: mean ↔ range; median ↔ IQR.

## Comprehensive worked example

The bar chart below (described in text) shows daily sales at two shops, A and B:

- Shop A: $40, 50, 60, 50, 45, 70, 80$ (Mon through Sun, units sold).
- Shop B: $58, 60, 56, 60, 55, 56, 60$.

(a) Find the total weekly sales for each shop.

(b) Find the mean daily sales for each shop.

(c) Find the range of daily sales for each shop.

(d) Compare the two shops in one short paragraph.

**Part (a) totals.** A: $40 + 50 + 60 + 50 + 45 + 70 + 80 = 395$. B: $58 + 60 + 56 + 60 + 55 + 56 + 60 = 405$.

**Part (b) means.** A: $395 / 7 = 56.4$. B: $405 / 7 = 57.9$. **[M1 each, A1 each]**

**Part (c) ranges.** A: $80 - 40 = 40$. B: $60 - 55 = 5$. **[B1 each]**

**Part (d) comparison.**

Shop B had a slightly higher mean daily sales ($57.9$ vs $56.4$), so on average B sold more units per day. Shop A has a MUCH larger range ($40$ vs $5$), so A''s daily sales were far more variable while B''s sales were very consistent across the week. **[B1 for the centre comparison; B1 for the spread comparison with interpretation]**

Full marks $= 6$.

## Common mistakes across the unit

- **Reading the wrong gridline** on a bar chart. Use a ruler if needed.
- **Forgetting the pictogram key**.
- **Comparing only one measure** when the question asks to compare two data sets.
- **Confusing total with mean**. Large total ≠ large mean; the sample sizes might differ.

## Quick summary

- Read tables and charts carefully. Note the scale, the key, and the units.
- Pie chart: angle $/ 360°$ = count $/$ total.
- Comparing data sets requires BOTH a centre statement AND a spread statement.
- "Typically" beats "always" in language.
