## What the formula says

For any triangle with two sides $a$ and $b$ and the **included** angle $C$ between them:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} ab \sin C$$

It's given on the formula sheet. Use it whenever you have two sides and the angle between them, the same setup as the cosine rule's find-a-side case.

## Why it works

Drop a perpendicular from $A$ down to side $a$. That perpendicular has length $b\sin C$ (because in the right triangle it makes with side $b$, the opposite to angle $C$ is the perpendicular and the hypotenuse is $b$). So:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times a \times b\sin C$$

It's the same $\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{base}\,\times\,\text{height}$ formula you've always known, dressed up for non-right triangles. The $\sin C$ factor is what computes the height for you from the slant side.

## Worked example: find the area

A triangle has sides $a = 9$ cm and $b = 6$ cm with the included angle $\angle C = 40°$. Find the area.

## Worked example: find the included angle from a given area

A triangle has area $24$ cm² and sides $a = 8$, $b = 10$. Find the included angle $C$ (assume $C$ is acute).

Rearrange:

$$24 = \tfrac{1}{2}(8)(10)\sin C = 40\sin C$$

$$\sin C = \dfrac{24}{40} = 0.6$$

$$C = \sin^{-1}(0.6) \approx 36.9°$$

(If the question allowed an obtuse triangle, $C = 180° - 36.9° = 143.1°$ would also fit.)

**[M1 for $24 = \tfrac{1}{2}(8)(10)\sin C$; M1 for $\sin C = 0.6$; A1 for $C = 36.9°$]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Which angle is "C"?

The formula is symmetric in side names: $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C = \tfrac{1}{2}bc\sin A = \tfrac{1}{2}ac\sin B$. All three forms compute the same area. Just match the angle to the pair of sides whose included it is.

In practice, Cambridge gives you two specific sides and an angle. If that angle is between them, plug in. If not, use the cosine rule first to find the missing piece, then apply the area formula.

## Why $C = 90°$ recovers the familiar case

If $C = 90°$, then $\sin C = 1$ and:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}ab \times 1 = \tfrac{1}{2}ab$$

That's the area of a right-angled triangle with legs $a$ and $b$, exactly $\tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{leg}_1 \times \text{leg}_2$. The general formula collapses to the special case smoothly.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong angle.** The angle MUST be the one between the two sides you're using. Picking a non-included angle gives the wrong area.
- **Forgetting the $\tfrac{1}{2}$.** Half. Always half. (The full $ab\sin C$ is the area of the PARALLELOGRAM with sides $a$, $b$, and angle $C$.)
- **Calculator in radians.** $\sin 90°$ should be $1$. If you get a different value, switch to DEG.
- **Reporting $\sin C$ instead of $C$ on inverse problems.** The question asks for the angle.

## Quick summary

- Area formula: $\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, where $C$ is the angle BETWEEN sides $a$ and $b$.
- Works for any triangle, right-angled or not.
- Collapses to $\tfrac{1}{2}ab$ when $C = 90°$.
- Reverse-direction: rearrange to find $\sin C$, then $\sin^{-1}$.
- Provided on the formula sheet: no need to memorise the form, but you must know which angle is "C".
