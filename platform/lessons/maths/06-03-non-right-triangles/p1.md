## Why this matters

So far every trigonometry problem you have seen has had a right angle in it. SOH-CAH-TOA needs that $90°$ corner, because the names "opposite", "adjacent", and "hypotenuse" are defined relative to it.

But Cambridge often gives you a triangle with **no right angle at all**. A coastline triangle, a roof truss, the diagonal of an irregular field. For those, sin/cos/tan in their right-triangle form cannot act directly. Two new rules close the gap: the **sine rule** and the **cosine rule**. Both extend trigonometry to any triangle, and both are given on the formula sheet.

The shortcut: if your known angle is **opposite a known side**, reach for the sine rule. If the known angle is **between two known sides**, reach for the cosine rule.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying SOH-CAH-TOA without a right angle.** No 90° → no hypotenuse → ratio is undefined.
- **Mismatching sides and angles.** Side $a$ goes with angle $A$. Always. Mix this up and the sine rule produces nonsense.
- **Forgetting the formula sheet.** All three formulas are provided. Look at the front of the paper before scribbling them from memory.

## Quick summary

- Right-angled trig works only when one angle is exactly $90°$.
- Most Cambridge triangle questions involve at least one non-right triangle somewhere.
- The sine rule and cosine rule together cover every triangle case.
- The formula sheet gives you sine rule, cosine rule, and $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. No memorisation required.
- Side $a$ is opposite vertex $A$, side $b$ opposite $B$, side $c$ opposite $C$.
