## Why this matters

WhatsApp messages get forwarded. One person sends to $5$ friends, each of whom forwards to $5$ more, and so on. After $4$ rounds, more than $600$ people have it. That's exponential growth, and it's why "going viral" really means "growing exponentially".

The same maths runs population growth, savings interest (the compound interest you met in 1.13), and bacteria cultures : in every case, the variable appears in the **exponent**. Cambridge tests $y = a \cdot b^x + c$ on Paper 2 directly, and the syllabus singles out exponential graphs in their own bullet.

## What an exponential function looks like

$$y = a \cdot b^x + c$$

- **$b$** is the **base** : a positive integer ($2, 3, 4, \ldots$) or a fraction ($\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{3}, \ldots$).
- **$a$** is the **scale** : how large the curve is at $x = 0$ (since $b^0 = 1$, the curve passes through $(0, a + c)$).
- **$c$** is the **vertical shift** : moves the whole curve up or down.

Two cases drive the shape:

| Case | $b$ | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | $b > 1$ | curve rises steeply on the right, flattens to $y = c$ on the left |
| Decay | $0 < b < 1$ | curve rises steeply on the left, flattens to $y = c$ on the right |

In both cases there is a **horizontal asymptote** at $y = c$ : the curve approaches but never crosses that height.

## Key features of the graph

For a standard $y = a \cdot b^x$ (with $c = 0$):

- **$y$-intercept** is at $(0, a)$. Always. Because $b^0 = 1$.
- **No $x$-intercept** for $a > 0$ and $b > 0$ : the curve stays above the $x$-axis everywhere.
- **Horizontal asymptote** $y = 0$ on one side (left for growth, right for decay).
- **Domain**: all real $x$. **Range**: $y > 0$ when $a > 0$.

When $c \neq 0$, the whole curve shifts up by $c$ : asymptote moves to $y = c$, $y$-intercept moves to $(0, a + c)$.

## Worked example: growth

A bacteria population $P$ doubles every hour. At $t = 0$ hours there are $50$ bacteria.

(a) Write a formula for $P$ in terms of $t$.

(b) Find $P$ after $5$ hours.

(c) Sketch $P$ against $t$ for $0 \leq t \leq 5$.

**Step 1 (a).** Doubling each hour means multiplying by $2$ each hour:

$$P = 50 \times 2^t$$

**Step 2 (b).** At $t = 5$:

$$P = 50 \times 2^5 = 50 \times 32 = 1600$$

**Step 3 (c).** Smooth curve passing through $(0, 50)$ and $(5, 1600)$, rising sharply. Below is the shape :

## Worked example: decay

A car worth $\$20{,}000$ today loses $20\%$ of its value each year.

(a) Write a formula for the value $V$ after $t$ years.

(b) Find $V$ after $3$ years.

**Step 1 (a).** Losing $20\%$ each year means multiplying by $0.8$ each year (keeping $80\%$):

$$V = 20{,}000 \times 0.8^t$$

**Step 2 (b).** At $t = 3$:

$$V = 20{,}000 \times 0.8^3 = 20{,}000 \times 0.512 = 10{,}240$$

**Answer:** **(a)** $V = 20{,}000 \times 0.8^t$. **(b)** $V = \$10{,}240$. **[B1 for formula; M1 for substituting; A1 for $10{,}240$]**

Full marks $= 3$.

This is a Cambridge sub-topic 1.17 problem too : exponential growth and decay shows up in both unit 1 (numerical) and unit 2 (graph form).

## Common mistakes

- Writing $y = a + b^x$ instead of $y = a \cdot b^x$. The constant multiplies (it doesn't add).
- Confusing "doubles every hour" with "grows by $2$ each hour". Doubling = multiply by $2$; growing by $2$ = adding $2$ (linear, not exponential).
- Forgetting that $b^0 = 1$. The $y$-intercept of $y = a \cdot b^x$ is always $(0, a)$.
- Drawing the curve crossing the $x$-axis. For $a > 0$ and $b > 0$, the curve is always above zero.

## Quick summary

- Exponential form: $y = a \cdot b^x + c$, with $b > 0$.
- $b > 1$ → growth (rises right). $b < 1$ → decay (falls right).
- $y$-intercept at $(0, a + c)$. Asymptote at $y = c$.
- "Doubles every hour" means $\times 2^t$. "Loses $20\%$ each year" means $\times 0.8^t$.
