## Why this matters

A recipe calls for $\tfrac{6x}{9x}$ tablespoons of sugar. You'd cancel the shared $\textcolor{#fff067}{3x}$ to get $\tfrac{2}{3}$ before measuring. **Simplifying a rational expression** in algebra works the same way: factor the top, factor the bottom, then cancel anything that's a factor of both.

This part is the algebraic-fraction version of "writing fractions in simplest form" from 1.4. Cambridge tests it directly on Paper 2 and uses it inside equation-solving and function-composition questions. The hard part isn't the cancelling, it's the **factorising**, which is why we did all of 2.2 first.

## The method

To simplify $\dfrac{\text{top}}{\text{bottom}}$:

1. **Factorise the top fully.** Common factors, DOTS, perfect square, factorising quadratics: whichever apply.
2. **Factorise the bottom fully.**
3. **Cancel any factor that appears in both.**
4. What's left is the simplified form.

The key word is **fully**. Until both top and bottom are written as products, you can't see what cancels.

## Example: factor a DOTS, cancel

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2}$.

**Step 1: Factor the top.** $x^2 - 4 = \textcolor{#fff067}{(x - 2)(x + 2)}$ (difference of squares).

**Step 2: Factor the bottom.** $x + 2$ is already in factored form.

**Step 3: Cancel.** $\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}$ appears in both top and bottom.

$$\dfrac{(x - 2)\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}}{\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}} \;=\; x - 2$$

So $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2} = x - 2$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: cancel only what's a FACTOR.** $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2}$ does NOT simplify to $x - \dfrac{4}{2} = x - 2$ by some "cancel parts" trick. It works because we factored to $(x-2)(x+2)$ first, and then $(x+2)$ is a true factor. Without the factoring step, you cannot cancel.

</div>

## Example: factor a quadratic, cancel

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{x - 2}$.

**Step 1: Factor the top.** Two numbers multiplying to $6$ and adding to $-5$: $-2$ and $-3$. So $x^2 - 5x + 6 = \textcolor{#fff067}{(x - 2)(x - 3)}$.

**Step 2: Factor the bottom.** $x - 2$ is a single factor.

**Step 3: Cancel $(x - 2)$.**

$$\dfrac{\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x - 2)}(x - 3)}{\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x - 2)}} \;=\; x - 3$$

## Example: both top and bottom factor

Simplify $\dfrac{2x + 4}{x^2 - 4}$.

**Step 1: Factor the top.** Common factor $2$: $2(x + 2)$.

**Step 2: Factor the bottom.** DOTS: $(x - 2)(x + 2)$.

**Step 3: Cancel $(x + 2)$.**

$$\dfrac{2\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}}{(x - 2)\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}} \;=\; \dfrac{2}{x - 2}$$

The answer keeps a factor on the bottom. That's normal: the simplified form often still has letters in the denominator.

## Example: numbers AND letters cancel

Simplify $\dfrac{3x^2 - 12}{6x + 12}$.

**Step 1: Factor the top fully.**

$$3x^2 - 12 \;=\; 3(x^2 - 4) \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{3}(x - 2)\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}$$

**Step 2: Factor the bottom fully.**

$$6x + 12 \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{6}\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}$$

**Step 3: Cancel $\textcolor{#fff067}{3}$ (the "3 into 6") and $\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x + 2)}$.**

$$\dfrac{\cancel{3}(x - 2)\cancel{(x + 2)}}{\cancel{6}^{2} \cancel{(x + 2)}} \;=\; \dfrac{x - 2}{2}$$

The $\textcolor{#fff067}{3}$ in the top divides into the $\textcolor{#fff067}{6}$ in the bottom, leaving a $2$.

## Worked example

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 2x}{x^2 - 5x + 6}$.

**Step 1: Factor the top.** Common factor $x$: $x(x - 2)$.

**Step 2: Factor the bottom.** Two numbers multiplying to $6$, adding to $-5$: $-2$ and $-3$. So $(x - 2)(x - 3)$.

**Step 3: Cancel $(x - 2)$.**

$$\dfrac{x\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x - 2)}}{\textcolor{#0fee89}{(x - 2)}(x - 3)} \;=\; \dfrac{x}{x - 3}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{\dfrac{x}{x - 3}}$. **[M1 for top; M1 for bottom; A1 for the simplified form]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Cancelling a term inside a sum. $\dfrac{x + 5}{x + 3}$ does NOT simplify to $\dfrac{5}{3}$. The $x$ is a term, not a factor of the whole top.
- Forgetting to fully factor first. If the top is $x^2 + 5x + 6$ and you leave it as is, no cancellation is visible. Factor to $(x + 2)(x + 3)$ first.
- Stopping before checking for a NUMERIC common factor. After cancelling letter factors, recheck whether the numbers can also simplify (e.g., $\tfrac{4}{6}$ goes to $\tfrac{2}{3}$).
- Wrong sign on the factored quadratic. Always verify by expanding back.

## Quick summary

- **Factor top fully**, **factor bottom fully**, then **cancel** any factor that appears in both.
- Cancellation requires a **factor** of the whole top or bottom, not just a term inside.
- Numeric common factors and bracket factors both cancel; check both.
- Simplified form usually still has letters; that's normal.
