## Why this matters

Similar shapes appear in almost every Cambridge geometry paper. They drive questions on triangles inside triangles (with parallel lines), on map and model scaling, and on surface area and volume of similar solids. The whole topic rests on one idea: if two shapes are similar, every length on the second is the same multiple of the matching length on the first. Get the length scale factor right and the rest follows. Get it wrong and you lose every mark in the question.

## What "similar" means

Two shapes are **similar** if they have the same shape but possibly different sizes. More precisely:

- **Corresponding angles** are equal.
- **Corresponding sides** are in the same ratio.

Think of a passport photo and a poster of the same photo. Every angle is identical; every length on the poster is some fixed multiple, say $5$ times, the matching length on the photo. That multiple is the **length scale factor**, written $k$.

## The length scale factor $k$

If two shapes are similar, the **length scale factor from shape $A$ to shape $B$** is

$$k = \dfrac{\text{a length on } B}{\text{matching length on } A}$$

A few things to remember:

- $k$ is just a number. It can be greater than $1$ (enlargement), less than $1$ (reduction), or even $1$ (the shapes are congruent).
- Use **matching** (corresponding) sides only. Pairing the wrong sides gives the wrong $k$.
- Once you find $k$, every other length on $B$ is $k$ times the matching length on $A$.

The most important sentence in this whole topic:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{new length} = k \times \text{old length}}$$

## How to identify corresponding sides

If a question gives a clean diagram with letters, corresponding sides are usually labelled in the same order. For triangles $ABC$ and $PQR$:

- $AB \leftrightarrow PQ$
- $BC \leftrightarrow QR$
- $CA \leftrightarrow RP$

If a question does not pair the letters for you, match by **angle**. The side opposite the smallest angle in one triangle corresponds to the side opposite the smallest angle in the other, and similarly for the middle and largest angles.

When two triangles share a vertex (very common in Cambridge), look at the diagram carefully: the shared angle pairs with itself, and the parallel lines tell you which other angles match.

When $DE \parallel BC$:

- $\angle A$ is shared.
- $\angle ADE = \angle ABC$ (corresponding angles, parallel lines).
- $\angle AED = \angle ACB$ (corresponding angles, parallel lines).

Three pairs of equal angles, so the triangles are similar. The corresponding sides are $AD \leftrightarrow AB$, $AE \leftrightarrow AC$, $DE \leftrightarrow BC$.

## Showing two triangles are similar

Cambridge does **not** ask you to prove congruence. It does sometimes ask you to **show** two triangles are similar by giving geometric reasons. The standard test is **AA** (two pairs of equal angles): if two pairs match, the third must match too because all angles in a triangle add to $180°$.

Common reasons to quote:

- Angles in the same segment are equal (circle work).
- Alternate or corresponding angles equal because lines are parallel.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- The triangles share an angle (sometimes called the "common angle").

For full marks Cambridge wants **two clear angle reasons** stated, then the conclusion "so the triangles are similar (AA)".

## Worked example

In triangle $ABC$, the point $D$ lies on $AB$ and the point $E$ lies on $AC$ with $DE \parallel BC$. $AD = 4$ cm, $AB = 10$ cm, and $BC = 7.5$ cm. Find the length of $DE$.

**Step 1: Show the triangles are similar.**

$\angle A$ is shared. $\angle ADE = \angle ABC$ (corresponding angles, $DE \parallel BC$). So triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar (AA). **[M1 for two correct angle pairs]**

**Step 2: Identify corresponding sides and find $k$.**

The corresponding pair we know is $AD \leftrightarrow AB$.

$$k = \dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{4}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}$$

Note: $k < 1$ because triangle $ADE$ is smaller than triangle $ABC$.

**Step 3: Apply $k$ to the side we want.**

$DE$ corresponds to $BC$, so

$$DE = k \times BC = \dfrac{2}{5} \times 7.5 = 3$$

**Answer:** $DE = 3$ cm. **[M1 for the scale factor; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Using non-corresponding sides for $k$. Always pair sides that are opposite equal angles.
- Forgetting to flip the scale factor. $k$ from small to large is the reciprocal of $k$ from large to small. Pick a direction and stick to it.
- Quoting only one angle reason when showing similarity. Cambridge wants two.
- Confusing "similar" with "congruent". Similar = same shape. Congruent = same shape AND same size.
- Skipping the geometric reason. "They look similar" earns zero marks. State the angle facts.

## Quick summary

- Similar shapes have <b style="color:#00abfa">equal corresponding angles</b> and <b style="color:#fff067">sides in the same ratio</b>.
- Length scale factor $k = \dfrac{\text{new}}{\text{old}}$, computed from one pair of matching sides.
- New length $= k \times$ old length.
- For triangles, two pairs of equal angles (AA) is enough to prove similarity.
- Cambridge wants <b style="color:#0fee89">two angle reasons</b> stated when you show similarity.
