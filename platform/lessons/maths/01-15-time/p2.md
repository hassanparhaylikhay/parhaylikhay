## Why this matters

Cambridge questions use both **12-hour** clock format ($3.15$ a.m., $7.45$ p.m.) and **24-hour** format ($03\,15$, $19\,45$). Most timetable questions and time-zone questions use 24-hour. The skill: switch fluently between the two and compute elapsed times across noon or midnight.

## The two formats

The 12-hour clock uses $1$ to $12$ with **a.m.** for morning (midnight to noon) and **p.m.** for afternoon and evening (noon to midnight).

The 24-hour clock uses $0$ to $23$ for the hour, with no a.m./p.m. needed. The hour is always written with two digits.

## The conversion rule

**12-hour to 24-hour:**

- For **a.m.** times (except $12$ a.m.): keep the hour as is. Pad with a leading zero if it's $1$ to $9$.
- For $12.XX$ a.m.: change to $00\,XX$ (midnight).
- For **p.m.** times (except $12$ p.m.): add $12$ to the hour.
- For $12.XX$ p.m.: keep as $12\,XX$ (noon).

So $3.15$ p.m. $\to 3 + 12 = 15$, giving $15\,15$.

**24-hour to 12-hour:**

- Hours $0$ to $11$: a.m. (with $0$ written as $12$).
- Hour $12$: $12$ p.m. (noon).
- Hours $13$ to $23$: subtract $12$, write p.m.

So $19\,45 \to 19 - 12 = 7$, giving $7.45$ p.m.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $12$ a.m. is MIDNIGHT, not noon.** This is the most common mistake. $12$ a.m. is at the start of the day ($00\,00$ in 24-hour). $12$ p.m. is at noon ($12\,00$). The "12-hour" clock isn't fully consistent here. When in doubt, use the 24-hour version.

</div>

## Calculating elapsed time

To find the time between two moments, **convert both to 24-hour format first** to avoid a.m./p.m. confusion. Then break the gap into a few easy pieces.

**Method.** Find the time from the first moment to the next "round hour", then from there to the target.

**Example.** Time from $09\,40$ to $11\,15$:

- $09\,40$ to $10\,00$: $20$ min.
- $10\,00$ to $11\,00$: $1$ h.
- $11\,00$ to $11\,15$: $15$ min.
- Total: $1$ h $35$ min.

## Crossing midnight

If the journey starts before midnight and ends after, do it in two chunks:

1. From start to $24\,00$ (midnight).
2. From $00\,00$ to the end time.
3. Add.

**Example.** Flight from $22\,30$ to $06\,15$ next morning.

- $22\,30$ to $24\,00$: $1$ h $30$ min.
- $00\,00$ to $06\,15$: $6$ h $15$ min.
- Total: $7$ h $45$ min.

## Adding a duration to a start time

To find an end time given a start time and duration, add the hours and minutes separately, then carry if minutes go over $60$.

**Example.** Start at $11\,30$, travel for $4$ h $45$ min.

- Hours: $11 + 4 = 15$.
- Minutes: $30 + 45 = 75$ min, which is $1$ h $15$ min. Carry the $1$ to the hours.
- Final: $15 + 1 = 16$ hours, $15$ minutes. So $16\,15$.

## Worked example

A bus leaves at $14\,20$ and arrives at $16\,55$. How long is the journey?

**Step 1: Break the gap into pieces at round hours.**

- $14\,20$ to $15\,00$: $40$ min.
- $15\,00$ to $16\,00$: $1$ h.
- $16\,00$ to $16\,55$: $55$ min.

**Step 2: Add.**

$$40 + 60 + 55 \;=\; 155 \text{ min} \;=\; 2 \text{ h } 35 \text{ min}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{2 \text{ h } 35 \text{ min}}$. **[M1 for breaking into pieces; A1 for the total]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting times directly as decimals: $16\,55 - 14\,20 = 2.35$ does NOT mean $2$ h $35$ min cleanly; it works here by coincidence but fails for $16\,15 - 14\,20$.
- Forgetting to carry. $30$ min $+ 45$ min is $1$ h $15$ min, NOT $75$ min in clock form.
- Confusing $12$ a.m. with noon. $12$ a.m. = midnight; $12$ p.m. = noon.
- Computing across midnight by subtracting directly. Split at midnight and add the two pieces.

## Quick summary

- 12-hour: $1$ to $12$ + a.m./p.m. 24-hour: $00$ to $23$ with no a.m./p.m.
- p.m. (except $12$): add $12$ to the hour. a.m. (except $12$): keep as is.
- $12$ a.m. $= 00\,00$ (midnight). $12$ p.m. $= 12\,00$ (noon).
- For elapsed time, break the gap into pieces at round hours.
- Crossing midnight: split into "before midnight" + "after midnight" and add the two parts.
