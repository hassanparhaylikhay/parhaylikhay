## Why this matters

Cambridge timetable and time-zone questions are easy marks if you have a clean method. The two skills: **read journey times off a timetable** (using the elapsed-time method from p2) and **convert between time zones** (add or subtract hours, watch for crossing midnight).

## Reading a timetable

A timetable lists departure and arrival times. To find a journey duration, find the **departure** and **arrival** times for the segment, then work out the elapsed time by breaking the gap into pieces at the round hours (the same approach used in p2).

**Example.** How long does Train A take from Lahore to Islamabad?

- Departs Lahore at $07\,30$, arrives Islamabad at $12\,15$.
- $07\,30$ to $08\,00$: $30$ min. $08\,00$ to $12\,00$: $4$ h. $12\,00$ to $12\,15$: $15$ min.
- Total: $30$ min $+ 4$ h $+ 15$ min $= 4$ h $45$ min.

If the train continues past midnight (e.g. $14\,30$ to $07\,15$ next day), split the journey at midnight and add the two pieces (same as in p2):

- $14\,30$ to $24\,00$: $9$ h $30$ min.
- $00\,00$ to $07\,15$: $7$ h $15$ min.
- Total: $9$ h $30$ min $+ 7$ h $15$ min $= 16$ h $45$ min.

## Time zones

The world is divided into **time zones**. Cities to the **east** are usually **ahead** in time; cities to the **west** are **behind**. Cambridge questions give you the offset (e.g. "Tokyo is $9$ h ahead of London") and ask you to convert.

| If A is X h **ahead** of B | A's time = B's time + X |
| If A is X h **behind** of B | A's time = B's time − X |

When you cross **midnight** (e.g. ahead by $9$ h from $22\,00$), the result lands on the **next day**. When you go below $00\,00$ (e.g. $5$ h behind from $03\,00$), the result is on the **previous day**.

## Crossing the date

To convert and check the date:

- $22\,00 + 9$ h $= 31\,00$, which is past midnight. Subtract $24$: $31 - 24 = 7$, so $07\,00$ on the **next day**.
- $03\,00 - 5$ h would be $-2\,00$. Add $24$: $24 - 2 = 22\,00$ on the **previous day**.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't forget the day change.** "$22\,00$ Monday in London is $07\,00$ on Tuesday in Tokyo", not Monday. Cambridge often awards a mark specifically for getting the day right.

## Worked example: combining flight time and time zones

A flight leaves London at $09\,30$ local time and lands in Dubai $7$ hours later. Dubai is $4$ hours ahead of London. What time does it land, in **local Dubai time**?

**Step 1: Find the arrival time in London time.**

$$09\,30 + 7 \text{ h} \;=\; 16\,30$$

**Step 2: Convert to Dubai local time.** Dubai is $4$ h ahead, so add $4$ h.

$$16\,30 + 4 \text{ h} \;=\; 20\,30$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{20\,30}$ Dubai time. **[M1 for arrival in London time; M1 for adding the offset; A1 for the local time]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Worked example: timetable

A train leaves at $14\,45$ and arrives at $19\,30$. How long is the journey?

**Step 1: Break the journey into pieces at the round hours.**

- $14\,45$ to $15\,00$: $15$ min.
- $15\,00$ to $19\,00$: $4$ h.
- $19\,00$ to $19\,30$: $30$ min.

**Step 2: Add.**

$$15 \text{ min} + 4 \text{ h} + 30 \text{ min} \;=\; 4 \text{ h } 45 \text{ min}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{4 \text{ h } 45 \text{ min}}$. **[M1 for the pieces; A1 for the total]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting times directly without breaking the gap into pieces. $19\,30 - 14\,45 \neq 4.85$ h cleanly. Break the gap at round hours instead.
- Forgetting to convert to local time at the destination. The flight's "duration" + start time is the destination arrival in the START's time zone, not local.
- Crossing midnight or the date silently. Always check whether the answer lands on the same day or the next/previous.
- Treating "behind" as "later". Behind means earlier in time. Ahead means later.

## Quick summary

- For elapsed time, break the gap into pieces at the round hours.
- For times across midnight, split into "before midnight" and "after midnight".
- Time zones: <b style="color:#fff067">ahead = add</b>; <b style="color:#0fee89">behind = subtract</b>.
- Watch for date changes when the offset crosses midnight.
- For flights with time zones, find arrival in start-zone time first, then add the offset to get local time.
