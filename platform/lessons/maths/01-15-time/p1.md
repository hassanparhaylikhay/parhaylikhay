## Why this matters

Imagine a cricket innings that lasts $3$ hours $45$ minutes. The commentator wants the total in minutes; you instinctively work out $3 \times 60 + 45 = 225$. Time conversions trip students up only because the units jump in 60s and 24s instead of nice tens, but the idea is exactly that everyday calculation.

Cambridge expects you to convert between time units cleanly: seconds to minutes, minutes to hours, days to weeks, weeks to years. The conversions are not in base 10 (unlike length or money), so they catch students who try to multiply or divide by 100 or 1000 by habit. Get the factors below memorised and the rest is mechanical.

A few things to note:

- Cambridge uses **$1$ year $= 365$ days** (no leap-year adjustments).
- Months have variable length ($28$ to $31$ days), but $1$ year $= 12$ months exactly for converting back and forth.
- $1$ hour $= 60$ minutes $= 60 \times 60 = 3\,600$ seconds. Memorise this.
- $1$ day $= 24 \times 60 = 1\,440$ minutes $= 86\,400$ seconds.

## Going down the chain (bigger to smaller): multiply

To convert from a **larger unit** to a **smaller unit**, multiply by the factor.

| Conversion | Multiply by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| hours $\to$ minutes | $60$ | $3$ h $\to 3 \times 60 = 180$ min |
| minutes $\to$ seconds | $60$ | $5$ min $\to 5 \times 60 = 300$ s |
| days $\to$ hours | $24$ | $2$ days $\to 2 \times 24 = 48$ h |
| weeks $\to$ days | $7$ | $4$ weeks $\to 28$ days |
| years $\to$ months | $12$ | $5$ years $\to 60$ months |
| years $\to$ days | $365$ | $2$ years $\to 730$ days |

For chained conversions (e.g. hours to seconds), apply the factors in sequence: $1$ h $= 60 \times 60 = 3\,600$ s.

## Going up the chain (smaller to bigger): divide

To convert from a **smaller** unit to a **larger** unit, divide by the factor.

| Conversion | Divide by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| seconds $\to$ minutes | $60$ | $300$ s $\to 5$ min |
| minutes $\to$ hours | $60$ | $90$ min $\to 1.5$ h |
| hours $\to$ days | $24$ | $48$ h $\to 2$ days |
| days $\to$ weeks | $7$ | $35$ days $\to 5$ weeks |
| months $\to$ years | $12$ | $30$ months $\to 2.5$ years |

If the answer doesn't divide evenly, leave it as a decimal (e.g. $90$ min $= 1.5$ h) or as a mixed unit ($1$ h $30$ min). The format Cambridge expects is in the question.

## Mixed-unit values

Many time quantities mix units: "$3$ h $20$ min" or "$2$ years $3$ months". To convert these:

1. Convert each piece to the **same** unit (the smaller one is usually easiest).
2. Add the pieces.

**Example.** $3$ h $20$ min in minutes:

- $3$ h $= 180$ min.
- Plus $20$ min: $180 + 20 = 200$ min.

**Example.** $2$ years $3$ months in months:

- $2$ years $= 24$ months.
- Plus $3$ months: $24 + 3 = 27$ months.

**Example (going the other way).** $200$ minutes in hours and minutes:

- $200 \div 60 = 3$ remainder $20$.
- So $200$ min $= 3$ h $20$ min.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't multiply by 100 or 1000.** Time isn't decimal. $1$ hour is $60$ minutes, not $100$. $1$ minute is $60$ seconds, not $1\,000$. If you're converting time and using $\times 1000$, you're doing it wrong.

</div>

## Worked example

A film lasts $2$ h $45$ min. The audience watches it twice in a row. How long do they spend watching, in total minutes?

**Step 1: Convert one viewing to minutes.**

$$2 \text{ h } 45 \text{ min} \;=\; 120 + 45 \;=\; 165 \text{ min}$$

**Step 2: Multiply by 2.**

$$2 \times 165 \;=\; 330 \text{ min}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{330}$ minutes. **[M1 for the conversion; A1 for the doubling]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Treating time as decimal. $2$ h $30$ min is NOT $2.30$ h; it's $2.5$ h.
- Forgetting to combine units. "$3$ h $20$ min in minutes" is $200$ min, NOT just $20$ min.
- Using the wrong factor. $1$ hour is $60$ minutes (not $100$); $1$ day is $24$ hours (not $20$).
- Trying to divide unevenly. $50$ min $\div 60$ doesn't give $0.83$ hours cleanly; either say $\approx 0.833$ h or write $50$ min as the answer.

## Quick summary

- $1$ min $= \textcolor{#0fee89}{60}$ s. $1$ h $= 60$ min $= 3\,600$ s.
- $1$ day $= 24$ h. $1$ week $= 7$ days. $1$ year $= 365$ days $= 12$ months.
- Bigger to smaller: <b style="color:#fff067">multiply</b>. Smaller to bigger: <b style="color:#0fee89">divide</b>.
- For mixed-unit values, convert pieces to the same unit and add.
