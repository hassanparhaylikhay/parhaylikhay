## Why this matters

By Paper 1 you'll see expressions that combine all three rules with negative and fractional indices in one go. The strategy is always: **simplify the structure first using the rules, THEN compute.** Computing too early forces you into ugly arithmetic.

Two extra rules that come up often:

- $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$: distribute the index over a product.
- $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$: distribute over a quotient.

Note these only work when the base itself is a product or quotient. **They do NOT apply to a sum.** $(a+b)^n \neq a^n + b^n$.

## Strategy: simplify, then evaluate

The Paper 1 routine for any messy indices expression:

1. Use the rules to combine all powers of the same base into a **single power**.
2. Convert any negative or fractional index to its plain form (reciprocal or root) at the END.
3. Compute the number.

Skipping step 1 forces you to compute big intermediate values. Always simplify first.

## Worked example 1

Simplify $\dfrac{2^4 \times 2^{-3}}{2^{-2}}$, leaving your answer as a single integer.

**Step 1: Combine the top.** Same base, multiply, add indices.

$$2^4 \times 2^{-3} \;=\; 2^{4 + (-3)} \;=\; 2^1$$

**Step 2: Divide by the bottom.** Same base, divide, subtract indices.

$$\dfrac{2^1}{2^{-2}} \;=\; 2^{1 - (-2)} \;=\; 2^3$$

**Step 3: Compute.** $2^3 = 8$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{8}$. **[M1 for combining indices; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example 2

Find the value of $9^{1/2} + 27^{2/3}$.

**Step 1: First term.** $9^{1/2} = \sqrt{9} = 3$.

**Step 2: Second term.** $27^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{27})^2 = 3^2 = 9$.

**Step 3: Add.** $3 + 9 = 12$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{12}$. **[B1 for $9^{1/2} = 3$; B1 for $27^{2/3} = 9$; A1 for the sum]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Worked example 3 (algebraic)

Simplify $\dfrac{x^5 \times x^{-3}}{x^{-1}}$.

**Step 1: Combine the top.** $x^5 \times x^{-3} = x^{5 + (-3)} = x^2$.

**Step 2: Divide by the bottom.** $\dfrac{x^2}{x^{-1}} = x^{2 - (-1)} = x^3$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{x^3}$. **[M1 for combining; A1 for the final index]**

Full marks $= 2$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: subtracting a negative index flips its sign.** $2^1 \div 2^{-2}$ is $2^{1 - (-2)} = 2^{3}$, NOT $2^{1 - 2} = 2^{-1}$. The minus from "subtract" and the minus from "negative index" combine into a plus. Watch your signs carefully when negatives stack.

</div>

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying the bases instead of combining indices. $3^4 \times 3^2$ is $3^6$, not $9^6$.
- Mis-stepping the subtract-negative chain. $5^2 \div 5^{-3} = 5^{2-(-3)} = 5^5$, not $5^{-1}$.
- Computing $a^{m/n}$ by raising to the power first when rooting is easier. $32^{3/5}$: take the fifth root first ($2$), then cube ($8$).
- Treating $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$. Wrong: $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. The product/quotient distribution rule does NOT extend to sums.

## Quick summary

- All six rules apply together: combine same-base powers first, evaluate at the end.
- $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$ and $\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \tfrac{a^n}{b^n}$, but never for sums.
- For Paper 1 work in this order: rules $\to$ negative-index reciprocals $\to$ fractional-index roots $\to$ arithmetic.
