You've now seen everything Cambridge tests in 1.5: the six comparison symbols and how to order quantities. Together they're a 1- or 2-mark question that shows up almost every paper.

## The skills you should have

- **Read and write** all six symbols: $=$, $\ne$, $\gt$, $\lt$, $\ge$, $\le$.
- **Distinguish strict** ($\gt , \lt $) from **inclusive** ($\ge, \le$).
- **Order numbers** on a number line, including negatives.
- **Convert mixed forms** (fractions, decimals, percentages) before comparing.
- **Sandwich roots** between integers using the recall list from 1.3.

## Marking patterns

| Question style | Marks | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| "Insert $\lt$ or $\gt$ between..." | 1 | Pure recall + direction check |
| "Order these from smallest to largest" | 1–2 | M1 conversion to common form, A1 correct order |
| "Which of these are solutions to $x \ge 7$?" | 1 | Equality matters |

## The single biggest mistake

**Negatives**. $-100 \lt -1$, but the gut reaction reads "100 is bigger so $-100$ must be bigger". Always picture the number line. The further left, the smaller.
