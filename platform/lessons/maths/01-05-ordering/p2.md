## Why this matters

Cambridge regularly asks "arrange these in order" with a mix of fractions, decimals, percentages, roots, or negative numbers. The method is always the same: **convert everything to the same form, then sort**. Negatives also flip your intuition, so we'll cover that explicitly.

## The number line is your friend

Every real number has exactly one place on the number line. **Smaller is further left, larger is further right.**

This is the only mental model you need for ordering. Plot the values, read them off left to right, done.

## Negatives reverse intuition

For positive numbers, "bigger" matches everyday meaning. For negatives, watch out:

- $-3$ vs $-7$: which is smaller? **$-7$**, because it sits further to the LEFT on the number line.
- The **size** of $-7$ is bigger than the size of $-3$, but the *value* is smaller.
- Rule of thumb: **for negatives, the one with the bigger size is the smaller value.**

This trips up nearly every student under exam pressure. If a question involves negatives, sketch a number line in the margin.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $-7 \lt -3$, not $-7 \gt -3$.** Even though $7 \gt 3$, sticking the minus sign on flips the comparison. Always think *number line*, not *raw size*.

</div>

## Mixed forms: convert first

When the list mixes fractions, decimals, and percentages, **convert everything to one common form** before sorting. Decimals are usually the easiest target because you can compare digit by digit.

**Example.** Order from smallest to largest: $\;\tfrac{3}{8}, \;0.4, \;35\%, \;\tfrac{7}{20}$.

Convert all to decimals:

- $\tfrac{3}{8} = 0.375$
- $0.4 = 0.4$
- $35\% = 0.35$
- $\tfrac{7}{20} = 0.35$

Sort: $0.35 = 0.35 \lt 0.375 \lt 0.4$.

Map back: $35\% \;=\; \tfrac{7}{20} \;\lt \; \tfrac{3}{8} \;\lt \; 0.4$.

Note that $35\%$ and $\tfrac{7}{20}$ are equal, so they can sit either order.

## Roots and irrationals

For roots like $\sqrt{50}$ or $\sqrt[3]{20}$, sandwich them between two integers using the recall list from 1.3:

- $\sqrt{50}$: $50$ is between $49 = 7^2$ and $64 = 8^2$, so $\sqrt{50}$ is between $7$ and $8$.
- For more precision, $\sqrt{50} \approx 7.07$.

For $\pi$, use $\pi \approx 3.14$.

## Worked example

Arrange in order from smallest to largest: $\;\tfrac{3}{4}, \;0.7, \;78\%, \;\tfrac{4}{5}$.

**Step 1: Convert everything to decimals** for easy comparison.

- $\textcolor{#00abfa}{\tfrac{3}{4}} = 0.75$
- $\textcolor{#fff067}{0.7} = 0.7$
- $\textcolor{#0fee89}{78\%} = 0.78$
- $\textcolor{#00abfa}{\tfrac{4}{5}} = 0.8$

**Step 2: Sort the decimals.**

$$0.7 \;\lt \; 0.75 \;\lt \; 0.78 \;\lt \; 0.8$$

**Step 3: Map back to original forms.**

**Answer:** $\boxed{0.7, \;\tfrac{3}{4}, \;78\%, \;\tfrac{4}{5}}$. **[M1 for converting; A1 for the order]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing fractions, decimals, and percentages in their raw forms. Always convert first.
- Forgetting that negatives flip the size-vs-value relationship. $-1000 \lt -1$.
- For roots, treating $\sqrt{50}$ as 50 itself. The whole point is the root is much smaller (about 7.07).
- Ordering "largest to smallest" when the question says "smallest to largest" (or vice versa). Read the direction the question asks for.

## Quick summary

- Plot on a <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">number line</span>: smaller is left, larger is right.
- For mixed forms (fractions / decimals / percentages), <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">convert to one form first</span>, usually decimals.
- For negatives, the one with the <span style="color:#ff4670;font-weight:600">bigger size is the smaller value</span>.
- For roots, sandwich between perfect squares/cubes you recall.
- Read whether the question asks "smallest to largest" or "largest to smallest" before answering.
