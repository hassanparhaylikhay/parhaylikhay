The yellow arrow is the **sum** of the blue and orange. Notice how it lands: drag the tip of $\mathbf{a}$, the yellow shifts to keep up; drag the tip of $\mathbf{b}$ (the orange arrow starting at $\mathbf{a}$'s tip), the yellow shifts again. Switch to $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ and the orange flips direction; that flipped arrow is $-\mathbf{b}$, and the yellow is now $\mathbf{a}$ followed by $-\mathbf{b}$.

## Head-to-tail: the picture rule

To add $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ visually:

1. Draw $\mathbf{a}$ first.
2. Pick up $\mathbf{b}$, slide it so its **tail** sits at $\mathbf{a}$'s **tip** (head-to-tail).
3. The sum $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ is the arrow from $\mathbf{a}$'s tail to $\mathbf{b}$'s new tip.

You can switch the order. $\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a}$ draws $\mathbf{b}$ first and slides $\mathbf{a}$ to its tip. The endpoint is the same; only the path is different. So **order does not matter** for addition.

## Components: just add the tops and bottoms

The picture rule has a tidy arithmetic version. To add two columns, add the top numbers and add the bottom numbers separately:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 + b_1 \\ a_2 + b_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

It is the exact same trick as adding $(3, 1)$ and $(-1, 4)$ as ordered pairs. No new arithmetic; just keep tops with tops, bottoms with bottoms.

## Subtraction: add the reverse

$\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ means "go along $\mathbf{a}$, then go BACKWARDS along $\mathbf{b}$". The reverse of $\mathbf{b}$ is $-\mathbf{b}$ (every component flips sign). So

$$\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} + (-\mathbf{b})$$

In components, subtraction works the same as addition with the sign flipped:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 - b_1 \\ a_2 - b_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

The trap: when $b$ has a negative number, subtracting a negative becomes adding. $5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8$, not $2$.

## The zero vector and reversal

A few clean facts that follow from the rules:

- $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{0} = \mathbf{a}$ for any $\mathbf{a}$. The zero vector $\mathbf{0} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ doesn't shift anything.
- $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$. Walk along $\mathbf{a}$, then walk back; you have moved nowhere.
- $\vec{AB} + \vec{BC} = \vec{AC}$. Go from $A$ to $B$, then from $B$ to $C$; the net journey is $A$ to $C$. That last identity is the head-to-tail rule written with point names; you will use it constantly in 7.4.

## Worked example: find $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$

$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ as column vectors.

**Step 1: Add the components for $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$.**

$$\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 + (-1) \\ 1 + 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Step 2: Subtract the components for $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$.**

$$\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 - (-1) \\ 1 - 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$$

Notice that $3 - (-1) = 4$, not $2$. Subtracting a negative adds.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}}$. **[B1 for $\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$ correct; B1 for $\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}$ correct]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- **Subtracting a negative without flipping the sign**. $3 - (-1) = 4$, not $2$. Whenever the second column has a negative, double-check the subtraction.
- **Mixing the components**. Tops always work with tops; bottoms with bottoms. Never combine the $x$-component of one with the $y$-component of the other.
- **Drawing the head-to-tail picture wrong**. The second vector starts at the FIRST vector's tip, not at the origin.
- **Forgetting that $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} \neq \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$**. They differ in sign: $\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} = -(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b})$. Order matters for subtraction even though it doesn't for addition.

## Quick summary

- Add two vectors by adding the tops and the bottoms separately.
- Subtract two vectors by subtracting the tops and the bottoms separately.
- Visually: $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ is head-to-tail with $\mathbf{a}$ then $\mathbf{b}$; $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$ is head-to-tail with $\mathbf{a}$ then the reverse of $\mathbf{b}$.
- Addition does not care about order: $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a}$. Subtraction does: $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = -(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$.
- $\vec{AB} + \vec{BC} = \vec{AC}$: the head-to-tail rule in point-name form.
