## Scale a vector

The blue arrow is $\mathbf{a}$. The yellow arrow is $k\mathbf{a}$. Drag the yellow tip along the dashed line and watch the number $k$ change. The two arrows are always on the same line through the origin: when $k$ is positive they point the same way, when $k$ is negative the yellow flips to the opposite side, and when $|k| > 1$ the yellow is longer than the blue, when $|k| < 1$ it is shorter.

That is the whole idea of **scalar multiplication**: a single number stretches, shrinks, or flips a vector along its own line.

## The arithmetic: multiply each component by k

To find $k\mathbf{a}$, multiply EACH component by $k$:

$$k \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} k a_1 \\ k a_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

So $3 \times \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $-\tfrac{1}{2} \times \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$.

A useful intuition: $k = -1$ flips $\mathbf{a}$ to point exactly the other way, which is the same as $-\mathbf{a}$ (the reverse you saw in p1). $k = -2$ does both: flips AND doubles.

## Parallel vectors

Two vectors are **parallel** if they sit on the same line of direction. Algebraically, that means one is a scalar multiple of the other:

$$\mathbf{b} = k\mathbf{a} \quad \text{for some nonzero } k$$

If $k > 0$ they point the same way; if $k < 0$ they point opposite ways. Either way, they are parallel.

**How to spot parallel vectors from columns.** Divide matching components. If both divisions give the SAME $k$, the vectors are parallel.

$$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\frac{3}{2} = 1.5, \quad \frac{6}{4} = 1.5$$

Same ratio, so $\mathbf{a} = 1.5\mathbf{b}$. The two are parallel.

If the two divisions give different numbers, the vectors are NOT parallel.

## Worked example: show two vectors are parallel

$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{c} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix}$.

(a) Find a number $k$ such that $\mathbf{c} = k\mathbf{a}$.

(b) Hence write down the geometric relationship between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c}$.

<iframe src="/widgets/transformations-step-explorer.html?preset=7p2-p3-parallel" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:620px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Part (a): find $k$.** Compare matching components.

$$\text{Top: } 6 \div 2 = 3, \quad \text{Bottom: } 9 \div 3 = 3$$

Both ratios are equal to $3$, so $\mathbf{c} = 3\mathbf{a}$.

**Part (b): the relationship.** Since $\mathbf{c}$ is a positive scalar multiple of $\mathbf{a}$, the two vectors are **parallel** and point in the same direction. $\mathbf{c}$ is three times as long as $\mathbf{a}$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\mathbf{c} = 3\mathbf{a}; \ \mathbf{c} \text{ is parallel to } \mathbf{a}}$. **[M1 for $k = 3$ from one component; A1 for $\mathbf{c} = 3\mathbf{a}$; B1 for stating parallel]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to multiply the second component.** Both top AND bottom get multiplied by $k$. Skipping one leaves the answer half-right.
- **Wrong sign with negative $k$.** $-2 \times (-3) = +6$, not $-6$. Negative times negative is positive.
- **Saying "parallel" without checking BOTH ratios.** Two vectors are parallel only if dividing matching components gives the SAME number for both. If the ratios differ, they are not parallel.
- **Confusing direction with magnitude.** "Parallel" means same line of direction; it does NOT mean same length. $\mathbf{a}$ and $-2\mathbf{a}$ are still parallel even though one is twice as long and pointing the other way.

## Quick summary

- $k\mathbf{a}$ multiplies each component of $\mathbf{a}$ by the scalar $k$.
- Positive $k$ keeps direction; negative $k$ flips it.
- $|k|$ controls the new length: $|k| > 1$ longer, $|k| < 1$ shorter.
- $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are parallel iff $\mathbf{b} = k\mathbf{a}$ for some $k \neq 0$.
- To check parallel from columns: divide matching components and confirm both quotients are equal.
