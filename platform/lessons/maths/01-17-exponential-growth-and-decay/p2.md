## Why this matters

Decay is just exponential growth with the multiplier less than $1$. Cars depreciate, radioactive material decays, populations shrink during epidemics, medicine clears from the bloodstream. All of these use the same formula structure as growth, just with a multiplier between $0$ and $1$.

## The exponential decay formula

If a quantity starts at $\textcolor{#fff067}{P}$ and decreases by $\textcolor{#00abfa}{r}\%$ per period, then after $\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}$ periods:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{A} \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{P} \times \left(1 - \dfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{r}}{100}\right)^{\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}$$

The only difference from growth is the **minus** sign. The multiplier $1 - \tfrac{r}{100}$ is between $0$ and $1$, so the value shrinks each period.

| Decay rate | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| $-10\%$ | $0.90$ |
| $-5\%$ | $0.95$ |
| $-20\%$ | $0.80$ |
| $-50\%$ | $0.50$ |
| $-25\%$ | $0.75$ |

## Depreciation

Depreciation is the most common decay context. Cars, equipment, and assets typically lose a fixed percentage of value each year.

**Example.** A car worth \$30\,000 depreciates $12\%$ per year. Value after $3$ years:

$$30\,000 \times 0.88^3 \;=\; 30\,000 \times 0.681472 \;\approx\; \$20\,444$$

The multiplier is $1 - 0.12 = 0.88$. Each year the car is worth $88\%$ of its previous value.

## Half-life

A **half-life** is the time it takes for a quantity to drop to **half** its current value. Often used for radioactive isotopes and some medicines.

If the half-life is $h$ time units and the start is $P$, then after $T$ time units (where $T$ is a multiple of $h$):

$$A \;=\; P \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{T/h}$$

**Example.** A $80$ g sample with half-life $5$ years. After $20$ years:

- Number of half-lives: $\tfrac{20}{5} = 4$.
- $A = 80 \times (\tfrac{1}{2})^4 = 80 \times \tfrac{1}{16} = 5$ g.

The number of half-lives is the **only** quantity that goes into the power. If you have $20$ years and a $5$-year half-life, that's $4$ half-lives, not $20$.

## Population decline

Populations can shrink (epidemic, migration, declining species). Same formula as depreciation.

**Example.** A population of $40\,000$ shrinks $5\%$ per year for $6$ years.

$$40\,000 \times 0.95^6 \;\approx\; 40\,000 \times 0.7351 \;\approx\; 29\,403$$

So roughly $29\,403$ people.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $r\%$ decay never reaches zero.** A car that loses $10\%$ each year will keep shrinking forever in this model: \$30\,000 $\to$ \$27\,000 $\to$ \$24\,300 $\to \ldots$ It approaches but never reaches \$0. This is the nature of exponential decay. (In real life, of course, the car eventually has zero value, but the formula doesn't capture that.)

## Finding the time to a target

For "after how many years does it drop BELOW $X$?", try whole-number $T$ values until the result first lands below the target.

**Example.** \$50\,000 depreciates $8\%$ per year. When does it first drop below \$30\,000?

- $T = 6$: $50\,000 \times 0.92^6 = 30\,318$. Just above \$30\,000.
- $T = 7$: $50\,000 \times 0.92^7 = 27\,893$. Below.

So it first drops below at year **$7$**. Cambridge convention: round up to the next whole period.

## Worked example

A radioactive sample of $400$ g has half-life $4$ days. How much remains after $20$ days?

**Step 1: Number of half-lives.**

$$\dfrac{20}{4} \;=\; 5$$

**Step 2: Apply the formula.**

$$A \;=\; 400 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^5 \;=\; 400 \times \dfrac{1}{32} \;=\; 12.5 \text{ g}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{12.5}$ g. **[M1 for finding the number of half-lives; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing depreciation with growth. Use $1 - \tfrac{r}{100}$, not $1 + \tfrac{r}{100}$.
- For half-life, putting the time straight into the power instead of dividing by the half-life first. $20$ days with half-life $4$ uses power $5$, not $20$.
- Rounding mid-calculation. Carry full precision until the final value.
- Treating exponential decay as eventually reaching zero. Mathematically it never does.

## Quick summary

- Decay formula: $\textcolor{#0fee89}{A} = \textcolor{#fff067}{P}(1 - \tfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{r}}{100})^{\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}$.
- Multiplier is always between $0$ and $1$ for decay.
- Half-life: $A = P(\tfrac{1}{2})^{T/h}$. The power is the number of half-lives, not the raw time.
- For "when does $A$ drop below $X$?", try whole-number $T$ values until the result first lands below the target.
