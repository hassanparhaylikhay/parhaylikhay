## Why this matters

Exponential growth is anything that increases by a **fixed percentage per time period**: populations, investments, viral spread, bacteria. It's also the skeleton of compound interest from 1.13 p3, but Cambridge tests it as a separate topic with broader contexts. The single formula handles all of them.

## The exponential growth formula

If a quantity starts at $\textcolor{#fff067}{P}$ and grows by $\textcolor{#00abfa}{r}\%$ per period, then after $\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}$ periods:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{A} \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{P} \times \left(1 + \dfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{r}}{100}\right)^{\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}$$

This is the same formula as compound interest, just with **any quantity** (not just money) and **any time unit** (year, day, hour, generation).

In multiplier form: each period, multiply by $1 + \tfrac{r}{100}$. After $T$ periods, you've multiplied by that number $T$ times, hence the power.

## Apply the formula directly

The shortcut: for any "find $A$ after $T$ years" question, just use the formula in one step.

**Example.** \$5\,000 invested at $6\%$ per year for $4$ years.

$$A \;=\; 5\,000 \times 1.06^4 \;=\; 5\,000 \times 1.26248 \;\approx\; 6\,312.38$$

So \$6\,312.38.

**Example.** A population of $50\,000$ at $2\%$ per year for $10$ years.

$$A \;=\; 50\,000 \times 1.02^{10} \;\approx\; 50\,000 \times 1.21899 \;\approx\; 60\,950$$

(Round populations to the nearest whole person.)

## Doubling periods

When the question says a quantity **doubles every $T$ periods** (or triples, or any fixed multiplier), the multiplier per period is just the doubling factor:

$$A \;=\; P \times d^N$$

where $d$ is the multiplier each period (e.g. $2$ for doubling) and $N$ is the number of periods.

**Example.** Bacteria double every $3$ hours, starting from $500$. After $9$ hours:

- Number of doublings: $\tfrac{9}{3} = 3$.
- Population: $500 \times 2^3 = 500 \times 8 = 4\,000$.

The trick: **count the doubling periods first**, not the raw time. If the time isn't a clean multiple of the doubling period, you'd need to use the percentage form.

## Finding the time

Sometimes Cambridge gives you the start, the rate, and a target value, and asks "after how many years does it reach the target?". The cleanest method is to **try increasing values of $T$ until the inequality flips**.

**Example.** A town of $50\,000$ grows at $2.5\%$ per year. When does it first exceed $60\,000$?

- $T = 5$: $50\,000 \times 1.025^5 \approx 56\,569$. Still below.
- $T = 7$: $50\,000 \times 1.025^7 \approx 59\,460$. Still below.
- $T = 8$: $50\,000 \times 1.025^8 \approx 60\,925$. Over.

So it first exceeds $60\,000$ during year **$8$**.

The Cambridge convention: round **up** to the next whole year. Even if the target is reached partway through year 8, the answer is "after $8$ years".

**Trap: don't compute growth year by year for large $T$.** Use the formula $P(1 + r/100)^T$ in one step. Computing $20$ years one at a time is tedious AND prone to rounding errors. The calculator's power function gives an exact answer in one step.

## Worked example

A bank pays $5\%$ compound interest per year. \$2\,000 is invested. The investment is left for $8$ years. What is the final value?

**Step 1: Identify the multiplier.** $1 + \tfrac{5}{100} = 1.05$.

**Step 2: Apply the formula.**

$$A \;=\; 2\,000 \times 1.05^8$$

**Step 3: Compute** (calculator).

$$1.05^8 \;=\; 1.4775\ldots, \qquad 2\,000 \times 1.4775 \;\approx\; 2\,954.91$$

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{2\,954.91}$. **[M1 for the formula; A1 for the value to 2 d.p.]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Adding the percentages over time instead of compounding. $5\%$ per year for $8$ years is NOT $40\%$ overall; it's $1.05^8 - 1 \approx 47.75\%$.
- Using simple growth instead of exponential. Simple grows by a flat amount each period; exponential grows by a percentage of the current total.
- Computing the multiplier wrong. $5\%$ growth gives multiplier $1.05$, not $0.05$ or $1 + 5 = 6$.
- Rounding mid-calculation. Carry full precision (use the Ans key) until the final step.

## Quick summary

- Exponential growth formula: $\textcolor{#0fee89}{A} = \textcolor{#fff067}{P}(1 + \tfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{r}}{100})^{\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}$.
- Use the formula in <b style="color:#0fee89">one step</b>, not year by year.
- For doubling/tripling problems, count the periods first and use $A = P \times d^N$.
- For "when does $A$ first exceed $X$?" type problems, try whole-number $T$ values until the answer flips.
