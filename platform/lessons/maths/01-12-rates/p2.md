## Why this matters

Speed is the most-tested rate in O-Level. Cambridge expects you to know the formula by heart (it is **NOT** given in the question), and to switch comfortably between units like km/h and m/s. The triangle below is the only thing you need to memorise.

## The speed triangle

Speed, distance, and time are connected by one formula:

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{\text{distance}} \;=\; \textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{speed}} \times \textcolor{#00abfa}{\text{time}}$$

Rearranged: speed $=$ distance $\div$ time, and time $=$ distance $\div$ speed. Many students remember this with a triangle.

How to use it: **cover the letter you want, and the formula is what's left**.

- Cover D: you see S and T side by side, so $D = S \times T$.
- Cover S: you see D over T, so $S = \tfrac{D}{T}$.
- Cover T: you see D over S, so $T = \tfrac{D}{S}$.

## Three direct examples

**Find distance.** A train moves at $80$ km/h for $3$ hours.

$$D \;=\; S \times T \;=\; 80 \times 3 \;=\; 240 \text{ km}$$

**Find speed.** A car travels $120$ km in $2$ hours.

$$S \;=\; \dfrac{D}{T} \;=\; \dfrac{120}{2} \;=\; 60 \text{ km/h}$$

**Find time.** A cyclist covers $45$ km at $15$ km/h.

$$T \;=\; \dfrac{D}{S} \;=\; \dfrac{45}{15} \;=\; 3 \text{ hours}$$

Each formula uses **the same triangle**, just rearranged.

## Mixed-unit times

Cambridge often gives times like "$3$ h $45$ min" or "$2$ h $30$ min". You **cannot** divide directly: you must first convert to a single unit. The cleanest method is to convert minutes to a decimal fraction of an hour:

| Minutes | Decimal hour |
|---|---|
| $15$ min | $0.25$ h |
| $30$ min | $0.5$ h |
| $45$ min | $0.75$ h |
| $20$ min | $\tfrac{1}{3}$ h $\approx 0.33$ h |

So $3$ h $45$ min $= 3.75$ h.

**Example.** A car travels $45$ km in $3$ h $45$ min. Speed:

$$S \;=\; \dfrac{45}{3.75} \;=\; 12 \text{ km/h}$$

If you forget to convert and divide by "$3.45$", you'll get $13.04$ km/h, which is wrong. Always convert mixed-unit times first.

## Converting km/h to m/s

A common Cambridge twist is to give a speed in km/h and ask for it in m/s (or vice versa). The conversion factor:

$$1 \text{ km/h} \;=\; \dfrac{1\,000 \text{ m}}{3\,600 \text{ s}} \;=\; \dfrac{5}{18} \text{ m/s}$$

So **multiply by $\tfrac{5}{18}$ to go from km/h to m/s**, or by $\tfrac{18}{5}$ to go from m/s to km/h.

**Example.** $72$ km/h in m/s:

$$72 \times \dfrac{5}{18} \;=\; \dfrac{360}{18} \;=\; 20 \text{ m/s}$$

A quicker version: just divide by $3.6$. So $72 \div 3.6 = 20$. Same answer.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $3$ h $45$ min is NOT $3.45$ hours.** Minutes don't decimalise that way. $45$ minutes is $\tfrac{45}{60} = 0.75$ of an hour, so $3$ h $45$ min $= 3.75$ h. Same logic for any non-zero minute count.

</div>

## Worked example

A bus leaves at $08\,15$ and arrives at $11\,00$. It covers a distance of $165$ km. What is its average speed?

**Step 1: Find the time taken.** From $08\,15$ to $11\,00$ is $2$ h $45$ min.

**Step 2: Convert to decimal hours.** $45$ min $= 0.75$ h, so $2$ h $45$ min $= 2.75$ h.

**Step 3: Apply $S = D / T$.**

$$S \;=\; \dfrac{165}{2.75} \;=\; 60 \text{ km/h}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{60}$ km/h. **[M1 for the time conversion; M1 for using $S = D/T$; A1 for the speed]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Using $3.45$ for $3$ h $45$ min. Convert minutes to decimal hours first ($45$ min $= 0.75$ h).
- Forgetting to convert km to m or h to s when units don't match.
- Mixing the formula direction: $D = S/T$ (wrong) when it should be $D = S \times T$. Use the triangle to be sure.
- Forgetting units in the answer. "$60$" is not "$60$ km/h"; Cambridge wants the unit.

## Quick summary

- $\textcolor{#fff067}{D} = \textcolor{#0fee89}{S} \times \textcolor{#00abfa}{T}$. Rearrange for $S$ or $T$ as needed.
- Mixed-unit times: convert minutes to a decimal hour ($45$ min $= 0.75$ h).
- km/h $\to$ m/s: multiply by $\tfrac{5}{18}$ (or divide by $3.6$).
- Always include units in your answer.
