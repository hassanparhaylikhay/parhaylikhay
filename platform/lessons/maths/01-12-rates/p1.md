## Why this matters

A rate is **how much of one quantity per unit of another**: dollars per hour, kilometres per litre, rupees per dollar, litres per minute. Rates run through dozens of Cambridge questions, often hidden inside word problems about pay, fuel, currency, or filling tanks. The skill is the same every time: identify the rate, then either multiply or divide depending on what you have and what you want.

> **See also:** rates and percentages overlap. Rates are "per unit" (e.g. \$/h, km/l). Percentages are "per 100" and have their own topic at 1.13 (price changes, profit, interest).

## What a rate is

A **rate** is a ratio between two quantities measured in different units. We write it with **per** or a slash:

| Rate | Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Hourly pay | \$/h | dollars per hour worked |
| Exchange rate | PKR/USD | rupees per dollar |
| Flow rate | l/min | litres per minute |
| Fuel consumption | km/l | kilometres per litre of fuel |

The notation always reads "<span style="color:#fff067">numerator unit</span> per <span style="color:#00abfa">denominator unit</span>". So \$15/h means $15$ **<span style="color:#fff067">dollars</span>** per $1$ **<span style="color:#00abfa">hour</span>**.

## The three things in any rate problem

Every rate problem has three things, and you'll always be given two of them:

- **Rate** (e.g. $15$ \$/h)
- **First quantity** (e.g. earnings, in \$)
- **Second quantity** (e.g. hours)

The relationship is always:

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{\text{first}} \;=\; \text{rate} \times \textcolor{#00abfa}{\text{second}}$$

Rearranged: rate $=$ first $\div$ second; second $=$ first $\div$ rate.

So if you know any two, you can find the third. **Always identify which two you have, then pick the matching formula.**

## Hourly pay

**Example.** A worker is paid \$15/h. How much do they earn in $7$ hours?

- Rate: $15$ \$/h
- Second quantity (time): $7$ h
- First quantity (earnings): rate $\times$ time $= 15 \times 7 = 105$, i.e. \$105.

**Reverse direction.** A worker earned \$540 in $36$ hours. What's the hourly rate?

$$\text{rate} \;=\; \dfrac{\$540}{36 \text{ h}} \;=\; \$15/\text{h}$$

## Exchange rates

Currency conversion is just a rate. "$1$ USD $= 280$ PKR" means the rate is $280$ PKR/USD.

**Convert \$50 to PKR.** Multiply by the rate:

$$50 \text{ USD} \times 280 \text{ PKR/USD} \;=\; 14\,000 \text{ PKR}$$

**Convert $7\,000$ PKR to USD.** Divide by the rate (or use the inverse rate):

$$\dfrac{7\,000 \text{ PKR}}{280 \text{ PKR/USD}} \;=\; 25 \text{ USD}$$

The pattern: **multiply when going in the direction of the rate, divide when going against it.** If you're not sure, write the units out and check they cancel.

## Flow rate

A pipe or tap that fills containers has a **flow rate** like $4$ l/min ("four litres per minute").

**Example.** A tap with flow rate $4$ l/min fills a tank for $15$ minutes. Volume:

$$4 \text{ l/min} \times 15 \text{ min} \;=\; 60 \text{ litres}$$

**Reverse.** A bucket of $20$ litres fills in $5$ minutes. Flow rate:

$$\dfrac{20 \text{ l}}{5 \text{ min}} \;=\; 4 \text{ l/min}$$

## Fuel consumption

Fuel consumption is usually **km per litre** in Pakistan and most of the world. (The UK uses miles per gallon.)

**Example.** A car runs $480$ km on $40$ litres. Consumption:

$$\dfrac{480 \text{ km}}{40 \text{ l}} \;=\; 12 \text{ km/l}$$

**Use it forward.** If the same car has $25$ litres in the tank, how far can it go?

$$25 \text{ l} \times 12 \text{ km/l} \;=\; 300 \text{ km}$$

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: include the units in your working.** "I divided 480 by 40 and got 12" without units gives no clue what the answer means. Always write **km/l** or **\$/h** or **l/min** alongside the number. Cambridge mark schemes often require the unit for the accuracy mark.

</div>

## Worked example

A worker earns \$13.50 per hour. They work $40$ hours one week and $35$ hours the next. What are their total earnings for the two weeks?

**Step 1: Total hours.** $40 + 35 = 75$ hours.

**Step 2: Total earnings.** Rate $\times$ total time:

$$13.50 \times 75 \;=\; 1\,012.50$$

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{1\,012.50}$. **[M1 for the multiplication; A1 for the answer in dollars]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting units. "$\tfrac{480}{40} = 12$" without writing "km/l" loses the unit mark.
- Multiplying when you should divide. To convert $7\,000$ PKR to USD, you DIVIDE by the rate, not multiply.
- Mixing up which direction the rate is. "$1$ USD $= 280$ PKR" can be confusing: the rate $280$ multiplies USD to give PKR; to go the other way, divide.
- Forgetting to convert times. If the rate is per hour and the question gives minutes, convert: $90$ min $= 1.5$ h.

## Quick summary

- Rate $=$ first quantity per one unit of the second quantity.
- <b style="color:#fff067">First</b> $=$ rate $\times$ <b style="color:#00abfa">second</b>; rearrange to get any of the three.
- Always include units (km/l, \$/h, l/min) in working.
- Convert units before computing if the question mixes h/min, km/m, l/ml, etc.
