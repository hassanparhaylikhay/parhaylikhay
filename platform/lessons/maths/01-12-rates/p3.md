## Why this matters

Density and pressure show up in O-Level word problems and on Paper 2 calculator questions. Cambridge always **gives you the formula** for these (and for population density and similar compound rates). Your job is to plug in the numbers correctly and report the answer with the right units.

## Density: mass per volume

The formula (given in the question):

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{density}} \;=\; \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{\text{mass}}}{\textcolor{#00abfa}{\text{volume}}}$$

Standard units: **g/cm³** for solids, **kg/m³** for larger objects. Density tells you how much mass is packed into a unit of volume. Lead is dense ($\approx 11$ g/cm³); water is $1$ g/cm³ at room temperature; air is much less.

The triangle works exactly like the speed triangle, with mass on top and density × volume on the bottom. The Greek letter $\rho$ (rho) is the standard symbol for density, but Cambridge usually writes "density" in full.

**Example.** A block has mass $200$ g and volume $50$ cm³. Find the density.

$$\rho \;=\; \dfrac{m}{V} \;=\; \dfrac{200}{50} \;=\; 4 \text{ g/cm}^3$$

**Reverse.** A liquid has density $0.8$ g/cm³ and volume $250$ cm³. Find the mass.

$$m \;=\; \rho \times V \;=\; 0.8 \times 250 \;=\; 200 \text{ g}$$

## Pressure: force per area

Cambridge gives the formula:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{pressure}} \;=\; \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{\text{force}}}{\textcolor{#00abfa}{\text{area}}}$$

Standard units: **N/m²** (newtons per square metre), also called **pascals (Pa)**.

**Example.** A force of $300$ N pushes on an area of $0.5$ m². Pressure:

$$P \;=\; \dfrac{F}{A} \;=\; \dfrac{300}{0.5} \;=\; 600 \text{ N/m}^2$$

The same triangle method applies: cover what you want, the formula is what's left.

## Population density

For population density, the same idea, just scaled up:

$$\text{population density} \;=\; \dfrac{\text{population}}{\text{area}}$$

Standard units: **people per km²**.

**Example.** A city has $1\,500\,000$ people in $300$ km². Density:

$$\dfrac{1\,500\,000}{300} \;=\; 5\,000 \text{ people/km}^2$$

## Other compound rates

Many Cambridge questions invent a rate-like formula and give it to you. The skill is the same: identify the formula, plug in the numbers, report with units. Examples:

| Rate | Formula | Typical units |
|---|---|---|
| Density | mass / volume | g/cm³, kg/m³ |
| Pressure | force / area | N/m² (Pa) |
| Population density | population / area | people/km² |
| Concentration | mass / volume | g/l, mol/l |

For all of them: **read the formula, decide whether you have the inputs or need to rearrange, then compute. Always include units.**

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: units must match the formula.** Density $= \tfrac{m}{V}$ assumes mass and volume use compatible units. If $V$ is in m³ but $m$ is in g, you'll get the wrong answer. Convert one of them first ($1$ m³ $= 1\,000\,000$ cm³).

</div>

## Worked example

A metal sphere has mass $640$ g and density $8$ g/cm³. What is its volume?

**Step 1: Identify the formula.** Density $= \tfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$, so volume $=$ mass $\div$ density.

**Step 2: Substitute and compute.**

$$V \;=\; \dfrac{m}{\rho} \;=\; \dfrac{640}{8} \;=\; 80 \text{ cm}^3$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{80}$ cm³. **[M1 for rearranging the formula; A1 for the volume with units]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the unit. Pressure of $600$ is meaningless; it must be $600$ N/m² or $600$ Pa.
- Mixing units. If volume is given in m³ and mass in g, convert one first.
- Forgetting to rearrange. The formula is given as density $=$ mass $/$ volume, but if the question asks for mass, rearrange to mass $=$ density $\times$ volume.
- Treating "denser" as "heavier". Higher density means more mass for the same volume. A small dense object can weigh more than a large light one.

## Quick summary

- Density $= \tfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$. Triangle works the same as SDT.
- Pressure $= \tfrac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$. Units N/m² $=$ Pa.
- Population density $= \tfrac{\text{population}}{\text{area}}$.
- For all compound rates: identify formula $\to$ rearrange if needed $\to$ plug in $\to$ report with units.
