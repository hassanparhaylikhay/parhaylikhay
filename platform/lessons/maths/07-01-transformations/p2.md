## Why this matters

Pick up your phone and slide it across the table without rotating it or flipping it: that is a translation. Every point on the phone moves the same distance in the same direction. Your speaker, your camera lens, the home button, all shift by exactly the same amount. A translation is a **uniform shift**, and Cambridge writes that shift as a **column vector**.

Translations are usually worth $2$ marks: $1$ for the word "translation" and $1$ for the column vector. They are the cleanest of the four transformations. If you can read a coordinate pair correctly, you can score full marks here.

## The column vector

A translation is fully described by a column vector $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$:

It is **always written as a column**, not a row. Cambridge mark schemes reject row form $(a, b)$ for a translation, that notation is for coordinates. Two stacked numbers in a tall bracket is the rule.

## See the column vector as an arrow

The most useful picture: a column vector $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$ is **literally an arrow from the origin to the point $(a, b)$**. The arrow shows the direction; its length shows the distance.

Try the preset $\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Every point slides $5$ units right, $0$ units up. Then try $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$, every point slides $3$ units down. Diagonal moves are just a combination of the two.

## The arithmetic: add the column to every vertex

If the triangle has vertices at $A(1, 1), B(4, 1), C(2, 3)$ and the translation is $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$, find the image vertices.

$$A' = (1 + 3,\; 1 + (-2)) = (4, -1)$$

$$B' = (4 + 3,\; 1 + (-2)) = (7, -1)$$

$$C' = (2 + 3,\; 3 + (-2)) = (5, 1)$$

Every vertex shifts by the same $(3, -2)$. Sides keep their length. Angles keep their size. The triangle is **congruent**: same shape, same size, just moved.

## Going backwards: find the vector from object and image

If the object and image are both given, the column vector is just the **difference**.

$$\text{column vector} = \text{image vertex} - \text{object vertex}$$

The order matters: image minus object, not the other way round. Pick any pair of matching vertices and subtract.

## Combining two translations

If you translate by $\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ b_1 \end{pmatrix}$ and then by $\begin{pmatrix} a_2 \\ b_2 \end{pmatrix}$, the single equivalent translation is:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ b_1 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a_2 \\ b_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 + a_2 \\ b_1 + b_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

You just add the columns. The order does not matter, $A$ then $B$ gives the same result as $B$ then $A$. (That is special to translations; other transformation pairs care about order.)

## Worked example: identify a translation

Triangle $P$ has vertices at $(1, 2), (3, 2), (1, 5)$. After a single transformation it has vertices at $(-3, 5), (-1, 5), (-3, 8)$. Describe fully the transformation.

**Step 1: Check matching vertex pairs.** The first vertex moved from $(1, 2)$ to $(-3, 5)$. The shift is $(-3 - 1,\; 5 - 2) = (-4, 3)$.

**Step 2: Confirm the second pair.** $(3, 2) \to (-1, 5)$ gives a shift of $(-1 - 3,\; 5 - 2) = (-4, 3)$. Same shift. Good.

**Step 3: Confirm the third.** $(1, 5) \to (-3, 8)$ gives $(-4, 3)$ again. All three vertices shifted by the same vector, so this is a translation.

**Step 4: Write the column vector.** $\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\text{Translation by } \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}}$. **[B1 for "translation"; B1 for the correct column vector]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing the vector as a row** $(a, b)$ instead of a column. Cambridge accepts only column form for translations.
- **Doing object minus image** instead of image minus object. The sign comes out wrong. Always: image minus object.
- **Forgetting the word "translation"** in the answer. The column vector alone earns one of the two marks; the type word earns the other.
- **Confusing the top and bottom numbers.** Top = $x$-shift (right is positive). Bottom = $y$-shift (up is positive). Mixing them up flips the picture sideways.
- **Negative signs swallowed.** A vector like $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ is NOT the same as $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$; one slides left and down, the other slides right and up. Re-check signs carefully.

## Quick summary

- Translation is the simplest transformation: every point shifts by the same column vector $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$.
- Top of the column is the $x$-shift; bottom is the $y$-shift.
- Image coordinate $=$ object coordinate $+$ column vector.
- To find the vector: image minus object, on any matching vertex.
- Two translations combine by adding their columns.
- Length, angle, and orientation are all preserved; the image is congruent to the object.

The next part is reflection, the first transformation that flips the shape rather than just sliding it.
