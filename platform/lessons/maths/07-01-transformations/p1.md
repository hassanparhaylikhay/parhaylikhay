## Why this matters

Imagine sliding your phone across the table without rotating it. That is a **translation**. Now pick it up and flip it over: a **reflection**. Spin it on the table: a **rotation**. Zoom into a photo on the same phone: an **enlargement**. Those four moves are the entire syllabus for 7.1, and they show up in Cambridge papers worth $3$ to $5$ marks each, almost every year.

The maths is not the hard part. The hard part is **describing the transformation precisely enough to earn full marks**. "It rotated a bit" is worth zero. "Rotation $90°$ anticlockwise about $(0, 0)$" is worth full marks. This first part is about the four moves themselves and what you must write down for each.

## The four transformations

  <div style="display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:8px;font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.6">
    <div>· <b style="color:#00abfa">Translation</b>: every point slides the same column vector \(\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}\). No flip, no spin, no resize.</div>
    <div>· <b style="color:#fff067">Reflection</b>: every point flips to the other side of a mirror line. The mirror is the perpendicular bisector of every point-to-image pair.</div>
    <div>· <b style="color:#ff822c">Rotation</b>: every point swings around a fixed centre through the same angle. Every point traces a circular arc.</div>
    <div>· <b style="color:#ff4670">Enlargement</b>: every point moves along a ray from a fixed centre. Distance from the centre is multiplied by the scale factor \(k\).</div>
  </div>
</div>

The first three preserve length and angle, so the image is **congruent** to the object. The fourth (unless $k = \pm 1$) changes size, so the image is **similar** but not congruent.

## The big idea: every transformation has a TYPE and a RULE

You always do two things when describing a transformation:

1. **Name the type** (translation, reflection, rotation, enlargement). One word. If you write two ("a rotation and a translation"), that means it is NOT a single transformation, the question wants ONE.
2. **State the rule** (the column vector, or the mirror line, or the centre + angle + direction, or the scale factor + centre).

If you forget either half you lose marks. Cambridge calls this "describing fully" and it is the single most common phrasing on this topic.

## Worked example: identify the transformation

Triangle $A$ has vertices at $(1, 1), (4, 1), (2, 3)$. Triangle $A'$ has vertices at $(4, -1), (7, -1), (5, 1)$. Describe fully the single transformation that maps $A$ onto $A'$.

<iframe src="/widgets/transformations-step-explorer.html?preset=p1-translation-identify" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:620px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Compare a vertex pair.** Vertex $(1, 1)$ has moved to $(4, -1)$. The shift is $(4 - 1, -1 - 1) = (3, -2)$.

**Step 2: Check the second pair.** Vertex $(4, 1) \to (7, -1)$ gives $(7 - 4, -1 - 1) = (3, -2)$. Same shift. So every point has slid by the same amount: this is a **translation**.

**Step 3: Write the column vector.** The shift $(3, -2)$ in column form is $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\text{Translation by } \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}}$. **[B1 for "translation"; B1 for the correct column vector]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- **Naming two transformations** when the question says "single transformation". Re-read the question. If two are needed, the question will ask for both separately.
- **Describing the mirror in words** ("reflected diagonally") instead of as an equation. Always write $y = x$, $y = -x$, $x = 0$, etc.
- **Forgetting the direction** in a rotation. "$90°$ about the origin" is not enough; you need clockwise OR anticlockwise. If unsure, check by hand: which way does $A$ swing to reach $A'$?
- **Skipping the centre** for rotation or enlargement. Both transformations need a centre coordinate.
- **Calling an enlargement a "reduction"** when the scale factor is less than $1$. Cambridge calls it an enlargement even if the image gets smaller.

## Quick summary

- Four transformations: translation, reflection, rotation, enlargement.
- Translation, reflection, rotation preserve length and angle (image is congruent). Enlargement does not (image is similar).
- "Describe fully" needs the TYPE plus the RULE: column vector, or mirror equation, or angle + direction + centre, or scale factor + centre.
- The four patterns are the entire mark scheme for this topic.
