## Why this matters

Pinch a photo on your phone to zoom in. Every pixel slides outward from the point you pinched, by the same factor. Pinch the other way to zoom out: every pixel slides back toward that point. That is an **enlargement**. The pinch point is the **centre**. The amount of zoom is the **scale factor**.

Enlargement is the only transformation that changes the size of the shape. Cambridge enlargement questions are usually worth $2$ to $3$ marks: one for the word "enlargement", one for the scale factor, one for the centre coordinate.

## The two facts that pin an enlargement

    <div>· The image is <b>similar</b> to the object: same shape, but a different size.</div>
    <div>· Sign of \(k\) matters. Positive: image is on the same side of the centre. Negative: image is on the other side, and also rotated \(180°\).</div>
  </div>
</div>

This is the first transformation where the image is not congruent to the object (unless $k = 1$ or $k = -1$). The image is **similar**: every length grows or shrinks by a factor of $k$, but angles stay the same.

## Negative scale factor: image on the opposite side

A negative scale factor is the bit students forget. $k = -2$ does NOT mean "minus two times the distance" in a vague way: it means the image lands on the **opposite side** of the centre, at a distance $2 \times$ further. Visually, the image is flipped through the centre (the same as a $180°$ rotation), then enlarged by $2$.

So $k = -1$ alone is exactly the same as a $180°$ rotation about the centre: same size, opposite side.

For Cambridge mark schemes: always include the sign of $k$. "Enlargement scale factor $2$" and "Enlargement scale factor $-2$" are different transformations and different marks.

## How to find the centre when it's not the origin

Same trick as for rotations: **the centre is the unique point such that the line through the centre, the object vertex, and the image vertex is straight**. So draw two such lines (call them rays); they cross at the centre.

The same picture also gives the scale factor:

$$k = \dfrac{\text{distance from centre to image vertex}}{\text{distance from centre to object vertex}}$$

with a positive sign if image and object are on the same side of the centre, and negative if they are on opposite sides.

## Worked example: identify an enlargement

A triangle has vertices $A(2, 1), B(3, 1), C(2, 3)$. After a single transformation, the image has vertices $A'(3, 2), B'(5, 2), C'(3, 6)$. Describe fully the transformation.

<iframe src="/widgets/transformations-step-explorer.html?preset=p5-enlargement-identify" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:660px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Spot that it's an enlargement.** The image is the same shape as the object but bigger. So this is an enlargement.

**Step 2: Find the scale factor.** Compare two matching sides:

$$|AB| = 1 \quad \text{(object)}, \quad |A'B'| = 2 \quad \text{(image)}$$

$$k = \dfrac{|A'B'|}{|AB|} = \dfrac{2}{1} = 2$$

**Step 3: Find the centre.** Draw the line through $A$ and $A'$, and a second line through $B$ and $B'$. Both lines extend back and cross at one point. That point is the centre.

Algebraically: the line through $A(2, 1)$ and $A'(3, 2)$ has gradient $1$, equation $y = x - 1$. The line through $B(3, 1)$ and $B'(5, 2)$ has gradient $\tfrac{1}{2}$, equation $y = \tfrac{1}{2}x - \tfrac{1}{2}$. Solving $x - 1 = \tfrac{1}{2}x - \tfrac{1}{2}$ gives $x = 1$, $y = 0$. Centre is $(1, 0)$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\text{Enlargement, scale factor } 2, \text{ centre } (1, 0)}$. **[B1 for "enlargement"; B1 for scale factor $2$; B1 for centre $(1, 0)$]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the sign of $k$**. Negative scale factor enlargements are common. If the image is on the opposite side of the centre from the object, $k$ is negative.
- **Confusing scale factor with area factor**. The scale factor scales LENGTHS. Areas scale by $k^2$, volumes by $k^3$. If the question asks for area or volume, square or cube the scale factor first.
- **Forgetting the centre**. "Enlargement scale factor $2$" alone is incomplete. You also need a centre coordinate.
- **Mixing up which is object and which is image** when computing $k$. The formula is image distance over object distance, not the other way round. Object $\to$ image means $k$ multiplies the object's distance from the centre to give the image's distance.
- **Calling a fractional $k$ a "reduction"**. Cambridge calls every such transformation an enlargement, even when the image is smaller. Stick to the word "enlargement".

## Quick summary

- Enlargement needs TWO things: scale factor $k$ and centre coordinate.
- Image distance from centre $= k \times$ object distance from centre.
- Positive $k$: image same side; negative $k$: opposite side (plus $180°$ flip).
- Lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$, volumes by $k^3$.
- The image is similar to the object: same shape, different size.
- Angles never change.
- Find the centre by drawing two rays through matching (object, image) vertex pairs; they cross at the centre.
