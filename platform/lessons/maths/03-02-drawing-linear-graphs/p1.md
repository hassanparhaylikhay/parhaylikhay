## Why this matters

Every straight line on a graph can be written as $y = mx + c$. Two numbers ($m$ and $c$) are enough to fix the line completely — its steepness and where it crosses the y-axis. Cambridge tests this directly on Paper 1: "draw the line $y = 2x - 3$ on the grid."

## What the two letters mean

$$y = mx + c$$

- **$m$** is the **gradient** (slope). It tells you how steeply the line rises (or falls) as you move from left to right. Bigger $|m|$ = steeper line.
- **$c$** is the **y-intercept**. It tells you where the line crosses the y-axis: at the point $(0, c)$.

The sign of $m$ also matters:

- $m > 0$ → line slopes UP (left to right).
- $m < 0$ → line slopes DOWN (left to right).
- $m = 0$ → line is horizontal.

## Drawing a line: two methods

### Method 1 — Two intercepts (most common)

1. Find the **y-intercept**: read $c$ off directly. Plot $(0, c)$.
2. Find the **x-intercept**: set $y = 0$, solve $mx + c = 0$ → $x = -\dfrac{c}{m}$. Plot $\left(-\dfrac{c}{m},\; 0\right)$.
3. Draw a straight line through both points with a ruler.

### Method 2 — y-intercept plus the gradient

1. Plot the y-intercept $(0, c)$.
2. From there, use the gradient as a "rise over run". For example, $m = 2$ means **up 2, right 1** — go to the next point and mark it.
3. Connect with a ruler.

The two-intercept method is easier when the numbers come out to integers. The slope method is faster for "nice" gradients like $1, 2, -1, -3$.

## Worked example

Draw the line $y = 2x - 4$, showing both intercepts.

**Step 1: y-intercept.** Set $x = 0$: $y = -4$. Plot $(0, -4)$.

**Step 2: x-intercept.** Set $y = 0$: $2x - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$. Plot $(2, 0)$.

**Step 3: ruler through both points.** Done.

**Answer:** Line through $(0, -4)$ and $(2, 0)$. Gradient $2$ (uphill), y-intercept $-4$. **[B1 y-intercept; B1 x-intercept; B1 ruled line]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing $m$ and $c$.** $m$ is steepness; $c$ is y-intercept. Practice reading them off equations.
- **Plotting points by eye and then "fitting" a line.** Use a ruler. The mark scheme awards a B mark for a properly drawn line.
- **Forgetting that $y = mx + c$ requires $x = 0$ to give $y = c$.** Always check by substituting $x = 0$.
- **Trying to plot 5 random points.** Two well-chosen points are enough — the intercepts.

## Quick summary

- $y = mx + c$: $m$ is gradient, $c$ is y-intercept.
- Plot the y-intercept first, then either the x-intercept or use the gradient.
- Use a ruler.
