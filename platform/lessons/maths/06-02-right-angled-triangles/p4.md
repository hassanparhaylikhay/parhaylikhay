## The reverse problem

Now the question flips: you know **two sides**, and you want **the angle**. The setup is the same SOH-CAH-TOA logic, but at the end you apply the **inverse** trig function ($\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$, $\tan^{-1}$) to recover $\theta$ from a ratio.

What is $\sin^{-1}$? It is the **inverse** of $\sin$. If $\sin\theta = 0.5$, then $\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30°$. The button on your calculator is usually labelled $\sin^{-1}$ or $\arcsin$, accessed by pressing **SHIFT** (or **2nd** or **INV**) before SIN. Same for COS and TAN.

## Pattern 1: angle from opp and hyp (use sin⁻¹)

The setup: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} = \dfrac{5}{10} = 0.5$, so $\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30°$.

## Pattern 2: angle from opp and adj (use tan⁻¹)

<iframe src="/widgets/trig-step-explorer.html?preset=find-angle-tan" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:520px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

For the $(3, 4, 5)$ triangle, with the angle between the side $4$ and the hypotenuse:

$$\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{4} = 0.75$$

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.75) \approx 36.9°$$

The other acute angle is $90° - 36.9° = 53.1°$.

## Pattern 3: angle from adj and hyp (use cos⁻¹)

The third case follows the same logic. If the adjacent is $8$ and the hypotenuse is $10$:

$$\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} = \dfrac{8}{10} = 0.8$$

$$\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.8) \approx 36.9°$$

(Notice this is the same angle as the $(3, 4, 5)$ triangle case above. Two sides of $(3, 4, 5)$ are $\text{adj} = 4$, $\text{hyp} = 5$, giving $\cos\theta = 0.8$, the same answer.)

## Worked example: angle of a ramp

A wheelchair ramp rises $0.6$ m over a horizontal distance of $5$ m. Find the angle of the ramp with the ground (to 1 d.p.).

## Domain note: acute angles only

In a right triangle, both acute angles are strictly between $0°$ and $90°$. So the inverse trig functions return a value in that range. If your calculator returns something outside that range, double-check: you may have mistyped, or the triangle isn't actually right-angled.

The exact-value table from the previous part tells you instantly when the angle is one of the special values:

- $\sin\theta = 0.5 \Rightarrow \theta = 30°$
- $\sin\theta = 0.7071 \Rightarrow \theta \approx 45°$
- $\sin\theta = 0.8660 \Rightarrow \theta \approx 60°$
- $\tan\theta = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 45°$

These are worth recognising on sight in the exam.

## Common mistakes

- **Pressing SIN instead of SIN⁻¹.** $\sin(0.5)$ in degrees gives $\approx 0.0087$, totally wrong. Use SHIFT first.
- **Picking the wrong ratio.** Identify the pair of sides (the two you know), then pick the matching ratio.
- **Calculator in radians.** A "wildly off" answer is the giveaway.
- **Forgetting to round at the end.** Keep full calculator value through, round only the final answer.
- **Using the inverse of the wrong number.** $\sin\theta = 5/10$ gives $\sin^{-1}(0.5)$, not $\sin^{-1}(5)$ which would error out.

## Quick summary

- Two sides known, want angle → use the inverse trig.
- opp+hyp → $\theta = \sin^{-1}(\text{opp}/\text{hyp})$.
- adj+hyp → $\theta = \cos^{-1}(\text{adj}/\text{hyp})$.
- opp+adj → $\theta = \tan^{-1}(\text{opp}/\text{adj})$.
- Press SHIFT (or 2nd / INV) before SIN/COS/TAN to access the inverse.
- Acute-angle answer is between $0°$ and $90°$.

The remaining parts bring sin, cos, tan into Cambridge contexts: real 2D problems, bearings, angles of elevation and depression.
