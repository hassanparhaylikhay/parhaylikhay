## The core insight

In any right triangle with a chosen acute angle $\theta$, the **ratio** of any two sides is fully determined by $\theta$. It does not depend on how big the triangle is. Two triangles with the same shape (same angles) have identical ratios, just scaled.

That single fact is what makes trigonometry work. Once we name those ratios, we can use them to unlock missing sides and angles in any right triangle.

## Naming the three sides

Before we write a ratio, we need names for the sides. **The names depend on which angle you are working from.**

<iframe src="/widgets/trig-step-explorer.html?preset=label-sides" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:520px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Walking through the three names, with $\theta$ at the bottom-left:

- **Hypotenuse** (pink): the side opposite the right angle. The longest side. **Doesn't change** with $\theta$.
- **Opposite** (orange): the side across from $\theta$. Neither endpoint touches the angle vertex.
- **Adjacent** (blue): the side next to $\theta$ that isn't the hypotenuse. One endpoint is at the angle vertex.

If you move $\theta$ to the other acute corner, the **opp and adj swap** (the hypotenuse stays). So always identify your $\theta$ first, then name the sides.

## The three ratios

For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle, define:

$$\sin\theta = \dfrac{{\color{#ff822c}\text{opp}}}{{\color{#ff4670}\text{hyp}}} \qquad \cos\theta = \dfrac{{\color{#00abfa}\text{adj}}}{{\color{#ff4670}\text{hyp}}} \qquad \tan\theta = \dfrac{{\color{#ff822c}\text{opp}}}{{\color{#00abfa}\text{adj}}}$$

These are the three **trigonometric ratios**. Each is a single number that depends only on $\theta$. Your calculator has $\sin$, $\cos$, $\tan$ buttons to compute them.

The mnemonic almost everyone uses is **SOH-CAH-TOA**:

Each block reads "ratio = first letter ÷ second letter". So **S**OH means $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{O}}{\text{H}}$. **C**AH means $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{A}}{\text{H}}$. **T**OA means $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{O}}{\text{A}}$.

Memorise the mnemonic. It will save you on every trig question.

That is the entire foundation of trigonometry: the three ratios depend on **angle only**. So once you know an angle, the ratios are fixed, and you can use them to compute any unknown side or angle.

## Some exact values worth knowing

Cambridge often uses angles where the ratios come out to clean numbers. Lock these in:

| $\theta$ | $\sin\theta$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\tan\theta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0°$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $30°$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ |
| $45°$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $60°$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $\sqrt{3}$ |
| $90°$ | $1$ | $0$ | undefined |

For everything else, use a calculator. Make sure your calculator is in **degree mode** (DEG, not RAD or GRAD), because Cambridge always asks in degrees. A common slip: leaving the calculator in RAD mode after a science class. Every trig answer in the maths paper will then be wrong.

## Worked example: computing all three ratios

A right triangle has legs $5$ cm and $12$ cm and hypotenuse $13$ cm. Find $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$, $\tan\theta$ for the angle $\theta$ that sits between the side of length $12$ and the hypotenuse.

The chosen angle $\theta$ has the side $12$ next to it (adjacent) and the side $5$ across from it (opposite). The hypotenuse is $13$.

So:

$$\sin\theta = \dfrac{{\color{#ff822c}5}}{{\color{#ff4670}13}}, \qquad \cos\theta = \dfrac{{\color{#00abfa}12}}{{\color{#ff4670}13}}, \qquad \tan\theta = \dfrac{{\color{#ff822c}5}}{{\color{#00abfa}12}}$$

Notice that $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ are both fractions less than $1$ (because the legs are shorter than the hypotenuse), while $\tan\theta$ can be any positive number.

**[B1 for $\sin\theta = \tfrac{5}{13}$; B1 for $\cos\theta = \tfrac{12}{13}$; B1 for $\tan\theta = \tfrac{5}{12}$]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## A check that always holds

For any angle $\theta$ between $0°$ and $90°$:

$$\sin^{2}\theta + \cos^{2}\theta = 1$$

This is just Pythagoras applied to the unit circle (a hypotenuse of $1$). Useful if you have $\sin\theta$ and want $\cos\theta$ without using the calculator's inverse.

## Common mistakes

- **Calculator in radians, not degrees.** Always check the mode indicator.
- **Naming sides without picking $\theta$ first.** opp/adj depend on which angle you are working from. Pick first, label second.
- **Mixing up sin and cos.** SOH (Sin = Opp/Hyp) vs CAH (Cos = Adj/Hyp). Saying the mnemonic out loud once before substituting catches this.
- **Forgetting that the hypotenuse is the longest.** If your "hyp" comes out shorter than another side, you have mislabelled.

## Quick summary

- The three ratios depend ONLY on the angle, not on the triangle's size.
- $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$.
- Mnemonic: **SOH-CAH-TOA**.
- Calculator must be in DEG mode for Cambridge.
- Memorise the exact values for $30°$, $45°$, $60°$.
