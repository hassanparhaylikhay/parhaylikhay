## The decision rule

To find a missing side you need: the angle $\theta$, one known side, and the rule for picking the right ratio.

The rule is just **SOH-CAH-TOA** read backwards. Look at what you have and what you want:

Find the **pair** of sides involved (the one you know and the one you want), and the ratio is determined.

## Three patterns of "find a side"

### Pattern 1: opp from hyp + angle (use sin)

<iframe src="/widgets/trig-step-explorer.html?preset=find-opp-sin" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:520px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

The setup: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$. The unknown is in the numerator, so multiply both sides by $\text{hyp}$:

$$\text{opp} = \text{hyp} \times \sin\theta$$

For $\theta = 35°$, $\text{hyp} = 12$: $\text{opp} = 12\sin 35° \approx 6.88$.

### Pattern 2: adj from hyp + angle (use cos)

<iframe src="/widgets/trig-step-explorer.html?preset=find-adj-cos" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:520px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Same shape. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, so $\text{adj} = \text{hyp}\cos\theta$.

### Pattern 3: opp from adj + angle (use tan)

<iframe src="/widgets/trig-step-explorer.html?preset=find-opp-tan" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:520px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$, so $\text{opp} = \text{adj}\tan\theta$.

In all three patterns the unknown side is in the **numerator**, so the formula is "known side × the trig ratio". This is the easy case.

## When the unknown is in the denominator

Sometimes the side you know is the OPPOSITE or ADJACENT, and you want the HYPOTENUSE. Then the unknown ends up in the denominator and you need an extra rearranging step.

<iframe src="/widgets/trig-step-explorer.html?preset=find-hyp-from-opp" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:520px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

For $\theta = 30°$, $\text{opp} = 5$:

$$\sin 30° = \dfrac{5}{x}$$

The unknown $x$ is in the denominator. Multiply both sides by $x$, then divide by $\sin 30°$:

$$x = \dfrac{5}{\sin 30°} = \dfrac{5}{0.5} = 10$$

The general rule: **when the unknown is in the denominator, divide instead of multiply**.

## Worked example: find the height of a tree

A tree casts a shadow $9$ m long. The angle from the tip of the shadow up to the top of the tree is $58°$. Find the height of the tree (to 1 d.p.).

Set up the right triangle: the shadow is the **adjacent** (along the ground), the tree is the **opposite** (vertical), the line of sight is the **hypotenuse**.

We have adj and want opp, with angle $58°$. The opp+adj pairing means **tan**:

$$\tan 58° = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{9}$$

$$\text{opp} = 9 \tan 58°$$

$$\text{opp} \approx 9 \times 1.6003 = 14.4\;\text{m}$$

**[M1 for $\tan 58° = h/9$ or equivalent; A1 for $h = 14.4$ m]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example: find the slope length

A ramp rises at an angle of $12°$ over a horizontal distance of $4.5$ m. Find the length of the ramp itself, to 2 d.p.

The "horizontal distance" is the adjacent. The ramp is the hypotenuse. Angle $12°$ is between them.

We have adj and want hyp. adj+hyp → cos:

$$\cos 12° = \dfrac{4.5}{x}$$

The unknown is in the denominator, so:

$$x = \dfrac{4.5}{\cos 12°} \approx \dfrac{4.5}{0.9781} \approx 4.60\;\text{m}$$

**[M1 for $\cos 12° = 4.5/x$; A1 for $x = 4.60$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Method-mark technique

Cambridge gives the method mark (M1) the moment you write a correct trig equation that links the right sides, even before you compute. So **always write the trig equation first**, even if you can do the arithmetic in your head. Skipping straight to the answer risks losing M1 if the answer has a small slip.

The order on the paper:

1. Identify $\theta$ and the pair of sides involved.
2. Write the trig equation: e.g. $\sin\theta = \tfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$ or $\tan\theta = \tfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$.
3. Substitute the known values.
4. Rearrange if the unknown is in the denominator.
5. Compute and round.

## Common mistakes

- **Picking the wrong ratio.** Always identify the PAIR of sides (known + unknown) before reaching for sin/cos/tan.
- **Forgetting to flip the equation when the unknown is in the denominator.** $\sin\theta = \tfrac{5}{x}$ does NOT mean $x = 5\sin\theta$.
- **Calculator in radians.** $\sin 30°$ should be $0.5$. If you get $-0.988$, your calculator is in RAD mode.
- **Mixing up opp and adj.** Pick $\theta$ first, then label.
- **Premature rounding.** Keep full calculator value; round only the final answer.

## Quick summary

- Pick the ratio by the **pair** of sides involved: opp+hyp → sin, adj+hyp → cos, opp+adj → tan.
- Write the trig equation first to earn the method mark.
- If the unknown is in the numerator: multiply (e.g. $\text{opp} = \text{hyp}\sin\theta$).
- If the unknown is in the denominator: divide (e.g. $\text{hyp} = \text{opp} / \sin\theta$).
- Calculator in DEG mode.
