## Why this matters

A Daewoo bus from Lahore charges Rs. $300$ to enter plus Rs. $25$ per kilometre. Your total fare is Rs. $475$. How far did you go? Setting up $300 + 25d = 475$ and solving for $d$ is a **linear equation**, and it's the most common type of equation Cambridge tests. Almost every multi-step problem on Paper 1 and Paper 2 has a linear-equation step inside it.

A linear equation is one where the unknown $x$ (or $d$, or any letter) appears to the **first power only**: no $x^2$, no $\sqrt{x}$, no $\dfrac{1}{x}$. Solving it means finding the single value of the unknown that makes both sides equal.

## The balance idea

Picture an old-style scale. **What you do to one side, you must do to the other**, otherwise the balance tips. That's the whole rule for solving any equation:

$$\text{LHS} = \text{RHS} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{LHS} \pm c = \text{RHS} \pm c$$

You can also **multiply or divide** both sides by the same non-zero number, and the equation still balances.

The goal is to **isolate $x$**: peel off whatever is around it (constants, coefficients, brackets) until you have $x$ alone on one side.

## See it on a graph

For an equation like $2x + 1 = 5$, picture two lines on a graph: $\textcolor{#fff067}{y = 2x + 1}$ (the left side, a sloping line) and $\textcolor{#0fee89}{y = 5}$ (the right side, a horizontal level). The solution is the **$x$-coordinate where the two lines cross**.

## The four moves

Almost every linear equation reduces to one of four basic moves applied repeatedly:

- **Add** the same thing to both sides.
- **Subtract** the same thing from both sides.
- **Multiply** both sides by the same non-zero number.
- **Divide** both sides by the same non-zero number.

You may also need to **expand brackets** (from 2.2 p2) or **combine like terms** before applying one of the four moves.

## Walk: a clean two-step

Solve $3x + 4 = 10$.

**Step 1: Get the $x$-term alone.** Subtract $4$ from both sides.

$$3x + 4 - 4 \;=\; 10 - 4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x = 6$$

**Step 2: Get $x$ alone.** Divide both sides by $3$.

$$\dfrac{3x}{3} \;=\; \dfrac{6}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 2$$

Always **check**: substitute $x = 2$ back. $3(2) + 4 = 10$ ✓.

## Walk: $x$ on both sides

When $x$ appears on both sides, **collect it on one side first**.

Solve $7x - 2 = 3x + 14$.

**Step 1: Subtract $3x$ from both sides** (so $x$ only sits on the left).

$$7x - 3x - 2 \;=\; 14 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4x - 2 = 14$$

**Step 2: Add $2$ to both sides.**

$$4x = 16$$

**Step 3: Divide by $4$.**

$$x = 4$$

Move $x$ toward whichever side keeps its coefficient positive. It's a small habit but saves sign mistakes.

## Walk: equation with brackets

Solve $4(x - 1) = 2x + 6$.

**Step 1: Expand the bracket** (from 2.2 p2).

$$4x - 4 \;=\; 2x + 6$$

**Step 2: Subtract $2x$.**

$$2x - 4 \;=\; 6$$

**Step 3: Add $4$.**

$$2x = 10$$

**Step 4: Divide by $2$.**

$$x = 5$$

Whenever brackets appear, expand first, then collect.

## Constructing equations from words

Cambridge often gives a word problem and expects you to **write** the equation before solving it. The recipe:

1. Identify the unknown. Give it a letter (often $x$).
2. Write each piece of information as an algebraic expression in $x$.
3. Use the "equals" word in the question (is, equals, total, sum, gives) to connect two expressions with $=$.
4. Solve.

**Example.** "A taxi charges Rs. $50$ plus Rs. $20$ per km. Find the distance for a fare of Rs. $130$."

- Let $d$ = distance in km.
- Total fare: $50 + 20d$.
- Equation: $50 + 20d = 130$.
- Solve: $20d = 80$, so $d = 4$ km.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: do the same operation to BOTH sides, every time.** $3x + 4 = 10$ does NOT become $3x = 10$ by "moving the $4$". You're subtracting $4$ from both sides; the right side becomes $6$, not stays at $10$. The "moving" shorthand is fine once you trust it, but only if you're doing the operation on both sides.

</div>

## Worked example

Solve $5(x + 2) - 3 = 2(x + 5)$.

**Step 1: Expand both brackets.**

$$5x + 10 - 3 \;=\; 2x + 10$$

$$5x + 7 \;=\; 2x + 10$$

**Step 2: Collect $x$ on the left.** Subtract $2x$.

$$3x + 7 \;=\; 10$$

**Step 3: Subtract $7$.**

$$3x \;=\; 3$$

**Step 4: Divide by $3$.**

$$x = 1$$

Check: $5(1 + 2) - 3 = 12$ and $2(1 + 5) = 12$ ✓.

**Answer:** $\boxed{x = 1}$. **[M1 for the expansion; M1 for collecting like terms; A1 for the value]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Doing an operation on only ONE side. Both sides must change together.
- Sign error when moving a term across. $5 - 2x = 11$ becomes $-2x = 6$, so $x = -3$. The minus sticks with the $2x$.
- Forgetting to expand brackets before collecting. $4(x - 1) = 2x + 6$ does NOT mean $4x - 1 = 2x + 6$. Distribute first.
- Dividing too early. $3(x + 5) = 12$: divide by $3$ first to get $x + 5 = 4$, then $x = -1$. Don't expand if dividing is faster.

## Quick summary

- Linear equations: unknown appears to the **first power only**.
- Solve by **doing the same operation to both sides** until $x$ is alone.
- The four basic moves: add, subtract, multiply, divide.
- Expand brackets and collect like terms BEFORE isolating $x$.
- For word problems: name the unknown, build the equation, solve.
- Always check by substituting back.
