## Why this matters

Cambridge gives you the formula for the area of a circle as $A = \pi r^2$. But what if you know the area and want the radius? You'd want the formula written **with $r$ as the subject**: $r = \sqrt{A/\pi}$. **Changing the subject** of a formula means rearranging it so a different letter sits alone on the left.

You'll meet this on Paper 1 directly ("make $x$ the subject of...") and inside Paper 2 problems where a formula gives you the wrong starting variable. The technique is exactly the same as solving an equation: do the same thing to both sides, peeling off operations one by one until the target letter is alone.

## The recipe

To make $\textcolor{#0fee89}{x}$ the subject:

1. Write the formula with everything else on one side, $x$ on the other (or as close to that as possible).
2. Apply **inverse operations** to peel away whatever is around $x$.
3. When $x$ appears more than once, use **factoring** to bring all the $x$-pieces together.
4. The final form has $x$ alone on the left, no $x$ on the right.

The four basic moves from p1 still apply: add, subtract, multiply, divide, plus square / square root for power-2 cases.

## Walk: simple linear

Make $x$ the subject of $y = 3x + 5$.

Subtract $5$ from both sides:

$$y - 5 \;=\; 3x$$

Divide by $3$:

$$x \;=\; \dfrac{y - 5}{3}$$

Done. Two moves, one subject change.

## Walk: power-2 case

Make $r$ the subject of $A = \pi r^2$.

Divide both sides by $\pi$:

$$\dfrac{A}{\pi} \;=\; r^2$$

Take the positive square root:

$$r \;=\; \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}$$

Take the **positive** root only when the original quantity (radius, length, mass) cannot be negative.

## Walk: subject inside a square

Make $u$ the subject of $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.

Subtract $2as$ to isolate the $u^2$ term:

$$v^2 - 2as \;=\; u^2$$

Square root:

$$u \;=\; \sqrt{v^2 - 2as}$$

The minus inside the square root **stays inside**; you cannot bring it out without changing the value.

## Walk: subject inside a fraction

Make $a$ the subject of $S = \dfrac{n(a + l)}{2}$.

Multiply both sides by $2$:

$$2S \;=\; n(a + l)$$

Divide by $n$:

$$\dfrac{2S}{n} \;=\; a + l$$

Subtract $l$:

$$a \;=\; \dfrac{2S}{n} - l$$

The order of moves matters: undo each "outer" operation in reverse order. The last thing the formula did to $a$ was add $l$ at the end; the first thing you undo is that.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: undo operations in REVERSE order.** If a formula does "multiply by $3$, then add $5$" to $x$, you undo it as "subtract $5$, then divide by $3$". Reversing the order on both halves matters; getting it wrong gives wrong values.

</div>

## When the subject appears twice: factor and isolate

Cambridge sometimes gives you a formula where $x$ shows up **on both sides** or in **two places**. The technique:

1. Move every $x$-term to one side (often using the four-moves rules).
2. **Factor out $x$** (from 2.2 p3).
3. Divide by whatever multiplies $x$.

**Example.** Make $x$ the subject of $\dfrac{x + 1}{x - 1} = y$.

**Step 1: Cross-multiply** (the fraction-equation rule from p2).

$$x + 1 \;=\; y(x - 1)$$

**Step 2: Expand the right side.**

$$x + 1 \;=\; xy - y$$

**Step 3: Move all $x$-terms to one side.** Subtract $xy$:

$$x + 1 - xy \;=\; -y$$

Subtract $1$:

$$x - xy \;=\; -y - 1$$

**Step 4: Factor out $x$.**

$$x(1 - y) \;=\; -(y + 1)$$

**Step 5: Divide by $(1 - y)$.**

$$x \;=\; \dfrac{-(y + 1)}{1 - y} \;=\; \dfrac{y + 1}{y - 1}$$

(In the last step we multiplied top and bottom by $-1$ to make the answer cleaner.)

These "subject appears twice" questions are the trickiest in this part. Memorise the **factor-then-isolate** flow.

## Worked example

Make $h$ the subject of $V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.

**Step 1: Multiply both sides by $3$.**

$$3V \;=\; \pi r^2 h$$

**Step 2: Divide both sides by $\pi r^2$.**

$$h \;=\; \dfrac{3V}{\pi r^2}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{h = \dfrac{3V}{\pi r^2}}$. **[M1 for clearing the fraction; A1 for the result]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Doing the operations in the wrong order. Reverse-order is essential.
- Taking only the positive square root when both should appear. For a length or radius, positive only is right; for an unsigned variable, write $\pm$.
- Failing to factor when the subject appears twice. $x$-terms must be collected and factored before isolating.
- Sign errors when moving terms across. The minus stays with the term, not with the side.

## Quick summary

- Changing the subject = **solving the formula for a different letter**.
- Apply the four basic moves and (when needed) square / square-root to peel off operations.
- Undo operations in **reverse order**.
- When the target letter appears twice: collect, **factor**, divide.
- Take the positive square root for naturally positive quantities; otherwise $\pm$.
