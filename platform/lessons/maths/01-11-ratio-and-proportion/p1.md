## Why this matters

Ratios appear in almost every Number question Cambridge sets. They show up in real life too: recipes, map scales, mixing concrete, sharing money. Cambridge marks the simplest form, so leaving an answer as $4 : 6$ when $2 : 3$ is expected costs you the accuracy mark. Once you can simplify any ratio cleanly, every other ratio question becomes easier.

## What a ratio is

A **ratio** compares two or more quantities. We write it with a colon, like **<span style="color:#00abfa">2 : 3</span>**, read as "two to three".

If a recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 3 cups of milk, the ratio of flour to milk is $2 : 3$. The **order matters**: $2 : 3$ is not the same as $3 : 2$.

## Simplifying: divide by the HCF

A ratio is in **simplest form** when both sides are whole numbers with **no common factor** other than $1$.

To simplify, **divide every part by the HCF** (highest common factor):

$$\textcolor{#00abfa}{12} : \textcolor{#fff067}{18} \;\xrightarrow{\div\, 6}\; \textcolor{#00abfa}{2} : \textcolor{#fff067}{3}$$

Both $12$ and $18$ are divisible by $6$ (the HCF), so dividing keeps the ratio the same but uses smaller numbers.

**Example.** Simplify $20 : 30 : 40$.

HCF of all three: $10$. Divide each: $2 : 3 : 4$.

The same rule works for ratios with more than two parts. Find the HCF of all numbers and divide.

## Ratios with decimals or fractions

If a ratio has decimals or fractions, **multiply both sides by something to clear them first**, then simplify.

**Decimal example.** Simplify $0.5 : 2$.

Multiply both by $2$: $1 : 4$. Done.

**Fraction example.** Simplify $\tfrac{2}{3} : \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Multiply both by the LCM of the denominators ($6$):

$$\tfrac{2}{3} \times 6 = 4, \qquad \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 = 3$$

So $\tfrac{2}{3} : \tfrac{1}{2} = 4 : 3$.

The rule: **whatever you multiply or divide one side by, you must do to the others.** That keeps the ratio the same.

## Ratios with units

If two values share the same unit, simplify them as if they were just numbers. If they have **different units, convert both to the same unit first**.

**Same units.** $400$ g $: 600$ g $= 400 : 600 = 2 : 3$.

**Different units.** $50$ cm $: 1$ m. Convert to the same unit: $50$ cm $: 100$ cm $= 1 : 2$.

Cambridge often hides this in word problems: "the ratio of a length in cm to a length in m". Always check the units match before simplifying.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't leave fractions or decimals in a simplified ratio.** $\tfrac{1}{2} : 1$ is technically a true ratio, but the simplest form is $1 : 2$. Cambridge wants whole-number ratios with no common factor.

</div>

## Worked example

A box contains red, blue, and green marbles in the ratio $24 : 30 : 18$. Write this ratio in its simplest form.

**Step 1: Find the HCF of all three numbers.**

- Factors of $24$: $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24$
- Factors of $30$: $1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30$
- Factors of $18$: $1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18$

Common: $1, 2, 3, 6$. Highest common factor is $\boxed{6}$.

**Step 2: Divide every part by $6$.**

$$24 \div 6 = 4, \qquad 30 \div 6 = 5, \qquad 18 \div 6 = 3$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{4 : 5 : 3}$. **[M1 for finding the HCF; A1 for the simplified ratio]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing by a common factor that's not the HCF. $12 : 18$ divided by $2$ gives $6 : 9$, which is still simplifiable. Always use the HCF in one step.
- Forgetting to convert units. $50$ cm $: 1$ m is NOT $50 : 1$. Convert first: $50 : 100 = 1 : 2$.
- Leaving fractions or decimals. $1.5 : 3$ should be cleared to $3 : 6 = 1 : 2$.
- Mixing up the order. The ratio of A to B is NOT the same as B to A.

## Quick summary

- A ratio compares quantities. The order matters.
- Simplest form: <b style="color:#0fee89">whole numbers</b>, no common factor other than $1$.
- Simplify by dividing every part by the <b style="color:#fff067">HCF</b>.
- For decimals or fractions, multiply both sides to clear them first.
- For different units, convert to the same unit first.
