## Why this matters

Proportional reasoning runs through dozens of Cambridge questions. Recipe scaling, map distances, fuel consumption, currency conversion, "best value" comparisons; they all use the same idea: **find one unit, then scale**. Once you have this habit, you stop relying on guesswork in word problems.

## The "find one unit" method

When two quantities are **directly proportional** (one doubles when the other doubles), the cleanest method is:

1. **Find the value of one unit** of one quantity.
2. **Multiply** by the new quantity you want.

This is sometimes called the "unitary method", and it works for almost every direct-proportion question.

## Scaling recipes

Recipes are direct proportion in disguise. If a recipe for $n$ people uses $X$ of an ingredient, then for $m$ people it needs:

$$\dfrac{X}{n} \times m \;=\; \dfrac{X \cdot m}{n}$$

The first form (find per-person, then multiply) is usually easier to think about.

**Example.** A recipe for $8$ people uses $300$ g of sugar. For $12$ people:

- Per person: $\tfrac{300}{8} = 37.5$ g.
- For $12$ people: $12 \times 37.5 = 450$ g.

## Map scales

Map scales are written as a ratio like $1 : 50\,000$. This means **$1$ unit on the map equals $50\,000$ of the same units in real life**.

If $1$ cm on a map represents $50\,000$ cm in reality, that is:

$$50\,000 \text{ cm} = 500 \text{ m} = 0.5 \text{ km}$$

So $1 : 50\,000$ tells us: every $1$ cm on the map = $0.5$ km on the ground.

**Example.** A road is $4$ cm long on a $1 : 50\,000$ map. Real length:

- $1$ cm map $\to$ $0.5$ km real.
- $4$ cm map $\to$ $4 \times 0.5 = 2$ km.

The same logic works in reverse: if a real road is $7$ km, on the map it is $\tfrac{7}{0.5} = 14$ cm.

## Best value (cost per unit)

To compare two products of different sizes, compute the **cost per unit** (per gram, per litre, etc.) and pick the lower number.

**Example.** $250$ g cereal costs \$3.00 vs $400$ g costs \$4.40. Which is better value?

- $250$ g pack: $\tfrac{300}{250} = 1.2$ cents per gram.
- $400$ g pack: $\tfrac{440}{400} = 1.1$ cents per gram.

Lower per-gram cost = better value. **The $400$ g pack wins.**

The same idea works for any "value" comparison: km per litre (higher is better for fuel efficiency), pence per minute (lower is cheaper), and so on. **Always work out a per-unit value and compare like with like.**

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't just compare the totals.** A $400$ g pack at \$4.40 might LOOK more expensive than a $250$ g pack at \$3.00, but per gram it's cheaper. Always compute a per-unit price (or per-unit anything) before deciding which is better value.

</div>

## Worked example: scaling

A recipe for $20$ biscuits uses $150$ g butter and $200$ g flour. How much butter and flour are needed for $30$ biscuits?

**Step 1: Find per-biscuit values.**

- Butter per biscuit: $\tfrac{150}{20} = 7.5$ g.
- Flour per biscuit: $\tfrac{200}{20} = 10$ g.

**Step 2: Multiply by the new quantity.**

- Butter for $30$ biscuits: $30 \times 7.5 = 225$ g.
- Flour for $30$ biscuits: $30 \times 10 = 300$ g.

**Answer:** $\boxed{225}$ g butter, $\boxed{300}$ g flour. **[M1 for finding the per-biscuit values; A1 each for the two values]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Worked example: best value

Two phones offer minutes packages:

- Plan A: $200$ minutes for \$15.
- Plan B: $350$ minutes for \$24.50.

Which is better value per minute?

**Plan A.** $\tfrac{1500}{200} = 7.5$ cents per minute.

**Plan B.** $\tfrac{2450}{350} = 7$ cents per minute.

**Plan B** is cheaper per minute, so it's the better value.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\text{Plan B}}$, at $7$ c/min vs $7.5$ c/min for Plan A. **[M1 for one cost per minute; A1 for the comparison]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing totals instead of per-unit values. Always compute per gram, per minute, per km, etc.
- Multiplying when you should divide (or vice versa) when finding the per-unit value. The "per one" value comes from DIVIDING; scaling up comes from MULTIPLYING.
- For map scales, forgetting to convert units. $1 : 50\,000$ in cm means $50\,000$ cm = $500$ m = $0.5$ km in real life.
- For inverse problems (e.g. "$5$ workers take $10$ days, how long for $10$ workers?"), this is INVERSE proportion (covered in 2.8). The "find one unit and multiply" approach above does NOT apply directly.

## Quick summary

- <b style="color:#fff067">Find one unit</b> first (per person, per cm, per gram).
- Then <b style="color:#0fee89">multiply</b> to scale up to the new quantity.
- Map scales: $1 : k$ means $1$ unit on the map = $k$ units in reality.
- Best value: compute <b style="color:#00abfa">cost per unit</b> for each option; pick the lower.
