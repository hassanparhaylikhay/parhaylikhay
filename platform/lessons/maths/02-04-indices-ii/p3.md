## Why this matters

Once index rules are second nature, Cambridge stacks them: cube something, divide by something else, then take a square root. The arithmetic gets longer but the rules don't change. **Treat each rule as a single step**, write the working out clean, and most Paper 2 index questions land within minutes.

This part is mostly drill: take a multi-rule expression, decompose it into steps, apply each rule in turn. The key skill is **bookkeeping**, not new theory.

## The five rules, layered

You've already met all of these. Combined questions just stack them:

- $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$
- $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$
- $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$
- $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$ and $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$
- $a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$ and $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$

The two new patterns to be especially clean about:

**Power of a quotient.** $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$. The index hits both top and bottom.

**Fractional indices as roots-and-powers.** $a^{m/n}$ means $\sqrt[n]{a^m}$. To evaluate $64^{2/3}$, take the cube root ($\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$), then square ($4^2 = 16$). The order doesn't matter mathematically, but doing the root first usually keeps the numbers small.

## Walk: power of a quotient

Simplify $\left(\dfrac{2x^5}{3}\right)^3$.

**Step 1: Apply the cube to top and bottom separately.**

$$\dfrac{(2x^5)^3}{3^3}$$

**Step 2: Apply power-of-a-power on the top.**

$$(2x^5)^3 \;=\; 2^3 \times x^{5 \times 3} \;=\; 8 x^{15}$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

$$\dfrac{8 x^{15}}{27}$$

Each step is one rule. The order: outer power → distribute to top and bottom → simplify each piece.

## Walk: fractional index evaluation

Evaluate $64^{2/3}$.

**Step 1: Recognise $64 = 4^3$.**

$$64^{2/3} \;=\; (4^3)^{2/3}$$

**Step 2: Power-of-a-power.**

$$(4^3)^{2/3} \;=\; 4^{3 \times 2/3} \;=\; 4^2 \;=\; 16$$

Alternative: take the cube root first ($\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$), then square ($4^2 = 16$). Same answer.

**Try this**: $27^{2/3}$. Cube root of $27$ is $3$, squared is $9$. So $27^{2/3} = 9$.

## Walk: division with negative and fractional indices

Simplify $\dfrac{2}{5}x^{1/2} \div 2x^{-2}$.

**Step 1: Keep, flip, multiply.**

$$\dfrac{2}{5}x^{1/2} \times \dfrac{1}{2x^{-2}}$$

But $\dfrac{1}{x^{-2}} = x^2$, so the second fraction becomes $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$.

$$\dfrac{2}{5}x^{1/2} \times \dfrac{x^2}{2}$$

**Step 2: Multiply coefficients and add indices.**

- Coefficients: $\dfrac{2}{5} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{5}$.
- Letters: $x^{1/2} \times x^2 = x^{1/2 + 2} = x^{5/2}$.

**Step 3: Combine.**

$$\dfrac{x^{5/2}}{5}$$

The shortcut for division-with-a-negative-index: dividing by $x^{-n}$ is the same as multiplying by $x^n$. So $\div x^{-2}$ becomes $\times x^2$, and the index in the answer is $\tfrac{1}{2} + 2 = \tfrac{5}{2}$.

## Walk: combined power, multiply, divide

Simplify $\dfrac{(2x^5)^3}{4x^7}$.

**Step 1: Apply the power.** $(2x^5)^3 = 8 x^{15}$.

**Step 2: Divide.**

$$\dfrac{8 x^{15}}{4 x^7} \;=\; \dfrac{8}{4} \times x^{15 - 7} \;=\; 2 x^8$$

Two operations, one answer. The order matters: do the bracket first (because of BIDMAS), then the division.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: a fractional index is not a fraction.** $x^{1/2}$ means $\sqrt{x}$, not $\tfrac{1}{2}x$. Similarly $x^{2/3}$ means $\sqrt[3]{x^2}$, not $\tfrac{2}{3}x$. Index notation and coefficient notation look similar but mean very different things.

</div>

## Worked example

Simplify $3x^{-4} \times \dfrac{2}{3} x^{1/2}$.

**Step 1: Multiply the coefficients.**

$$3 \times \dfrac{2}{3} \;=\; 2$$

**Step 2: Add the indices on $x$.**

$$x^{-4} \times x^{1/2} \;=\; x^{-4 + 1/2} \;=\; x^{-7/2}$$

**Step 3: Combine.**

$$2 x^{-7/2}$$

Or, written without a negative index: $\dfrac{2}{x^{7/2}}$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{2x^{-7/2}}$ (equivalently $\dfrac{2}{x^{7/2}}$). **[M1 for the coefficients; M1 for the index sum; A1 for either form]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the power applies to BOTH top and bottom of a quotient. $\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \tfrac{a^n}{b^n}$, not $\tfrac{a^n}{b}$.
- Confusing fractional indices with coefficients. $x^{1/2}$ is $\sqrt{x}$.
- Sign errors in subtracting indices. $x^{1/2} \div x^{-2} = x^{1/2 - (-2)} = x^{5/2}$, NOT $x^{-3/2}$.
- Failing to spot a common base when one is hidden. $64^{2/3}$ comes alive once you write $64 = 4^3$ (or $2^6$).

## Quick summary

- Combined questions are just stacked rules. Do one rule per step.
- Power of a quotient: $\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \tfrac{a^n}{b^n}$.
- Fractional index $a^{m/n}$: take the $n$th root, raise to the $m$th power. Either order works.
- Dividing by $x^{-n}$ is the same as multiplying by $x^n$.
- Coefficients and indices are tracked separately; combine each in its own arithmetic.
