## Why this matters

Fractions and decimals are two notations for the same value. Cambridge expects you to flip between them fluently, both ways. Most papers have at least one question that's faster in one form than the other, so knowing the conversion is direct mark-saving.

## Fraction → decimal

A fraction is just a division: $\tfrac{a}{b}$ means $a \div b$. So to convert a fraction to a decimal, **divide top by bottom**.

$$\tfrac{3}{4} \;=\; 3 \div 4 \;=\; 0.75$$

After writing the fraction, **simplify** to lowest terms (the rule from Part 1).

## Worked example

Write $0.84$ as a fraction in its simplest form.

**Step 1: Read off place value.** Two decimal places means denominator $100$.

$$0.84 = \tfrac{84}{100}$$

**Step 2: Simplify.** HCF of 84 and 100. Prime factorise: $84 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $100 = 2^2 \times 5^2$. Shared: $2^2 = 4$.

$$\tfrac{84}{100} = \tfrac{84 \div 4}{100 \div 4} = \tfrac{21}{25}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{\tfrac{21}{25}}$. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving $\tfrac{84}{100}$ instead of $\tfrac{21}{25}$. Place-value gives a *valid* fraction; you still have to simplify.
- Confusing $\tfrac{1}{4} = 0.25$ with $\tfrac{1}{4} = 0.4$ or "0.14". Memorise the small recall list.
- For $0.6$, writing $\tfrac{6}{1}$ or $\tfrac{1}{6}$. The denominator depends on **decimal places**: 1 dp → 10.
- Treating $\tfrac{1}{3}$ as $0.3$. It's actually $0.333\ldots$ (recurring, covered in Part 4).

## Quick summary

- Fraction → decimal: divide <span style="color:#00abfa;font-weight:600">top</span> by <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">bottom</span>.
- Decimal → fraction: count decimal places to get the <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">denominator</span> (10, 100, 1000…), then simplify.
- A fraction terminates iff its simplified denominator is built from only 2s and 5s.
