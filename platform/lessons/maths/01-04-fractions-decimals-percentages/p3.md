## Why this matters

Imagine a friend says they got "$\tfrac{17}{20}$" in a test, you got "$84\%$", and a third friend got "$0.86$". Who actually scored the highest? You cannot answer until everyone is in the same form. That is exactly the skill this part trains.

Percentages are the third way to write the same value. Cambridge expects you to switch among <span style="color:#00abfa;font-weight:600">fractions</span>, <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">decimals</span>, and <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">percentages</span> on demand. Many comparison questions are easier once everything is in the same form.

## What "percent" means

The word **per cent** literally means "per hundred". So a percentage is just a fraction with denominator 100, written with a `%` sign instead of `/100`.

$$42\% \;=\; \tfrac{42}{100} \;=\; 0.42$$

This single equation is the whole topic. Everything else is just rearranging it.

## Decimal ↔ percentage (the easy one)

<span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">Decimal</span> → <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">percentage</span>: **multiply by 100** (shift the decimal point two places right, then add `%`).

$$0.42 \;\xrightarrow{\times 100}\; 42\%$$

<span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">Percentage</span> → <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">decimal</span>: **divide by 100** (shift the decimal point two places left, drop the `%`).

$$57\% \;\xrightarrow{\div 100}\; 0.57$$

## Fraction ↔ percentage

The cleanest path is **via decimal**: fraction → decimal → percentage (or the reverse).

**Fraction → percentage:** divide top by bottom to get a decimal, then multiply by 100.

$$\tfrac{3}{20} \;\xrightarrow{\div}\; 0.15 \;\xrightarrow{\times 100}\; 15\%$$

**Percentage → fraction:** drop the `%`, write over 100, simplify.

$$35\% \;=\; \tfrac{35}{100} \;\xrightarrow{\text{simplify}}\; \tfrac{7}{20}$$

## Worked example

Arrange in order, smallest to largest: $\;\;\tfrac{3}{8}, \;0.4, \;35\%, \;\tfrac{7}{20}\;$.

**Step 1: Convert everything to percentages** (any one form works; percentages are usually the cleanest for comparing).

- <span style="color:#00abfa">$\tfrac{3}{8}$</span> $= 0.375 = $ <span style="color:#0fee89">$37.5\%$</span>
- <span style="color:#fff067">$0.4$</span> $= $ <span style="color:#0fee89">$40\%$</span>
- <span style="color:#0fee89">$35\%$</span> stays $= 35\%$
- <span style="color:#00abfa">$\tfrac{7}{20}$</span> $= 0.35 = $ <span style="color:#0fee89">$35\%$</span>

**Step 2: Order them.** $35\% \;=\; 35\% \;<\; 37.5\% \;<\; 40\%$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{35\%, \;\tfrac{7}{20}, \;\tfrac{3}{8}, \;0.4}$. **[B1]**

(Note that $35\%$ and $\tfrac{7}{20}$ are equal, so they can come in either order.)

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Reading $7\%$ as $0.7$ instead of $0.07$. Two-place shift always.
- For $\tfrac{3}{8}$ → percentage, treating $\tfrac{3}{8}$ as $\tfrac{3}{8}\%$. The `%` only attaches when you've already divided by 100.
- Comparing fractions, decimals, and percentages directly without converting. $\tfrac{3}{8}$ and $40\%$ are not directly comparable until you put them in the same form.
- Forgetting that $1.0 = 100\%$, not $1\%$. The whole-number 1 means "everything", not "one in a hundred".

## Quick summary

- Percent = "per hundred". $n\% = \tfrac{n}{100}$.
- <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">Decimal</span> ↔ <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">percentage</span>: shift decimal point 2 places (× 100 or ÷ 100).
- <span style="color:#00abfa;font-weight:600">Fraction</span> ↔ <span style="color:#0fee89;font-weight:600">percentage</span>: go via decimal.
- For comparing/ordering, convert everything to one common form first.
