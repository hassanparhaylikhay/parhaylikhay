## Why this matters

The same number can be written as a fraction, a decimal, or a percentage. Cambridge needs you to recognise these three forms, switch between them on demand, and always present a fraction in **simplest form**. This part covers the fraction-to-fraction conversions and simplification; the next parts cover decimals and percentages.

The <span style="color:#00abfa;font-weight:600">numerator</span> sits on top, the <span style="color:#fff067;font-weight:600">denominator</span> sits on the bottom. The denominator says *how many equal parts make a whole*; the numerator says *how many of those parts you have*.

## Mixed ↔ improper

These two forms describe the same number. Cambridge expects you to flip between them on demand. Both directions look procedural, so we'll show each one visually.

In words: $\textcolor{#00abfa}{2}\tfrac{\textcolor{#0fee89}{3}}{\textcolor{#fff067}{4}} = \dfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{2} \times \textcolor{#fff067}{4} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{3}}{\textcolor{#fff067}{4}} = \dfrac{11}{4}$.

The denominator never changes. The numerator is *(whole × bottom) + (old top)*.

The three things to read off the layout:

1. **Quotient** $\textcolor{#0fee89}{2}$ on top of the bar — that's how many *whole* 4s fit into 11.
2. **Remainder** $\textcolor{#fff067}{3}$ at the bottom — what's left after pulling out two 4s. This becomes the new numerator.
3. **Same denominator** $\textcolor{#fff067}{4}$ — never changes.

Reading it off: $\dfrac{11}{4} = \textcolor{#0fee89}{2}\,\dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{3}}{\textcolor{#fff067}{4}}$.

If long division feels rusty, you can always check by going the other way (mixed → improper) and seeing if you land back at $\tfrac{11}{4}$.

## Simplifying fractions

A fraction is in **simplest form** (or "lowest terms") when the numerator and denominator have no common factors above 1. To simplify, divide both by their **HCF**.

<div class="diagram">
<div class="diagram-caption">SIMPLIFYING \(\tfrac{18}{24}\) IN ONE STEP</div>
<div style="font-family:Geist Mono,monospace;font-size:16px;text-align:center;color:#f0eeea;line-height:2">
HCF of <span style="color:#00abfa">18</span> and <span style="color:#fff067">24</span> = <span style="color:#0fee89">6</span><br>
\(\dfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{18}}{\textcolor{#fff067}{24}} = \dfrac{18 \div \textcolor{#0fee89}{6}}{24 \div \textcolor{#0fee89}{6}} = \dfrac{3}{4}\)
</div>
</div>

If you can't spot the HCF, you can simplify in stages by dividing by any common factor and repeating. <span style="color:#00abfa">18</span>/<span style="color:#fff067">24</span> → divide by 2 → 9/12 → divide by 3 → <span style="color:#0fee89">3/4</span>. Same destination, just slower.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't stop too early.** $\tfrac{6}{8}$ "looks simpler" than $\tfrac{18}{24}$, but it isn't simplest. $\tfrac{6}{8}$ still divides by 2. Keep going until no common factor remains. Cambridge mark schemes typically credit only the lowest-terms answer.

</div>

## Worked example

Simplify $\tfrac{42}{56}$ and write the answer as a mixed number if possible.

**Step 1: Find the HCF of 42 and 56.** Prime factorise: $42 = 2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $56 = 2^3 \times 7$. Common factors: $2$ and $7$, so HCF $= 2 \times 7 = 14$.

**Step 2: Divide both by the HCF.**

$$\tfrac{42}{56} = \tfrac{42 \div 14}{56 \div 14} = \tfrac{3}{4}$$

**Step 3: Mixed number?** $\tfrac{3}{4}$ is a *proper* fraction (numerator < denominator), so it stays as is. No mixed conversion possible.

**Answer:** $\boxed{\tfrac{3}{4}}$. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the answer as $\tfrac{6}{8}$ or $\tfrac{9}{12}$ instead of $\tfrac{3}{4}$. Equivalent does not mean simplest; mark schemes want lowest terms.
- Mixed → improper using $whole + \tfrac{n}{d}$ literally, getting $\tfrac{2+3}{4} = \tfrac{5}{4}$ for $2\tfrac{3}{4}$. The whole multiplies the denominator first.
- Improper → mixed forgetting the remainder. $\tfrac{17}{5} = 3$ is wrong; $17 = 5 \times 3 + 2$, so the remainder $2$ becomes the new numerator.
- Calling $\tfrac{4}{4}$ a proper fraction. It equals $1$, which is improper (or whole).

## Quick summary

- **Proper:** numerator < denominator (less than 1).
- **Improper:** numerator ≥ denominator (1 or more).
- **Mixed:** whole + proper fraction. Same value as the matching improper form.
- **Simplest form:** divide by the HCF of numerator and denominator. Always required.
