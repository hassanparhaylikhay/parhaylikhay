## Why this matters

Money questions look easy but eat marks when students drop the second decimal place or miscount cents. Cambridge expects every money answer in the form **\$X.YZ** (two decimal places), and the arithmetic is just decimal arithmetic from 1.6, but with the constant rule that money is to two decimal places.

> **See also:** percentage profit, percentage loss, and percentage commission all use the multiplier method from 1.13. If those terms feel unfamiliar, work through 1.13 first.

## The two-decimal rule

Every money answer must have **two decimal places**. So:

- \$5 is written as **\$5.00**.
- \$0.40 is written as **\$0.40** (NOT \$.40 or \$0.4).
- \$1.50 is written as **\$1.50** (NOT \$1.5).

This rule applies to the **final answer**. During working you can drop trailing zeros for clarity, but always restore the two decimal places before writing the final answer.

## Adding and subtracting money

Treat the money like decimal numbers and align the decimal points (rule from 1.6 p3).

**Example.** \$1.45 + \$3.80.

$$1.45 + 3.80 \;=\; 5.25$$

So the total is \$5.25. Pad with trailing zeros if needed: \$1.45 + \$3.8 should be done as $1.45 + 3.80 = 5.25$.

**Example.** \$20 − \$13.65.

$$20.00 - 13.65 \;=\; 6.35$$

So the change is \$6.35. Note: write \$20 as \$20.00 to align the decimal points.

## Multiplying and dividing money

Multiply by a quantity to get a total. Divide by a quantity to share equally.

**Example.** A pen costs \$1.20. Five pens cost:

$$5 \times 1.20 \;=\; 6.00$$

So \$6.00 total. (Five pens at \$1.20 each, so $5 \times 1.20 = 6.00$ dollars.)

**Example.** \$45 split between $3$ people:

$$45 \div 3 \;=\; 15$$

So \$15.00 each.

When the division doesn't divide evenly, you'll usually round to the nearest cent (2 d.p.). For \$10 split between $3$ people: $10 \div 3 = 3.333\ldots$, rounded to **\$3.33** each (with a small leftover that someone needs to absorb).

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't drop the trailing zero on a "clean" money answer.** \$15 is incomplete; \$15.00 is the correct form. Cambridge marks the form, so always pad with zeros.

## Combining with rates

Money calculations often combine with the rates from 1.12 (especially hourly pay).

**Example.** Earnings at \$8.50/h for $4$ h $30$ min:

- Convert time: $4.5$ h.
- Multiply: $8.50 \times 4.5 = 38.25$.
- Earnings: \$38.25.

The trick is to convert time to **decimal hours** before multiplying, just like in 1.12 and 1.15.

## Profit and loss

Two key formulas:

- **Profit** $=$ selling price $-$ cost price.
- **Loss** $=$ cost price $-$ selling price (when selling for less than you paid).

**Example.** A shop buys an item for \$25 and sells it for \$32. Profit:

$$32 - 25 \;=\; 7$$

So the profit is **\$7.00** per item.

**Per item vs total.** "Profit per orange" is just one item's profit. "Total profit on $50$ oranges" is per-item profit times the quantity.

## Worked example

A bakery sells loaves at \$3.25 each. The materials cost \$1.45 per loaf. If they sell $80$ loaves in a day, what is the total profit?

**Step 1: Profit per loaf.**

$$3.25 - 1.45 \;=\; 1.80$$

So \$1.80 per loaf.

**Step 2: Total profit.**

$$80 \times 1.80 \;=\; 144.00$$

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{144.00}$. **[M1 for the per-item profit; M1 for the multiplication; A1 for the total in dollars]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Writing money with one decimal place. \$3.5 is wrong; it should be \$3.50.
- Misaligning decimal points when adding. \$1.45 + \$3.8 done as $1.45 + 3.8$ (wrong column alignment) gives the wrong answer. Pad to \$3.80 first.
- Forgetting to convert time before multiplying by hourly pay. $8.50 \times 4.5$ for $4$ h $30$ min, NOT $8.50 \times 4.30$.
- Confusing profit with selling price. The selling price is the total received; profit is selling price minus cost.

## Quick summary

- All money answers go to <b style="color:#0fee89">two decimal places</b>: \$5.00, \$0.40, \$1.50.
- Add and subtract by aligning decimal points; pad with trailing zeros.
- Multiply for totals from per-item rates.
- Profit = selling price − cost price.
- Combine with rates by converting time to decimal hours first.
