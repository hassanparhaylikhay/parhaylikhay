## Why this matters

Currency conversion is a Cambridge favourite. The questions are simple if you remember one rule: **multiply when going in the direction of the rate, divide when going against it**. With Pakistan often exchanging USD, GBP, EUR, AED, this is also useful in real life.

## What an exchange rate means

An **exchange rate** like "$1$ USD $= 280$ PKR" is just a rate (from 1.12), where the units are two different currencies. The rate $280$ has units **PKR per USD** (rupees per dollar).

## Direction of the rate

The rate $280$ goes from **USD to PKR** in the **multiply** direction:

- USD $\to$ PKR: **multiply** by $280$.
- PKR $\to$ USD: **divide** by $280$.

The rule of thumb: **multiply when going in the direction of the rate, divide when going against it**. Or check: if you're going to a "smaller-unit" currency (a higher number), the result should be bigger (multiply). If you're going to a "bigger-unit" currency (lower number), the result should be smaller (divide).

| From | To | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| USD | PKR | $\times 280$ |
| PKR | USD | $\div 280$ |
| USD | EUR (rate $0.92$ EUR/USD) | $\times 0.92$ |
| EUR | USD | $\div 0.92$ |

## Quick examples

**Example 1.** Convert \$50 to PKR (rate $1$ USD $= 280$ PKR).

$$50 \times 280 \;=\; 14\,000 \text{ PKR}$$

**Example 2.** Convert $7\,000$ PKR to USD (same rate).

$$7\,000 \div 280 \;=\; 25 \text{ USD}$$

So \$25.

**Example 3.** Convert £$120$ to PKR (rate $1$ GBP $= 350$ PKR).

$$120 \times 350 \;=\; 42\,000 \text{ PKR}$$

## Three-currency chains

Cambridge sometimes asks you to convert through one currency to another (e.g. PKR to USD to EUR). The method is just two conversions in a row.

**Example.** $50\,000$ PKR to EUR, given $1$ USD $= 280$ PKR and $1$ USD $= 0.92$ EUR.

- PKR $\to$ USD: $50\,000 \div 280 \approx 178.57$, i.e. \$178.57.
- USD $\to$ EUR: $178.57 \times 0.92 \approx 164.29$ EUR.

When chaining, **don't round the intermediate**. Use the full precision (Ans key on the calculator) and only round the final answer.

## Commission and fees

Banks and money exchangers usually charge a **commission** (a percentage of the exchanged amount). This combines a percentage with a conversion.

**Example.** Exchange \$200 at $1$ USD $= 280$ PKR. The bank takes $2\%$ commission. How many rupees do you receive?

- Method 1: convert first, then take the commission.
  - $200 \times 280 = 56\,000$ PKR.
  - Bank keeps $2\%$, so you receive $98\%$: $56\,000 \times 0.98 = 54\,880$ PKR.

- Method 2: take the commission first, then convert.
  - You exchange $98\%$ of \$200 = \$196.
  - $196 \times 280 = 54\,880$ PKR.

Same answer. The order doesn't matter as long as the commission is applied to the right amount.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't subtract the commission before exchanging unless the question is clear about what's commissioned.** Read carefully: is the commission on the source amount, the target amount, or both? Most O-level questions apply commission to the source (the amount you give to the bank).

</div>

## Worked example

A traveller exchanges \$300 to British pounds at a rate of $1$ GBP $= 1.25$ USD. The exchange office takes a $3\%$ commission. How many pounds does the traveller receive?

**Step 1: Convert without commission.** Going from USD to GBP means **dividing** by the rate (since the rate is GBP→USD direction).

$$\dfrac{300}{1.25} \;=\; 240 \text{ GBP}$$

**Step 2: Apply commission.** $3\%$ commission means the traveller receives $97\%$.

$$240 \times 0.97 \;=\; 232.80 \text{ GBP}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{£232.80}$. **[M1 for the conversion; M1 for the commission; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying when you should divide. \$50 to PKR is $50 \times 280 = 14\,000$. PKR to USD is $7\,000 \div 280 = 25$. The rate $280$ is "PKR per USD", so to go FROM USD you multiply, TO USD you divide.
- Rounding intermediate values in a chain. Always carry full precision through the calculation.
- Misreading the rate direction. "$1$ USD $= 280$ PKR" means $280$ rupees per dollar, NOT $280$ dollars per rupee.
- Applying commission as $-2\%$ of the wrong amount. Re-read the question to see which amount is commissioned.

## Quick summary

- Exchange rate $1$ A $= k$ B means: A $\to$ B by <b style="color:#0fee89">multiplying by $k$</b>; B $\to$ A by <b style="color:#fff067">dividing by $k$</b>.
- For chains, use full precision (Ans key) until the final step.
- Commission is a percentage applied before or after conversion (the order doesn't matter; both give the same result).
