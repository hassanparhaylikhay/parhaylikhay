## Why this matters

In 5.4 you learned the seven basic solids. Real Cambridge problems almost always combine them: a cylinder with a hemispherical cap, a cone on top of a cylinder (ice-cream cone style), a frustum-shaped trough. The skill is **counting the exposed faces** correctly while adding (or subtracting) the volumes.

This part is about **combining**. Subtracting and frustums come in p4.

In this part:

- Adding volumes for joined solids.
- The "join cancels" rule for surface area.
- When two glued faces don't have to be the same area (yes, that case exists too).
- A library of common composites and what their SA equation looks like.

## Adding volumes

Volumes always **add**. There's no overlap inside, so:

$$V_\text{total} = V_\text{part 1} + V_\text{part 2} + \ldots$$

For a hemisphere ($r$) with a cone ($r$, height $h$) on top:

$$V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 + \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$

For a cylinder ($r$, $h_\text{cyl}$) with a cone ($r$, $h_\text{cone}$) on top:

$$V = \pi r^2 h_\text{cyl} + \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h_\text{cone}$$

## The "join cancels" rule for SA

Surface area only counts the **exposed** faces. When two solids are glued:

1. If the faces at the join match exactly, BOTH cancel. Subtract them both from the SA.
2. If the faces don't match (different sizes), the larger one has a "ring" exposed and the smaller one is fully buried.

For a cylinder + hemisphere with the **same radius**, both faces are circles of $\pi r^2$ at the join, and they cancel exactly:

$$\text{SA} = \underbrace{2\pi r h}_{\text{cylinder curved}} + \underbrace{2\pi r^2}_{\text{hemisphere curved}} + \underbrace{\pi r^2}_{\text{cylinder bottom}}$$

Notice: cylinder curved + hemisphere curved + cylinder bottom. The cylinder TOP and hemisphere FLAT side are both gone (joined together).

## Worked example: cone + cylinder with the same radius

A solid is formed by joining a cone to the top of a cylinder. Both have radius $6.3$ cm. The cone has slant height $8.7$ cm. The ratio of the cone's height to the cylinder's height is $2 : 3$. Find the total surface area.

**Step 1: cone height (Pythagoras).**

$$h_\text{cone} = \sqrt{8.7^2 - 6.3^2} = \sqrt{75.69 - 39.69} = \sqrt{36} = 6\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for $\sqrt{l^2 - r^2}$]**

**Step 2: cylinder height.** Cone : cylinder $= 2 : 3$. Cone height $= 6$, so each "part" $= 3$, and cylinder height $= 9$ cm.

**Step 3: count the exposed faces.**

- Cone curved: $\pi r l$ ✓ exposed
- Cone base: $\pi r^2$ ✗ glued to cylinder top
- Cylinder top: $\pi r^2$ ✗ glued to cone base
- Cylinder curved: $2\pi r h$ ✓ exposed
- Cylinder bottom: $\pi r^2$ ✓ exposed

So:

$$\text{SA} = \pi r l + 2\pi r h + \pi r^2$$

**Step 4: substitute.**

$$= \pi(6.3)(8.7) + 2\pi(6.3)(9) + \pi(6.3)^2$$

$$= 54.81\pi + 113.4\pi + 39.69\pi$$

$$= 207.9\pi \approx \boxed{653}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M2 for the three terms; A1 for value]**

Full marks $= 5$. This is **Cambridge Winter 2024 Paper 21, Q6(c)**.

Three small lessons from this question:

1. The cone and cylinder share the radius, so both glued faces cancel. Three exposed faces.
2. Pythagoras goes both ways: given slant and radius, find the height.
3. A ratio with a known size lets you find both heights.

## Different-sized faces at the join

Sometimes the two solids meeting at the join don't have matching faces. Example: a cuboid sitting on top of a wider cuboid (a step shape). The smaller cuboid's bottom is glued to part of the larger cuboid's top, but the larger cuboid's top has more area than the smaller's bottom.

In that case:

- The smaller cuboid's bottom is fully glued (cancels).
- The larger cuboid's top has the **frame** exposed: top area − smaller bottom area.

For a $4 \times 4$ cube on top of a $10 \times 10 \times 6$ cuboid:

- Cube bottom $= 16$ cm² (cancels).
- Larger top $= 100$ cm². Exposed frame $= 100 - 16 = 84$ cm².
- Plus all the other exposed faces of both shapes.

This pattern reappears in **frustums** (p4), where the large cone has a circular ring exposed at the top because the small cone is narrower.

## Worked example: hemisphere + cone (same radius)

A solid is formed by placing a cone on top of a hemisphere. Both have radius $r = 6$ cm. The cone's slant height is $10$ cm.

(a) Find the cone's perpendicular height.
(b) Volume in terms of π.
(c) Total surface area in terms of π.

**(a) Cone height.**

$$h = \sqrt{l^2 - r^2} = \sqrt{100 - 36} = \sqrt{64} = 8\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1; A1 for 8]**

**(b) Volume.**

$$V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 + \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(216) + \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(36)(8) = 144\pi + 96\pi = \boxed{240\pi}\;\text{cm}^3$$

**[M1 for hemisphere; M1 for cone; A1 for $240\pi$]**

**(c) SA: the join cancels.** Curved hemisphere + curved cone:

$$\text{SA} = 2\pi r^2 + \pi r l = 72\pi + 60\pi = \boxed{132\pi}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for cancellation reasoning; M1 for $2\pi r^2 + \pi r l$; A1 for $132\pi$]**

Full marks $= 8$. The full Cambridge analogue is **Cambridge Summer 2025 Paper 22, Q21** (different dimensions, same structure).

## Library of common composites

| Composite | Volume | Total SA (all faces exposed) |
|---|---|---|
| Hemi + cone (same $r$) | $\tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 + \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ | $2\pi r^2 + \pi r l$ |
| Cyl + cone (same $r$) | $\pi r^2 h_1 + \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h_2$ | $\pi r l + 2\pi r h_1 + \pi r^2$ |
| Cyl + hemi dome (same $r$) | $\pi r^2 h + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3$ | $2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2$ |
| Cuboid + cuboid (different sizes) | $V_1 + V_2$ | sum of all faces, with the matching small bottom face cancelling |

## Common mistakes

- **Adding both faces at the join.** Each face is counted ONCE on the boundary of its own solid; when they cancel, BOTH are removed from the surface area.
- **Forgetting the bottom of the cylinder when there's a cone on top.** The cone's curved face is exposed AND the cylinder's bottom is exposed. Easy to miss the bottom.
- **Slant height vs perpendicular height.** Cone curved SA uses the **slant**, not the perpendicular. Pythagoras is the link.
- **Different radii, glued faces don't cancel.** Stay alert for "the cylinder has radius 5, the hemisphere has radius 4": the faces are different sizes and the calculation has an exposed annular ring.

## Quick summary

The two-step recipe for any 3D compound:

1. **Add the volumes**. No cancellation. Just sum every part.
2. **List every face, then erase the glued ones**. What's left is the SA. Pay extra attention when the radii differ at the join.

The visual habit: before computing anything, draw the composite and **mark each face as "exposed" or "hidden"**. That step alone catches the most expensive mistake on the paper.
