## Why this matters

When part of a circle appears in a compound shape, the calculation needs $\pi$ but the **principle is the same** as p1: decompose, then add or subtract. The new pieces here are **semicircles** (half a circle) and **quadrants** (quarter circle).

You already have:

- Full circle: $A = \pi r^2$, $C = 2\pi r$ (5.3 p1)
- Sector: $A = \tfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2$, arc $= \tfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ (5.3 p2, p3)

What's new in this part:

- The "running track" shape (rectangle with semicircular ends).
- Semicircle and quadrant perimeter (don't forget the straight edge).
- Compound shapes mixing curves and straights.

## Semicircle: half a circle, but with the diameter on the outside

A semicircle has:

- Curved arc length: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2\pi r = \pi r$.
- Straight diameter: $2r$.
- Area: $\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2$.

For its **perimeter**, you need BOTH the arc AND the straight diameter, because the diameter IS part of the boundary:

$$\text{semicircle perimeter} = \pi r + 2r = r(\pi + 2)$$

Forgetting the diameter is the most common slip on a semicircle perimeter question.

## Quadrant: quarter of a circle

A quadrant is one-quarter of a circle, like a slice of pie cut at right angles. It has:

- Curved arc length: $\tfrac{1}{4} \times 2\pi r = \tfrac{\pi r}{2}$.
- TWO straight radii (the two cut edges), each of length $r$.
- Area: $\tfrac{1}{4}\pi r^2$.

Perimeter:

$$\text{quadrant perimeter} = \tfrac{\pi r}{2} + 2r = r\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} + 2\right)$$

## Worked example: rectangle with a semicircle cut out

A $20$ cm by $14$ cm rectangle has a semicircle of radius $7$ cm cut from the centre of one of its long edges. Find the area, in terms of π.

**Step 1: rectangle.** $20 \times 14 = 280$ cm².

**Step 2: semicircle.** Radius $r = 7$, area $= \tfrac{1}{2}\pi(7)^2 = \tfrac{49\pi}{2}$ cm².

**Step 3: subtract.**

$$A = 280 - \tfrac{49\pi}{2}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for the rectangle and semicircle; A1 for $280 - \tfrac{49\pi}{2}$]**

For the perimeter, the cut adds the semicircle's curved arc ($7\pi$ cm) and removes the part of the long edge the cut took out (a chord of $14$ cm, but actually it removes the diameter $14$ from the long edge). Net effect on perimeter: replace $14$ with $7\pi$.

$$P = 2(20) + 14 + (14 - 14 + 7\pi) = 40 + 14 + 7\pi = 54 + 7\pi\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for the area split; A1 for $280 - \tfrac{49\pi}{2}$; M1 for the perimeter trace; A1 for $54 + 7\pi$]**

Full marks $= 4$.

Tracing carefully: bottom $20$, right side $14$, top half-then-arc-then-half ($3$ + arc $7\pi$ + $3$), left side $14$. Total $20 + 14 + 3 + 7\pi + 3 + 14 = 54 + 7\pi$.

## Segments: a chord cuts a circle

A **segment** is the region between a chord and the arc. Two types:

- **Minor segment**: the smaller piece, between the chord and the shorter arc.
- **Major segment**: the bigger piece, between the chord and the longer arc.

To find a segment area:

$$\text{segment} = \text{sector} - \text{triangle}$$

The sector has the same chord as its base. The triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord. Subtract the triangle and you're left with the curved segment area.

This is most useful when the chord and the radii form a right angle (quadrant) or a "nice" angle. For a $90°$ segment with radius $r$:

- Sector area: $\tfrac{1}{4}\pi r^2$
- Triangle area: $\tfrac{1}{2} r \times r = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2$
- Segment area: $\tfrac{1}{4}\pi r^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}r^2 = \tfrac{r^2}{4}(\pi - 2)$

For a general angle $\theta$, you'd need the area-of-triangle formula $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ from 6.3 (which uses two sides and the included angle), so segment problems beyond the right-angle case usually wait until trigonometry.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the diameter on a semicircle perimeter.** A semicircle's outline has TWO parts: arc + straight diameter.
- **Counting the join twice.** When two semicircles "complete" a circle, just use $\pi r^2$ once. Adding two halves separately and then once more is double-counting.
- **Using $\pi d$ when you mean half.** Semicircle arc $= \pi r$, NOT $\pi d$. The full circumference is $\pi d = 2\pi r$.
- **Mixing radius and diameter.** If the question gives diameter $14$, the radius is $7$. Halve before squaring.

## Quick summary

| Part | Area | Curved length | Outline straights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semicircle | $\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2$ | $\pi r$ | diameter $2r$ |
| Quadrant | $\tfrac{1}{4}\pi r^2$ | $\tfrac{\pi r}{2}$ | two radii: $2r$ |
| Sector ($\theta$°) | $\tfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2$ | $\tfrac{\theta}{360}(2\pi r)$ | two radii: $2r$ |
| Segment ($\theta$°) | sector − triangle | sector arc only | chord (no radii) |

The pattern: a **part of a circle has its curved arc plus straight edges where it was cut from the full circle**. The full perimeter is curved + straight. Forgetting the straight is the single most common slip.
