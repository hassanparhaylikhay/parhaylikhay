## Why this matters

In Pakistan-style Cambridge papers, you will see at least one parallel-line question every year. They are usually 2 to 4 marks each: half the marks are for the angle value, half for stating the parallel-line rule by name. So this is a topic where the right wording is worth real marks.

The setup is always the same: two parallel lines (drawn with little arrows showing they are parallel) crossed by a third line called a **transversal**. Eight angles form, and three named relationships connect them.

## Rule 1: corresponding angles are equal (the F-shape)

Pairs of angles that sit in the **same position** at each intersection are corresponding angles.

<iframe src="/widgets/angle-rules-explorer.html?mode=corr&lock=1" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:480px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Each pair forms an **F-shape** (or backwards F) along the transversal. They are equal.

**Cambridge wording:** "corresponding angles are equal" (or "corresponding angles, parallel lines").

## Rule 2: alternate angles are equal (the Z-shape)

Pairs of angles that sit between the two parallel lines, on **opposite sides** of the transversal, are alternate angles.

<iframe src="/widgets/angle-rules-explorer.html?mode=alt&lock=1" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:480px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Each pair forms a **Z-shape** (or backwards Z). They are equal.

**Cambridge wording:** "alternate angles are equal" (or "alternate angles, parallel lines").

## Rule 3: co-interior angles sum to $180°$ (the C-shape)

Pairs of angles that sit between the two parallel lines, on the **same side** of the transversal, are co-interior angles. They are sometimes called **allied** angles.

<iframe src="/widgets/angle-rules-explorer.html?mode=coint&lock=1" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:480px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Each pair forms a **C-shape** (or U-shape). They are NOT equal: they add up to $180°$.

**Cambridge wording:** "co-interior angles sum to $180°$" (or "allied angles sum to $180°$"). Just writing "co-interior" without "$180°$" is risky.

## Vertically opposite at one intersection

Even at the single transversal intersection (without the second parallel line), vertically opposite angles still hold. This often gets used as a stepping-stone in parallel-line questions.

**Step a: Above $CD$, right of $PQ$.** The given angle (below $AB$, right of $PQ$) and the required angle both lie BETWEEN the parallel lines on the SAME side of the transversal, so they are co-interior, summing to $180°$. So the required angle $= 180° - 58° = 122°$ (co-interior angles, $AB \parallel CD$). **[B1 for $122°$; B1 for the reason]**

**Step b: Below $CD$, right of $PQ$.** The given angle and the required angle sit in the SAME position at their respective intersections, so they are corresponding angles, equal. So the required angle $= 58°$ (corresponding angles, $AB \parallel CD$). **[B1 for $58°$; B1 for the reason]**

**Answer:** (a) $\boxed{122°}$  ·  (b) $\boxed{58°}$.

Full marks $= 4$.

## Common mistakes

- Saying "F-angles" or "Z-angles" instead of the formal name. Cambridge sometimes accepts these but you should write "corresponding" or "alternate" to be safe.
- Forgetting to mention "parallel lines" in the reason. The full reason is "corresponding angles, parallel lines" or "alternate angles, $AB \parallel CD$".
- Setting co-interior angles equal. They are NOT equal: they sum to $180°$.
- Using these rules when the lines are NOT marked parallel. No parallel arrows = no parallel-line rule. You may need to first prove the lines are parallel (rare at O-Level, but it does come up).
- Mis-identifying the F, Z, or C shape. Trace the path: F goes along the transversal then turns; Z crosses through the middle; C stays on one side and curls.

## Quick summary

- **Corresponding (F-shape):** equal. Same position at each intersection.
- **Alternate (Z-shape):** equal. Between parallels, opposite sides of transversal.
- **Co-interior (C-shape):** sum to $180°$. Between parallels, same side of transversal.
- All three rules need parallel lines.
- Always state the rule by full name AND mention "parallel lines" in your reason.
