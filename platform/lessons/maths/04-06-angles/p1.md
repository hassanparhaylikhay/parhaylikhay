## Why this matters

Almost every Cambridge geometry question worth 2 or more marks asks you to give a **reason** alongside your answer. The reason is its own mark. If you write the right number but no reason, you lose half the marks. If your reason is fuzzy (like "because the line is straight"), the marker cannot give you the B1.

In this part we lock down five rules you will use in almost every angle question, and the exact wording Cambridge wants for each.

## Rule 1: angles at a point sum to $360°$

If several angles meet at a single point and fill the whole space around it, they add up to $360°$.

## Rule 5: angles in a quadrilateral sum to $360°$

A quadrilateral is a four-sided shape. Its four interior angles always add to $360°$. You can see why by drawing one diagonal, splitting the quadrilateral into two triangles. Each triangle is $180°$, and $180 + 180 = 360°$.

## Three-letter angle notation

Cambridge requires you to name angles using **three letters**, with the **vertex letter in the middle**.

- Angle $ABC$ means: start at $A$, go to vertex $B$, then on to $C$. The angle is at $B$.
- Angle $XYZ$ means the angle at $Y$.
- Writing just "angle $B$" can be accepted if only one angle meets at $B$, but it is risky. Use three letters to be unambiguous.

If the question gives you a diagram with points labelled, always answer with three-letter notation. It is what the mark scheme expects.

## Worked example

In the figure, $A$, $E$, $B$ are points on a straight line (so $AEB$ is a straight line). Two other lines meet at $E$ on the upper side of $AB$, going to points $C$ and $D$ with $C$ between $A$ and $D$ on the upper side. Angle $AEC = 65°$ and angle $DEB = 50°$. Find angle $CED$, and give a reason.

**Step 1: spot the rule.** $A$, $E$, $B$ are collinear, so the angles on the upper side of line $AB$ at $E$ sum to $180°$.

**Step 2: list those angles.** On the upper side at $E$ we have angle $AEC$, angle $CED$, and angle $DEB$.

**Step 3: write the equation.**

$$\text{angle } AEC + \text{angle } CED + \text{angle } DEB = 180°$$

**Step 4: substitute.**

$$65 + \text{angle } CED + 50 = 180$$

**Step 5: solve.**

$$\text{angle } CED = 180 - 65 - 50 = 65°$$

**Answer:** angle $CED = 65°$, angles on a straight line sum to $180°$. **[M1 set up straight-line equation; A1 final value; B1 reason]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "angle $B$" when several angles meet at $B$. The marker cannot tell which one you mean. Use angle $ABC$.
- Giving the right number but no reason. Reason marks are usually B1 and they are easy marks to lose.
- Saying "because of the triangle rule" instead of "angles in a triangle sum to $180°$". The mark scheme wants the rule itself stated.
- Confusing "vertically opposite" with "alternate" or "corresponding". Vertically opposite is for two crossing lines (no parallel involved). Alternate and corresponding need parallel lines, which we cover in p2.
- Using $360°$ for a triangle or $180°$ for a quadrilateral. Triangles are $180°$, quadrilaterals are $360°$.

## Quick summary

- Angles at a point: $360°$.
- Angles on a straight line: $180°$.
- Vertically opposite angles: equal.
- Angles in a triangle: $180°$.
- Angles in a quadrilateral: $360°$.
- Always name angles with three letters, vertex in the middle.
- Always quote the rule by name when giving a reason.
