## Why this matters

Polygon questions appear in almost every Cambridge paper. They are usually 2 to 3 marks and the marks split between **method** (using the correct formula) and **answer** (the right value). They are pure formula-application: if you know the three formulas in this part, you can score full marks every time.

## The three formulas

You need exactly three relationships. Memorise all three.

### 1. Interior angle sum of any $n$-sided polygon

$$\text{Sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180°$$

This works for any polygon, regular or not.

**Where it comes from:** any $n$-sided polygon can be split into $(n - 2)$ triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex. Each triangle is $180°$.

### 2. Each interior angle of a REGULAR $n$-gon

A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. So:

$$\text{Each interior angle} = \dfrac{(n - 2) \times 180°}{n}$$

This formula only works if the polygon is regular. For an irregular polygon you can only use the sum (formula 1).

### 3. Exterior angle of a regular $n$-gon

If you "walk around" the outside of any convex polygon turning at each vertex, the total turn is one full rotation: $360°$. So:

$$\text{Sum of all exterior angles} = 360° \quad \text{(any convex polygon)}$$

For a **regular** $n$-gon, all exterior angles are equal, so:

$$\text{Each exterior angle} = \dfrac{360°}{n}$$

### And the bridge: interior + exterior $= 180°$

At any vertex of any polygon, the interior angle and exterior angle together form a straight line, so:

$$\text{interior} + \text{exterior} = 180°$$

This means you can always switch between "find interior" and "find exterior" by subtracting from $180°$.

If you walk along the boundary of any convex polygon and turn through the exterior angle at every vertex, by the time you return to your start you have rotated through one full turn: $360°$. This works for regular and irregular polygons alike, which is why it's such a powerful check.

## Worked example 1: interior angle of a regular octagon

Find the size of one interior angle of a regular octagon.

**Step 1: spot the formula.** Regular octagon means $n = 8$ and all interior angles are equal.

**Step 2: apply the interior-sum formula.**

$$\text{Sum} = (n - 2) \times 180 = (8 - 2) \times 180 = 6 \times 180 = 1080°$$

**Step 3: divide by $n$ to get one angle.**

$$\text{Each interior angle} = \dfrac{1080}{8} = 135°$$

**Answer:** $135°$. **[M1 use $(n-2) \times 180$; M1 divide by $n$; A1 final value]**

Full marks $= 3$.

**Faster route (using exterior angles):** exterior $= \dfrac{360}{8} = 45°$, then interior $= 180 - 45 = 135°$. Both routes get full marks.

## Worked example 2: number of sides from an interior angle

A regular polygon has each interior angle equal to $156°$. Find the number of sides.

**Step 1: switch to exterior angles** (the easiest route).

$$\text{Each exterior angle} = 180 - 156 = 24°$$

**Step 2: apply the exterior formula.** For a regular $n$-gon, exterior $= \dfrac{360}{n}$, so:

$$24 = \dfrac{360}{n}$$

**Step 3: solve.**

$$n = \dfrac{360}{24} = 15$$

**Answer:** $n = 15$ sides. **[M1 find exterior angle; M1 use $\dfrac{360}{n}$; A1 final value]**

Full marks $= 3$.

This is a very common Cambridge style. The mark scheme allows EITHER route (interior sum or exterior shortcut). The exterior route is usually faster and less error-prone.

## Worked example 3: irregular pentagon

Four angles of a pentagon are $100°$, $115°$, $130°$, and $90°$. Find the fifth angle.

**Step 1: interior sum of any pentagon.**

$$(n - 2) \times 180 = (5 - 2) \times 180 = 540°$$

**Step 2: subtract the four known angles.**

$$\text{fifth angle} = 540 - 100 - 115 - 130 - 90 = 105°$$

**Answer:** $105°$. **[M1 use $(n-2) \times 180 = 540$; A1 final value]**

Full marks $= 2$.

The pentagon is NOT regular here (the angles are different sizes), so we cannot use $\dfrac{(n-2) \times 180}{n}$. We can only use the sum.

## Common mistakes

- Using $(n - 2) \times 180$ for ONE angle instead of the SUM. That formula gives the total: you still need to divide by $n$ for a regular polygon.
- Using $\dfrac{(n-2) \times 180}{n}$ on an IRREGULAR polygon. This formula assumes all angles are equal. For irregular, use the sum and subtract knowns.
- Confusing interior and exterior. Interior is inside the shape; exterior is the angle between an extended side and the next side.
- Getting $n$ wrong. Hexagon = 6, octagon = 8, decagon = 10. Pentagon = 5, heptagon = 7, nonagon = 9.
- Not using the exterior-sum check. Whenever you find an exterior angle, multiply by $n$. The total should be $360°$. If not, your answer is wrong.
- Forgetting interior + exterior $= 180°$. This is the fastest way to switch between them.

## Quick summary

- Interior sum $= (n - 2) \times 180°$ for any $n$-gon.
- Each interior of a REGULAR $n$-gon $= \dfrac{(n - 2) \times 180°}{n}$.
- Exterior sum $= 360°$ for any convex polygon (regular or not).
- Each exterior of a REGULAR $n$-gon $= \dfrac{360°}{n}$.
- Interior $+$ exterior $= 180°$ at every vertex.
- The exterior route is usually the fastest. Use it as a check too.
