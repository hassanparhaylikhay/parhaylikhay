## Why this matters

The gradient formula gives you a number. But what does that number TELL you about the line? Sign and size both carry meaning. This part covers how to read a gradient at a glance.

- **$m > 0$** (positive): line slopes UP as you move right.
- **$m < 0$** (negative): line slopes DOWN as you move right.
- **$m = 0$**: horizontal line. No tilt at all.
- **$m$ undefined**: vertical line. The formula divides by zero (run = 0).

Cambridge wants the answer "undefined" for vertical lines, not "infinity". $\dfrac{1}{0}$ has no value.

## Size of the gradient (steepness)

The **size** of $m$ — its absolute value $|m|$ — tells you how steep the line is:

- $|m|$ close to $0$: nearly horizontal.
- $|m| = 1$: rises (or falls) by $1$ unit for every $1$ unit across — a $45°$ slope.
- $|m|$ large: very steep.

$y = 5x + 1$ (gradient $5$) is steeper than $y = 2x - 3$ (gradient $2$). $y = -4x$ (gradient $-4$) is steeper than $y = 2x$ (gradient $2$), even though one is uphill and the other downhill — magnitude wins.

## Reading "rise per run" off the gradient

If the gradient is $\dfrac{3}{4}$, that means: every time $x$ goes up by $4$, $y$ goes up by $3$. (Or: every time $x$ goes up by $1$, $y$ goes up by $\dfrac{3}{4}$.)

Negative gradients work the same way with a sign flip. Gradient $-2$: every time $x$ goes up by $1$, $y$ goes DOWN by $2$.

This "rise per run" reading is the fastest way to draw a line from a point and a gradient: place a dot, then count $1$ across and $m$ up (or down), then connect with a ruler.

## Worked example: identifying line type from two points

For each pair, find the gradient and describe the line.

(a) $(2, 3)$ and $(7, 3)$.

(b) $(4, -1)$ and $(4, 5)$.

(c) $(0, 0)$ and $(3, 6)$.

**Step 1 (a).** $m = \dfrac{3 - 3}{7 - 2} = \dfrac{0}{5} = 0$. Horizontal line.

**Step 2 (b).** $m = \dfrac{5 - (-1)}{4 - 4} = \dfrac{6}{0}$ — undefined. Vertical line.

**Step 3 (c).** $m = \dfrac{6 - 0}{3 - 0} = 2$. Positive gradient — line goes uphill.

**Answer (a):** Gradient $0$ (horizontal). **[B1]**

**Answer (b):** Undefined gradient (vertical). **[B1]**

**Answer (c):** Gradient $2$ (uphill). **[B1]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying "infinity" for vertical lines.** Use "undefined".
- **Reporting just $|m|$ for negative slopes.** Sign matters. $m = 3$ and $m = -3$ are different lines (mirror image).
- **Confusing zero gradient with undefined.** Horizontal = $0$. Vertical = undefined. They're opposite, not the same.
- **Mixing up which is steeper.** $|m| = 3$ is steeper than $|m| = \dfrac{1}{2}$, regardless of sign.

## Quick summary

- Sign tells direction: $+$ uphill, $-$ downhill, $0$ flat.
- Magnitude tells steepness: bigger $|m|$ = steeper.
- Vertical line: gradient **undefined** (not infinity).
- Horizontal line: gradient $0$.
