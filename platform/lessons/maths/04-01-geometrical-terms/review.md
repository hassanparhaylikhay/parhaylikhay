## What you should know by now

If you've worked through P1 you can:

- Name the parts of a circle (centre, radius, diameter, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment).
- Classify a triangle by its sides (equilateral / isosceles / scalene) and by its angles (right / acute / obtuse).
- Pick the most specific name for a quadrilateral (square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, kite, trapezium).
- Tell parallel from perpendicular and write bearings as three-figure angles.
- Recognise the named solids (cube, cuboid, prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone, sphere, hemisphere, frustum) and their parts (face, edge, vertex).
- Distinguish similar from congruent.

## Marking patterns at a glance

Cambridge "name the shape / state the reason" questions are nearly always **B1 each**. They are independent marks : you do not lose them by getting an earlier part wrong. So:

- If you have **any** sensible guess at a one-word answer, write it.
- Use the precise vocabulary. "Right angle" and "$90°$" both work; "square corner" does not.
- For circle facts, write the full reason in the wording the mark scheme expects, e.g. "tangent perpendicular to radius at point of contact", not just "$90°$".
- For bearings, write three digits with the degree symbol: $062°$, not $62$ or $62°$.

A mark that is awarded for a correct **reason** can still be lost if your reason is fuzzy. "Same side" is not a reason. "Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal" is.

## The hierarchy of quadrilaterals

Square $\to$ rectangle $\to$ parallelogram. Square $\to$ rhombus $\to$ parallelogram. So a square is also a rhombus, also a rectangle, also a parallelogram. When asked for "the name", give the **most specific** one that fits.

A trapezium sits outside this chain : it has only **one** pair of parallel sides, while parallelograms have two.

## Quick reference: angle types

- Acute: $0° < \theta < 90°$
- Right: $\theta = 90°$
- Obtuse: $90° < \theta < 180°$
- Straight: $\theta = 180°$
- Reflex: $180° < \theta < 360°$

## Quick reference: circle parts

- Centre, radius, diameter, circumference.
- Chord (any line joining two points on the circle).
- Tangent (touches at exactly one point; perpendicular to the radius there).
- Arc (a piece of circumference); minor or major.
- Sector ("pizza slice", two radii + arc); minor or major.
- Segment (chord + arc); minor or major.

## Worked example

The diagram shows a regular octagon $ABCDEFGH$ inscribed in a circle, with centre $O$. The point $T$ is on the circle, between $A$ and $B$, and a line $TX$ touches the circle at $T$.

(a) Write down the size of one interior angle of the octagon.

(b) Name the line segment $OA$.

(c) Name the line $TX$.

(d) State the size of $\angle OTX$, giving a reason.

**Step 1 (a).** Exterior angle of a regular octagon $= \dfrac{360°}{8} = 45°$.

**Step 2 (a).** Interior angle $= 180° - 45° = 135°$.

**Step 3 (b).** $O$ is the centre and $A$ is on the circle, so $OA$ is a radius.

**Step 4 (c).** $TX$ touches the circle at exactly one point, so it is a tangent.

**Step 5 (d).** A tangent meets the radius at the point of contact at $90°$.

**Step 6 (d).** $OT$ is a radius; $TX$ is a tangent at $T$. So $\angle OTX = 90°$.

**Answer (a):** $135°$. **[B1]**

**Answer (b):** $OA$ is a **radius**. **[B1]**

**Answer (c):** $TX$ is a **tangent**. **[B1]**

**Answer (d):** $\angle OTX = 90°$ because the tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. **[B1 for $90°$; B1 for reason]**

Full marks $= 5$.

## Common mistakes recap

- "Sector" vs "segment" : sector is bounded by two radii and an arc; segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.
- "Diameter" vs "chord" : every diameter is a chord, but only one passing through the centre is a diameter.
- "Reflex" : means $> 180°$, NOT just any angle outside the shape.
- Writing bearing as $62°$ instead of $062°$ : always three digits.
- Naming a shape too generally ("quadrilateral" when the answer wanted is "rhombus").
