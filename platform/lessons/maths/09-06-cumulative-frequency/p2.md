## Why this matters

This is the killer skill of cumulative frequency: read the MEDIAN, QUARTILES, and IQR off the curve. Worth $3$ to $5$ marks on most papers.

## The reading positions

Three special cumulative frequencies, given total $n$:

- **Lower quartile** $Q_1$: read off at CF $= \dfrac{n}{4}$.
- **Median**: read off at CF $= \dfrac{n}{2}$.
- **Upper quartile** $Q_3$: read off at CF $= \dfrac{3n}{4}$.

For $n = 60$: read at CF $= 15$ ($Q_1$), $30$ (median), $45$ ($Q_3$).

For $n = 100$: read at $25, 50, 75$.

For $n = 200$: $50, 100, 150$.

## The reading routine

For each of the three CF positions:

1. **Find the CF** on the y-axis (the vertical scale).
2. **Move horizontally** across to the curve.
3. **Drop vertically** down to the x-axis.
4. **Read the value** on the x-axis. That is your $Q_1$, median, or $Q_3$.

The marker on the widget above does exactly this. The dashed lines are your pencil work.

## Interquartile range (IQR)

The IQR measures spread, like the range, but is more robust:

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$

It captures the spread of the MIDDLE $50\%$ of the data. Outliers above $Q_3$ or below $Q_1$ are excluded.

Use IQR (instead of range) when:

- The data has outliers.
- You want a robust spread measure.
- The paper specifically asks for IQR.

## Worked example: median and IQR from a CF curve

The CF curve shows test scores for $60$ students. Find the median and the interquartile range.

**Step 1:** $n = 60$, so the reading positions are CF = $15$ ($Q_1$), $30$ (median), $45$ ($Q_3$).

**Step 2: Read each from the curve.** (From the data in the widget above.)

- At CF = $15$: score $\approx 26$. So $Q_1 \approx 26$.
- At CF = $30$: score $\approx 38$. So median $\approx 38$.
- At CF = $45$: score $\approx 49$. So $Q_3 \approx 49$.

**Step 3: IQR.**

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 49 - 26 = 23$$

**Answer:** median $\approx \boxed{38}$, IQR $\approx \boxed{23}$. **[B1 for the median; B1 for $Q_1$; B1 for $Q_3$; B1 for IQR]**

Full marks $= 4$.

## "How many less than $X$" questions

The exam may also ask, "Estimate the number of students who scored less than $50$." Use the curve in REVERSE:

1. Find $50$ on the x-axis.
2. Move vertically up to the curve.
3. Move horizontally across to the y-axis.
4. Read the cumulative frequency.

In our example, $50$ on the x-axis maps to CF $\approx 46$. So about $46$ students scored less than $50$.

If asked "how many scored MORE than $50$": $n - 46 = 60 - 46 = 14$.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong CF position**. $Q_1$ is at $n/4$, not at $25$ (unless $n = 100$).
- **Reading the median value as $n/2$** instead of the score at CF $= n/2$. The median is on the x-axis.
- **Forgetting to subtract** when computing IQR.
- **Trying to use a CF curve where one doesn''t exist**. The curve only works for grouped or sufficiently large datasets.
- **Not labelling the dashed lines** in your answer. Mark schemes often want to see the construction.

## Quick summary

- **Median**, $Q_1$, $Q_3$ read off the CF curve at $\dfrac{n}{2}$, $\dfrac{n}{4}$, $\dfrac{3n}{4}$.
- **IQR** $= Q_3 - Q_1$. Robust spread measure.
- **Procedure**: across from y-axis, down to x-axis (or reverse for count questions).
- Always **label the dashed lines** on your answer diagram.
