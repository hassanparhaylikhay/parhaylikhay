## Why this matters

A cumulative frequency (CF) curve turns a frequency table into a graph that answers "how many values are at most $x$?" for any $x$ you choose. From the curve you read off:

- The MEDIAN (at CF = $n/2$).
- QUARTILES (at $n/4$ and $3n/4$).
- The INTERQUARTILE RANGE ($Q_3 - Q_1$).
- "How many students scored less than $50$?" type counts.

CF questions appear on most papers, worth $3$ to $6$ marks. Drawing the curve is half the work; reading values off is the other half.

## How CF works

Take a frequency table with classes. For each class, compute the running total of frequencies UP TO AND INCLUDING that class. That running total is the cumulative frequency.

The CF column is the SCAFFOLDING for the graph.

## Plot and connect

Plot each (upper boundary, cumulative frequency) point on a grid:

- $(10, 2), (20, 8), (30, 19), (40, 33), (50, 46), (60, 55), (70, 59), (80, 60)$
- Also include $(0, 0)$ at the very start (lower bound of the first class with CF $= 0$).

Then draw a smooth curve through these points. The curve will only EVER GO UP, never down, because CF is a running total.

## Rules for the curve

- **Plot at the upper class boundary**, not the midpoint.
- **Start at the lower boundary of the FIRST class with CF $= 0$.**
- **End at the upper boundary of the LAST class with CF $= n$.**
- **Smooth curve** (not straight-line segments, though those are sometimes acceptable for cumulative frequency POLYGONS as opposed to CURVES).
- **Use grid paper** and a ruler for axes.

## Worked example: build a CF curve

Given the frequency table above ($60$ students''  test scores), describe what the cumulative frequency curve looks like.

**Step 1: CF column.** Already computed: $2, 8, 19, 33, 46, 55, 59, 60$ at upper bounds $10, 20, \ldots, 80$.

**Step 2: Plot points.** $(0, 0)$ then $(10, 2), (20, 8), \ldots, (80, 60)$.

**Step 3: Smooth curve.** Connect with an S-shaped curve. The curve climbs slowly at first, steeply in the middle (where most data is), then flattens at the top.

**Step 4: Check.** Curve passes through every plotted point. Starts at $(0, 0)$, ends at $(80, 60)$. Only goes up.

## Common mistakes

- **Plotting at midpoints** instead of upper class boundaries.
- **Connecting points with straight lines** when the question asks for a smooth curve (or vice versa).
- **The curve coming back down** at some point. CF only ever rises or stays flat.
- **Forgetting the $(0, 0)$ or starting point.**
- **Not labelling the axes** ("Cumulative frequency" on y, "Score" on x).

## Quick summary

- **CF at $x$** = count of data values at or below $x$.
- **Plot points** at (upper class boundary, cumulative frequency).
- **Start** at (lower bound, $0$); **end** at (upper bound, $n$).
- **Smooth curve** that only goes up.
- The curve is the foundation for reading median and quartiles (next page).
