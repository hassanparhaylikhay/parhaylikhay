## Why this matters

"Shade the region that represents X" is one of the most common Sets questions on Cambridge papers. It's worth one or two marks each time, and it's purely a matter of pattern recognition once you see the regions clearly. This part is about reading and shading; no computation.

The four regions are:

- **$A$ only** (left lens, just blue): $A \cap B'$ &nbsp;*in $A$, not in $B$*
- **$B$ only** (right lens, just yellow): $A' \cap B$ &nbsp;*in $B$, not in $A$*
- **Overlap** (middle lens): $A \cap B$ &nbsp;*in both*
- **Outside both** (corner of $\xi$ box): $(A \cup B)' = A' \cap B'$ &nbsp;*in neither*

Every element of $\xi$ falls into exactly one of these four regions.

## Shading common expressions

To shade a region: identify which of the four regions are inside the description, then colour them in.

| Expression | Which regions to shade |
|---|---|
| $A$ | $A$-only, plus the overlap |
| $B$ | $B$-only, plus the overlap |
| $A'$ | $B$-only, plus the outside corner (everything not in $A$) |
| $A \cup B$ | $A$-only, $B$-only, and the overlap |
| $A \cap B$ | only the overlap |
| $(A \cup B)'$ | only the outside corner |
| $A \cap B'$ | only $A$-only |
| $A' \cap B'$ | only the outside corner (same as $(A \cup B)'$) |

The last row is worth memorising: $(A \cup B)'$ and $A' \cap B'$ shade the same region.

The eight regions:

1. $A$ only: $A \cap B' \cap C'$
2. $B$ only: $A' \cap B \cap C'$
3. $C$ only: $A' \cap B' \cap C$
4. $A$ and $B$, not $C$: $A \cap B \cap C'$
5. $A$ and $C$, not $B$: $A \cap B' \cap C$
6. $B$ and $C$, not $A$: $A' \cap B \cap C$
7. All three: $A \cap B \cap C$ &nbsp;*the centre*
8. None of them: $A' \cap B' \cap C' = (A \cup B \cup C)'$ &nbsp;*the outside corner*

You don't need to memorise the formal expressions for each. You need to **read a region and identify which sets it belongs to**.

## How to shade a given expression

A reliable method: go through every region of the Venn and ask "is this region in the expression?" If yes, shade it.

**Example.** Shade $A \cap B'$ on a 2-set Venn.

- $A$ only? In $A$ ✓, not in $B$ ✓. **Yes, shade.**
- Overlap? In $A$ ✓, in $B$ ✗ (we need *not* in $B$). **No.**
- $B$ only? Not in $A$. **No.**
- Outside both? Not in $A$. **No.**

Result: shade only the $A$-only crescent.

## Worked example

On a 2-set Venn diagram with sets $A$ and $B$ inside universal set $\xi$, shade the region representing $A' \cap B$.

**Step 1: Decode the expression.** $A' \cap B$ means "**not** in $A$, **and** in $B$".

**Step 2: Test each region.**

- $A$ only? In $A$. We need *not* in $A$. **No.**
- Overlap ($A \cap B$)? In $A$. **No.**
- $B$ only? Not in $A$ ✓, in $B$ ✓. **Yes, shade this.**
- Outside both? Not in $B$. **No.**

**Step 3: Shade the $B$-only crescent** (the part of $B$ that doesn't overlap $A$).

**Answer:** the right-hand crescent of $B$ alone is shaded. **[B1]** for correct region; **[B1]** for clear shading. *(2 marks total.)*

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Shading the whole circle instead of just the right region. $A \cap B'$ is *part* of $A$, not all of $A$.
- Forgetting the outside-both corner. $(A \cup B)'$ shades **only** that corner, nothing inside the circles.
- Mixing $\cup$ and $\cap$ when reading the expression. Union shades more regions; intersection shades fewer.
- On a 3-set Venn, missing the centre region. $A \cap B \cap C$ is the small triangle right in the middle.

## Quick summary

- A 2-set Venn has 4 regions; a 3-set has 8.
- To shade an expression, check each region: "is this region inside the description?"
- $(A \cup B)' = A' \cap B'$: both shade the same outside corner.
- The centre of a 3-set Venn is $A \cap B \cap C$.
