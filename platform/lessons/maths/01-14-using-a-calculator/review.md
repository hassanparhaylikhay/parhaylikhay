You've now seen the calculator-specific habits Cambridge expects. The two big ideas: **don't round mid-calculation**, and **convert between calculator format and human format cleanly** (for money, time, and angles).

## The skills you should have

- **Type the whole expression** in one go, with brackets where the question shows a fraction.
- **Round only the final answer** to the asked-for d.p. or s.f.
- **Read displays carefully**: $4.8$ in money is \$4.80; $3.25$ in time is $3$ h $15$ min.
- **Enter human formats** correctly: $2$ h $30$ min types as $2.5$, not $2.30$.
- **Always include units** in the final answer, even if the calculator just shows a number.

## The single biggest mistake

**Rounding inputs at the start of a calculation.** A length might be $\sqrt{37}$ exactly, but using $6.08$ instead chains errors through any further computation. Always work with the full-precision value (use Ans or memory) and round only the final answer.
