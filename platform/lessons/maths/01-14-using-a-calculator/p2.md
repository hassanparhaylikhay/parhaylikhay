## Why this matters

Cambridge often gives quantities in formats the calculator can't directly use, like "$2$ h $30$ min" or "$7$° $30$''", and expects answers in the same format. The skill is converting cleanly between the human format (hours and minutes, degrees and minutes, dollars and cents) and the decimal format the calculator likes. Get this clean and you'll stop dropping marks at the boundary between calculator and answer paper.

## Money: read 2 decimal places, even if the calculator drops them

A calculator showing $4.8$ for a money calculation means **\$4.80**. Always write money to **two decimal places** because that's how cents work.

| Calculator display | Money interpretation |
|---|---|
| $4.8$ | \$4.80 |
| $12$ | \$12.00 |
| $3.5$ | \$3.50 |
| $0.7$ | \$0.70 (or $70$ cents) |
| $25.07$ | \$25.07 |

The opposite direction: when entering money, **type with the decimal point in the right place**. \$3.25 enters as `3.25`, not `325`. \$0.50 enters as `0.5` or `0.50`.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: never write a money answer with one decimal place.** "\$4.8" is not the right form; "\$4.80" is. Cambridge wants two decimal places for money, with a leading zero if needed (like \$0.50, never \$.50).

## Time: convert minutes to a decimal hour

Time values like "$2$ hours $30$ minutes" are NOT $2.30$ hours. Minutes don't go up to $100$; they go up to $60$. Convert by dividing the minutes by $60$.

$$\text{decimal hour} \;=\; \text{whole hours} + \dfrac{\text{minutes}}{60}$$

| Time | Decimal hours |
|---|---|
| $2$ h $30$ min | $2.5$ h |
| $1$ h $15$ min | $1.25$ h |
| $3$ h $45$ min | $3.75$ h |
| $0$ h $20$ min | $\tfrac{1}{3}$ h $\approx 0.333$ h |

**Going the other way** (calculator $\to$ time):

$$\text{minutes} \;=\; \text{decimal part} \times 60$$

So $3.25$ h $= 3$ h $+ 0.25 \times 60$ min $= 3$ h $15$ min.

## Angles: degrees, minutes, seconds

Angles in Cambridge are usually in **degrees** with a decimal: $7.5$°. But sometimes they appear as **degrees, minutes, and seconds**: $7$° $30$' $0$''.

The relationships:

- $1$ degree $= 60$ minutes (written as $60$').
- $1$ minute $= 60$ seconds (written as $60$'').

So $7.5$° $= 7$° $+ 0.5 \times 60$' $= 7$° $30$'.

For the most part, Cambridge sticks with **decimal degrees** (e.g. $42.7$°). The DMS format is rare but you should recognise it.

## Recurring decimals on the display

Some calculators show recurring decimals using a bar (like $0.4\overline{3}$ or $0.\dot{4}\dot{3}$). Others show many digits and expect you to spot the pattern: $0.43333333$.

To convert to a fraction, use the method from 1.4 p4 (multiply by $10^k$, subtract). For $0.4\overline{3}$:

$$x = 0.4333\ldots, \quad 10x = 4.333\ldots, \quad 10x - x = 4.333\ldots - 0.4333\ldots = 3.9$$

$$9x = 3.9 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = \dfrac{3.9}{9} = \dfrac{39}{90} = \dfrac{13}{30}$$

Most calculators have a fraction key (often labelled $a\tfrac{b}{c}$ or $\tfrac{\square}{\square}$) that does this for you.

## Worked example

A car travels at an average speed of $48$ km/h for $1$ hour $45$ minutes. How far does it travel?

**Step 1: Convert the time.** $1$ h $45$ min $= 1 + \tfrac{45}{60} = 1.75$ h.

**Step 2: Apply $D = S \times T$.**

$$D \;=\; 48 \times 1.75 \;=\; 84 \text{ km}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{84}$ km. **[M1 for converting the time to $1.75$ h; A1 for the distance]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example: reading time back

A worker works $8.4$ hours in a day. Express this in hours and minutes.

**Step 1: Identify whole hours and decimal part.** $8.4 = 8 + 0.4$.

**Step 2: Convert decimal to minutes.** $0.4 \times 60 = 24$.

**Answer:** $\boxed{8 \text{ h } 24 \text{ min}}$. **[B1 for the conversion]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Reading $4.8$ as \$4.80 in working but writing \$4.8 on the answer line. Money always has 2 d.p.
- Treating $2$ h $30$ min as $2.30$ hours. It's $2.5$ hours.
- Converting decimal hours by multiplying by $100$ instead of $60$. $0.25$ of an hour is $15$ min, NOT $25$ min.
- Forgetting to specify units when reading off the display. Always include km, m/s, kg, $°$, etc. as appropriate.

## Quick summary

- Money: always 2 d.p. \$4.80, not \$4.8.
- Time: $\text{minutes} = \text{decimal} \times 60$. So $2.5$ h $= 2$ h $30$ min and $3.25$ h $= 3$ h $15$ min.
- Angles: decimal degrees usually fine; DMS with $1$° $= 60$', $1$' $= 60$''.
- Recurring decimals on the display can be converted to fractions by the multiply-and-subtract method.
