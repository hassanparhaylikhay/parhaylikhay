You've now seen the full toolbox of percentage skills: finding $P\%$ of a quantity, expressing one quantity as a percentage of another, increasing and decreasing, simple and compound interest, depreciation, and reverse percentages. Cambridge mixes these freely, often inside money or population contexts.

## The skills you should have

- **Find $P\%$ of $Q$**: multiply by $\tfrac{P}{100}$.
- **Express A as a percentage of B**: $\tfrac{A}{B} \times 100\%$.
- **Increase by $P\%$**: multiply by $1 + \tfrac{P}{100}$.
- **Decrease by $P\%$**: multiply by $1 - \tfrac{P}{100}$.
- **Percentage change**: $\tfrac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%$.
- **Simple interest**: $I = \tfrac{PRT}{100}$.
- **Compound interest / growth / depreciation**: $A = P(1 + \tfrac{R}{100})^T$ (or with $-$ for shrink).
- **Reverse percentage**: divide by the multiplier.

## The single biggest mistake

**Treating reverse percentage as just "subtract the percentage of the after value".** "After $20\%$ tax, price is \$540" does NOT make the pre-tax \$540 - 20% × \$540 = \$432. The correct method is to DIVIDE \$540 by $1.20$, giving \$450. Always reverse with the multiplier.
