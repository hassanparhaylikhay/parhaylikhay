## Why this matters

Reverse percentages catch students out more than any other percentage topic. The question gives you a value AFTER an increase or decrease and asks for the BEFORE. The trap: students multiply when they should divide. Once you see this clearly using the multiplier method, every reverse-percentage question becomes mechanical.

## The reverse direction

If multiplying by some factor takes you from "before" to "after", then **dividing by the same factor takes you from "after" back to "before"**.

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{before}} \times \text{multiplier} \;=\; \textcolor{#ff822c}{\text{after}}$$

Rearranging:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{before}} \;=\; \dfrac{\textcolor{#ff822c}{\text{after}}}{\text{multiplier}}$$

That's the entire idea. **Find the multiplier, then divide.**

## Reverse a discount

**Example.** After a $15\%$ discount, a jacket costs \$204. Original price?

- Multiplier for $-15\%$: $0.85$.
- Original: $\tfrac{204}{0.85} = 240$, i.e. \$240.
- Check: $240 \times 0.85 = 204$ ✓.

## Reverse a tax inclusion

**Example.** A phone is sold for \$540 **including** $20\%$ tax. What is the pre-tax price?

- Pre-tax $\times 1.20 =$ post-tax.
- Pre-tax $= \tfrac{540}{1.20} = 450$, i.e. \$450.
- Check: $450 \times 1.20 = 540$ ✓.

The trap students fall into: subtracting $20\%$ of \$540 to get the pre-tax price. That gives \$540 × 0.80 = \$432, which is wrong. The $20\%$ is of the **pre-tax** amount, not the post-tax. Always divide.

## Reverse a profit calculation

**Example.** A car is sold for \$15\,000, a $25\%$ profit on the cost price. What was the cost?

- Cost $\times 1.25 =$ selling price.
- Cost $= \tfrac{15\,000}{1.25} = 12\,000$, i.e. \$12\,000.
- Check: $12\,000 \times 1.25 = 15\,000$ ✓.

The same logic: $25\%$ profit means the selling price is $125\%$ of the cost, so divide by $1.25$ to recover the cost.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't subtract a percentage of the AFTER value.** "After $20\%$ tax, price is \$540" doesn't mean pre-tax is \$540 − 20% × \$540 = \$432. The $20\%$ is calculated on the PRE-TAX amount, not the post-tax. The only correct method is **divide by the multiplier**.

</div>

## Reverse with depreciation

**Example.** A population is now $44\,000$, a $10\%$ decrease from last year. What was it last year?

- Multiplier for $-10\%$: $0.90$.
- Last year: $\tfrac{44\,000}{0.90} \approx 48\,889$ people.

Check: $48\,889 \times 0.90 = 44\,000$ ✓.

For a population this would round to $48\,889$ (nearest whole person).

## Worked example

A shop has $20\%$ off everything. A customer buys a shirt for \$48 in the sale. What was the original price?

**Step 1: Identify the multiplier.** $-20\%$ gives multiplier $0.80$.

**Step 2: Divide the sale price by the multiplier.**

$$\dfrac{48}{0.80} \;=\; 60 \quad\text{(in dollars)}$$

**Step 3: Check.** \$60 × 0.80 = \$48 ✓.

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{60}$. **[M1 for the multiplier; A1 for the original price]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example: profit

A book is sold for \$45, giving a $50\%$ profit on the cost. Find the cost price.

**Step 1: Set up.** Cost × 1.50 = \$45.

**Step 2: Solve.**

$$\text{cost} \;=\; \dfrac{45}{1.50} \;=\; 30 \quad\text{(in dollars)}$$

**Step 3: Check.** \$30 × 1.50 = \$45 ✓.

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{30}$. **[M1 for $\div 1.50$; A1 for the cost]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting (or adding) the percentage from the AFTER value. The percentage is always relative to the BEFORE, so you must divide by the multiplier.
- Using the wrong multiplier. For "$25\%$ profit", the multiplier is $1.25$ (cost grows by $25\%$ to give selling price). Not $0.25$, not $1 - 0.25$.
- Forgetting to check. A reverse-percentage answer should multiply BACK to the original given value: always verify.
- Mixing reverse with regular. "After $20\%$ off, price is \$80" $\to$ original is \$100, not \$96.

## Quick summary

- Reverse percentage: divide the AFTER value by the multiplier.
- $+20\%$ multiplier $1.20$. $-15\%$ multiplier $0.85$. $25\%$ profit multiplier $1.25$.
- Always check: original $\times$ multiplier should equal the AFTER value.
- Never subtract a percentage of the AFTER value; that gives the wrong answer.
