## Why this matters

Percentages run through every part of O-Level maths and almost every part of life: marks, profits, taxes, discounts, growth rates, statistics. Cambridge expects you to handle two basic skills with no hesitation: **find a percentage of a quantity**, and **express one quantity as a percentage of another**. Get these clean and the harder topics in 1.13 (increase, decrease, interest, reverse) all build on top.

## What "per cent" means

The word "per cent" literally means **per hundred**. So $25\%$ is just another way of writing $\tfrac{25}{100} = \tfrac{1}{4} = 0.25$.

To convert a percentage to a useful number, just divide by $100$:

| Percentage | As fraction | As decimal |
|---|---|---|
| $50\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ | $0.5$ |
| $25\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{4}$ | $0.25$ |
| $10\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{10}$ | $0.1$ |
| $1\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{100}$ | $0.01$ |
| $33.33\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{3}$ | $0.\overline{3}$ |

## Skill 1: percentage of a quantity

To find $\textcolor{#fff067}{P}\%$ of a quantity $\textcolor{#00abfa}{Q}$, multiply by $\textcolor{#fff067}{P}/100$ (or by the decimal form):

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{P}\% \text{ of } \textcolor{#00abfa}{Q} \;=\; \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{P}}{100} \times \textcolor{#00abfa}{Q}$$

**Example.** $25\%$ of \$80.

$$\dfrac{25}{100} \times 80 \;=\; 0.25 \times 80 \;=\; 20$$

So $25\%$ of \$80 is \$20.

**Example.** $15\%$ of $240$ kg.

$$0.15 \times 240 \;=\; 36 \text{ kg}$$

The shortcut for nice percentages: convert to a fraction first.

- $50\% = \tfrac{1}{2}$, so halve.
- $25\% = \tfrac{1}{4}$, so quarter.
- $10\% = \tfrac{1}{10}$, so divide by 10.
- $1\% = \tfrac{1}{100}$, so divide by 100.

For $15\%$ of $240$ without a calculator: $10\%$ is $24$, then $5\%$ is half of that ($12$), so $15\%$ is $24 + 12 = 36$. Same answer.

## Skill 2: one quantity as a percentage of another

To express **<span style="color:#fff067">one quantity</span> as a percentage of <span style="color:#00abfa">another</span>**, divide them and multiply by $100$:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{\text{percentage}} \;=\; \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{\text{part}}}{\textcolor{#00abfa}{\text{whole}}} \times 100\%$$

**Example.** A class of $30$ students has $18$ boys. What percentage are boys?

$$\dfrac{18}{30} \times 100\% \;=\; 60\%$$

**Example.** A student scores $34$ out of $40$. Their percentage:

$$\dfrac{34}{40} \times 100\% \;=\; 85\%$$

The order matters: **the part goes on top, the whole on the bottom**. If the question asks "what percentage of the class are boys", boys is the part, total students is the whole.

## Worked example

A pair of shoes costs \$80. The shop charges $5\%$ sales tax. How much tax is added?

**Step 1: Convert the percentage.** $5\% = 0.05$.

**Step 2: Multiply.**

$$0.05 \times 80 \;=\; 4$$

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{4}$ tax. **[B1 for the multiplication; A1 for the answer in dollars]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to multiply by $100$ when expressing a fraction as a percentage. $\tfrac{18}{30} = 0.6$, but the percentage is $60\%$, not $0.6\%$.
- Putting the whole on top by mistake. $\tfrac{30}{18}$ would give $167\%$, which is nonsense for "boys out of total".
- Treating $25\%$ as $25$. To use it in a calculation, convert: $25\% = 0.25$ or $\tfrac{1}{4}$.
- Forgetting the unit. "$36$" alone is unclear; "$36$ kg" is the right form.

## Quick summary

- $P\% = \tfrac{P}{100}$. Multiply by this to find $P\%$ of any quantity.
- "Express A as a percentage of B" $\to$ $\tfrac{A}{B} \times 100\%$. Whole on the bottom.
- Common percentages have shortcuts: $50\%$ halves, $25\%$ quarters, $10\%$ divides by $10$.
