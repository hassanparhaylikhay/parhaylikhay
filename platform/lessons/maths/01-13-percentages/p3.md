## Why this matters

Cambridge tests two kinds of interest, and you must know both. **Simple interest** adds the same amount each year. **Compound interest** adds a percentage of the **growing total** each year, so the growth itself grows. The same idea covers depreciation (compound shrinking) and population growth.

## Simple interest

Simple interest is paid on the **original** amount only. The formula:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{I} \;=\; \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{P} \times \textcolor{#00abfa}{R} \times \textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}{100}$$

where $\textcolor{#fff067}{P}$ is the **principal** (starting amount), $\textcolor{#00abfa}{R}$ is the rate (as a percentage, e.g. $5$ for $5\%$), and $\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}$ is the time in years.

**Example.** \$1\,000 at $5\%$ simple interest for $3$ years.

$$I \;=\; \dfrac{1000 \times 5 \times 3}{100} \;=\; \$150$$

Total amount: \$1\,000 + \$150 = \$1\,150.

The interest is the same each year (\$50), so over $3$ years it is just $3 \times 50 = 150$, i.e. \$150.

## Compound interest

Compound interest is paid on the **growing total**, so the second year's interest is on a slightly bigger amount than the first year's. The formula:

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{A} \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{P} \times \left(1 + \dfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{R}}{100}\right)^{\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}$$

where $\textcolor{#0fee89}{A}$ is the **final amount** (principal + interest combined), and the other letters mean the same as above.

In multiplier language: each year you multiply by $1 + \tfrac{R}{100}$. After $T$ years you've multiplied by that number $T$ times, hence the power.

**Compound vs simple comparison.** \$1\,000 at $5\%$ for $3$ years:

- Simple: total amount \$1\,150.
- Compound: total amount \$1\,157.63.

Compound is bigger because the interest in year 2 and year 3 is on the larger balance. Over longer time periods this difference grows fast.

## Depreciation and other repeated decreases

When a value goes **down** by the same percentage each year (a car losing value, a phone depreciating, a population shrinking), use a multiplier less than $1$ raised to a power.

$$\textcolor{#0fee89}{A} \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{P} \times \left(1 - \dfrac{\textcolor{#00abfa}{R}}{100}\right)^{\textcolor{#ff822c}{T}}$$

**Example.** A car worth \$24\,000 depreciates $10\%$ per year. Value after $2$ years:

$$24\,000 \times 0.90^2 \;=\; 24\,000 \times 0.81 \;=\; \$19\,440$$

## Population growth

Same formula as compound interest with a positive rate.

**Example.** A population of $50\,000$ grows $3\%$ per year. After $4$ years:

$$50\,000 \times 1.03^4 \;=\; 50\,000 \times 1.12551 \;\approx\; 56\,275 \text{ people}$$

(Always round populations to the nearest whole person.)

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $5\%$ per year for $3$ years is NOT $15\%$ overall.** Compound growth means the percentages don't simply add. $1.05^3 = 1.157625$, so the effective increase is $\approx 15.76\%$, not $15\%$. The longer the time, the bigger the gap.

</div>

## Worked example

A bank pays $4\%$ compound interest per year. \$2\,000 is invested for $5$ years. How much is the investment worth at the end?

**Step 1: Identify the multiplier.** $1 + \tfrac{4}{100} = 1.04$.

**Step 2: Apply the compound formula.**

$$A \;=\; 2\,000 \times 1.04^5$$

**Step 3: Compute** (calculator).

$$1.04^5 \;=\; 1.21665\ldots, \qquad 2\,000 \times 1.21665 \;\approx\; 2\,433.31$$

**Answer:** \$$\boxed{2\,433.31}$. **[M1 for $1.04^5$; A1 for the answer to 2 d.p.]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Using simple interest when the question says "compound" (or vice versa). Read the wording.
- Forgetting the power. "$5\%$ for $3$ years" means $\times 1.05^3$, not $\times 1.05 \times 3$.
- Mixing the rate format. $5\%$ becomes $0.05$ as a decimal, so the multiplier is $1.05$, not $1 + 5 = 6$.
- For depreciation, using $1 + \tfrac{R}{100}$ instead of $1 - \tfrac{R}{100}$. Loss in value means a multiplier less than $1$.

## Quick summary

- <b style="color:#fff067">Simple</b>: $I = \tfrac{PRT}{100}$. Same interest each year.
- <b style="color:#0fee89">Compound</b>: $A = P(1 + \tfrac{R}{100})^T$. Each year multiplies the previous total.
- Depreciation: same as compound with $-$ instead of $+$ in the multiplier.
- Don't add percentages over time: use the power.
