## What you should know by now

After P1 and P2 you should be able to:

- Measure a length to the nearest mm and an angle to the nearest degree.
- Draw a line of given length or an angle of given size accurately.
- Construct a triangle from three given side lengths using ruler and compasses, with both arcs visible at the third vertex.
- Identify and sketch nets of cubes, cuboids, prisms (including triangular and cylindrical), and pyramids.
- Calculate surface area and volume of a solid from its net.

## Marking patterns at a glance

For **constructions**:

- B$2$ for a complete construction within tolerance ($\pm 0.2$ cm on each side) **with arcs visible**.
- B$1$ if either (a) one side is wrong and the rest correct with arcs, OR (b) the triangle is correct but no arcs are visible.
- For a measured length / angle: B$1$ for the value within tolerance ($\pm 0.1$ cm or $\pm 1°$).

For **nets and surface area / volume**:

- M$1$ for showing a correct method (e.g. listing the right faces, writing the right formula).
- A$1$ for the correct numerical answer in the correct units.
- Often B$1$ for naming the solid first, before any calculation.

If the question asks you to **show** something, you must write the substituted formula. If it asks you to **calculate**, the working line $+$ answer is enough.

## Tolerance reminders

- Lengths measured / drawn: $\pm 0.1$ cm.
- Angles measured / drawn: $\pm 1°$ (sometimes $\pm 2°$).
- Constructed triangle sides: $\pm 0.2$ cm.

Outside the tolerance: zero on the accuracy mark, even if the method is right. (M marks may still be awarded if a clear correct method is shown.)

## What is NOT in the syllabus

For 4024 (the syllabus we are studying), you do **NOT** need:

- Compass-and-ruler constructions of perpendicular bisectors.
- Compass-and-ruler constructions of angle bisectors.
- Constructions of perpendiculars from a point to a line.

If a textbook drills these, skip those pages. They will not be tested. The only construction expected is "construct a triangle from three given sides".

## Worked example

A solid is a triangular prism. The triangular cross-section is a right-angled triangle with legs $5$ cm and $12$ cm. The length of the prism is $20$ cm.

(a) Calculate the length of the hypotenuse of the triangular face.

(b) Sketch the net (no scale required) and label every dimension.

(c) Calculate the total surface area of the prism.

(d) Calculate the volume of the prism.

**Step 1: (a) Apply Pythagoras to the cross-section.** $h^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$. **[M1 for $5^2 + 12^2$]**

**Step 2: (a) State the hypotenuse.** $h = \sqrt{169} = \boxed{13}$ cm. **[A1 for $13$]**

**Step 3: (b) Identify the net pieces.** $2$ right-angled triangles (legs $5$ and $12$, hypotenuse $13$) plus $3$ rectangles ($5 \times 20$, $12 \times 20$, $13 \times 20$), all dimensions labelled. **[B1 for net]**

**Step 4: (c) Set up the surface-area sum.** TSA $= 2 \times \dfrac{1}{2}\times 5 \times 12 + (5+12+13)\times 20$. **[M1 for setup]**

**Step 5: (c) Evaluate.** TSA $= 60 + 600 = \boxed{660}$ cm². **[A1 for $660$]**

**Step 6: (d) Set up the volume.** $V = (\text{area of cross-section}) \times \text{length} = \dfrac{1}{2}\times 5\times 12 \times 20$. **[M1 for setup]**

**Step 7: (d) Evaluate.** $V = 30 \times 20 = \boxed{600}$ cm³. **[A1 for $600$]**

Full marks $= 7$.

## Common mistakes recap

- Erasing construction arcs to make the page look neat. Never.
- Writing $7$ cm instead of $7.0$ cm or $7.4$ cm: Cambridge wants the millimetre detail.
- Using "triangle area $= $ base $\times$ height" without the $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- Forgetting the base of a pyramid when computing surface area.
- Confusing slant height with vertical height: use Pythagoras to switch.
- Counting only $5$ faces of a cuboid in TSA (forgetting one of the bottom or back).

## Quick reference

| Solid | Surface area | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Cube (side $s$) | $6 s^2$ | $s^3$ |
| Cuboid ($\ell, w, h$) | $2(\ell w + \ell h + w h)$ | $\ell w h$ |
| Prism (cross-section $A$, length $L$, perimeter $P$) | $2 A + P L$ | $A L$ |
| Cylinder ($r, h$) | $2 \pi r^2 + 2 \pi r h$ | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| Square pyramid (base $s$, slant $\ell$) | $s^2 + 2 s \ell$ | $\dfrac{1}{3} s^2 h$ |

(The volume formulas for prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone, sphere are GIVEN on the formula sheet in the exam. Surface area formulas for prism and cuboid are NOT given: you must know them.)
