## Why this matters

A typical exam question gives you a figure (triangle, parallelogram, sometimes a trapezium) with vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ labelled along two of the sides. Then it asks you to find every other vector in the figure in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. Each part is worth $1$ to $2$ marks, and they stack to make a $4$ to $6$-mark question.

The strategy is the same every time. **Find a head-to-tail route through points whose vectors you know.** Add or subtract along the route. Tidy up.

The questions get harder by hiding the route. You might need to use a midpoint, a ratio division, opposite sides of a parallelogram, or a side that has been extended. All four show up on this page.

## Parallelograms: opposite sides are equal vectors

When $OABC$ is a parallelogram, opposite sides have the same length AND point the same way. So they are equal as VECTORS:

$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OC}, \qquad \overrightarrow{CB} = \overrightarrow{OA}$$

That gives you four pre-built arrows around the figure as soon as you label $\mathbf{a} = \overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\mathbf{c} = \overrightarrow{OC}$.

## Worked example: diagonals of a parallelogram

$OABC$ is a parallelogram. $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. Find $\overrightarrow{AC}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c}$.

<iframe src="/widgets/vector-geometry-step-explorer.html?preset=p2-parallelogram-diagonals" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:700px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Diagonal $\overrightarrow{AC}$.** Route via $O$:

$$\overrightarrow{AC} = \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{OC} = -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$$

**Step 2: Diagonal $\overrightarrow{OB}$.** Route along the two sides $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}$:

$$\overrightarrow{OB} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AB}$$

In the parallelogram, $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$ (opposite sides equal). So:

$$\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$$

**Answer:** $\overrightarrow{AC} = \boxed{\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \boxed{\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}}$. **[B1 for $\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$; B1 for $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

The two diagonals of a parallelogram are always $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$ and $\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$ when the labelling is $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. Memorising those two patterns saves a step on the exam.

## Midpoints

If $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, then $M$ is exactly halfway from $A$ to $B$. So $\overrightarrow{AM} = \tfrac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{AB}$. To find the position vector $\overrightarrow{OM}$:

$$\overrightarrow{OM} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AM} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{b} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$$

That last line is worth committing to memory: **the position vector of the midpoint is the average of the two end position vectors**. It is the same idea as the midpoint formula from 3.4, written in vector language.

## A second example: a vector through a midpoint

In the figure, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{BM}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

**Step 1: $\overrightarrow{BM}$.** $M$ is the midpoint, so $\overrightarrow{BM} = \tfrac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{BA}$. And $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$. So:

$$\overrightarrow{BM} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b})$$

**Step 2: $\overrightarrow{OM}$.** Route via $B$ (you could also route via $A$).

$$\overrightarrow{OM} = \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{BM} = \mathbf{b} + \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{b} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$$

Same answer for $\overrightarrow{OM}$ whichever route you choose. That is a useful sanity check: when two routes through a figure give different answers, one of them has a sign mistake.

## Working strategy

When you see a "find $\overrightarrow{XY}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$" question, follow this routine:

1. **Mark the diagram.** Write $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ on the two given sides. Tick off any equal sides (parallelogram) or midpoints.
2. **Pick a route.** Find a head-to-tail path from $X$ to $Y$ that only passes through points you already have vectors to. Two segments is the most common.
3. **Write the route as an equation.** $\overrightarrow{XY} = \overrightarrow{X?} + \overrightarrow{?Y}$. Substitute the known vectors.
4. **Tidy up.** Expand any brackets, collect $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ separately, write the coefficients as tidy fractions.

The method marks come from steps 2 and 3; the accuracy mark comes from step 4. Show all of them on paper, in order, even if some look obvious.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BA}$.** Half of every wrong vector geometry answer is a sign error from this. Check the direction of the arrow against the order of the letters.
- **Skipping the head-to-tail step.** Writing "$\overrightarrow{AC} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$" without explaining the route loses the method mark in a long question. Show $\overrightarrow{AC} = \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{OC}$ first.
- **Forgetting that opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.** You can spend ten lines re-deriving what is a one-line property of the figure.
- **Multiplying instead of scaling.** $\tfrac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{AB}$ means HALF the displacement; the result is a vector, not a scalar.
- **Treating $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ as if they have specific numbers.** They are arbitrary vectors. You manipulate them symbolically, like $x$ and $y$ in algebra. Do not write $\mathbf{a} = (2, 1)$ unless the question gives you a column.

## Quick summary

- **Three building blocks**: displacement ($\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$), midpoint ($\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$), parallelogram side equality.
- **Strategy**: mark the figure, pick a head-to-tail route, write the equation, tidy up.
- **Diagonals of $OABC$ parallelogram**: $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$.
- **Sanity check**: if two routes give different answers, recheck signs.
