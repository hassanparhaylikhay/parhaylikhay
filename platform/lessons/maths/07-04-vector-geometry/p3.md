## Why this matters

This part is the home of the longest single question on the vector paper, the one that runs across three or four sub-parts (a), (b), (c) and accumulates $4$ to $6$ marks. Every such question asks one of three things:

1. **Show that two vectors are parallel.** Worth $1$ to $2$ marks.
2. **Show that three points are collinear.** Usually $2$ marks, sometimes more.
3. **Find a position vector given a ratio split.** $2$ to $3$ marks.

All three rely on the same tool: write everything in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, then read off the structure. Parallel means "one is a number times the other". Collinear means "parallel AND sharing a starting point". Ratio splits start with $\overrightarrow{AT} = \tfrac{m}{m+n}\overrightarrow{AB}$.

## Parallel test

Two non-zero vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are parallel if and only if one is a scalar multiple of the other:

$$\mathbf{u} \text{ parallel to } \mathbf{v} \iff \mathbf{v} = k\mathbf{u} \text{ for some non-zero } k$$

To check, factor out a common multiplier. Look at the coefficients of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$:

- $4\mathbf{a} + 6\mathbf{b}$ and $2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$ → factor of $2$ on the first → parallel.
- $2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$ and $4\mathbf{a} + 5\mathbf{b}$ → coefficients give $\tfrac{4}{2} = 2$ but $\tfrac{5}{3} \neq 2$ → not parallel.

The two coefficient ratios MUST agree. Same idea as the parallel check from 7.2 p3, just written symbolically instead of numerically.

## Collinear test

Three points $P, Q, R$ are **collinear** if they lie on the same straight line. The vector test:

$$P, Q, R \text{ collinear} \iff \overrightarrow{PQ} = k\,\overrightarrow{PR} \text{ for some non-zero } k$$

(Two non-zero vectors starting at $P$ that point along the same line: that is what "collinear" means for three points.) An exam answer needs to mention BOTH parts: the scalar multiple AND the common starting point. A complete answer looks like:

> $\overrightarrow{PQ} = 2\overrightarrow{PR}$, and both vectors start at $P$, so $P, Q, R$ lie on a straight line.

Skipping the "both start at $P$" sentence is a common one-mark loss. You proved the arrows are parallel; you also need to anchor them at the same point.

## Worked example: show three points are collinear

In a figure, $\overrightarrow{PX} = 2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{PY} = 4\mathbf{a} + 6\mathbf{b}$. Show that $P, X, Y$ are collinear.

<iframe src="/widgets/vector-geometry-step-explorer.html?preset=p3-parallel-test" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:680px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Factor.** Compare $\overrightarrow{PY}$ to $\overrightarrow{PX}$:

$$\overrightarrow{PY} = 4\mathbf{a} + 6\mathbf{b} = 2(2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}) = 2\,\overrightarrow{PX}$$

**Step 2: State the conclusion in full.**

$\overrightarrow{PY} = 2\,\overrightarrow{PX}$, so $\overrightarrow{PY}$ is parallel to $\overrightarrow{PX}$. Both vectors start at $P$, so $P, X, Y$ lie on a straight line.

**Answer:** $\overrightarrow{PY} = 2\,\overrightarrow{PX} \Rightarrow \boxed{P, X, Y \text{ are collinear}}$. **[M1 for $\overrightarrow{PY} = 2\,\overrightarrow{PX}$ or factor of $2$ stated; A1 for the conclusion with the "both start at $P$" reasoning]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Ratio splits

If $T$ lies on $AB$ with $AT : TB = m : n$, the segment $AB$ is divided into $m + n$ equal parts, with $T$ sitting $m$ of those parts from $A$. So:

$$\overrightarrow{AT} = \frac{m}{m + n}\,\overrightarrow{AB}, \qquad \overrightarrow{TB} = \frac{n}{m + n}\,\overrightarrow{AB}$$

To find the position vector $\overrightarrow{OT}$, route via $A$:

$$\overrightarrow{OT} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AT} = \mathbf{a} + \frac{m}{m+n}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$$

Expand and collect:

$$\overrightarrow{OT} = \frac{n}{m+n}\,\mathbf{a} + \frac{m}{m+n}\,\mathbf{b}$$

The coefficients add up to $1$ (good check) and they FLIP relative to the ratio: $AT : TB = 3 : 2$ gives $\overrightarrow{OT} = \tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{b}$. The reason: the COEFFICIENT of $\mathbf{a}$ tells you how much weight the point $A$ carries; if $T$ is far from $A$, that weight is small.

## Worked example: T on AB with AT:TB = 3:2

In a triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $T$ lies on $AB$ with $AT : TB = 3 : 2$. Find $\overrightarrow{OT}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

<iframe src="/widgets/vector-geometry-step-explorer.html?preset=p3-ratio-split" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:700px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Interpret the ratio.** $AT : TB = 3 : 2$ splits $AB$ into $5$ equal parts, with $T$ three of them from $A$:

$$\overrightarrow{AT} = \tfrac{3}{5}\,\overrightarrow{AB} = \tfrac{3}{5}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$$

**Step 2: Route from $O$ to $T$.** Go via $A$:

$$\overrightarrow{OT} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AT} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$$

**Step 3: Expand and collect.**

$$\overrightarrow{OT} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{b} - \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{a} = \tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{b}$$

**Answer:** $\overrightarrow{OT} = \boxed{\tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{b}}$. **[M1 for $\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AT}$ route; M1 for $\tfrac{3}{5}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$ expanded; A1 for the tidy form]**

Full marks $= 3$.

Quick check: $\tfrac{2}{5} + \tfrac{3}{5} = 1$. Coefficients sum to one, so $T$ is on the segment $AB$ as required. If the coefficients had summed to anything else, there would be a sign error somewhere.

## Worked example: a point on a parallelogram side

$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{b}$. $N$ lies on $AB$ with $AN : NB = 1 : 3$. Find $\overrightarrow{ON}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

**Step 1:** $AN : NB = 1 : 3$, so $\overrightarrow{AN} = \tfrac{1}{4}\overrightarrow{AB}$. In the parallelogram $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{b}$.

$$\overrightarrow{AN} = \tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b}$$

**Step 2:** Route from $O$ to $N$ via $A$:

$$\overrightarrow{ON} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AN} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b}$$

**Answer:** $\overrightarrow{ON} = \boxed{\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{b}}$. **[M1 for $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b}$ used; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

Notice the difference: $\overrightarrow{AB}$ in the triangle question of step 1 was $\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$, but in this parallelogram it is just $\mathbf{b}$, because $AB$ is parallel and equal to $OC$. Always check WHAT FIGURE you are in before reaching for $\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping "both start at the same point" in collinearity.** Parallel arrows alone do not prove three points are on a line. You also need a shared origin.
- **Flipping the ratio the wrong way.** $AT : TB = 3 : 2$ gives $\overrightarrow{AT} = \tfrac{3}{5}\overrightarrow{AB}$, not $\tfrac{3}{2}\overrightarrow{AB}$. The fraction is "first piece over total", never "first piece over second piece".
- **Forgetting the FLIP in the position vector.** $\overrightarrow{OT}$ with $AT : TB = 3 : 2$ becomes $\tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{b}$. The bigger piece $3$ goes with the OTHER endpoint's vector.
- **Treating $\overrightarrow{AB}$ as $\mathbf{b}$ in a triangle.** In a triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$, not $\mathbf{b}$. The parallelogram shortcut $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OC}$ only works inside a parallelogram.
- **Stopping at the un-tidied expression.** Markers want $\overrightarrow{OT} = \tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}\mathbf{b}$, not $\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{3}{5}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$. The accuracy mark is for the tidy form.

## Quick summary

- **Parallel test**: $\mathbf{u} = k\mathbf{v}$ for some non-zero $k$. Coefficient ratios must agree.
- **Collinear test**: $\overrightarrow{PQ} = k\,\overrightarrow{PR}$ AND both start at $P$. State both parts in the answer.
- **Ratio split** $AT : TB = m : n$ gives $\overrightarrow{AT} = \dfrac{m}{m+n}\overrightarrow{AB}$.
- **Position vector** of $T$: $\overrightarrow{OT} = \dfrac{n}{m+n}\mathbf{a} + \dfrac{m}{m+n}\mathbf{b}$. Coefficients flip; they always sum to $1$.
- **Sanity check**: coefficients of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ in any "point on segment" answer must add up to $1$.

The review combines a parallel test, a ratio split, and a collinearity proof into one multi-part question. That structure is the most common long vector question on the paper.
