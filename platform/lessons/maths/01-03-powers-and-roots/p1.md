## Why this matters

Cambridge expects you to **recall** the squares from $1^2$ to $15^2$ and their roots without a calculator. These appear directly as 1-mark questions ("Write the value of $\sqrt{169}$") and are also building blocks for almost every Number topic. Memorise them once and you save thinking time on every paper.

## What is a square?

The **<span style="color:#fff067">square</span>** of a number $n$ is $n \times n$, written $n^2$.

$$5^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25$$

The little $2$ is called an **index** or **power**. We say "five squared".

For negatives: $(-3)^2 = (-3) \times (-3) = +9$. A negative times a negative is positive, so squaring any real number gives a non-negative result.

## What is a square root?

The **<span style="color:#0fee89">square root</span>** of $n$ is the number you square to get $n$. The symbol is $\sqrt{\ }$.

$$\sqrt{25} = 5 \quad \text{because} \quad 5^2 = 25$$

By convention, $\sqrt{n}$ means the **positive** root only. So $\sqrt{25} = 5$, never $-5$. (When solving $x^2 = 25$ you do get both, but $\sqrt{}$ on its own is positive.)

The square roots are the same list, read backwards: $\sqrt{1} = 1$, $\sqrt{4} = 2$, ..., $\sqrt{225} = 15$.

## Estimating non-perfect roots

If a number is *between* two perfect squares, its root is between the two roots.

$\sqrt{50}$: $50$ is between $49 = 7^2$ and $64 = 8^2$. So $\sqrt{50}$ is between $7$ and $8$. (Closer to $7$ because $50$ is closer to $49$.)

This is enough for most Cambridge "estimate" questions. A calculator gives the exact value when needed.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: not every root is a whole number.** $\sqrt{49} = 7$, but $\sqrt{50}$ is irrational ($\approx 7.07$). The square root only "comes out clean" when the number under the root is a **perfect square** (one from the recall list above). Always check before assuming.

</div>

## Worked example

Find the value of $\sqrt{169} - \sqrt{25}$.

**Step 1: Recall each root.** $\sqrt{169} = 13$ (since $13^2 = 169$), and $\sqrt{25} = 5$.

**Step 2: Subtract.**

$$\sqrt{169} - \sqrt{25} = 13 - 5 = 8$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{8}$. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing $n^2$ with $2n$. $7^2 = 49$, but $2 \times 7 = 14$. Different operations.
- Forgetting that $(-n)^2 = +n^2$. Squaring kills the sign.
- Writing $\sqrt{49} = \pm 7$. By convention, $\sqrt{}$ is the positive root only.
- Trying to compute $\sqrt{50}$ as a clean integer. $50$ isn't a perfect square; the root is irrational.

## Quick summary

- $\textcolor{#fff067}{n^2 = n \times n}$. Squaring always gives a non-negative number.
- $\textcolor{#0fee89}{\sqrt{n}}$ is the positive value whose square is $n$.
- Memorise $1^2$ through $15^2$ and their roots.
- For non-perfect squares, sandwich the root between two integers using the recall list.
