You've now seen everything Cambridge tests in 1.3. The whole topic boils down to **recall plus careful arithmetic**: know the squares, know the six cubes, and apply order of operations when expressions combine them.

## The skills you should have

- **Recall every square** from $1^2$ to $15^2$ (and the matching roots).
- **Recall the six cubes**: $1^3, 2^3, 3^3, 4^3, 5^3, 10^3$ and their roots.
- **Compute higher powers** like $2^4$, $2^5$, $3^4$ from first principles when needed.
- **Compute higher roots** like $\sqrt[4]{16}$, $\sqrt[5]{32}$ by recognising the perfect-power.
- **Apply order of operations**: indices (powers + roots) before multiplication, division, addition, subtraction.

## Marking patterns

Cambridge Powers and Roots questions tend to be straightforward 1- or 2-mark questions:

| Question style | Marks | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| "Write the value of $\sqrt{169}$" | 1 | Pure recall |
| "Work out $5^2 \times \sqrt[3]{8}$" | 1 | Indices first, then multiply |
| "From this list, identify the perfect square / cube" | 1 | Match against recall list |
| Multi-step calculation involving powers + other ops | 2 | M1 method (each power/root computed) + A1 final |

## The single biggest mistake

Forgetting that **squaring is not multiplying by 2**, and **cubing is not multiplying by 3**. $7^2 = 49$, not $14$. $4^3 = 64$, not $12$. This trip-up costs more marks than any other in 1.3.

## Final check

Six mixed questions. Pure recall for the easy ones; combined for the trickier ones.
