## Why this matters

This is the most common Cambridge question type in unit 3: "a line has gradient $m$ and passes through point $P$ - find its equation." The recipe takes three steps and is worth M1 + A1 every time.

## The recipe

You need two pieces of information to fix a line uniquely. Here you have:

- The **gradient** $m$ (already known).
- One **point** $(x_1, y_1)$ on the line.

Plug both into $y = mx + c$ to find the only remaining unknown, $c$.

**Step 1.** Write down $y = mx + c$ with $m$ filled in.

**Step 2.** Substitute the point: replace $x$ with $x_1$ and $y$ with $y_1$.

**Step 3.** Solve for $c$.

**Step 4.** Write the final equation $y = mx + c$.

## Worked example: easy point

A line has gradient $2$ and passes through $(3, 7)$. Find its equation.

**Step 1: write $y = 2x + c$.**

**Step 2: substitute $(3, 7)$.**

$$7 = 2(3) + c$$

**Step 3: solve.**

$$7 = 6 + c \;\Rightarrow\; c = 1$$

**Step 4: final equation.**

**Answer:** $y = 2x + 1$. **[M1 substitute; A1 simplify]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example: negative gradient and y-intercept gift

A line has gradient $-3$ and passes through $(0, 5)$. Find its equation.

**Step 1: write $y = -3x + c$.**

**Step 2: substitute $(0, 5)$.**

$$5 = -3(0) + c$$

**Step 3: solve.**

$$5 = c$$

When the point is on the y-axis (i.e. $x = 0$), $c$ is just the y-coordinate. No work needed.

**Answer:** $y = -3x + 5$. **[B1 - single step]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Alternative form: point-gradient

There's a second formula you may see in textbooks:

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

This is just the rearrangement of $y = mx + c$ with the point built in. Either form gets you the same answer; Cambridge accepts both. The recipe above (substitute and solve for $c$) is usually faster for O-Level.

## Common mistakes

- **Substituting into the wrong slot.** $(x_1, y_1)$ means $x \to x_1$ and $y \to y_1$. Don't put $y_1$ where $x$ should go.
- **Forgetting to actually solve for $c$.** Some students stop after substitution. Finish the algebra.
- **Sign error with negative coordinates.** Bracket negatives: $-3(2) = -6$, but $-3 \times -2 = 6$. Watch the signs.
- **Writing the final answer with $m$ and $c$ unsimplified.** "$y = 2x + (7 - 6)$" is wrong - simplify to $y = 2x + 1$.

## Quick summary

- Recipe: write $y = mx + c$ with $m$, substitute the point, solve for $c$.
- If the point has $x = 0$ (on y-axis), $c$ is just $y_1$ - done in one step.
- Either substitute into $y = mx + c$ or use $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ - same answer.
