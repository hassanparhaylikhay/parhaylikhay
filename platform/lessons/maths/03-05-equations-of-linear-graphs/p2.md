## Why this matters

Two points are enough to fix a line uniquely (any two distinct points). Cambridge's most common Paper-2 coordinate-geometry question asks: "find the equation of the line through $A$ and $B$, giving your answer in the form $y = mx + c$."

The recipe is **two-step**: find $m$ first, then find $c$.

## The two-step recipe

You have two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$.

**Step 1: find the gradient.**

$$m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

**Step 2: find $c$ by substituting either point.** Plug $m$ and one of the two points into $y = mx + c$ and solve for $c$.

It doesn't matter which point you pick — both give the same $c$. (You can use the second as a check if you want.)

## Worked example

Find the equation of the line through $A(1, 4)$ and $B(5, 12)$.

**Step 1: find the gradient.**

$$m = \dfrac{12 - 4}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$$

**Step 2: substitute one point into $y = mx + c$.** Use $A$:

$$4 = 2(1) + c$$

**Step 3: solve for $c$.**

$$4 = 2 + c \;\Rightarrow\; c = 2$$

**Step 4: write the final equation.**

**Answer:** $y = 2x + 2$. **[M1 gradient; M1 substitute; A1 final]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Worked example: with negative coordinates

Find the equation of the line through $P(-2, 3)$ and $Q(4, -3)$.

**Step 1: find the gradient.**

$$m = \dfrac{-3 - 3}{4 - (-2)} = \dfrac{-6}{6} = -1$$

**Step 2: substitute $P$ into $y = mx + c$.**

$$3 = -1(-2) + c = 2 + c$$

**Step 3: solve.**

$$c = 1$$

**Answer:** $y = -x + 1$. **[M1 gradient; M1 substitute; A1 final]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Three special cases

### Both points have the same $x$-coordinate

The line is **vertical**: equation is $x = x_1$. The gradient is undefined; you can't use $y = mx + c$.

E.g., through $(3, 1)$ and $(3, 7)$: equation is $x = 3$.

### Both points have the same $y$-coordinate

The line is **horizontal**: equation is $y = y_1$. Gradient is $0$.

E.g., through $(2, -4)$ and $(5, -4)$: equation is $y = -4$.

### Both points are the same

Then they don't define a unique line. Cambridge won't ask this — but if a question gives identical points, flag it.

## Common mistakes

- **Mismatched order in numerator and denominator of $m$.** Be consistent: $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, not $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_1 - x_2}$.
- **Forgetting to find $c$.** Computing $m$ alone doesn't give the equation.
- **Substituting both points and getting confused.** One point is enough to find $c$ — don't substitute both.
- **Not writing the final equation.** A bare $m = 2$ and $c = 2$ isn't the answer — the question wants $y = 2x + 2$.

## Quick summary

- Step 1: $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
- Step 2: substitute one point into $y = mx + c$ and solve for $c$.
- Step 3: write the equation $y = mx + c$.
- Same x-coordinate → vertical line $x = k$. Same y-coordinate → horizontal line $y = k$.
