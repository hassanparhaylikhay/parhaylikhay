## Why this matters

A small classroom: $4$ m × $3$ m. Area: $12$ m². Now express that in cm².

The wrong instinct: "$4 \times 3 = 12$, then $12 \times 100 = 1200$ cm². Done."

The right answer: $120\,000$ cm². Off by a hundred.

Cambridge knows this is the most common error in mensuration. They put it in nearly every paper. The fix is simple once you see it: when you change the LENGTH unit, the AREA factor SQUARES.

In this part:

- Why the factor squares (visual proof).
- The three-step recipe that makes the conversion automatic.
- The exact wording Cambridge wants for full marks.

## The squared factor: visual proof

Imagine a $1$ m × $1$ m square tile. Now lay a centimetre grid on top of it. How many tiny $1$ cm × $1$ cm cells are inside?

Not $100$. The square has $100$ cells along the bottom and $100$ rows up the side. Total $= 100 \times 100 = 10\,000$ cells.

That is the proof. The widget below lets you reveal the grid and count. Try the cm² $\leftrightarrow$ m² pair first, since that is the one Cambridge tests most.

The pattern is the same for every pair on the syllabus:

- $1$ cm² $= (10)^2 = 100$ mm²
- $1$ m² $= (100)^2 = 10\,000$ cm²
- $1$ km² $= (1000)^2 = 1\,000\,000$ m²

If you ever forget the area factor, derive it: write the linear factor, then square it.

## The three-step recipe

For any area conversion, do these three steps in order. Cambridge mark schemes love this because each step is its own mark.

**Step 1: Linear factor.** Write the conversion between the *lengths*.

- e.g. for m² $\to$ cm²: $1$ m $= 100$ cm.

**Step 2: Square it.** Square the linear factor to get the area factor.

- $1$ m² $= (100\;\text{cm})^2 = 100 \times 100 = 10\,000$ cm².

**Step 3: Multiply (or divide).** Apply the area factor to the value you have.

- $3.5$ m² $\times 10\,000 = 35\,000$ cm².

Three steps, three potential marks. Even if you slip on step 3, your work on steps 1 and 2 still earns method marks if it is on the page.

## Going DOWN vs UP: same rule as length

Same DOWN/UP rule from p1 still applies:

- DOWN (bigger area unit $\to$ smaller area unit): MULTIPLY by the squared factor.
- UP (smaller $\to$ bigger): DIVIDE by the squared factor.

Sanity check: did the number get bigger when going down to a smaller unit? Yes? Good. No? Re-do the step.

## Worked example

A rectangular garden has length $5.2$ m and width $3.5$ m.

(a) Find the area in m².

(b) Express the area in cm².

**Step a: Area in m².** Length × width:

$$A = 5.2 \times 3.5 = 18.2\;\text{m}^2$$

**[M1 for length × width; A1 for $18.2$]**

**Step b: Convert to cm².** Three-step recipe.

Linear factor: $1$ m $= 100$ cm.

Squared: $1$ m² $= 100^2 = 10\,000$ cm².

Apply: $18.2 \times 10\,000 = \boxed{182\,000}$ cm².

**[M1 for the squared factor $10\,000$; A1 for $182\,000$ cm²]**

Full marks $= 4$.

Notice how each part of the recipe pulls in a mark. If you skipped to "$18.2 \times 100 = 1820$" you would lose two marks: the squared step and the answer.

## Marking patterns at a glance

- **B1** for the linear factor (e.g. "$1$ m $= 100$ cm") when "show your working" is asked.
- **M1** for stating or using the squared factor ($\times 10\,000$ for m² $\leftrightarrow$ cm²).
- **A1** for the final value with units.

If a question asks for an area in a specific unit, the unit is part of the answer. Writing the right number with the wrong unit (e.g. "$182\,000$ m²") loses the A1.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to square.** The classic. Multiplying by $100$ instead of $10\,000$. Whenever the question switches *area* unit, write the linear factor and immediately put a $^2$ on it before doing anything else.
- **Squaring the wrong way.** $1$ m $= 100$ cm, so $1$ m² $= (100)^2$ cm² $= 10\,000$ cm². Do not square just one side: the relationship $1$ m² $= 100$ cm² is wrong.
- **Mixing up cm and cm² in the same line.** Write the unit on every quantity in your working. "$3.5$ m² $\times 10\,000 = 35\,000$ cm²" not "$3.5 \times 10\,000 = 35\,000$".
- **Forgetting the answer unit.** As above, this is the most painful way to lose a mark.

## Quick summary

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| mm² $\to$ cm² | $\div 100$ |
| cm² $\to$ mm² | $\times 100$ |
| cm² $\to$ m² | $\div 10\,000$ |
| m² $\to$ cm² | $\times 10\,000$ |
| m² $\to$ km² | $\div 1\,000\,000$ |
| km² $\to$ m² | $\times 1\,000\,000$ |

The recipe: write the linear factor, square it, then multiply or divide. Always include the area unit in your answer.
