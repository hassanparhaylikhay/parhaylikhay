## Why this matters

A water tank that is $1$ m on every side holds $1000$ litres. Now scale every side to $2$ m. Many students say "$2000$ litres". Wrong. The new tank holds $8000$ litres, which is $2^3$ times the original.

That is the cubed factor. When the LENGTH unit changes, the VOLUME factor cubes. And there is a bonus relationship that Cambridge loves: $1$ cm³ of water $= 1$ ml. The cube and the bottle measure the same thing.

In this part:

- The cubed factor (visual proof).
- The capacity link: cm³ $\leftrightarrow$ ml $\leftrightarrow$ litre $\leftrightarrow$ m³.
- The Cambridge favourite: m³ $\leftrightarrow$ litres.

## The cubed factor: visual proof

Take a $1$ m × $1$ m × $1$ m cube. Cover its inside with $1$ cm × $1$ cm × $1$ cm sugar cubes. How many fit?

Not $100$. Not $10\,000$. The big cube holds $100$ cells along the bottom, $100$ rows wide, AND $100$ layers deep: $100 \times 100 \times 100 = 1\,000\,000$ small cubes.

The pattern across the syllabus:

- $1$ cm³ $= (10)^3 = 1000$ mm³
- $1$ m³ $= (100)^3 = 1\,000\,000$ cm³

If you forget the volume factor, derive it: write the linear factor, then cube it.

## Capacity ↔ volume: the link

This is one of the most useful tricks in the syllabus, and it works because $1$ ml of water occupies exactly $1$ cm³.

- $1$ cm³ $= 1$ ml
- $1$ litre $= 1000$ ml $= 1000$ cm³
- $1$ m³ $= 1\,000\,000$ cm³ $= 1\,000\,000$ ml $= 1000$ litres

So a $10$ cm × $10$ cm × $10$ cm cube has volume $1000$ cm³ and holds $1$ litre. A $1$ m × $1$ m × $1$ m cube has volume $1$ m³ and holds $1000$ litres. The widget below lets you flow values between any two of these units.

You will use this link a LOT in past papers. Volume of a cuboid in m³, then asked for capacity in litres? Multiply by $1000$. A bottle's capacity is $750$ ml? That is $750$ cm³.

## The three-step recipe (now with cubes)

Same three-step structure as area, but with a $^3$ instead of $^2$.

**Step 1: Linear factor.** $1$ m $= 100$ cm.

**Step 2: Cube it.** $1$ m³ $= (100)^3 = 1\,000\,000$ cm³.

**Step 3: Multiply (or divide).** $0.4$ m³ $\times 1\,000\,000 = 400\,000$ cm³.

Each step earns its mark. Even a slip on step 3 leaves your method marks intact if 1 and 2 are on the page.

## Worked example

A rectangular water tank has internal dimensions $0.4$ m × $0.5$ m × $0.8$ m.

(a) Find the volume of the tank in m³.

(b) Express this volume in cm³.

(c) The tank is filled to the top. How many litres of water does it hold?

**Step a: Volume in m³.** The tank is a cuboid, so multiply length × width × height:

$$V = 0.4 \times 0.5 \times 0.8 = 0.16\;\text{m}^3$$

**[M1 for the multiplication; A1 for $0.16$]**

**Step b: Convert to cm³.** Three-step recipe.

Linear factor: $1$ m $= 100$ cm.

Cubed: $1$ m³ $= 100^3 = 1\,000\,000$ cm³.

Apply: $0.16 \times 1\,000\,000 = \boxed{160\,000}$ cm³.

**[M1 for the cubed factor; A1 for $160\,000$ cm³]**

**Step c: Express in litres.** Use the capacity link directly: $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres.

$$0.16 \times 1000 = \boxed{160}\;\text{litres}$$

**[M1 for $\times 1000$; A1 for $160$ litres]**

(Alternative for step c: $160\,000$ cm³ $\div 1000 = 160$ litres. Either route earns the same marks.)

Full marks $= 6$.

## Marking patterns at a glance

- **B1** for stating "$1$ m $= 100$ cm" or "$1$ cm³ $= 1$ ml" when shown working is required.
- **M1** for using the cubed factor ($\times 1\,000\,000$ for m³ $\to$ cm³) or the capacity link ($\times 1000$ for m³ $\to$ litres).
- **A1** for the final value, with the unit.

When a question is in litres but you work in cm³, convert at the END. The marker reads the units; an answer in cm³ when litres was asked may still get the M mark but loses the A.

## Common mistakes

- **Squaring instead of cubing.** Volume cubes the linear factor. If you wrote $\times 10\,000$ for m³ $\to$ cm³, that is the *area* factor (wrong dimension).
- **Forgetting that $1$ ml $= 1$ cm³.** This is not optional knowledge. Write it on top of every working that mixes ml/litres with cm³/m³.
- **Mixing m³ and litres without converting.** "Volume is $0.16$, capacity is $0.16$ litres." Wrong: capacity is $160$ litres ($\times 1000$).
- **Using $1000$ for both m → cm and m³ → cm³.** Linear is $\times 100$. Volume is $\times 100^3 = \times 1\,000\,000$. Different powers.
- **Stopping at the wrong unit.** Question asks for litres. You finish at cm³. Apply the link.

## Quick summary

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| mm³ $\to$ cm³ | $\div 1000$ |
| cm³ $\to$ mm³ | $\times 1000$ |
| cm³ $\to$ m³ | $\div 1\,000\,000$ |
| m³ $\to$ cm³ | $\times 1\,000\,000$ |
| ml $\to$ litre | $\div 1000$ |
| litre $\to$ ml | $\times 1000$ |
| cm³ $\to$ ml | $\times 1$ (same!) |
| m³ $\to$ litre | $\times 1000$ |
| litre $\to$ m³ | $\div 1000$ |

The recipe: write the linear factor, cube it (for m³ $\leftrightarrow$ cm³ $\leftrightarrow$ mm³), and multiply or divide. For capacity, remember the two anchor facts: $1$ cm³ $= 1$ ml and $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres.
