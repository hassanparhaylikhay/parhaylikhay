## Why this matters

Look around you. Your phone screen is around 150 mm tall. Your desk is 1.2 m long. Your school might be 800 m from home. The drive to the next city is 25 km. Same idea, different units.

Cambridge expects you to switch between mm, cm, m, km without thinking. Same for grams and kilograms. The questions are usually short: "Convert 4.5 km to m." But they hide inside bigger problems: a tank's volume given in m³, a sector's arc length given in mm, a speed given in m/s but the distance in km. If the units do not line up, the calculation is wrong even when the formula is right.

In this part:

- The length ladder: mm → cm → m → km.
- The mass ladder: g → kg.
- A reliable rule for whether to multiply or divide.

## The length ladder

Each step on the ladder has a fixed factor.

- $1$ cm $= 10$ mm
- $1$ m $= 100$ cm
- $1$ km $= 1000$ m

A useful way to see it: every step on the ladder is a power of $10$.

- mm → cm: $\times 10$
- cm → m: $\times 100$
- m → km: $\times 1000$

## The mass ladder

Mass on this syllabus uses just two units:

- $1$ kg $= 1000$ g

There is no "milligram" on the 4024 syllabus, so questions stop at g and kg. Easy to remember: $1$ kg of feathers, $1$ kg of bricks, both are $1000$ g.

## Multiply or divide? The DOWN/UP rule

This is the rule that stops the confusion:

- Going **DOWN** the ladder (BIGGER unit → SMALLER unit): the number gets **bigger**, so you **MULTIPLY**.
- Going **UP** the ladder (SMALLER unit → BIGGER unit): the number gets **smaller**, so you **DIVIDE**.

Example: $4.5$ km $\to$ m. You are going DOWN (km is bigger than m). The number must get bigger. Multiply: $4.5 \times 1000 = 4500$ m. The bigger number is right.

Example: $850$ mm $\to$ cm. You are going UP (mm is smaller than cm). The number must get smaller. Divide: $850 \div 10 = 85$ cm. The smaller number is right.

A sanity check after every conversion: does the number look bigger or smaller than the input? If it looks the wrong size, you multiplied where you should have divided.

## Worked example

A delivery van leaves the warehouse and travels $5.2$ km to a junction, then a further $1450$ m to the customer.

(a) Convert $1450$ m to km.

(b) Find the total distance, in km.

**Step a: Convert $1450$ m to km.** Going UP the ladder (m → km), so divide:

$$1450 \div 1000 = \boxed{1.45}\;\text{km}$$

**[M1 for $\div 1000$ or stating $1$ km $= 1000$ m; A1 for $1.45$]**

**Step b: Total distance in km.** Both parts of the journey now in the same unit, so add:

$$5.2 + 1.45 = \boxed{6.65}\;\text{km}$$

**[A1 for $6.65$ with km units]**

Full marks $= 3$.

A small point that earns marks consistently: write **km** at the end. "$6.65$" alone, no unit, can lose you the A1 even though the number is right.

## Marking patterns

For pure conversion questions:

- **B1** for stating the conversion factor (e.g. "$1$ km $= 1000$ m") if the question asks you to show working.
- **M1** for setting up the multiplication or division correctly.
- **A1** for the value, **with the unit**.

When the conversion is part of a bigger problem (volume, area, perimeter), the conversion may not earn a separate mark, but a wrong unit will sink the final A mark for the whole question.

## Common mistakes

- **Multiplying when you should divide, or vice versa.** Always do the DOWN/UP sanity check after the answer: if you converted to a bigger unit and got a bigger number, that is wrong.
- **Forgetting the unit.** A number with no unit is incomplete in Cambridge land. Write "m" or "kg" every time.
- **Mixing units mid-calculation.** If one length is in km and the other in m, convert one of them first, then add or subtract. Never add $5.2$ and $1450$ directly.
- **Using $100$ instead of $1000$ for km → m.** A common slip. The pattern is $\times 10, \times 100, \times 1000$ as you step down, not all $\times 100$.

## Quick summary

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| mm $\to$ cm | $\div 10$ |
| cm $\to$ mm | $\times 10$ |
| cm $\to$ m | $\div 100$ |
| m $\to$ cm | $\times 100$ |
| m $\to$ km | $\div 1000$ |
| km $\to$ m | $\times 1000$ |
| g $\to$ kg | $\div 1000$ |
| kg $\to$ g | $\times 1000$ |

DOWN the ladder (bigger $\to$ smaller unit): MULTIPLY. UP the ladder: DIVIDE. Always include the unit in your answer.
