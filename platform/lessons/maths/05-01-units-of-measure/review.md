## What you should know by now

If you have worked through P1, P2 and P3 you can:

- Convert between mm, cm, m, km using $\times 10, \times 100, \times 1000$.
- Convert between g and kg using $\times 1000$.
- Convert between mm², cm², m², km² using the SQUARE of the linear factor ($\times 100, \times 10\,000, \times 1\,000\,000$).
- Convert between mm³, cm³, m³ using the CUBE of the linear factor.
- Use the capacity link: $1$ cm³ $= 1$ ml and $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres.

## Marking patterns at a glance

In Cambridge 4024, unit-conversion questions reward this structure:

- **B1** for stating the linear conversion (e.g. "$1$ m $= 100$ cm") when "show working" is asked.
- **M1** for setting up the right operation: $\times 10\,000$ for m² $\to$ cm², $\times 1\,000\,000$ for m³ $\to$ cm³, $\times 1000$ for m³ $\to$ litres.
- **A1** for the final value, **with the correct unit**.

When the conversion lives inside a bigger problem (volume of a tank, surface area of a frustum, capacity of a container), the conversion may not earn its own M and A marks, but a wrong unit at the end is the easiest way to drop the final A mark. Always re-read the question to check what unit it asks for.

## Comprehensive worked example

A rectangular swimming pool has length $25$ m, width $12$ m and uniform depth $1.5$ m.

(a) Calculate the volume of water the pool can hold, in m³.

(b) Express this volume in litres.

(c) Water is pumped into the empty pool at a constant rate of $50$ litres per second. Calculate the time, in minutes, to fill the pool.

**Step a: Volume in m³.** The pool is a cuboid, so:

$$V = 25 \times 12 \times 1.5 = \boxed{450}\;\text{m}^3$$

**[M1 for $25 \times 12 \times 1.5$; A1 for $450$]**

**Step b: Express in litres.** Use the link $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres:

$$V = 450 \times 1000 = \boxed{450\,000}\;\text{litres}$$

**[M1 for $\times 1000$; A1 for $450\,000$]**

**Step c: Time to fill, in minutes.** Time $=$ total volume $\div$ rate:

$$t = \dfrac{450\,000}{50} = 9000\;\text{seconds}$$

Convert to minutes ($1$ min $= 60$ s):

$$t = \dfrac{9000}{60} = \boxed{150}\;\text{minutes}$$

**[M1 for total volume / rate; M1 for $\div 60$ to convert seconds to minutes; A1 for $150$ minutes]**

**Answer:** (a) $\boxed{450}$ m³, (b) $\boxed{450\,000}$ litres, (c) $\boxed{150}$ minutes.

Full marks $= 7$.

A typical Paper 2 question rolls many small skills into one. Volume of a cuboid (P3 of this unit), capacity link, and a side conversion (seconds to minutes, which is unit 1.15). One careless slip on a unit costs marks even when the arithmetic is fine.

## Quick reference (memorise these)

The non-negotiable list. If any of these still feel slow, drill them in the trainer until they are automatic.

- $1$ km $= 1000$ m
- $1$ m $= 100$ cm
- $1$ cm $= 10$ mm
- $1$ kg $= 1000$ g
- $1$ m² $= 10\,000$ cm² &nbsp; *(linear $\times 100$, squared)*
- $1$ cm² $= 100$ mm²
- $1$ km² $= 1\,000\,000$ m²
- $1$ m³ $= 1\,000\,000$ cm³ &nbsp; *(linear $\times 100$, cubed)*
- $1$ cm³ $= 1000$ mm³
- $1$ cm³ $= 1$ ml
- $1$ litre $= 1000$ ml $= 1000$ cm³
- $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres

The single biggest unit-conversion habit that earns marks: every time you finish a working line, **check the unit attached to the answer** before moving on.
