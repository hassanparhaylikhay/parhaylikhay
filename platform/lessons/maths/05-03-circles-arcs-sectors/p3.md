## Why this matters

Sector area is the **area** version of the same idea you used for arc length: the sector takes up a fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the full circle, so its area is the same fraction of $\pi r^2$.

$$\boxed{\;A = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2\;}$$

This formula is **NOT** given on the exam paper. Memorise it, or remember the principle ("fraction of the full circle") and rebuild it on demand.

- Reverse problems: given the area, find the angle or the radius.

## Build the formula yourself

Same logic as arc length. The full circle has area $\pi r^2$. A sector with angle $\theta$ at the centre is $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the full circle, so the sector area is:

$$A = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$$

There is no special trick. The arc length formula uses $2\pi r$ (the circumference); the sector area formula uses $\pi r^2$ (the full area). Both wrap a fraction of the full circle.

## Worked example: arc length AND sector area in one go

A sector $OAB$ has radius $9$ cm and the angle $AOB$ is $80°$. Find:

(a) the arc length $AB$,
(b) the area of the sector.

Both in terms of π.

<iframe src="/widgets/sector-step-explorer.html" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:600px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step (a): arc length.**

$$\text{arc} = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r = \dfrac{80}{360} \times 2\pi \times 9 = \dfrac{2}{9} \times 18\pi = \boxed{4\pi}\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for the formula; A1 for $4\pi$]**

**Step (b): sector area.**

$$A = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2 = \dfrac{80}{360} \times \pi \times 81 = \dfrac{2}{9} \times 81\pi = \boxed{18\pi}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for the formula; A1 for $18\pi$]**

Full marks $= 4$.

This pair of formulas (arc and area) is one of the highest-value little memory items in the syllabus. A typical question gives you $r$ and $\theta$ and asks for both in one go.

## Major sectors

A **minor sector** has angle less than $180°$. A **major sector** has angle more than $180°$. Use the angle the question asks about; there's no separate formula.

If a circle has radius $6$ cm and the minor sector has angle $90°$, then:

- Minor sector area: $\frac{90}{360} \times \pi(6)^2 = \frac{1}{4} \times 36\pi = 9\pi$ cm².
- Major sector area: $\frac{270}{360} \times 36\pi = \frac{3}{4} \times 36\pi = 27\pi$ cm².

Sanity check: $9\pi + 27\pi = 36\pi$ cm², which IS the area of the full circle. ✓

## Reverse: find the angle from the area

If you're given the area of the sector and the radius, rearrange:

$$\dfrac{\theta}{360} = \dfrac{A}{\pi r^2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \theta = \dfrac{A \times 360}{\pi r^2}$$

Example: sector area $12\pi$ cm² with radius $6$ cm.

$$\theta = \dfrac{12\pi \times 360}{\pi \times 36} = \dfrac{12 \times 360}{36} = 120°$$

The π cancels and you get a clean integer.

## Common mistakes

- **Using $\theta$ raw instead of $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$.** Same as arc length. The fraction is essential.
- **Using $r$ instead of $r^2$.** Sector area is "fraction of the AREA of the circle", and area uses $r^2$. Don't confuse with arc length, which uses $r$ once.
- **Wrong unit.** Sector area is cm² or m², not cm or m.
- **Picking the wrong angle.** If the question says "the major sector", use the major angle ($360 - \theta$).

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Formula | Mirrors |
|---|---|---|
| Arc length | $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ | a fraction of the circumference |
| Sector area | $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$ | a fraction of the circle's area |

Both are "fraction of the full circle". Remember that one phrase and you can rebuild either formula on the spot.
