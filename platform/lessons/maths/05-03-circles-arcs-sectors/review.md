## What you should know by now

If you've worked through P1 to P3 you can:

- Compute area and circumference of a full circle, in terms of π or as a decimal.
- Spot when a question gives the diameter and remember to halve.
- Compute arc length and sector area as fractions of the full circle ($\frac{\theta}{360}$).
- Tell the difference between minor and major sectors.
- Reverse-engineer the radius or the angle when given the area or arc length.

## Marking patterns at a glance

For an "in terms of π" question:

- **M1** for setting up the formula with the right values.
- **A1 (cao)** for the value with π. Converting to a decimal forfeits the A.

For a decimal answer:

- **M1** for the formula and substitution.
- **A1** for the value rounded to the precision the question asks (1 d.p., 3 sf, etc.) with the correct unit.

For sector questions you usually get **M1 for the fraction** (like $\frac{80}{360}$) and **M1 for multiplying by $2\pi r$** or $\pi r^2$, with the **A1** for the final value.

## Comprehensive worked example

A sector $OAB$ is part of a circle, centre $O$ and radius $r = 12$ cm. The angle $AOB = 75°$.

(a) Calculate the arc length $AB$, in terms of π.

(b) Calculate the area of the sector $OAB$, in terms of π.

(c) The chord $AB$ joins the two endpoints of the arc. Calculate the perimeter of the sector $OAB$ (the two radii plus the arc), giving your answer in terms of π.

**Step a: arc length.**

$$\text{arc} = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r = \dfrac{75}{360} \times 2\pi(12) = \dfrac{75}{360} \times 24\pi$$

$$= \dfrac{75 \times 24}{360}\pi = \dfrac{1800}{360}\pi = \boxed{5\pi}\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for the formula; A1 for $5\pi$]**

**Step b: sector area.**

$$A = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2 = \dfrac{75}{360} \times \pi(12)^2 = \dfrac{75}{360} \times 144\pi$$

$$= \dfrac{75 \times 144}{360}\pi = \dfrac{10800}{360}\pi = \boxed{30\pi}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for the formula; A1 for $30\pi$]**

**Step c: sector perimeter.** Two radii of $12$ cm each, plus the arc:

$$P = 2 \times 12 + 5\pi = \boxed{24 + 5\pi}\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for $2r + $ arc; A1 for $24 + 5\pi$]**

Full marks $= 6$.

A typical Paper 2 question packs all three together. The work flows naturally once the arc length is found: the sector area uses the same fraction $\frac{75}{360}$, and the perimeter uses the arc plus the two radii.

## Quick reference (memorise these)

- $C = 2\pi r$ &nbsp; (or $\pi d$)
- $A = \pi r^2$
- arc $= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$
- sector $A = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$
- minor + major arc $= 2\pi r$ (sanity check)
- minor + major sector area $= \pi r^2$ (sanity check)

The phrase that ties the four together: **"a sector is a fraction of the full circle, and its arc and area are the same fraction of the circle's circumference and area"**. Memorise that and the formulas come back to you on demand.
