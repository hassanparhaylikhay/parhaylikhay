## Why this matters

An arc is just a piece of the circumference of a circle. So instead of using $C = 2\pi r$ for the **full** circle, you use a **fraction** of it.

If a sector takes up an angle $\theta$ at the centre, that sector takes up $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the full circle. So the arc length is:

$$\boxed{\;\text{arc} = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r\;}$$

This formula is **NOT** given on the exam paper. You have to remember it. The good news: once you understand "fraction of the full circle", you can derive it on the spot.

In this part:

- The arc length formula above.
- Minor arc (the shorter one, where $\theta < 180°$) and major arc (the longer one, where $\theta > 180°$).
- Reverse problems: given the arc length, find the angle or the radius.

## Build the formula yourself

Take a sector with radius $r$ and angle $\theta$ at the centre.

- The full circle has circumference $2\pi r$.
- The sector with angle $\theta$ is $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the full circle.
- So the sector's arc is $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of $2\pi r$, which is $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$.

That is the whole derivation. The formula is just "what fraction of the full circle is this sector?".

## Worked example

A sector $OAB$ has radius $9$ cm and the angle $AOB$ is $80°$. Find the arc length $AB$, in terms of π.

**Step 1: write the formula.**

$$\text{arc} = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$$

**Step 2: substitute.**

$$\text{arc} = \dfrac{80}{360} \times 2\pi \times 9$$

**Step 3: simplify.**

$$\text{arc} = \dfrac{2}{9} \times 18\pi = \boxed{4\pi}\;\text{cm}$$

**[M1 for the formula; M1 for substituting correctly; A1 for $4\pi$]**

Full marks $= 3$.

The numbers work out cleanly because $\frac{80}{360}$ simplifies to $\frac{2}{9}$, and $9$ cancels with the $9$ from $r$. Cambridge often picks "nice" angles like $30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, 120°$ that make the fraction reduce.

## Major arcs

If the sector you want is the bigger one (angle $> 180°$ at the centre), use the **major angle** in the formula. There is no separate "major" formula; just use whichever angle the question is asking about.

If a circle has radius $10$ cm and the angle of the minor sector is $120°$, then the major arc has angle $360° - 120° = 240°$:

$$\text{major arc} = \dfrac{240}{360} \times 2\pi(10) = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 20\pi = \dfrac{40\pi}{3}\;\text{cm}$$

A reliable check: the minor arc is $\frac{120}{360} \times 20\pi = \frac{20\pi}{3}$ cm. Adding both arcs: $\frac{40\pi}{3} + \frac{20\pi}{3} = \frac{60\pi}{3} = 20\pi$ cm, which is the full circumference. ✓

## Reverse: find the angle

If the question gives you the arc length and the radius and asks for the angle, rearrange:

$$\dfrac{\theta}{360} = \dfrac{\text{arc}}{2\pi r} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \theta = \dfrac{\text{arc} \times 360}{2\pi r}$$

Example: arc length $6\pi$ cm, radius $9$ cm.

$$\theta = \dfrac{6\pi \times 360}{2\pi \times 9} = \dfrac{2160\pi}{18\pi} = 120°$$

The π cancels, leaving an exact angle.

## Common mistakes

- **Using $\theta$ in the formula instead of $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$.** That gives an arc 360 times too big.
- **Using radians by accident.** This syllabus uses degrees throughout. The formula is for degrees: $\frac{\theta}{360}$ where $\theta$ is in degrees.
- **Picking the wrong arc.** If the question says "the major arc", use the major angle ($360 - \theta$). If it just says "the arc" with a sector picture, pick the side that matches the picture.
- **Wrong unit.** Arc length is a length (cm, m), so it has plain units, not squared.

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Arc length | $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ |
| Major arc | use angle $360 - \theta$ in the same formula |
| Sanity | minor arc + major arc = $2\pi r$ |

Memorise the phrase: **"the arc is the same fraction of the circumference as the angle is of 360°"**. The formula is just that sentence written in symbols.
