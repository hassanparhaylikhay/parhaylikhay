## What you should know by now

After P1 and P2 you can:

- Read a scale (verbal or ratio form) and convert real distances to drawn ones, and vice versa.
- Convert between scale forms ($1$ cm to $5$ m $\equiv 1 : 500$) by matching units.
- Draw to a given scale within $\pm 0.1$ cm tolerance.
- Write bearings as three-digit angles measured clockwise from North.
- Apply the reverse-bearing rule (add or subtract $180°$).
- Match cardinal directions to their bearings ($000°, 090°, 180°, 270°$).
- Combine scale drawings with bearings for navigation-style problems.

## Marking patterns at a glance

For **scale conversions**:

- B$1$ for the correctly converted distance, with units.
- M$1$ if a calculation method is shown but the arithmetic slips.

For **bearings**:

- B$1$ for a bearing answer with **three digits and the degree sign** within tolerance ($\pm 1°$ to $\pm 2°$ when measured from a drawing).
- Cambridge frequently writes the answer in the mark scheme as e.g. "$062$": if you wrote $62°$, the mark may still be awarded, but $62$ alone (no degree sign, two digits) usually loses it.

For **scale drawings combined with bearings**:

- B$1$ for a correct base length (within $\pm 0.1$ cm).
- B$1$ for the correct angle from a North line (within $\pm 1°$).
- M$1$ for an extracted real-world value that uses correctly drawn measurements.
- A$1$ for the final value within tolerance.

## Worked example

Three towns are A, B, and C in a flat region of Punjab. The bearing of B from A is $068°$, and the distance AB is $12$ km. The bearing of C from A is $158°$, and the distance AC is $9$ km.

(a) On a scale drawing using $1$ cm $: 2$ km, work out the drawn lengths AB and AC.

(b) After completing the drawing (which you would do on paper), the distance BC measured on the drawing is $7.5$ cm. Find the real distance BC, in km.

(c) The bearing of B from A is $068°$. State the bearing of A from B.

(d) The angle BAC at vertex A in the scale drawing is the difference of the two bearings. Find this angle and state whether it is acute or obtuse.

**Step 1 (a).** Drawn AB $= 12 / 2 = 6$ cm.

**Step 2 (a).** Drawn AC $= 9 / 2 = 4.5$ cm.

**Step 3 (b).** Real BC $= 7.5 \times 2 = 15$ km.

**Step 4 (c).** Original bearing $068°$ is less than $180°$, so add $180°$.

**Step 5 (c).** Bearing of A from B $= 068° + 180° = 248°$.

**Step 6 (d).** $\angle BAC = 158° - 068° = 90°$. This is a right angle (which is conventionally classified separately from acute and obtuse).

**Answer (a):** AB $= 6$ cm, AC $= 4.5$ cm. **[B1 + B1]**

**Answer (b):** $15$ km. **[B1]**

**Answer (c):** $248°$. **[B1]**

**Answer (d):** $\angle BAC = 90°$, a right angle. **[B1 for value; B1 for classification]**

Full marks $= 6$.

## Common mistakes recap

- **Scale**: forgetting to convert units before dividing or multiplying. Always cm-to-cm, or m-to-m, before applying the scale factor.
- **Scale**: picking your own scale instead of the one given. Mark schemes test against the question's scale.
- **Bearing**: missing the leading zero. $25°$ must be $025°$; $7°$ must be $007°$.
- **Bearing**: anticlockwise. Always clockwise.
- **Bearing**: forgetting the degree sign. The mark scheme expects "$062°$", not "$062$".
- **Reverse bearing**: arithmetic outside $0$ to $360$. Stay in range: add $180$ if original $< 180$, subtract $180$ otherwise.
- **Mixing up "of B from A" with "of A from B"**. Re-read the question.

## Tolerance reminder

| Quantity | Tolerance |
|---|---|
| Drawn length | $\pm 0.1$ cm |
| Drawn / measured angle | $\pm 1°$ (sometimes $\pm 2°$) |
| Bearing on a scale drawing | $\pm 2°$ |
| Real distance computed from drawn | $\pm$ (scale factor $\times 0.1$ cm) |
