## Why this matters

You cannot fit a real cricket ground onto a piece of paper. You shrink it: every metre on the field becomes a few millimetres on paper. That shrunken plan is a **scale drawing**. The same idea makes Google Maps, blueprints for a house in Bahria Town, and the architect's plan for a new school: all are scale drawings.

For Cambridge, scale-drawing questions live mostly on Paper $2$, often combined with bearings (which we cover in P2). The skill is small but precise:

- Read a scale and convert distances correctly between paper and reality.
- Draw to a given scale.
- Tolerate measuring errors of up to $\pm 1$ mm.

Get the unit conversions right and these questions almost mark themselves.

## Two ways to write a scale

Cambridge uses two equivalent forms:

1. **Verbal scale**: "$1$ cm represents $5$ m". Read it as: every $1$ cm on the drawing stands for $5$ m in real life.
2. **Ratio scale**: "$1 : 500$". Both numbers are in the **same** unit. So $1 : 500$ means $1$ cm on the drawing represents $500$ cm in real life (which is $5$ m).

Converting between them is a unit-matching exercise:

- "$1$ cm $: 5$ m" $\to$ change the right side to cm: $5$ m $= 500$ cm. Ratio is $1 : 500$.
- "$1 : 100\,000$" $\to$ both sides in cm. So $1$ cm represents $100\,000$ cm $= 1000$ m $= 1$ km. Verbal: "$1$ cm represents $1$ km".

Cambridge mark schemes accept either form unless the question specifies one.

## The two operations

There are exactly two things you might be asked to do:

**(A) Real distance from drawn distance.** You measure something on the drawing and want the real-world value.

$$\text{real} = \text{drawn} \times \text{scale factor}$$

If the scale is "$1$ cm $: k$ m", multiply the drawn length (in cm) by $k$ to get the real length in metres.

**(B) Drawn distance from real distance.** You know the real value and want to know how big to draw it on paper.

$$\text{drawn} = \dfrac{\text{real}}{\text{scale factor}}$$

If the scale is "$1$ cm $: k$ m", divide the real length (in m) by $k$ to get the drawn length in cm.

If you forget which way to go, ask: *am I shrinking or enlarging?* Going from real to paper, you are **shrinking**, so divide. Going from paper to real, you are **enlarging**, so multiply.

## Worked walk: drawn from real

A field is $80$ m long. You want to draw it on a $1 : 1000$ scale plan. How long should the drawn line be?

Both numbers in the ratio are in cm. So convert the real length to cm: $80$ m $= 8000$ cm.

$$\text{drawn} = \dfrac{8000}{1000} = 8 \text{ cm}$$

So the field is drawn as an $8$ cm line on the plan.

## Worked walk: ratio with mismatched units

A drawing has scale "$2$ cm represents $1$ km". A road on the drawing is $5$ cm long. Find its real length.

Two ways to do this.

**Way 1: scale per cm.** "$2$ cm $: 1$ km" means "$1$ cm $: 0.5$ km" (divide both sides by $2$). So $5$ cm represents $5 \times 0.5 = 2.5$ km.

**Way 2: ratio scaling.** Drawn $5$ cm is $\dfrac{5}{2} = 2.5$ "scale units" of $1$ km each, so $2.5$ km.

Same answer either way. Go with whichever feels more direct.

## When the question gives a ratio without explicit units

A scale of "$1 : 25\,000$" means: $1$ unit on the drawing corresponds to $25\,000$ of the same unit in real life. The unit can be anything (cm, mm, inches), but it must be the **same** on both sides.

So $1$ cm on the drawing $= 25\,000$ cm $= 250$ m $= 0.25$ km in real life.

A drawn distance of $4$ cm represents $4 \times 0.25 = 1$ km.

If you want to draw a real distance of $3$ km on this map: $3$ km $= 300\,000$ cm. Drawn $= 300\,000 / 25\,000 = 12$ cm.

## Drawing to scale: practical tips

1. **Convert units first.** Pick one unit (cm is usual) and put both the scale ratio and the real distance into that unit.
2. **Compute the drawn length on paper.** Round to $0.1$ cm if the answer is messy: Cambridge accepts $\pm 0.1$ cm tolerance.
3. **Use a sharp pencil and a ruler.** Mark both endpoints first, then connect.
4. **Label the drawing with the actual real-world distance** (not the drawn length), since that is the information that matters.

If the question says "use a scale of $1$ cm : $2$ m", it means **use that scale**. Picking your own different scale, even one that fits the page better, will lose marks.

## Tolerance: how close is close enough?

For drawn-from-real construction (you have to draw it):

- Drawn length within $\pm 0.1$ cm of the target.
- Drawn angle (when scale drawing involves bearings) within $\pm 1°$.

For real-from-drawn measurement (you have to measure):

- Cambridge allows the spread that a $\pm 0.1$ cm ruler reading would translate into. So if the scale is $1$ cm $: 5$ m, the real-world tolerance is roughly $\pm 0.5$ m on top of the answer.

The mark scheme will quote a range like "$11.5$ to $12.5$": any value in that range is full marks.

## Worked example

A scale drawing of a rectangular cricket field uses a scale of $1$ cm to $10$ m.

(a) The field is $120$ m long and $80$ m wide. Calculate the dimensions of the rectangle on the drawing.

(b) The drawing shows a flagpole at point $F$, located $4.2$ cm from one corner of the rectangle. Find the real distance of the flagpole from that corner, in metres.

(c) The pavilion is $65$ m from the same corner. How far from the corner should it be drawn, in cm?

**Step 1 (a).** Length on drawing $= \dfrac{120}{10} = 12$ cm.

**Step 2 (a).** Width on drawing $= \dfrac{80}{10} = 8$ cm.

**Step 3 (b).** Real distance $= 4.2 \times 10 = 42$ m.

**Step 4 (c).** Drawn distance $= \dfrac{65}{10} = 6.5$ cm.

**Answer (a):** $12$ cm by $8$ cm. **[B1 for length; B1 for width]**

**Answer (b):** $42$ m. **[B1]**

**Answer (c):** $6.5$ cm. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 4$.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to convert units.** Scale $1 : 50\,000$ on a map: this means cm to cm, NOT cm to km. Convert the real distance into cm before dividing.
- **Multiplying when you should divide** (or vice versa). Real-to-drawn $=$ divide; drawn-to-real $=$ multiply.
- **Reading "$1 : 50\,000$" as "$50\,000$ cm $: 1$ cm".** The first number is the drawing, the second is reality. Always.
- **Picking your own scale** instead of the one the question gives. Even if your scale is more convenient, the question's scale is the one you're marked against.
- **Drawing without first computing the length.** Always compute, mark the endpoints, then draw. Eyeballing on the page costs $1$ to $2$ mm of accuracy and that's enough to miss the tolerance.
- **Ratio with mismatched units.** "$1 : 200$" alone is unitless. "$1$ cm $: 200$ m" requires you to convert before comparing: these are NOT the same scale.

## Quick summary

- Scale tells you how much real distance one unit of paper distance stands for.
- Two forms: ratio "$1 : k$" (same units both sides) and verbal "$1$ cm $: k$ m" (different units).
- **Real from drawn:** multiply by the scale factor.
- **Drawn from real:** divide by the scale factor.
- Always convert to a single unit (usually cm) before computing.
- Cambridge tolerance: $\pm 0.1$ cm on drawn lengths; $\pm 1°$ on drawn angles.
