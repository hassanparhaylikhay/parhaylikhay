You've now seen the three pillars of standard form: writing it, reading it back, and computing with it. Cambridge will mix all three across Paper 1 and Paper 2, often inside a real-world context (planetary distances, particle masses, populations, atom diameters).

## The skills you should have

- **Recognise** correct standard form: $1 \le A \lt 10$ and $n$ an integer.
- **Convert** ordinary $\to$ standard: place decimal after first non-zero digit, count moves, sign by direction.
- **Convert** standard $\to$ ordinary: move decimal $|n|$ places (right for positive, left for negative).
- **Multiply / divide**: combine $A$s, add/subtract indices, then re-normalise if $A$ falls outside $[1,10)$.
- **Add / subtract**: only with the same index, or rewrite one term first.
- **Compare**: bigger index $\to$ bigger number; equal indices $\to$ compare $A$.

## The single biggest mistake

**Leaving $A$ outside the range $[1, 10)$.** Every year students lose accuracy marks for writing $13 \times 10^4$ or $0.6 \times 10^{-2}$ instead of $1.3 \times 10^5$ and $6 \times 10^{-3}$. The numerical value is right, but the form is wrong. Cambridge marks the form, not just the value.
