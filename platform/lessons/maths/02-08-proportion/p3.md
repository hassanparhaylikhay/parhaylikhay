## Why this matters

Cambridge wraps direct and inverse proportion inside word problems on Paper 2. The work itself is small: identify the type, write the equation, find $k$, use it. The challenge is **reading the words carefully** to set up the right form. This part drills the translation step until it's automatic.

## The translation table

The hardest part of any proportion question is converting English to algebra. Here are the standard phrasings and their equations:

| English | Form |
|---|---|
| "$y$ is directly proportional to $x$" | $y = kx$ |
| "$y$ is proportional to $x$ squared" / "to the square of $x$" | $y = kx^2$ |
| "$y$ is proportional to the square root of $x$" | $y = k\sqrt{x}$ |
| "$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$" | $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ |
| "$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$ squared" | $y = \dfrac{k}{x^2}$ |
| "$y$ is proportional to the cube of $x$" | $y = kx^3$ |

The two key signal words: **"directly"** → $kx$; **"inversely"** → $k/x$. Plus any modifier (squared, cubed, square root) tells you what to do to $x$.

## The 4-step recipe (works for every proportion problem)

1. **Read** the words to identify direct/inverse and any power.
2. **Write** the equation with $k$ unknown.
3. **Substitute** the given pair to find $k$.
4. **Use** the equation to find the asked-for value.

The order matters. Find $k$ first, then plug in the new value.

## Walk: a paint problem

The cost $C$ of paint is directly proportional to the area $A$ painted. Painting $20 \text{ m}^2$ costs Rs. $1500$. How much will it cost to paint $35 \text{ m}^2$?

**Step 1: Identify.** Directly proportional → $C = kA$.

**Step 2: Equation written.**

$$C = kA$$

**Step 3: Find $k$ from the known pair.**

$$1500 = k \cdot 20 \;\Rightarrow\; k = 75$$

So the cost-per-square-metre is Rs. $75$.

**Step 4: Use the equation at $A = 35$.**

$$C = 75 \cdot 35 = 2625$$

**Answer:** Rs. $\boxed{2625}$.

## Walk: a pressure-volume problem

The pressure $P$ of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume $V$. At $V = 8$ litres, $P = 3$ atmospheres. Find $P$ at $V = 12$ litres.

**Step 1: Identify.** Inversely proportional → $P = k/V$.

**Step 2: Equation.** $P = \dfrac{k}{V}$.

**Step 3: Find $k$.** $3 = \dfrac{k}{8} \;\Rightarrow\; k = 24$.

**Step 4: At $V = 12$.** $P = \dfrac{24}{12} = 2$ atmospheres.

## Walk: a square-power problem

The kinetic energy $E$ of a moving object is proportional to the square of its speed $v$. When $v = 4$ m/s, $E = 32$ joules. Find $E$ when $v = 10$ m/s.

**Step 1: Identify.** Directly proportional to the SQUARE → $E = kv^2$.

**Step 2: Equation.** $E = kv^2$.

**Step 3: Find $k$.** $32 = k \cdot 16 \;\Rightarrow\; k = 2$.

**Step 4: At $v = 10$.** $E = 2 \cdot 100 = 200$ joules.

## "What happens to $y$ when $x$ doubles?"

A common Cambridge follow-up: not asking for a specific value, but asking how $y$ scales with $x$.

For $y = kx^n$ and $x \to 2x$:

$$y_{\text{new}} = k (2x)^n = 2^n \cdot k x^n = 2^n \cdot y_{\text{old}}$$

| Form | When $x$ doubles, $y$ becomes... |
|---|---|
| $y \propto x$ | $\textcolor{#0fee89}{2 y}$ (doubles) |
| $y \propto x^2$ | $\textcolor{#0fee89}{4 y}$ (quadruples) |
| $y \propto x^3$ | $\textcolor{#0fee89}{8 y}$ (× $8$) |
| $y \propto \sqrt{x}$ | $\textcolor{#0fee89}{\sqrt{2} \cdot y}$ |
| $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}$ | $\textcolor{#ff4670}{\dfrac{y}{2}}$ (halves) |
| $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ | $\textcolor{#ff4670}{\dfrac{y}{4}}$ (quarters) |

The same scaling logic works for any factor: tripling $x$ in $y \propto x^2$ multiplies $y$ by $9$. Halving $x$ in $y \propto 1/x$ doubles $y$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't skip step 3.** Even if the question says "find $y$ when $x$ doubles", a student tempted to "just double $y$" gets the wrong answer for square or cube proportion. Always work via the equation.

</div>

## Worked example

The time $T$ taken for a pendulum to complete one swing is directly proportional to the square root of its length $L$.

A pendulum of length $L = 100$ cm has $T = 2$ seconds.

(a) Express $T$ in terms of $L$.

(b) Find $T$ when $L = 25$ cm.

(c) Find $L$ when $T = 3$ seconds.

**Step 1: Identify.** $T \propto \sqrt{L}$, so $T = k \sqrt{L}$.

**Step 2 (a): Find $k$.** $2 = k \sqrt{100} = 10 k$, so $k = 0.2$.

$$T = 0.2 \sqrt{L}$$

**Step 3 (b): At $L = 25$.** $T = 0.2 \sqrt{25} = 0.2 \cdot 5 = 1$ s.

**Step 4 (c): At $T = 3$.** $3 = 0.2 \sqrt{L}$, so $\sqrt{L} = 15$, giving $L = 225$ cm.

**Answer:** **(a)** $T = 0.2 \sqrt{L}$. **(b)** $T = 1$ s. **(c)** $L = 225$ cm. **[M1 for $k$; A1 for (b); M1 for solving; A1 for (c)]**

Full marks $= 4$.

## Common mistakes

- Plugging the new value in BEFORE finding $k$. Always find $k$ first.
- Reading "proportional to the square of $x$" as $y^2 = kx$ instead of $y = kx^2$.
- Mixing up direct and inverse. The word "inversely" is the giveaway.
- For the "what happens to $y$" questions, doubling everything proportionally instead of applying the power.

## Quick summary

- **The recipe**: read → write the equation → find $k$ → use the equation.
- "Directly proportional" means $kx$. "Inversely proportional" means $k/x$. Powers/roots modify the variable on the right.
- For $y \propto x^n$, doubling $x$ multiplies $y$ by $2^n$.
- For $y \propto 1/x^n$, doubling $x$ divides $y$ by $2^n$.
