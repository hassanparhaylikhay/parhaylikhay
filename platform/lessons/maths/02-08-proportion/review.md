## What you should know by now

If you've worked through P1 to P3 you can:

- Translate "$y$ is proportional to $x$" (and its variants) into the right algebraic equation.
- Find the **constant of proportionality** $k$ from one given pair.
- Use the equation to find any other unknown.
- Handle direct, inverse, and powered/root variants ($x^2$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{x}$, $1/x^2$).
- Predict how $y$ scales when $x$ doubles (or any factor) for any of the forms.
- Recognise direct proportion's straight-line-through-origin graph and inverse proportion's hyperbola.

## Marking patterns at a glance

This sub-topic is heavily tested on Paper 2:

- **B1** for "find the constant of proportionality" given a direct equation.
- **M1 + A1** for "find $y$ when $x = N$" (set up + answer).
- **M1, M1, A1** for a multi-part word problem (write the equation, find $k$, use it).
- **B1** for "what happens to $y$ when $x$ doubles" : apply the power.

The trap markers Cambridge uses repeatedly:

- "Inversely proportional to $x^2$" → $k/x^2$, not $1/(kx^2)$.
- "Proportional to the square of $x$" → $kx^2$, not $\sqrt{kx}$.
- Graphs: a straight line that does NOT pass through the origin is NOT direct proportion ($y = mx + c$ with $c \ne 0$).

## Worked example: a 4-mark Cambridge style

The brightness $B$ of a star is inversely proportional to the square of its distance $d$ from Earth.

A star at distance $d = 5$ light-years has brightness $B = 36$ units.

(a) Find the constant of proportionality.

(b) Find $B$ at $d = 12$ light-years.

(c) At what distance is $B = 4$ units?

**Step 1.** $B = \dfrac{k}{d^2}$.

**Step 2 (a).** $36 = \dfrac{k}{25}$, so $k = 900$.

**Step 3 (b).** $B = \dfrac{900}{144} = 6.25$ units.

**Step 4 (c).** $4 = \dfrac{900}{d^2}$, so $d^2 = 225$ and $d = 15$ light-years.

**Answer:** **(a)** $k = 900$. **(b)** $B = 6.25$. **(c)** $d = 15$. **[M1 for setup; A1 for $k$; A1 for (b); M1 + A1 for (c)]**

Full marks $= 5$.

## Quick reference

- **Direct**: $y = kx^n$. Graph through origin.
- **Inverse**: $y = k/x^n$. Hyperbola, both axes as asymptotes.
- **The recipe**: write equation → find $k$ → use it.
- **Scaling**: doubling $x$ multiplies $y$ by $2^n$ (direct) or divides by $2^n$ (inverse).
