## Why this matters

If $4$ workers paint a fence in $6$ days, then $8$ workers paint the same fence in $3$ days. More workers means less time. The time is **inversely proportional** to the number of workers: when one goes up, the other goes down by the same factor.

Cambridge tests inverse proportion alongside direct proportion on Paper 2. The mechanics are identical to direct, with one symbol change: $\propto \dfrac{1}{x}$ instead of $\propto x$.

## What inverse proportion means

Two quantities are **inversely proportional** if one is always a constant divided by the other:

$$y \;\propto\; \dfrac{1}{x} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y = \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{k}}{x}$$

Equivalently, $y \cdot x = k$. The **product** of the two quantities is constant : at every pair, $y$ times $x$ gives the same $k$.

If $x$ doubles, $y$ halves. If $x$ triples, $y$ is divided by $3$. If $x$ halves, $y$ doubles.

## The recipe (same as direct)

1. **Translate the proportion statement** into an equation with $k$ as unknown.
2. **Substitute the given pair** to find $k$.
3. **Use the equation** to find any other unknown.

**Example.** $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}$. When $x = 4$, $y = 5$. Find $y$ when $x = 10$.

**Step 1.** $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$.

**Step 2.** Substitute $(x, y) = (4, 5)$: $5 = \dfrac{k}{4}$, so $k = 20$.

**Step 3.** $y = \dfrac{20}{x}$. At $x = 10$: $y = \dfrac{20}{10} = 2$.

## Variants: $1/x^2$, $1/\sqrt{x}$, etc.

Like direct proportion, inverse proportion has variants with powers and roots:

| Statement | Equation |
|---|---|
| $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}$ | $y = \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{k}}{x}$ |
| $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ | $y = \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{k}}{x^2}$ |
| $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x^3}$ | $y = \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{k}}{x^3}$ |
| $y \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ | $y = \dfrac{\textcolor{#fff067}{k}}{\sqrt{x}}$ |

**Example.** $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x^2}$. When $x = 2$, $y = 9$. Find $y$ when $x = 6$.

**Step 1.** $y = \dfrac{k}{x^2}$.

**Step 2.** $9 = \dfrac{k}{4}$, so $k = 36$.

**Step 3.** $y = \dfrac{36}{x^2}$. At $x = 6$: $y = \dfrac{36}{36} = 1$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: read inverse proportion carefully.** "$y$ is inversely proportional to $x^2$" means $y = \dfrac{k}{x^2}$, NOT $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ or $y \cdot x^2 = k$ written upside-down. Always set up $y = \dfrac{k}{(\text{something with } x)}$.

</div>

## Going both ways

Once you have the equation $y = k/x$, you can solve for either variable:

- **Find $y$ from $x$**: substitute $x$.
- **Find $x$ from $y$**: rearrange to $x = k/y$ (or solve the equation for $x$).

**Example.** $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}$. When $x = 3$, $y = 8$. Find $x$ when $y = 2$.

**Step 1.** $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$. From $(3, 8)$: $k = 8 \cdot 3 = 24$.

**Step 2.** $\dfrac{24}{x} = 2$, so $x = 12$.

## Worked example

The intensity $I$ of light from a torch is inversely proportional to the square of the distance $d$ from it. At $d = 2$ m, $I = 25$ lux.

(a) Find the constant of proportionality.

(b) Find $I$ at $d = 5$ m.

**Step 1: Equation.** $I = \dfrac{k}{d^2}$.

**Step 2 (a): Substitute.** $25 = \dfrac{k}{4}$, so $k = 100$.

**Step 3 (b): Find $I$ at $d = 5$.**

$$I = \dfrac{100}{25} = 4 \text{ lux}$$

**Answer:** **(a)** $\boxed{k = 100}$. **(b)** $\boxed{I = 4 \text{ lux}}$. **[M1 for setting up; M1 for $k$; A1 for the value]**

Full marks $= 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing inverse with direct. "Inversely proportional" means $\dfrac{k}{x}$, not $-x$ or $-kx$.
- Forgetting the squaring/rooting in the variant forms. $y \propto 1/x^2$ has $x^2$ in the denominator, not $x$.
- Sign confusion: as $x$ INCREASES, inversely-proportional $y$ DECREASES.
- Mixing up "find $y$" with "find $x$". Read the question.

## Quick summary

- **Inverse proportion**: $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}$ means $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ for some constant $k$.
- Equivalently, $xy = k$ (the product is constant).
- Variants: $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, $\dfrac{1}{x^3}$, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$, etc. Same recipe.
- The graph is a hyperbola; it has the $x$-axis and $y$-axis as asymptotes.
