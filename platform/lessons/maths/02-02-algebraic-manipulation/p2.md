## Why this matters

Imagine your group of $4$ friends each orders $1$ burger and $2$ fries. The total is $4 \times (1 \text{ burger} + 2 \text{ fries})$. You instinctively distribute the $4$: $4$ burgers $+$ $8$ fries. That distributing-of-multiplication-over-addition is exactly **expanding a bracket** in algebra. Cambridge expects you to do it cleanly across single, double, and triple brackets, and to handle every sign correctly.

Expanding shows up in almost every algebra question, often as a small step inside a longer problem (solving an equation, simplifying a fraction, finding the area of a rectangle). Get the patterns automatic and the rest of the topic eases up.

The same rule with three terms:

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{a}(b + c + d) \;=\; \textcolor{#fff067}{a}b + \textcolor{#fff067}{a}c + \textcolor{#fff067}{a}d$$

The multiplier can be a number, a letter, or a combination (like $-3x$). It still distributes over every term inside.

**Examples.**

- $3(2x + 5) = 6x + 15$
- $5(x - 4) = 5x - 20$
- $x(x + 7) = x^2 + 7x$
- $2x(3x - 1) = 6x^2 - 2x$

## A negative multiplier flips signs

When the multiplier is negative, **every term inside flips sign**.

$$-(x - 4) \;=\; -x + 4$$

$$-2(x - 5) \;=\; -2x + 10$$

The minus is a $-1$ being distributed through, so each term gets multiplied by $-1$ in addition to whatever number is there. A minus times a minus gives a plus.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't lose the second sign.** $-2(x - 4)$ becomes $-2x + 8$, NOT $-2x - 8$. The $-2$ multiplies the $-4$ to give $+8$. Whenever a negative multiplier meets a minus inside, write the result with brackets first ($(-2)(-4)$) and then evaluate.

</div>

A useful mnemonic some teachers use is **FOIL** (First, Outer, Inner, Last). The four pairs are:

- **F**irst: $\textcolor{#fff067}{a} \cdot \textcolor{#00abfa}{c} = \textcolor{#fff067}{ac}$
- **O**uter: $\textcolor{#fff067}{a} \cdot \textcolor{#ff822c}{d} = \textcolor{#fff067}{ad}$
- **I**nner: $\textcolor{#0fee89}{b} \cdot \textcolor{#00abfa}{c} = \textcolor{#0fee89}{bc}$
- **L**ast: $\textcolor{#0fee89}{b} \cdot \textcolor{#ff822c}{d} = \textcolor{#0fee89}{bd}$

After all four, **combine any like terms** to write the expansion in simplest form.

**Example.** Expand $(\textcolor{#fff067}{x} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{3})(\textcolor{#00abfa}{x} + \textcolor{#ff822c}{2})$.

$$\textcolor{#fff067}{x} \cdot \textcolor{#00abfa}{x} + \textcolor{#fff067}{x} \cdot \textcolor{#ff822c}{2} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{3} \cdot \textcolor{#00abfa}{x} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{3} \cdot \textcolor{#ff822c}{2}$$

$$= \;x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 \;=\; x^2 + 5x + 6$$

The two middle terms ($2x$ and $3x$) are like terms; they combine to $5x$.

## Triple brackets: expand two, then multiply by the third

For $(p)(q)(r)$, expand any two of the three brackets first to get an expression, then multiply that result by the remaining bracket. The order doesn't change the answer.

**Example.** Expand $(x + 1)(x + 2)(x + 3)$.

**Step 1: Expand the first two.**

$$(x + 1)(x + 2) \;=\; x^2 + 3x + 2$$

**Step 2: Multiply that by the third bracket.**

$$(x^2 + 3x + 2)(x + 3)$$

Each term in the first bracket meets each term in the second. Six products this time:

$$x^2 \cdot x + x^2 \cdot 3 + 3x \cdot x + 3x \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot x + 2 \cdot 3$$

$$= \;x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x^2 + 9x + 2x + 6$$

**Step 3: Combine like terms.**

$$x^3 + 6x^2 + 11x + 6$$

Triple-bracket questions are worth 2 to 3 marks: M for the first expansion, M for the second, A for the simplified answer.

## Worked example

Expand and simplify $(2x - 1)(x + 4)$.

**Step 1: Multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second.**

$$2x \cdot x + 2x \cdot 4 + (-1) \cdot x + (-1) \cdot 4$$

$$= \;2x^2 + 8x - x - 4$$

**Step 2: Combine like terms.**

$$2x^2 + 7x - 4$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{2x^2 + 7x - 4}$. **[M1 for full expansion; A1 for the simplified form]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting one of the four products in a double bracket. $(a+b)(c+d)$ has FOUR products, not three.
- Sign errors with negative multipliers. $-(x - 5) = -x + 5$, NOT $-x - 5$.
- Treating $(x + 3)^2$ as $x^2 + 9$. It's actually $(x+3)(x+3) = x^2 + 6x + 9$. The middle terms matter.
- Adding instead of multiplying powers. $x \cdot x = x^2$, not $2x$.

## Quick summary

- Single bracket: the multiplier hits **every** term inside.
- Double bracket: each term in the first meets each in the second (FOIL gives four products).
- Negative multiplier: every term inside flips sign.
- Triple bracket: expand any two first, then multiply by the third.
- Always **combine like terms** at the end.
