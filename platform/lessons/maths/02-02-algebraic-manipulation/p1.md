## Why this matters

Imagine you have $3$ mangoes and $2$ apples, then a friend gives you $5$ more mangoes and $1$ more apple. You'd say you have $8$ mangoes and $3$ apples. You wouldn't add mangoes and apples together to get "$11$ fruits". They're different categories, you keep them separate. Algebra works exactly the same way: you can only combine things of the same "category", which we call **like terms**.

We met like terms briefly in 2.1. This part nails the rule when expressions get longer, when there are multiple letters or different powers, and when negatives sneak in. Cambridge will hand you a six-term expression on Paper 1 and ask you to simplify it; the marks are easy if you don't trip over signs.

## Same letter, same power

A reminder from 2.1: two terms are **like** if and only if their letter parts are identical, including all powers.

| Term A | Term B | Like? |
|---|---|---|
| $4x$ | $-9x$ | yes |
| $2x^2$ | $5x^2$ | yes |
| $3xy$ | $-7xy$ | yes |
| $2x$ | $2x^2$ | no (different powers) |
| $2xy$ | $2x^2y$ | no (extra $x$) |
| $5ab$ | $5ba$ | yes ($ab = ba$) |
| $x$ | $7$ | no (one has a letter, the other does not) |

The order of letters doesn't matter ($ab = ba$), but the powers and the set of letters must match exactly.

## Combining like terms

When two like terms add or subtract, **add or subtract the coefficients**. The letter part stays put.

$$5a + 3a \;=\; (5+3)a \;=\; 8a$$

$$7x^2 - 2x^2 \;=\; 5x^2$$

$$4ab - 9ab \;=\; -5ab$$

If you have a long expression, group the like terms first, then combine each group.

## Watching the signs

The minus sign **belongs to the term it sits in front of**. Always treat it as part of that term's coefficient.

$$4x - 7x \;=\; (4 - 7)x \;=\; -3x$$

$$-2x - 3x \;=\; (-2 - 3)x \;=\; -5x$$

$$-x + 5x \;=\; (-1 + 5)x \;=\; 4x$$

The bare letter $x$ has coefficient $1$ (or $-1$ if it sits behind a minus). Don't drop that mental $1$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: signs travel with their term.** In $7a - 3b + 2a$, the $-3b$ stays as $-3b$. When you re-order to group, write $7a + 2a - 3b$, NOT $7a + 2a + 3b$. Losing the minus is a classic exam-day mistake.

</div>

## Multiple powers and multiple letters

Once expressions involve $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, $xy$, $x^2y$, etc., grouping takes more care. Each power and each letter combination is its own pile.

**Example.** Simplify $7x^2 + 3x - 2x^2 + x$.

- $x^2$-pile: $7x^2 - 2x^2 = 5x^2$.
- $x$-pile: $3x + x = 4x$ (remember that bare $x$ has coefficient $1$).

Combined: $\boxed{5x^2 + 4x}$. The two piles do not merge; $5x^2 + 4x$ is the simplest form.

**Example.** Simplify $2xy - 4x + 5xy + x$.

- $xy$-pile: $2xy + 5xy = 7xy$.
- $x$-pile: $-4x + x = -3x$.

Combined: $7xy - 3x$.

## Worked example

Simplify $a^2 - 3a + 2a^2 + 5a - 1$.

**Step 1: Identify the piles.** $a^2$-terms, $a$-terms, and a constant.

**Step 2: Combine each pile.**

- $a^2$-pile: $a^2 + 2a^2 = 3a^2$.
- $a$-pile: $-3a + 5a = 2a$.
- Constant: $-1$.

**Step 3: Write the simplified form, conventionally in descending order of powers.**

**Answer:** $\boxed{3a^2 + 2a - 1}$. **[B1]**

Full marks $= 1$.

## Common mistakes

- Combining unlike terms. $2x + 3y$ does NOT simplify; do not write $5xy$ or $5$.
- Treating $x^2$ and $x$ as the same pile. They are different powers, so they are different terms.
- Dropping a minus sign when re-ordering. The sign belongs to the term it precedes.
- Forgetting the implicit coefficient of $1$. $x + 3x = 4x$, not $3x$.

## Quick summary

- Like terms have **identical letter parts** including all powers.
- Combine like terms by adding or subtracting the **coefficients**; the letter part stays.
- The minus sign in front of a term is part of that term's coefficient.
- Different powers ($x$ vs $x^2$) and different letter combinations ($xy$ vs $xy^2$) cannot merge.
