## Why this matters

If a samosa costs $p$ rupees, two samosas cost $2p$. Once you know the actual price, say $p = 50$, two samosas cost $100$. That swap from "letter" back to "number" is **substitution**, and it's the bread-and-butter of every algebra question Cambridge sets.

You'll meet substitution in two flavours: into a plain expression like $3x + 5$ (this part), and into a formula like $v = u + at$ (next part). The mechanics are the same; only the layout differs.

## The substitution idea

To **substitute** means to replace each letter with a given number, then evaluate.

Example: evaluate $3x + 5$ when $x = 4$.

Notice the brackets in step 2: $3(4) = 3 \times 4 = 12$. **Always wrap a substituted value in brackets**, even when the value is positive. It saves you from sign mistakes when negatives appear.

## Watch the order of operations

After substituting, you have a plain arithmetic expression. Apply BIDMAS/BODMAS in the usual order:

1. **B**rackets
2. **I**ndices (powers and roots)
3. **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication (left to right)
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right)

So evaluate powers before adding, multiplications before subtractions, etc.

**Example.** $a^2 + 2b$ with $a = 2$ and $b = 5$.

$$a^2 + 2b \;=\; (2)^2 + 2(5) \;=\; 4 + 10 \;=\; 14$$

Indices first ($2^2 = 4$), then multiply ($2 \times 5 = 10$), then add.

## Negative substitutions: brackets save you

When the value is negative, brackets are **non-negotiable**. Without them you'll lose marks.

**Example.** Evaluate $x^2$ when $x = -3$.

- With brackets: $x^2 = (-3)^2 = (-3) \times (-3) = +9$. ✓
- Without brackets: $-3^2 = -(3^2) = -9$. ✗ Wrong.

The minus sign in $-3^2$ doesn't belong to the $3$ unless you wrap it: $(-3)^2$. BIDMAS applies the index first, so $-3^2$ means "the negative of $3^2$" which is $-9$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: $(-3)^2 = 9$, but $-3^2 = -9$.** When substituting a negative value, always write it inside brackets first. This single habit prevents most sign errors in Cambridge questions.

</div>

## Multiple letters

If the expression has two or three letters, substitute each one **independently**, then combine.

**Example.** $5p - 4q$ with $p = 3$ and $q = -2$.

$$5p - 4q \;=\; 5(3) - 4(-2) \;=\; 15 - (-8) \;=\; 15 + 8 \;=\; 23$$

The two minus signs in $-(-8)$ flip to a plus.

| Subbing in $-2$ | Result |
|---|---|
| $4q = 4(-2)$ | $-8$ |
| $-4q = -4(-2)$ | $+8$ |
| $q^2 = (-2)^2$ | $+4$ |
| $-q^2 = -(-2)^2$ | $-4$ |

Whenever you swap a value in, write the substituted form fully (with brackets) before you simplify.

## Worked example

Find the value of $\dfrac{n^2 - 4}{n + 2}$ when $n = 4$.

**Step 1: Substitute $n = 4$ into the top.**

$$n^2 - 4 \;=\; (4)^2 - 4 \;=\; 16 - 4 \;=\; 12$$

**Step 2: Substitute $n = 4$ into the bottom.**

$$n + 2 \;=\; 4 + 2 \;=\; 6$$

**Step 3: Divide.**

$$\dfrac{12}{6} \;=\; 2$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{2}$. **[M1 for substituting; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting brackets around negatives. $-3^2 = -9$ but $(-3)^2 = 9$. Use brackets always.
- Multiplying instead of evaluating powers. $a^2$ with $a = 5$ is $25$, NOT $2 \times 5 = 10$.
- Mishandling subtraction of a negative. $15 - (-8) = 15 + 8 = 23$, NOT $7$.
- Combining values across different letters. If $a = 2$ and $b = 5$, then $ab = 10$, not $25$ (don't write them next to each other as a two-digit number).

## Quick summary

- To substitute, replace each letter with its given number, then evaluate the arithmetic.
- Always put a substituted value in **brackets**, especially negatives.
- Apply **BIDMAS**: indices before multiplications before additions.
- $(-x)^2$ is positive; $-x^2$ is negative. Brackets matter.
