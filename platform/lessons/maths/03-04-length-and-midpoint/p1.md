## Why this matters

Coordinate geometry often asks "how long is the segment from $A$ to $B$?" - for example to verify that a triangle is isosceles, or that a quadrilateral is a square. The **distance formula** is just Pythagoras' theorem applied to the differences in coordinates.

## Where the formula comes from

Suppose $A = (x_1, y_1)$ and $B = (x_2, y_2)$. Drop a horizontal line from $A$ and a vertical line from $B$; they meet at the corner $C = (x_2, y_1)$. Now $ABC$ is a **right triangle**:

- Horizontal leg $AC$ has length $|x_2 - x_1|$.
- Vertical leg $BC$ has length $|y_2 - y_1|$.
- The hypotenuse is the segment $AB$.

By Pythagoras:

$$AB^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2$$

Take the square root:

$$\boxed{AB \;=\; \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}}$$

That's the distance formula. Note: because we square the differences, you don't need absolute values - the sign disappears when you square.

## Worked example: clean answer (Pythagorean triple)

Find the distance from $A(1, 2)$ to $B(7, 10)$.

**Step 1: differences.** $\Delta x = 7 - 1 = 6$, $\Delta y = 10 - 2 = 8$.

**Step 2: squares.** $6^2 = 36$, $8^2 = 64$.

**Step 3: sum and square root.**

$$AB = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

**Answer:** $AB = 10$. **[M1 substitute; A1 simplify]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Worked example: surd answer (no clean root)

Find the distance from $P(-1, 1)$ to $Q(3, 4)$.

**Step 1:** $\Delta x = 3 - (-1) = 4$, $\Delta y = 4 - 1 = 3$.

**Step 2:** $4^2 = 16$, $3^2 = 9$.

**Step 3:** $\sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

**Answer:** $PQ = 5$. **[M1 substitute; A1 simplify]**

Full marks $= 2$.

(If the sum under the root isn't a perfect square, leave the answer in surd form like $\sqrt{29}$ - Cambridge accepts that.)

## Common mistakes

- **Adding instead of summing squares.** $\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2}$, NOT $\sqrt{\Delta x + \Delta y}$.
- **Forgetting brackets around negatives.** $(-1 - 3)^2 = (-4)^2 = 16$. Without brackets: $-1^2 = -1$ (wrong sign).
- **Taking $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ as $a + b$.** Only true when one of $a, b$ is zero. In general $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, not $3 + 4 = 7$.
- **Forgetting to square root.** You compute $(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2$ and forget the final $\sqrt{}$.

## Quick summary

- Distance formula: $AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.
- It's just Pythagoras applied to the right triangle made by $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$.
- Bracket negative coordinates when subtracting.
- Leave non-perfect roots in surd form.
