## What you should know by now

If you've worked through P1 to P2 you can:

- Use Pythagoras' theorem in the form of the **distance formula**: $AB = \sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2}$.
- Compute distances and leave them as integers (when from a Pythagorean triple) or as surds.
- Use the **midpoint formula**: average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates.
- Reverse the midpoint formula to find a missing endpoint.

## Marking patterns at a glance

For "find the length":

- **M1** for substituting both differences correctly into the formula.
- **A1** for the correct value (or surd form).

For "find the midpoint":

- **M1** for forming the average expressions.
- **A1** for the final coordinate.

## Worked example: combining length and midpoint

$A(-3, 1)$ and $B(5, 7)$.

(a) Find the length of $AB$.

(b) Find the midpoint of $AB$.

**Step 1 (a, differences).** $\Delta x = 5 - (-3) = 8$, $\Delta y = 7 - 1 = 6$.

**Step 2 (a, distance).**

$$AB = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

**Step 3 (b, average x).**

$$\dfrac{-3 + 5}{2} = 1$$

**Step 4 (b, average y).**

$$\dfrac{1 + 7}{2} = 4$$

**Answer (a):** $AB = 10$. **[M1 substitute; A1 simplify]**

**Answer (b):** Midpoint is $(1, 4)$. **[M1 substitute; A1 simplify]**

Full marks $= 4$.

## Quick reference

- Length: $AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.
- Midpoint: $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$.
- Distance uses **subtraction** then squaring; midpoint uses **addition** then halving.
