## Why this matters

A **cylinder** is a prism with a circular cross-section. Cans, pipes, water tanks: all cylinders. Cambridge tests them in volume, surface area, and rate-of-fill problems.

Two formulas come straight off the formula sheet:

$$V = \pi r^2 h \qquad \text{curved SA} = 2\pi r h$$

But for **total** surface area on a closed cylinder, you also need the two end-circles, $2\pi r^2$ each, which you add yourself.

In this part:

- Volume $= \pi r^2 h$ (a prism with circular cross-section).
- Curved SA $= 2\pi r h$ (unroll the side into a rectangle).
- Total SA $= 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$.
- Open vs closed cylinders, and reverse problems.

## Build curved SA yourself

Take a cylinder and cut it along its length, then unroll the curved face. You get a rectangle:

- One side has length $2\pi r$ (the circumference of the base).
- The other side has length $h$ (the height of the cylinder).

So:

$$\text{curved SA} = 2\pi r \times h = \boxed{2\pi r h}$$

Full top and bottom give two more circles:

$$\text{total SA} = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$$

The factor of $2$ in $2\pi r^2$ counts both end-circles, not the same circle twice.

## Worked example: in terms of π

A cylinder has radius $4$ cm and height $9$ cm. Find its volume and total surface area, in terms of π.

**Volume.**

$$V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 4^2 \times 9 = \pi \times 16 \times 9 = \boxed{144\pi}\;\text{cm}^3$$

**[M1 for $\pi r^2 h$ with substitution; A1 for $144\pi$]**

**Total surface area.**

$$\text{curved} = 2\pi r h = 2\pi \times 4 \times 9 = 72\pi\;\text{cm}^2$$

$$\text{ends} = 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi \times 16 = 32\pi\;\text{cm}^2$$

$$\text{total} = 72\pi + 32\pi = \boxed{104\pi}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for $2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$; A1 for $104\pi$]**

Full marks $= 4$.

A common slip on total SA: students add only ONE end-circle (forgetting one end), getting $72\pi + 16\pi = 88\pi$. Always two ends on a closed cylinder.

## Open cylinders

Some questions describe an "open" cylinder, like a glass with no lid, or a pipe with no caps.

- **Open at one end** (a glass): curved SA $+$ ONE end-circle $= 2\pi r h + \pi r^2$.
- **Open at both ends** (a pipe): just curved SA $= 2\pi r h$.

Read the question carefully. "A cylindrical tank with no lid" means open at the top, so leave one $\pi r^2$ off.

## Worked example: decimal answer

A cylindrical can has diameter $8$ cm and height $12$ cm. Find its volume to $1$ decimal place.

**Step 1: find the radius.** $r = d \div 2 = 4$ cm.

**Step 2: substitute.**

$$V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 4^2 \times 12 = 192\pi\;\text{cm}^3$$

**Step 3: convert to decimal.**

$$192\pi = 192 \times 3.14159\ldots = 603.18\ldots \approx \boxed{603.2}\;\text{cm}^3$$

**[M1 for $\pi \times 4^2 \times 12$; A1 for $603.2$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

The $\pi$ is left in until the very last step, then a calculator decimal gives the rounded answer. **Don't round $\pi$ to $3.14$ early**, or you can lose accuracy.

## Reverse problems

If you are given volume $V$ and one of $r$ or $h$, rearrange.

From $V = \pi r^2 h$:

- given $V$ and $r$: $h = \dfrac{V}{\pi r^2}$.
- given $V$ and $h$: $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{V}{\pi h}}$.

Cambridge often phrases this as "the cylinder has volume $V$ and the same height as another solid, find the radius".

## Composite: cylinder with a cone or hemisphere on top

These are common in p6 and review. The cylinder contributes:

- Volume $\pi r^2 h$.
- Curved SA $2\pi r h$.
- Just the bottom circle $\pi r^2$ (the top is covered by the next solid).

The "join" between two solids cancels: you don't see the inside, so you don't add it. We will set this up properly in **5.4 p6**.

## Common mistakes

- **Diameter instead of radius.** If the question says "diameter $14$", use $r = 7$. Squaring the diameter is the most expensive slip on a cylinder question.
- **Missing one or both end-circles.** "Total surface area" of a closed cylinder always has two circles, not one and not zero.
- **Wrong units.** Volume is cm³ or m³. Surface area is cm² or m². The π carries through when "in terms of π" is asked.
- **Rounding π too early.** Leave $\pi$ symbolic, do the algebra, then convert at the end.

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Formula | Given on paper? |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | $V = \pi r^2 h$ | Yes (via prism formula) |
| Curved SA | $2\pi r h$ | Yes |
| Two end-circles | $2 \pi r^2$ | No (add yourself) |
| Total SA (closed) | $2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$ | Curved part given; total you build |
| Open at one end | $2\pi r h + \pi r^2$ | Pick one end-circle |

The mental picture: a cylinder is a circle "pulled up" into a third dimension. Volume = circle area × height. Curved SA = circle perimeter × height.
