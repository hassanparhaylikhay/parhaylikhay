## Why this matters

A cuboid (a box) is the most common 3D shape in Cambridge questions. You will see it in volume calculations, surface area calculations, fill rate problems, and packaging questions.

The two things to lock in:

1. **Volume** $= l \times w \times h$. Length cubed (cm³, m³). The answer counts how many unit cubes fit inside.
2. **Surface area** $= 2(lw + wh + lh)$. Length squared (cm², m²). The total of all six rectangular faces.

The cuboid's volume is covered by the prism formula on the sheet. Surface area is **not given**. Understand the structure (three pairs of identical faces) and you can rebuild it on demand.

In this part:

- $V = lwh$ for a cuboid; $V = s^3$ for a cube.
- SA $= 2(lw + wh + lh)$ for a cuboid; SA $= 6s^2$ for a cube.
- Reverse problems and fill rate.

## Build the surface area formula yourself

A cuboid has six rectangular faces in three matching pairs:

- top and bottom: each $l \times w$
- front and back: each $l \times h$
- left and right: each $w \times h$

Sum:

$$\text{SA} = 2(lw) + 2(lh) + 2(wh) = \boxed{2(lw + lh + wh)}$$

There is no shortcut. Pair them up by face, then add. Don't forget any face: a common mistake on Paper 2 is missing the bottom face or one of the side pairs.

## Worked example: full cuboid

A cuboid measures $3$ cm by $5.2$ cm by $4.7$ cm. Find the surface area.

**Step 1: list the three pairs.**

$$lw = 3 \times 5.2 = 15.6 \quad lh = 3 \times 4.7 = 14.1 \quad wh = 5.2 \times 4.7 = 24.44$$

**Step 2: double and add.**

$$\text{SA} = 2(15.6 + 14.1 + 24.44) = 2 \times 54.14 = \boxed{108.28}\;\text{cm}^2$$

**[M1 for $2(3 \times 5.2 + 4.7 \times 5.2 + 3 \times 4.7)$ or equivalent; A1 for $108.28$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

This is **w25_21 Q3(a)** almost word for word. The M1 is awarded for showing the structure (three pairs); the A1 is for the final number with units.

## Worked example: fill rate

A cuboid tank has length $1.2$ m, width $0.6$ m, and unknown height $h$ m. The volume of the tank is $1.8$ m³. Calculate $h$.

**Step 1: write the formula.**

$$V = l \times w \times h$$

**Step 2: substitute and solve.**

$$1.2 \times 0.6 \times h = 1.8$$

$$0.72 h = 1.8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad h = \dfrac{1.8}{0.72} = \boxed{2.5}\;\text{m}$$

**[M1 for $1.2 \times 0.6 \times h = 1.8$; A1 for $h = 2.5$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

This is **w24_22 Q3(a)(i)**. Notice the units stay metres throughout because the volume is given in m³.

## Worked example: fill rate with seconds

A cuboid tank has dimensions $10$ cm $\times$ $8$ cm $\times$ $9$ cm. Water flows in at $6$ ml/s. How long does it take to fill?

Use $1$ ml $= 1$ cm³.

**Step 1: volume.**

$$V = 10 \times 8 \times 9 = 720\;\text{cm}^3 = 720\;\text{ml}$$

**[M1 for $10 \times 8 \times 9$]**

**Step 2: divide by rate.**

$$t = \dfrac{V}{\text{rate}} = \dfrac{720}{6} = \boxed{120}\;\text{seconds}$$

**[M1 for $\dfrac{720}{6}$; A1 for $120$]**

Full marks $= 3$. This is **w25_11 Q12**.

A clean unit chain: cm³ converts to ml directly (the link from 5.1), then divide by the per-second rate to get seconds.

## Reverse problems

Given two of (volume, length, width, height), the fourth is just division.

If volume is $98$ cm³ and the base is a square of side $s$ with height $8$ cm, then:

$$8 s^2 = 98 \quad\Rightarrow\quad s^2 = 12.25 \quad\Rightarrow\quad s = 3.5\;\text{cm}$$

This appears in **w23_21 Q9(b)**, where the next step is to find the diagonal $PQ$ across the cuboid using 3D Pythagoras (covered in 6.4).

## Common mistakes

- **Missing a face on surface area.** Pair the faces (top-bottom, front-back, left-right). Six faces total. If you only get four, you have forgotten the bottom or the back.
- **Mixing up units.** Volume must end in cm³ or m³ (length cubed). Surface area in cm² or m². If your final number is right but the unit is squared instead of cubed, the A mark goes.
- **Doubling the wrong thing.** SA $= 2(lw + lh + wh)$, not $2lwh$ or $(2l)(2w)(2h)$. The structure is "three pairs of faces, each pair has two equal rectangles".

## Quick summary

| Quantity | Cuboid | Cube |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | $V = l \times w \times h$ | $V = s^3$ |
| Surface area | $2(lw + lh + wh)$ | $6 s^2$ |
| Unit | cm³, m³ for volume; cm², m² for area | same |
| On formula sheet? | Volume yes (via prism formula); SA no | same |

The four-step habit on every cuboid question: write the formula, substitute the three numbers, calculate, write the unit. Doing all four earns both marks.
