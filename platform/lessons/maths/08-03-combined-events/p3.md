## Why this matters

This is the most common "long" probability question on the paper. A bag of counters, drawn without replacement, asked to find $P(\text{both same colour})$ or $P(\text{at least one red})$. Worth $3$ to $4$ marks, sometimes $5$.

The single key difference from p2: **the second draw uses a smaller bag**. When you draw a counter and do not put it back, both the count of that colour AND the total go down by $1$.

This makes the events DEPENDENT: the result of the first draw changes the probabilities for the second draw. The tree diagram still works, but the second-stage branch labels DIFFER depending on which first-stage branch you came from.

## The denominator drops, the numerator may drop too

Suppose a bag has $8$ red and $7$ blue counters. Total $= 15$.

First draw: $P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{8}{15}$. 

If you drew red and did NOT replace, the bag now holds $7$ red and $7$ blue. Total $= 14$.

Second draw, given first was red:

$$P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{7}{14} = \dfrac{1}{2}, \qquad P(\text{blue}) = \dfrac{7}{14} = \dfrac{1}{2}$$

If you drew blue first, the bag now holds $8$ red and $6$ blue. Total $= 14$. Second draw probabilities:

$$P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{8}{14} = \dfrac{4}{7}, \qquad P(\text{blue}) = \dfrac{6}{14} = \dfrac{3}{7}$$

So the second-stage branches in your tree depend on what happened at stage 1. The label "$P(\text{red})$" on the upper branch is different from the label on the lower branch.

## Worked example: both red

A bag has $8$ red and $7$ blue counters. Two are drawn at random without replacement. Find $P(\text{both red})$.

<iframe src="/widgets/probability-step-explorer.html?preset=p3-without-replacement" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:620px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: First draw.** Total counters $= 8 + 7 = 15$. Reds $= 8$.

$$P(\text{first red}) = \dfrac{8}{15}$$

**Step 2: Second draw, given first was red.** A red was taken out, so the bag now has $7$ reds and $7$ blues, total $14$.

$$P(\text{second red} \mid \text{first red}) = \dfrac{7}{14} = \dfrac{1}{2}$$

**Step 3: Multiply along the path.**

$$P(\text{both red}) = \dfrac{8}{15} \times \dfrac{7}{14} = \dfrac{56}{210} = \dfrac{4}{15}$$

**Answer:** $P(\text{both red}) = \boxed{\dfrac{4}{15}}$. **[M1 for $\tfrac{8}{15} \times \tfrac{7}{14}$; A1 for $\tfrac{4}{15}$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

The second-draw fraction $\tfrac{7}{14}$ shows BOTH the new numerator (one red removed: $7$ left) and the new denominator (one counter removed: $14$ left). Markers want to see this stage clearly in the working.

## The "different colours" question

A very common variant: "find the probability the two counters are different colours". Now there are two cases on the tree: RB (red then blue) and BR (blue then red). Add them.

Using the same bag of $8$ red, $7$ blue, two draws without replacement:

$$P(\text{RB}) = \dfrac{8}{15} \times \dfrac{7}{14} = \dfrac{56}{210} = \dfrac{4}{15}$$

$$P(\text{BR}) = \dfrac{7}{15} \times \dfrac{8}{14} = \dfrac{56}{210} = \dfrac{4}{15}$$

$$P(\text{different colours}) = \dfrac{4}{15} + \dfrac{4}{15} = \dfrac{8}{15}$$

Notice that RB and BR produce the SAME probability: the numerator is $8 \times 7 = 56$ either way. This is a useful pattern. For "different colours" with two colours, you always get $2 \times \dfrac{R \times B}{N(N-1)}$.

## Worked example: at least one red (complement shortcut)

The question "find $P(\text{at least one X})$" comes up almost every paper. Direct counting requires checking MULTIPLE paths: one X, two Xs, three Xs (if there are three draws), and so on. Tedious.

The complement turns it into ONE path: **no Xs**. From there, subtract from $1$.

$$P(\text{at least one X}) = 1 - P(\text{no X})$$

For the same $8R + 7B$ bag, two draws without replacement, find $P(\text{at least one red})$:

<iframe src="/widgets/probability-step-explorer.html?preset=p3-at-least-one-complement" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:620px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Complement.** $P(\text{at least one red}) = 1 - P(\text{no reds})$.

**Step 2: $P(\text{no reds})$ is the single path BB.**

$$P(\text{no reds}) = \dfrac{7}{15} \times \dfrac{6}{14} = \dfrac{42}{210} = \dfrac{1}{5}$$

**Step 3: Subtract.**

$$P(\text{at least one red}) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{4}{5}$$

**Answer:** $P(\text{at least one red}) = \boxed{\dfrac{4}{5}}$. **[M1 for $1 - \tfrac{7}{15} \times \tfrac{6}{14}$; M1 for $\tfrac{1}{5}$; A1 for $\tfrac{4}{5}$]**

Full marks $= 3$.

Direct counting of "at least one red" would have required three paths (RR, RB, BR), each multiplied out and then summed. The complement gets the same answer with one multiplication.

## Three or more draws

The same logic extends to three draws (or more). Each new draw, the denominator drops by another $1$, and the numerator drops by $1$ if you drew that colour.

Example: $4$ red, $6$ blue. Three draws without replacement. Find $P(\text{all three red})$:

$$P(\text{RRR}) = \dfrac{4}{10} \times \dfrac{3}{9} \times \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{24}{720} = \dfrac{1}{30}$$

Each fraction shows: how many reds left over how many counters left. As you "use up" reds, the numerators shrink fast: $4 \to 3 \to 2$. The denominators also shrink: $10 \to 9 \to 8$.

## Common mistakes

- **Keeping the denominator at $N$**. The single biggest mistake. If the question says "without replacement", denominators MUST drop. Mark scheme awards SC1 (special-case) for the with-replacement answer, but the rest of the marks are lost.
- **Forgetting to drop the numerator too**. After drawing a red, there are FEWER reds left as well as fewer total counters. Both shrink.
- **Adding paths that should multiply.** Inside one path, multiply. Across paths, add.
- **Missing the order**. RB and BR are TWO different paths even though they have the same multiplied probability. Both count for "different colours".
- **Not using the complement**. For "at least one", direct counting is usually slower and more error-prone than $1 - P(\text{none})$.

## Quick summary

- **Without replacement** $\Rightarrow$ denominator and (sometimes) numerator decrease on each new draw.
- **Conditional**: second-stage branch labels depend on the first-stage outcome.
- **Multiply along a path**, **add across paths** (same rules as p2).
- **"Different colours" with 2 colours** $= 2 \times \dfrac{R \times B}{N(N-1)}$.
- **"At least one X"** $= 1 - P(\text{no X})$. Always faster.
- **SC1 mark** for using the with-replacement method by mistake; the full marks need the actual without-replacement multiplication.
