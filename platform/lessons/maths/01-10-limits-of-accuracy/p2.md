## Why this matters

Once you know the bounds of each measured value, Cambridge wants you to combine them in a calculation. The pitfall: combining maxes and mins is **not always intuitive**. For sums it's straightforward; for differences it flips. Get this clear and you stop losing 2-mark questions.

## Adding: max + max, min + min

For a **sum** $a + b$, the bounds combine in the obvious way:

- **Upper bound** of $a + b$ uses the **upper** of each: $\textcolor{#ff4670}{a_{\max}} + \textcolor{#ff4670}{b_{\max}}$.
- **Lower bound** of $a + b$ uses the **lower** of each: $\textcolor{#0fee89}{a_{\min}} + \textcolor{#0fee89}{b_{\min}}$.

To make $a + b$ as **big as possible**, both ingredients should be as big as possible. To make it as **small as possible**, both should be as small as possible. Adding doesn't reverse the natural matching.

## Subtracting: max − min, min − max

For a **difference** $a - b$, the bounds **swap on $b$**:

- **Upper bound** of $a - b$: $\textcolor{#ff4670}{a_{\max}} - \textcolor{#0fee89}{b_{\min}}$.
- **Lower bound** of $a - b$: $\textcolor{#0fee89}{a_{\min}} - \textcolor{#ff4670}{b_{\max}}$.

Why the flip? To make $a - b$ as big as possible, you want $a$ as big as possible AND $b$ as small as possible (since you're taking $b$ AWAY). To make $a - b$ as small as possible, you want $a$ as small as possible AND $b$ as big as possible.

The shortcut: **for the upper of a subtraction, use the OPPOSITE bound on the second value**. Same for the lower.

The rule in one line: **for subtraction, use opposite bounds on $b$**.

## Worked example: perimeter

A rectangle has length $L = 5.0$ cm and width $W = 3.0$ cm, both to $1$ d.p. Find the upper and lower bounds of its perimeter $P = 2(L + W)$.

**Step 1: Find the bounds of each value.** $1$ d.p. $\to$ half-unit $0.05$.

| Side | Stated | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| $L$ | $5.0$ cm | $4.95$ | $5.05$ |
| $W$ | $3.0$ cm | $2.95$ | $3.05$ |

**Step 2: Apply the sum rule.** Upper of $L + W$ uses both upper bounds. Lower of $L + W$ uses both lower bounds.

- $(L + W)_{\max} = L_{\max} + W_{\max} = 5.05 + 3.05 = 8.10$
- $(L + W)_{\min} = L_{\min} + W_{\min} = 4.95 + 2.95 = 7.90$

**Step 3: Multiply by 2** (a constant factor doesn't change the upper/lower direction).

- $P_{\max} = 2 \times 8.10 = 16.20$ cm
- $P_{\min} = 2 \times 7.90 = 15.80$ cm

**Answer:** $\boxed{15.80 \le P \le 16.20}$ cm. **[M1 for the bounds; A1 each]**

Full marks $= 3$.

<div class="trap-callout">

**Trap: don't naively use the upper bound everywhere.** A common error: "find the upper bound of $L - W$" $\to$ student writes $L_{\max} - W_{\max}$. Wrong: use $L_{\max} - W_{\min}$ to make the difference biggest. Always think: "to maximise this expression, do I want $b$ big or small?"

</div>

## Worked example: difference (margin of error)

A wall is $H = 240$ cm tall to the nearest cm, and a wardrobe is $h = 195$ cm to the nearest cm. Find the upper bound of the gap above the wardrobe ($H - h$).

**Step 1: Bounds.** Half-unit $0.5$.

- $H_{\min} = 239.5$, $H_{\max} = 240.5$
- $h_{\min} = 194.5$, $h_{\max} = 195.5$

**Step 2: Apply the difference rule.** Upper of $H - h$ uses $H_{\max} - h_{\min}$.

$$\text{upper of gap} \;=\; 240.5 - 194.5 \;=\; 46 \text{ cm}$$

**Answer:** $\boxed{46}$ cm. **[M1 for using the right bounds; A1 for the answer]**

Full marks $= 2$.

## Common mistakes

- Using same bounds for difference. For $a - b$ upper, you need $a$ at max and $b$ at MIN, not both at max.
- Mixing up the half-unit. Different precisions give different halves: nearest cm $\to 0.5$; $1$ d.p. $\to 0.05$; nearest 10 m $\to 5$.
- Adding the half-units instead of using the bounds. "$12 + 8$ to nearest cm" upper is $21$ (using $12.5 + 8.5$), NOT $20 + 1 = 21$ via "add half-units" by accident; that happens to coincide in this case but is the wrong reasoning.
- Forgetting that constant factors (e.g. $2$ in $P = 2(L+W)$) just scale the bound; they don't flip max/min.

## Quick summary

- <b style="color:#ff4670">Upper</b> of $a + b$: $a_{\max} + b_{\max}$.
- <b style="color:#0fee89">Lower</b> of $a + b$: $a_{\min} + b_{\min}$.
- <b style="color:#ff4670">Upper</b> of $a - b$: $a_{\max} - b_{\min}$.
- <b style="color:#0fee89">Lower</b> of $a - b$: $a_{\min} - b_{\max}$.
- The "swap" only happens around the $-$ operation. Sums match naturally.
