## Why this matters

Imagine two friends pinning their houses on Google Maps. One is at coordinates $A(2, 3)$, the other at $B(7, 9)$ (units in $\,\text{km}$, say). They want to know how far apart they actually live. The answer is the LENGTH of the straight line between the two pins, ignoring the streets.

You can think of the journey as: $5\,\text{km}$ east, then $6\,\text{km}$ north. So the journey by car would be $5 + 6 = 11\,\text{km}$ if the roads cooperate. But the straight-line distance is shorter, given by the diagonal: $\sqrt{5^{2} + 6^{2}} = \sqrt{61} \approx 7.81\,\text{km}$.

This is one of the most common $2$-mark and $3$-mark questions in the vector section of your exam. The trick is to handle it in two clean stages: first find $\vec{AB}$, then find its magnitude. Both stages use rules you already know.

You can collapse the two stages into a single formula:

$$|\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{(x_{B} - x_{A})^{2} + (y_{B} - y_{A})^{2}}$$

But the two-stage version is safer in exam conditions. Writing the column on its own line first earns the method mark for free, even if you slip up on the arithmetic afterwards.

## Order does not matter for distance

$\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{BA}$ are different vectors: one points from $A$ to $B$, the other points the other way. Every component has the opposite sign. But when you square each component, the signs disappear. So $|\vec{AB}|$ and $|\vec{BA}|$ come out equal. The DISTANCE between two points is the same whichever order you go.

## Watch the double-negative trap

When one of the coordinates is negative, the subtraction can flip signs in a way that catches you off guard. For $A(-3, 5)$ and $B(1, -3)$:

- Top of $\vec{AB}$: $x_{B} - x_{A} = 1 - (-3) = 1 + 3 = 4$. The minus times minus becomes plus.
- Bottom of $\vec{AB}$: $y_{B} - y_{A} = -3 - 5 = -8$. Here you really are taking $5$ away from $-3$.

So $\vec{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -8 \end{pmatrix}$, NOT $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. A clean way to avoid this trap: write each coordinate IN BRACKETS before subtracting. The brackets keep the sign attached and remind you to flip when it crosses a minus.

## Worked example: distance between two points

Points $A(2, 3)$ and $B(7, 9)$ lie on a coordinate grid. Find the distance $AB$, giving your answer correct to $3$ significant figures.

<iframe src="/widgets/magnitude-step-explorer.html?preset=p2-distance-two-points" style="width:100%;max-width:696px;height:680px;border:0;border-radius:10px;display:block;margin:18px auto" loading="lazy"></iframe>

**Step 1: Find the column vector.** Image minus object, top then bottom:

$$\vec{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 - 2 \\ 9 - 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Step 2: Pythagoras on the column.** Square each component, add:

$$|\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{5^{2} + 6^{2}} = \sqrt{25 + 36} = \sqrt{61}$$

**Step 3: Convert to a decimal.** $\sqrt{61} = 7.8102\ldots$, which rounds to $7.81$ to $3$ s.f.

**Answer:** $|\vec{AB}| = \boxed{7.81}$. **[M1 for $\sqrt{5^{2} + 6^{2}}$ or for $\vec{AB} = (5, 6)$ shown; A1 for $7.81$ or $\sqrt{61}$]**

Full marks $= 2$.

The method mark is generous here: writing $\vec{AB}$ on its own line OR writing the Pythagoras expression with the correct components both earn it. Show your working in both places if you can, then the accuracy mark is the only one in play.

## A three-mark variant

Sometimes the question splits the marks across the two stages. Then writing the column is worth its own mark, separate from the magnitude calculation. Example: "Given $A(-3, 5)$ and $B(1, -3)$, (a) find $\vec{AB}$ as a column vector, (b) find $|\vec{AB}|$."

- (a) is worth $2$ marks (often $\text{B1}$ for one correct component, $\text{B1}$ for the other).
- (b) is worth $2$ marks ($\text{M1}$ for $\sqrt{4^{2} + (-8)^{2}}$, $\text{A1}$ for $\sqrt{80}$ or $8.94$).

When the question splits like that, the marker is testing whether you can produce a clean column on its own. So write the column on a separate line, in column form (not as a row $(4, -8)$ and not as coordinates $(4, -8)$). A row form is for coordinates; vectors live in columns.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up image minus object.** Writing $\vec{AB} = A - B$ instead of $B - A$. Easy to do under exam pressure. Just remember: the vector goes from $A$ TO $B$, so its tail is at $A$, its head is at $B$, and the change is $B - A$.
- **Sign flip in the subtraction.** $1 - (-3)$ is $4$, not $-2$. Bracket negative coordinates before subtracting.
- **Adding instead of squaring.** $5 + 6 = 11$ is the road journey, not the straight-line distance. Pythagoras squares first.
- **Forgetting the square root.** Stopping at $61$ rather than $\sqrt{61}$. That gives the SQUARED distance, not the distance.
- **Giving the answer as a column.** "Find $|\vec{AB}|$" wants a number (the length), not the column. The two upright bars mean a length, not a vector.

## Quick summary

- $|\vec{AB}|$ is the distance from $A$ to $B$.
- Find the column first: $\vec{AB} = B - A$, top then bottom.
- Then apply Pythagoras: $|\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{(x_{B} - x_{A})^{2} + (y_{B} - y_{A})^{2}}$.
- Distance is the same either way: $|\vec{AB}| = |\vec{BA}|$.
- The column on its own line earns the method mark, even if the calculator misbehaves.
