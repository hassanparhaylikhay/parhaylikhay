Mark Scheme Aware

Every step. Every mark. Explained.

Cambridge uses four mark types. Every Parhaylikhay explanation and worked example is annotated with exactly which line earns which mark — and why.

M1 — Method

The Method Mark

Awarded for the correct approach — even if the final answer is wrong. Always show your method. Writing the formula earns M1 before you calculate anything.

A1 — Accuracy

The Accuracy Mark

Awarded for the correct answer. Depends on M1 — no method shown means no A1, even if the answer is right. "nfww" means the correct answer must come from valid working.

B1 — Independent

The Independent Mark

Awarded regardless of method — for a correct stated fact, formula, or value. These are the "free marks" if you know your theory. Miss them and you've left marks on the table.

ECF — Follow Through

Error Carried Forward

If part (a) is wrong but you correctly apply that wrong value in part (b), you still earn marks. Understanding ECF can recover marks most students think they've permanently lost.

Cambridge 4024 — Worked Example: Solve 2x² + x − 6 = 0 Full marks = 3. Here is exactly how to write your working to earn all three marks — and what happens if you skip a step.
2x² + x − 6 = (2x − 3)(x + 2)
Correct factorisation — any valid method (quadratic formula also accepted for M1)
M1
x = 3/2     or     x = −2
Both values correct and stated — awarded because M1 was earned first
A1A1
⚠ Cambridge tip: Writing x = 3/2 and x = −2 without any working scores 0 — the M1 mark unlocks both A1 marks. Never skip your method, even if it seems obvious.
