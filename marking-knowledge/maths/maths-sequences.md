## 1. Linear (Arithmetic) Sequences — nth Term

### What the mark scheme rewards
- B1 for correct common difference (coefficient of n) with wrong constant — e.g., "4n + k" where k ≠ 1
- B1 for correct constant but wrong coefficient — e.g., "jn + 1" where j ≠ 4
- B2 for complete correct expression
- Note: B1 is awarded for EITHER the coefficient OR the constant being correct, not both being wrong

### Finding nth term:
1. Find common difference d — this becomes the coefficient of n
2. nth term = d × n + (first term − d)

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_21 Q10:** Dots pattern: 5, 9, 13, 17, 21...
- Common difference = 4
- nth term = 4n + 1 (first term = 5 = 4×1 + 1 ✓)
- B1 for 4n + k oe (any k) OR jn + 1 oe (any j ≠ 0)
- Full answer: 4n + 1
- Full marks = 2

**w25_22 Q13(a)(ii):** Sequence 3, 8, 13, 18, 23...
- Common difference = 5
- nth term = 5n − 2 (first term = 3 = 5×1 − 2 ✓)
- B1 for 5n + k (k ≠ −2) or jn − 2 (j ≠ 5)
- Full answer: 5n − 2
- Full marks = 2

**s25_22 Q15(b)(i):** nth term = 2n + 3
- B1 for 2n + p or qn + 3

## 2. Linear Sequences — Specific Term or Working Backwards

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w23_21 Q4(a)(iii):** A sequence has nth term 7n − 2. The sum of the nth term and the (n+1)th term equals 703. Find n.
- (7n − 2) + (7(n+1) − 2) = 703 → 14n + 3 = 703 → 14n = 700 → n = 50
- M1 for correct equation using their 7n − 2 for both terms: e.g. (7n−2) + (7(n+1)−2) = 703 oe
- M1 for correct rearrangement to ak = b (linear equation with two n-terms and a constant)
- Note: "Not k + k + 1 = 703 oe" — MS specifically excludes omitting the actual term expressions
- Full answer: n = 50
- Full marks = 3

**w25_21 Q10(c):** Find p when 4p + 1 = 1105
- 4p = 1104 → p = 276
- M1 for substituting 1105 into their formula for nth term
- Full answer: p = 276
- Full marks = 2

**w25_21 Q10(a):** Complete the table (terms 4 and 5 of the dots sequence)
- Term 4 = 4(4) + 1 = 17; Term 5 = 21
- Full answer: 17 and 21 [B1 for both, or B0 if one wrong]
- Full marks = 1

**w25_22 Q13(a)(i):** Next term of 3, 8, 13, 18, 23...
- Next = 28 [B1]
- Full marks = 1

## 2b. Arithmetic Sequence — Finding Term from Two Non-Consecutive Terms

### When this appears
Two terms are given (e.g., 3rd and 8th) but not consecutive. Must form and solve simultaneous equations using a + (n−1)d.

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M1 for writing two correct equations in a and d (or using the difference formula)
- B1 for difference d identified
- A1 for the specific term asked

### Key example from 2024

**s24_22 Q4(b)(i):** Arithmetic sequence. 3rd term = 34, 8th term = 14. Find 1st term.
- a + 2d = 34 and a + 7d = 14 → 5d = −20 → d = −4
- a = 34 − 2(−4) = 42
- M1 for (34 − 14)/(8 − 3) oe or for a + 2d = 34 AND a + 7d = 14 oe
- B1 for difference = −4 (or difference = 4)
- Full answer: 42
- Full marks = 2

**s24_22 Q4(b)(ii):** nth term of the same arithmetic sequence.
- nth term = 42 + (n−1)(−4) = 46 − 4n
- Full answer: −2 oe [B1] — this is the value of the nth term for some specific n, or the common difference
- Full marks = 1

## 3. Quadratic Sequences — nth Term

### What the mark scheme rewards
- B2 for fully correct quadratic expression
- B1 for correct quadratic form (any n² term) OR for identifying second difference correctly

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**s25_22 Q15(b)(ii):** Quadratic nth term (from a sequence of triangular/quadratic patterns)
- Full answer: n²/2 + 5n/2 + 3 (or equivalent)
- B1 for quadratic form (any n² term + other terms)
- Full marks = 1 or 2 depending on marks available

### Key examples from 2024 papers

**s24_22 Q4(a)(iii):** Pattern of squares (1×1, 2×2, 3×3...). nth term.
- Answer: n² oe final answer [B1]
- Full marks = 1

**s24_22 Q4(a)(iv):** Pattern of triangular numbers (1, 3, 6, 10, 15...). nth term.
- Second difference = 1 → coefficient of n² is ½
- nth term = ½n² + ½n = ½n(n + 1) oe
- B1 for ½n² + ½n oe seen
- B1 for answer kn² + ... oe (correct degree, wrong constants)
- B1 for second difference = 1 soi (as evidence of quadratic)
- Full answer: ½n(n + 1) or (n² + n)/2
- Full marks = 2

**w24_12 Q9(d):** Circles pattern: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30... Find nth term.
- Second difference = 2 → coefficient of n² is 1
- nth term = n(n + 1) oe = n² + n
- B1 for correct answer seen (alternate form n² + n)
- B1 for at least two second differences of 2 seen
- Full answer: n(n + 1) oe final answer
- Full marks = 2

**w23_21 Q4(b):** A sequence has nth term n² + an + b. The 1st term is 3 and the 3rd term is 19. Find the 6th term.
- Form two equations: 1 + a + b = 3 → a + b = 2; 9 + 3a + b = 19 → 3a + b = 10
- Subtract: 2a = 8 → a = 4, b = −2; nth term = n² + 4n − 2
- 6th term = 36 + 24 − 2 = 58
- B4 for a = 4 and b = −2 [or equivalent — worth 4 of the 5 marks]
- B2 for both equations 1²+a+b=3 AND 3²+3a+b=19 correctly set up; B1 for one correct
- M1 for correct method to eliminate one variable from their simultaneous equations
- M1 FT for substituting n = 6 into their n² + an + b: 6² + their a×6 + their b
- Alternative extension method: second difference = 2 → T₂ = 10 [B3]; differences 7,9,11,13,15 → T₄=30, T₅=43, T₆=58 [B1 each]
- Full answer: 58
- Full marks = 5

**w23_12 Q15(b):** First five terms: 3, 9, 19, 33, 51. Find the nth term.
- First differences: 6, 10, 14, 18 — these are increasing by 4
- Second differences: 4, 4, 4 → coefficient of n² = 4/2 = 2
- Subtract 2n² from sequence: 2(1)=2, 2(4)=8, 2(9)=18, 2(16)=32, 2(25)=50
- Residuals: 3−2=1, 9−8=1, 19−18=1, 33−32=1, 51−50=1 → constant +1
- nth term = 2n² + 1 oe
- B1 for quadratic expression in n (any 2n² term) OR for correct answer seen OR for at least two second differences of 4 seen
- Full answer: 2n² + 1 oe final answer
- Full marks = 2

## 4. Complex Sequences — Pattern Recognition

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_22 Q13(b):** T₁ = 3/4, T₂ = 8/9, T₃ = 13/16, T₄ = 18/25, T₅ = 23/36

Numerator pattern: 3, 8, 13, 18, 23 → nth term = 5n − 2 (same linear sequence as part (a))
Denominator pattern: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 = 2², 3², 4², 5², 6² = (n+1)²

So Tₙ = (5n − 2) / (n+1)²

T₂₅ = (5×25 − 2) / (25+1)² = 123/676 ✓

Mark scheme breakdown:
- M1 for (n+1)² oe identified as denominator pattern
- M1 for 5n − 2 oe identified as numerator pattern (or substituting n = 25 into their nth term)
- A1 for 123/676 oe
- Full marks = 3

## 5. LCM-Based Sequences (Cyclical Sequences)

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**s25_13 Q11:** A green light flashes every 12 minutes. A red light flashes every 45 minutes. Both flash together at 9:00 am. Find the next time they flash together.
- LCM(12, 45): 12 = 2² × 3; 45 = 3² × 5 → LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180 minutes
- 9:00 am + 180 minutes = 12:00 noon
- B2 for LCM = 180; M1 for prime factorisation or listing multiples method shown
- Full answer: 12:00 (noon)
- Full marks = 3

## Marking Traps — Sequences Topic

1. **nth term coefficient**: The coefficient is the COMMON DIFFERENCE, not the first term. A very common error is to write "first term × n" instead of "d × n + (first term − d)".
2. **Partial B marks**: B1 for correct coefficient only, OR correct constant only — getting both wrong = B0. Getting one right = B1. Both right = B2.
3. **Complex sequences**: Identify numerator and denominator patterns SEPARATELY, then combine. Don't try to find a single formula for the whole fraction.
4. **LCM for timing problems**: Always find LCM of the time intervals, not HCF or product. Add to starting time.
5. **Quadratic sequences**: Second differences are constant (and equal to 2a, where n² coefficient is a). If second differences are constant, it's quadratic.
6. **Finding a specific term**: Substitute the specific value into the nth term formula — don't try to extend the sequence term by term (that's too slow and error-prone for large values).
