## 1. Area and Circumference of a Circle

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Area = πr² — answer "in terms of π" means leave π unsimplified
- Circumference = 2πr
- "cao" means exact form only — 36π, not 113.1

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q10:** Circle has diameter 12 cm. Find area in terms of π.
- r = 6 cm
- M1 for π × (12/2)² oe (must show radius = 6, not diameter = 12)
- Full answer: 36π cm² cao
- Full marks = 2

## 3. Volume of Prism

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q17(a):** Volume of triangular prism = 180 cm³ (show that)

- Height = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12

## 10. Finding Radius from Surface Area Equation

### When this appears
The total or curved surface area is given, and you must solve for r first before finding volume or slant height.

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M1 for writing the correct surface area equation (e.g., 2πr² = 145)
- M1 FT for correctly solving for r²
- B1 for r = [correct value]
- Then subsequent marks for volume using their r

### Key example from 2024

**s24_22 Q8(a)(i):** A solid is formed by placing a cone on top of a hemisphere. Both the cone and hemisphere have radius r cm. Total height of the solid = 10 cm. The curved surface area of the hemisphere is 145 cm². Show that r = 4.80 correct to 2 d.p.
- Curved surface of hemisphere = 2πr² = 145 (the 145 cm² refers to the hemisphere curved surface only, not the cone)
- M1 for 2πr² = 145 or 4πr² = 290 oe
- M1FT for r² = 145/(2π) or r² = 23.07... oe
- B1 for r = 4.803 to 4.804 (leading to 4.80 ✓)
- Full marks = 3

**s24_22 Q8(a)(ii):** Volume of hemisphere + cone using r found in (i).
- B1 for height of cone = 10 − 4.8 soi
- M1 for (1/3)π(4.80)²(their 5.2) oe [cone volume]
- M1 for (2/3)π(4.80)³ oe [hemisphere volume]
- Full answer: 357 to 358
- Full marks = 4

**s24_22 Q8(a)(iii):** Slant height of cone.
- M2 for slant² = r² + h² = 4.80² + 5.2² oe
- M1 for r² + h² seen without square root
- Full answer: 107 or 106.7 to 106.8 [area of curved cone surface = πrl]
- Full marks = 4

## 11. Percentage Change of Combined Shape Dimensions

### When this appears
A sector (or shape) changes in two dimensions simultaneously. Find the percentage change in area.

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M3 for expressing the new area as (factor₁) × (factor₂)² × original area
- B1/B2 for identifying the new value of each changing dimension
- Percentage change = (new/original − 1) × 100

### Key example from 2024

**s24_22 Q8(b):** The diagram shows two sectors of circles: Sector A has angle x and radius y. Sector B has angle 20% smaller than Sector A, and radius 20% longer than Sector A. Calculate the area of Sector B as a percentage of the area of Sector A.
- New angle = 0.8x (20% smaller); new radius = 1.2y (20% longer)
- New area = (0.8x/360) × π(1.2y)² = 0.8 × 1.44 × (x/360)πy² = 1.152 × original area
- M3 for 0.8 × (1.2)² [× 100] oe (both changes applied and combined correctly)
- B1 for 0.8x oe OR 1.2y oe (identifying each new dimension)
- M2 for [new area]/[original area] with both changes applied
- After 0 scored, SC1 for (x − 0.2)/360 × (y − 0.2)² style partial method
- Full answer: 115.2 (new area is 115.2% of original)
- Full marks = 4

## Marking Traps — Mensuration Topic

1. **Diameter vs radius**: Area = πr², NOT πd². Very common error — using diameter instead of radius. M1 requires π × (12/2)² showing the radius halving.
2. **"In terms of π"**: Must leave π in answer. Writing 113.1 instead of 36π = B0 for cao.
3. **Similar solids**: Area ratio = k²; Volume ratio = k³. Forgetting to cube/square the linear ratio is the most common error.
4. **Composite solids — which faces to include**: The general rule is: the join circle cancels. For hemisphere + cone: curved hemisphere + lateral cone only (no flat base circles). For cylinder + cone (cone on top): lateral cone + curved cylinder + base circle of cylinder (the top and bottom of the join cancel; the open base of the cylinder is exposed). Always draw which faces are visible from outside and count only those.
5. **Volume units**: 1 cm³ = 1 mL. If rate is in mL/sec, convert volume to mL (cm³) not m³.
6. **Rounding up**: When finding "how many rolls/packets/items needed", always round UP even if the decimal is small (e.g., 30.9 rolls → 31 rolls, not 30).
7. **Show that**: All steps must be shown explicitly. Cannot skip from "h² = 144" directly to "volume = 180" — the intermediate "h = 12" and "area = 60" steps are needed for M marks.
8. **"Curved surface area of the hemisphere = X" does NOT involve the cone**: When the question says the curved surface area of the hemisphere equals some value, the equation is 2πr² = X (hemisphere curved surface only). The cone is a separate part of the composite solid. Do not add πrl to the left side.
