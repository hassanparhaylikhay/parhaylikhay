## 6. Algebraic Fractions

### Simplifying (cancelling)

**w25_12 Q18:** Multi-step simplification of an algebraic fraction involving sphere and cone surface area expressions. All components of the final simplified fraction must be correct.
- Steps need all components in final answer correct

**w23_11 Q21(c):** Simplify (2c² − 8c) / (2c² − 5c − 12).
- Factorise numerator: 2c² − 8c = 2c(c − 4) [B1 for 2c(c − 4) seen]
- Factorise denominator: 2c² − 5c − 12 = (2c + 3)(c − 4) [B1 for (2c + 3)(c − 4) seen]
- Cancel common factor (c − 4): result = 2c / (2c + 3) [A1]
- Key: this requires factorising BOTH the numerator (common factor extraction) AND the denominator (quadratic factorisation) before the common factor becomes visible
- Full answer: 2c / (2c + 3)
- Full marks = 3

- M1 for setting up equation with both fractions
- M1 for common denominator removal
- M1 for bracket expansion

**w23_11 Q24:** Solve 3x/(x + 1) − 2/(x − 1) = 3.
- Multiply through by (x + 1)(x − 1): 3x(x − 1) − 2(x + 1) = 3(x + 1)(x − 1)
- Expand left: 3x² − 3x − 2x − 2 = 3x² − 5x − 2
- Expand right: 3(x² − 1) = 3x² − 3
- Key insight: the x² terms cancel → 3x² − 5x − 2 = 3x² − 3 → −5x − 2 = −3 → −5x = −1 → x = 1/5
- This question gives a LINEAR result (not a quadratic), because the x² terms cancel identically on both sides
- M1 for multiplying by (x + 1)(x − 1) oe (clearing denominators)
- M1 for correct expansion of at least two of the three terms
- M1 for recognising that x² terms cancel and collecting to a linear equation
- A1 for x = 1/5
- Full answer: x = 1/5
- Full marks = 4

## 15. Direct and Inverse Variation (Proportion)

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M1 for correctly identifying the constant of proportionality k (often "k soi")
- M1/A1 for correct final answer after substituting the new value
- B1 for k = [value] if a simpler formula is used (partial credit when wrong model applied)

### Variation types seen in 2024 papers

**Direct variation:** y ∝ xⁿ → y = kxⁿ

**Inverse variation:** y ∝ 1/xⁿ → y = k/xⁿ

**Combined variation (eliminate intermediate variable):** y ∝ w² and x ∝ 1/w → eliminate w to find y in terms of x.

**s23_12 Q22:** y is directly proportional to w². x is inversely proportional to w. When w = 10, y = 5 and x = 0.4. Find y in terms of x in its simplest form.
- y = kw²: 5 = k(10²) → k = 1/20, so y = w²/20
- x = c/w: 0.4 = c/10 → c = 4, so x = 4/w → w = 4/x
- Substitute: y = (4/x)²/20 = 16/(20x²) = 4/(5x²)
- B2 for y = w²/20 oe and x = 4/w oe (both proportional relationships found)
- B1 for either y = w²/20 oe OR x = 4/w oe (one correct relationship)
- M1 for substituting their w = 4/x into their y = w²/20 oe
- Full answer: y = 4/(5x²) or 0.8/x²
- Full marks = 4

**s24_11 Q18:** A table shows values of p and q where p ∝ q². The table is: p = 12 when q = 2; p = a when q = 7; p = 48 when q = b. Find a and b.
- k = p/q² = 12/4 = 3
- a = 3 × 7² = 3 × 49 = 147
- b² = 48/3 = 16 → b = ±4
- M2 for 7²/12 × 48 oe (combining the two substitutions efficiently)
- M1 for k = 12/4 = 3 soi (constant of proportionality found)
- MIFT for a = their k × 49 or for b = √(48/their k)
- Full answers: a = 147, b = ±4
- Full marks = 3

**w24_11 Q19:** y ∝ 1/x². Given y = 16 when x = 2, find y when x = 32.
- k = y × x² = 16 × 4 = 64
- When x = 32: y = 64/1024 = 1/16
- B1 for k = 8 if y = k/x (wrong model — inverse proportion, not inverse square) is used
- M1 for correct k × x² = k × x² cross-multiplication: e.g. 2² × 16 = 32² × y
- M1FT for y = √(their k/32) (using their k from whichever model applied)
- Full answer: 1/16 nfww
- Full marks = 2

**w23_11 Q17:** y is directly proportional to the square root of x. When x = 16, y = 2. Find y when x = 25.
- y = k√x → 2 = k√16 = 4k → k = 1/2
- When x = 25: y = (1/2)√25 = (1/2)(5) = 5/2
- B1 for k = 1/2 soi (constant of proportionality found)
- M1 for 2√25 = y√16 oe (correctly equating the two relationships)
- Full answer: y = 5/2
- Full marks = 2

### Marking trap
- Distinguishing y ∝ 1/x from y ∝ 1/x² from y ∝ x² is crucial — using the wrong model gets B1 (partial) not full marks
- The Cambridge MS grants B1 for k found under the wrong proportionality formula — showing compassion for partial understanding
- For y ∝ √x specifically: the cross-ratio form is 2/√16 = y/√25 oe

## 16. Rearranging Formulae When the Variable Appears Twice (Factorise Method)

### When this appears
Questions of type "make x the subject" where x appears in multiple terms with different coefficients — requires factorising before dividing.

### Method
1. Expand all brackets
2. Collect all terms involving x on one side
3. Factorise out x
4. Divide both sides by the bracket

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M1 for correct elimination of fractions
- M1 FT for isolating terms in x (collecting x terms on one side)
- M1 FT for factorising and completing to x = ...
- **"Max 2 marks if final answer not correct"** — consistently applied across papers
- A0 if any errors in the working chain

### Key examples from 2024 papers

**s24_11 Q22:** Make x the subject of ax(b) + c = dx + e (variables across multiple terms).
- Answer: x = (5b − 3a) / (a²) oe final answer
- M1 for elimination of fraction; M1FT for isolation of x terms; M1FT for factorising to x = ...
- "Max 2 marks if answer incorrect"
- Full marks = 3

## 18. Algebraic Fractions → Quadratic Equation Formation

### When this appears
Questions of type: "show that this equation leads to ax² + bx + c = 0" where the starting point involves fractions with algebraic denominators.

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M2 for correctly eliminating all fractions (multiplying by common denominator applied to ALL terms)
- M1 for partial elimination (one fraction cleared or equivalent partial progress)
- M1 for correct expansion of brackets after elimination
- A1 for reaching the required quadratic "with no errors or omissions" (A0 if any error)
- B4 maximum structure: B-mark chain rather than M/A chain

### Key examples from 2024 papers

**s23_12 Q21:** Solve 5x/(x−3) = x + 4. [Single algebraic fraction = linear expression]
- Multiply both sides by (x−3): 5x = (x+4)(x−3)
- Expand right side: 5x = x² + x − 12
- Rearrange: x² − 4x − 12 = 0
- Factorise: (x−6)(x+2) = 0
- B2 for x² − 4x − 12 [= 0] oe (correct quadratic formed)
- M1 for [5x =] x² + 4x − 3x − 12 oe (partial expansion after multiplying through)
- M1 for (x−6)(x+2) [= 0] oe factorised, or quadratic formula with their 3-term quadratic
- Full answer: x = −2 or x = 6
- Full marks = 4

**s24_21 Q10(d):** 2/(x+2) + 5/(x−3) = 1. Show that x² − 6x − 17 = 0.
- Common denominator: (x+2)(x−3)
- M2 for 2(x−2) + 5(x+3) = (x+3)(x+2) oe, or equivalent full elimination
- M1 for 2(x+2) + 5(x−3) seen (partial — numerators correct but denominator not cleared)
- M1 for x² + 3x − 2x − 6 expanded correctly
- Quadratic formula FT: x = [6 ± √(36 + 68)] / 2 oe → x = 8.10, −2.10
- B marks carry "A0 if any errors or omissions" at the formation stage
- Full marks = 4 (B4) + quadratic formula B1 + roots B1 = structure varies by paper variant

**w24_21 Q10(c):** Algebraic fraction equation → x² − 6x − 72 = 0 — A1 for correct rearrangement, A0 if errors
- M1 for correct removal of fractions, M1 for bracket expansion
- Full marks = 3 (M2A1 chain), then factorisation and solution separately marked

## Marking Traps — Algebra Topic

1. **Factorisation**: "Final answer" means fully factorised — partial credit only if bracket conditions met
2. **Quadratic equation from context**: A0 if working has errors — "no errors or omissions" required for the A mark
3. **Simultaneous equations**: SC1 only when A0 — it's a consolation mark, not a method mark
4. **Rearranging**: Each step is M1 — show each step separately; one algebraic error costs the A mark
5. **Sequences**: B1 for "correct coefficient" OR "correct constant" — need BOTH for full marks
6. **Algebraic fractions**: After combining fractions, do not cancel prematurely — the mark scheme checks if the final answer is fully simplified "final answer"
7. **Functions**: fg means "do g first, then f" — the order matters and marks are awarded for the correct composition
8. **Show that**: Must show every step — working backward from the given answer is not awarded
9. **Variation**: Using y ∝ 1/x when y ∝ 1/x² is needed gets B1 for k only — the wrong model cannot earn full marks
10. **Rearranging with x on both sides**: Forgetting to factorise x after collecting terms = answer wrong; max 2 out of 3 marks
11. **Quadratic from algebraic fractions**: Any error anywhere in the elimination/expansion chain = A0 for the formation mark; M marks can still be earned for correct partial steps
