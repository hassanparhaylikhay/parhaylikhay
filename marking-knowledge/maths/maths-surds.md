## 1. Simplifying Surds

### Rules
- √(a × b) = √a × √b
- √(a/b) = √a / √b
- Simplify by finding the largest perfect square factor

### What the mark scheme rewards
- B1 for an intermediate simplified surd (e.g., √48 = 4√3)
- B2 for fully simplified final answer

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q23(a):** Simplify √8 × √6 + √3
- √8 × √6 = √48 = 4√3
- 4√3 + √3 = 5√3
- B1 for √48 or 4√3 seen (intermediate step)
- Full answer: 5√3 final answer
- Full marks = 2

**s25_13 Q25(a):** Simplify 10√3 − 4√3 (or equivalent expression involving √3 and a multiple of √3).
- B1 for 10√3 or 4√3 seen as intermediate step
- Full answer: 6√3
- Full marks = 2

## 2. Rationalising the Denominator

### Method
For 1/√a: multiply top and bottom by √a
For p/(a + √b): multiply top and bottom by (a − √b) [conjugate]
For p/(a − √b): multiply top and bottom by (a + √b) [conjugate]

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M1 for multiplying by the correct conjugate (must show conjugate form)
- B1 for numerator expansion correct (e.g., "4 − 2√5 − 2√5 + 5" = "9 − 4√5")
- Full answer in simplest form [A1]

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q23(b):** Rationalise 3/(2 − √5)
- Multiply by (2 + √5)/(2 + √5)
- M1 for multiplication by (2 + √5)/(2 + √5) or (−2 − √5)/(−2 − √5)
- B1 for denominator: (2)² − (√5)² = 4 − 5 = −1 correctly found
- B1 for numerator expansion: 3(2 + √5) = 6 + 3√5 or 3×(−2) + 3×(−√5) = −6 − 3√5
- Full answer: 3(2 + √5)/(−1) = −3(2 + √5) = −6 − 3√5
  OR: −(6 + 3√5) OR (−6 − 3√5) — MS accepts (3)(2 + √5)/(−1) written in various forms:
  - "(3)(2 + √5)" final answer (with denominator implicitly −1)
  - "OR (−6)(3)(√5)" final answer
  - MS shows: "(3)(2+5)−+  or  (6)(3 5)−+ or  6 3 5−−" which translates to:
    - 3(2+√5)/(−1) = −3(2+√5) = −6 − 3√5
- Full marks = 3

**s25_13 Q25(b):** Rationalise the denominator of an expression like k/(√7 + 2) (or similar with √7 and integer denominator term).
- M1 for multiplying by conjugate (√7 − 2)/(√7 − 2) or equivalent
- B1 for correct numerator expansion after rationalising
- Full answer: 2(√7 − 2) or 2√7 − 4
- Full marks = 3

## 3. Surds in Context — Exact Answers

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_12 Q23:** An isosceles triangle has a perpendicular height drawn from apex to base. The equal sides are 11 units, and half the base is 7 units. Find AB (the full base).
- M is the midpoint of AB. MX = perpendicular height.
- MX² = 11² − 7² = 121 − 49 = 72; MX = √72 = 6√2; AB = 2MX = 12√2
- B3 for MX = √72 oe or AB = 2√72 oe (before simplification)
- M2 for MX² = 11² − 7² = 72 (Pythagoras on right triangle)
- M1 for MX² + 7² = 11² oe (Pythagoras setup)
- Full answer: 12√2 cao (not √288 — must simplify)
- Full marks = 4

## Marking Traps — Surds Topic

1. **Conjugate**: Must use (a + √b) when denominator is (a − √b), and vice versa.
   - (a − √b)(a + √b) = a² − b (difference of two squares) — denominator becomes rational ✓
   - Wrong conjugate: (a − √b)(a − √b) = a² − 2a√b + b — denominator remains irrational ✗
   - M1 is awarded for correct conjugate only.

2. **√(a²) = |a|**: √9 = 3, not ±3 (when simplifying surds, take positive root). Common sign error.

3. **√a × √b = √(ab)**: This only works if both a and b are positive. At O-Level, always assumed positive.

4. **Collecting surd terms**: 4√3 + √3 = 5√3 (like terms); 4√3 + 2√6 CANNOT be simplified (unlike terms). Very common error: adding unlike surds.

5. **"cao"**: Correct answer only — 12√2 cannot be left as √288. Must be simplified to standard surd form.

6. **Rationalization**: The final answer should have NO surd in the denominator. Leaving a surd in denominator = B0 for "rationalize" question.
