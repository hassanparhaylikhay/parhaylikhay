## 1. Arithmetic Operations & Order of Operations

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Correct application of BODMAS/BIDMAS — brackets first, then indices, then ÷ and ×, then + and −
- Inserting brackets correctly to make a calculation true

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_21 Q2(a):** Insert brackets to make (4.2 − 3) × 5 + 1.2 = 7.2 correct
- Answer: brackets around (4.2 − 3)
- Mark: B1

**w25_21 Q2(b):** Insert brackets to make 5 − 3²− (√64 − 2) + 3 = −10 correct
- Mark: B1

**s25_12 Q1:** Order of operations
- Q1(a): 6 − 2 × (−4) = 14 [B1]
- Q1(b): 80 × 0.2 = 16 [B1]

### What earns zero
- No working shown when the calculation is multi-step (but single-step operations are B1 and need no working)

## 3. Percentages

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Percentage decrease: price × (1 − rate/100) or price − rate% × price
- Percentage increase: price × (1 + rate/100) or price + rate% × price
- Reverse percentage: divide by (1 − rate/100) or (1 + rate/100)
- SC1 often awarded for adding instead of subtracting (finding new amount instead of reduction)

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q3:** Shirt costs $24, reduced 15% in sale
- M1 for (15/24)/24 × 100 oe or B1 for answer 3.60
- Full answer: $20.40
- Full marks = 2

**s25_12 Q13(a):** $85 reduced by 20% → $68
- M1 for 85 − (20/100) × 85 oe

**s25_12 Q13(b):** (100 − 20)/100 × x = 40 → x = $50
- M1 for 0.8x = 40 oe (reverse percentage)

**s24_21 Q1(a)(ii):** Reverse percentage. Item costs $67.60 after 4% discount. Find original price.
- Original × (100 − 4)/100 = 67.60 → original = 67.60 × 100/96 = 65... wait — answer 65.
- M1 for (100/(100−4)) × 67.60 oe or (100 − 4)/100 × x = 67.60 soi
- Full answer: 65.00 nfww
- Full marks = 2

**w24_22 Q1(d):** Find original price. Item reduced by 62% costs $285.
- 38% of original = 285 → original = 285/0.38
- M1 for (100 − 62)% oe equated to 285 soi
- Full answer: 465
- Full marks = 2

### What earns zero
- Finding the reduction amount instead of the sale price (SC1 often given for this)
- "SC1 for answer 268.20" (w25_21 Q8 — student added interest to principal instead of finding interest only)

**s25_23 Q9(b):** $600 at 2.7% compound, 4 years → interest = $67.47

**w25_21 Q14:** Population 5.4 million grows at 3.6%/year. Find years to reach 8 million.

## 11. HCF, LCM, Prime Factors

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Prime factorisation shown
- LCM: product of highest powers of all prime factors
- HCF: product of lowest common powers

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**s23_12 Q14(b):** P = x^n y² and Q = x^(n−1) y⁴, where x and y are prime. Find the HCF of P and Q in terms of x, y and n.
- HCF takes the lowest power of each prime factor
- x: lowest power is min(n, n−1) = n−1; y: lowest power is min(2, 4) = 2
- Full answer: x^(n−1) y² final answer
- B1 for x^(n−1) y^k (any k) or x^k y² (any k) — one component correct
- Full marks = 2

**s25_13 Q11:** Two flashing lights: green flashes every 12 minutes, red flashes every 45 minutes. They flash together at 11:00. Find the next time they flash together.
- LCM(12, 45): 12 = 2² × 3; 45 = 3² × 5; LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180 minutes = 3 hours
- Next simultaneous flash: 11:00 + 3 hours = 14:00
- B2 for LCM = 180 seen
- M1 for prime factor method or listing multiples of 12 and 45
- Full answer: 14:00 (or 2pm)
- Full marks = 3

**w25_22 Q14(a):** N = 2³ × 3^x × 5^y; LCM(N, 360) = 16200
- M1 for 16200 = 2³ × 3⁴ × 5² or for 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5 or for 45 = 3² × 5
- Full answer: x = 4, y = 2
- Full marks = 2

**w25_22 Q14(b):** HCF(N, 360) = 90 [B1; FT answer 30 if their x = 1]

**w25_22 Q14(c):** Smallest k such that kN is a cube number = 180 [B1]

**w25_21 Q15:** x = 2² × 3^n × 7; y = 2³ × 3^(n+1) × 7. HCF in terms of n.
- B1 for 2² or 3^n or 7 in final answer
- Full answer: 2² × 3^n × 7
- Full marks = 2

**s23_21 Q5(b)(i):** M = 2^(2x) × 3⁴ × 5 × 7 and N = 2³ × 3^(x−y) × 5². The LCM of M and N is 2⁸ × 3⁶ × 5² × 7. Find x and y.
- For the factor 2: max(2x, 3) = 8 → 2x = 8 → x = 4
- For the factor 3: max(4, x−y) = 6 → x−y = 6 → 4−y = 6 → y = −2
- B1 for x = 4 (from 2x = 8)
- B1 FT for y = (their x) − 6 (accept y = −2 if FT gives this)
- Full marks = 2

**s23_21 Q5(b)(ii):** Using x = 4, find the largest square number that is a factor of M = 2⁸ × 3⁴ × 5 × 7.
- Square factors require all prime powers to be even: 2⁸ and 3⁴ are already even powers; 5 and 7 have odd power 1 so excluded
- Largest square factor = 2⁸ × 3⁴ = 256 × 81 = 20736
- Full answer: 20736 [B1]
- Full marks = 1

**s23_21 Q5(b)(iii):** Using x = 4, y = −2, find the HCF of M and N as a product of prime factors.
- M = 2⁸ × 3⁴ × 5 × 7; N = 2³ × 3⁶ × 5²
- HCF = min powers of each common prime: 2^min(8,3) × 3^min(4,6) × 5^min(1,2) = 2³ × 3⁴ × 5
- Full answer: 2³ × 3⁴ × 5 [B1]
- Full marks = 1

**w23_12 Q9(b):** 36 = 2² × 3² and N = 2² × 3 × k, where k > 3. 180 is the LCM of 36 and N. Find k.
- 180 = 2² × 3² × 5 (prime factorisation of 180)
- LCM(36, N) = LCM(2² × 3², 2² × 3 × k) = 2² × 3² × k (if k introduces a new prime not already in 36)
- For LCM = 180 = 2² × 3² × 5: the extra factor must be k = 5
- Check: LCM(2²×3², 2²×3×5) = 2²×3²×5 = 180 ✓; k=5>3 ✓
- Full answer: k = 5 cao [B1]
- Full marks = 1

## 16. Bounds — Upper/Lower Bound of a Formula (Cylinder Volume)

### When this appears
Two measurements are given with different precisions. Find the upper (or lower) bound of a formula that uses both.

### What the mark scheme rewards
- B1 for identifying each bound correctly
- M1 for substituting BOTH upper bounds into the formula (for upper bound of product)
- nfww applies — the answer must come from correct bound values, not from the nominal values

### Key example from 2023 papers

**w23_22 Q10(b):** The height of a cylinder is 13 cm, correct to the nearest centimetre. The radius of the base is 4.5 cm, correct to the nearest 0.1 cm. Calculate the upper bound of the volume in cm³.
- Upper bound of h: 13.5 cm (nearest cm → half-unit = 0.5)
- Upper bound of r: 4.55 cm (nearest 0.1 cm → half-unit = 0.05)
- Upper bound V = π × 4.55² × 13.5 = π × 20.7025 × 13.5 ≈ 878.1 cm³
- B1 for 13.5 seen or 4.55 seen [either bound identified]
- M1 for π × their 4.55² × their 13.5 oe
- Full answer: 878 or 878.0 to 878.1 nfww
- Full marks = 3

## Marking Traps — Number Topic

1. **Percentage**: "Find the interest" vs "find the total amount" — SC1 for the wrong one
2. **Ratio**: "18 more" translates to difference of parts → divide by (8−5) = 3 to get unit value
3. **Compound interest**: Always show A = P(1+r/100)^n clearly — M1 requires formula visible
4. **Standard form**: If answer is 0.034, must write as 3.4 × 10⁻² — leaving as 0.034 loses B1
5. **Bounds**: "Upper bound of a/b" = upper bound of a / lower bound of b (opposite for division)
6. **Recurring decimal**: Must show the algebraic process — answer alone may not earn nfww
7. **LCM/HCF**: Show prime factor trees or factor method explicitly — B marks depend on method being shown
