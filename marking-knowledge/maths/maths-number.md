## 1. Arithmetic Operations & Order of Operations

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Correct application of BODMAS/BIDMAS — brackets first, then indices, then ÷ and ×, then + and −
- Inserting brackets correctly to make a calculation true

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_21 Q2(a):** Insert brackets to make (4.2 − 3) × 5 + 1.2 = 7.2 correct
- Answer: brackets around (4.2 − 3)
- Mark: B1

**w25_21 Q2(b):** Insert brackets to make 5 − 3²− (√64 − 2) + 3 = −10 correct
- Mark: B1

**s25_12 Q1:** Order of operations
- Q1(a): 6 − 2 × (−4) = 14 [B1]
- Q1(b): 80 × 0.2 = 16 [B1]

### What earns zero
- No working shown when the calculation is multi-step (but single-step operations are B1 and need no working)

## 2. Fractions — Operations

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Division: multiply by reciprocal — M1 for seeing the reciprocal multiplication set up
- Addition/subtraction: common denominator method — M1 for correct LCD used
- Mixed numbers: convert to improper fractions first

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**s25_12 Q1(c):** 2/9 ÷ 5/6 = 4/15
- M1 for seeing 2/9 × 6/5
- Full marks = 2

**w25_11 Q13:** 2¾ + 3¾ = 5⅚ (mixed number, simplest form)
- B2 for 77/12 seen (equivalent)
- M1 for correct common denominator used (e.g. 8/12 and 9/12 seen) OR correct conversion of one mixed number to improper fraction
- Full marks = 3
- Mark scheme says: "Give answer as mixed number in its simplest form"

**w25_12 Q10:** 1⅗ ÷ 2/3/10 → answer 4⅓
- B2 for 70/15 seen
- M1 for 7/5 seen
- M1 for (their 7/5) × (their 10/3)
- Full marks = 3

### Marking pattern for fractions
- Always check: is the answer required as a mixed number? As a fraction in simplest form? "cao" means exact form required.
- Common LCD steps earn M1 even if final answer is wrong
- Reciprocal set-up earns M1 even if multiplication is wrong

## 3. Percentages

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Percentage decrease: price × (1 − rate/100) or price − rate% × price
- Percentage increase: price × (1 + rate/100) or price + rate% × price
- Reverse percentage: divide by (1 − rate/100) or (1 + rate/100)
- SC1 often awarded for adding instead of subtracting (finding new amount instead of reduction)

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q3:** Shirt costs $24, reduced 15% in sale
- M1 for (15/24)/24 × 100 oe or B1 for answer 3.60
- Full answer: $20.40
- Full marks = 2

**s25_12 Q13(a):** $85 reduced by 20% → $68
- M1 for 85 − (20/100) × 85 oe

**s25_12 Q13(b):** (100 − 20)/100 × x = 40 → x = $50
- M1 for 0.8x = 40 oe (reverse percentage)

**s24_21 Q1(a)(ii):** Reverse percentage. Item costs $67.60 after 4% discount. Find original price.
- Original × (100 − 4)/100 = 67.60 → original = 67.60 × 100/96 = 65... wait — answer 65.
- M1 for (100/(100−4)) × 67.60 oe or (100 − 4)/100 × x = 67.60 soi
- Full answer: 65.00 nfww
- Full marks = 2

**w24_22 Q1(d):** Find original price. Item reduced by 62% costs $285.
- 38% of original = 285 → original = 285/0.38
- M1 for (100 − 62)% oe equated to 285 soi
- Full answer: 465
- Full marks = 2

### What earns zero
- Finding the reduction amount instead of the sale price (SC1 often given for this)
- "SC1 for answer 268.20" (w25_21 Q8 — student added interest to principal instead of finding interest only)

## 6. Compound Interest / Exponential Growth and Decay

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Formula: A = P(1 + r/100)^n — all components correct
- Finding interest earned: A − P
- Finding years: logarithmic method or systematic trial and check (both accepted if working shown)
- "nfww" sometimes applied

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**s25_22 Q11(b):** $2750 at 2.1% compound interest, 3 years → interest = $176.91
- M2 for 2750 × (1 + 2.1/100)³ − 2750
- M1 for 2750 × (1 + 2.1/100)³ (total amount only)

**s25_23 Q9(b):** $600 at 2.7% compound, 4 years → interest = $67.47
- M2 for 600 × (1 + 2.7/100)⁴ − 600
- M1 for total amount without subtracting P

**w25_21 Q14:** Population 5.4 million grows at 3.6%/year. Find years to reach 8 million.
- B2 for 11 [years] nfww, or for 9.6 to 9.7 seen
- M2 for evaluating 5.4 × (1 + 3.6/100)^n for n = 9 and n = 10 and showing 1.166 or 1.1663 to 1.1664
- M1 for 5.4 × (1 + 3.6/100)^n oe where n > 1
- Full marks = 3

**w25_22 Q16(a):** Population 120,000 decreases to 102,885 in 3 years. Find rate x.
- M2 for ∛(102885/120000) oe
- M1 for 102885 = 120000k³ oe
- Full answer: x = 5
- Full marks = 3

**w25_22 Q16(b):** After 2023 population increases at 1.6%/year. Find year > 120,000 first.
- B2 for 10 [years] nfww or 9.6 to 9.7
- M2 for evaluating 102885 × (1 + 1.6/100)^9 or ^10
- M1 for 102885 × (1 + 1.6/100)^k oe
- Full answer: 2033
- Full marks = 3

**w24_21 Q1(d):** Compound interest split across two periods — different principals.
- $12,000 invested at 1.5% for 4 years, then a further $12,000 added, full amount at 1.5% for 3 more years.
- M2 for 12000(1 + 1.5/100)⁴ + 12000(1 + 1.5/100)³ oe
- OR: M2 for 24180 × (1 + 1.5/100)³ oe where 24180 = first principal after 4 years
- M1 for 12000(1 + 1.5/100)^k oe where k = 3 or 4 (one period only)
- After 0 scored, SC1 for 24180 × (1 + 1.5/100)⁴ (wrong number of remaining years)
- Full answer: 5284.50
- Full marks = 3

**w24_22 Q4(c):** Find the compound interest rate r given start and end values over 3 years.
- 600 × (1 + r/100)³ = 600 + 21.86 = 621.86
- (1 + r/100) = ∛(621.86/600)
- M2 for ∛(621.86/600) oe
- M1 for 600k³ = 621.86 oe (identifying the structure)
- Full answer: 1.2[0] nfww
- Full marks = 3

### What earns zero
- Using simple interest formula for compound interest questions
- Not subtracting P when "interest earned" is required (loses A mark)

## 7. Estimation / Significant Figures

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Rounding each value to 1 significant figure correctly
- Then performing the calculation with rounded values
- B1 usually for 2 out of 3 values correctly rounded

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q9:** By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the value of (√102.5 × 8.5) / 27.
- Round each: 102.5 → 100 (so √100 = 10); 8.5 → 9; 27 → 30
- Estimate = (10 × 9) / 30 = 90/30 = 3
- B1 for any two of {100, 9, 30} seen as rounded values soi
- Full answer: 3
- Full marks = 2

## 11. HCF, LCM, Prime Factors

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Prime factorisation shown
- LCM: product of highest powers of all prime factors
- HCF: product of lowest common powers

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**s25_13 Q11:** Two flashing lights: green flashes every 12 minutes, red flashes every 45 minutes. They flash together at 11:00. Find the next time they flash together.
- LCM(12, 45): 12 = 2² × 3; 45 = 3² × 5; LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180 minutes = 3 hours
- Next simultaneous flash: 11:00 + 3 hours = 14:00
- B2 for LCM = 180 seen
- M1 for prime factor method or listing multiples of 12 and 45
- Full answer: 14:00 (or 2pm)
- Full marks = 3

**w25_22 Q14(a):** N = 2³ × 3^x × 5^y; LCM(N, 360) = 16200
- M1 for 16200 = 2³ × 3⁴ × 5² or for 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5 or for 45 = 3² × 5
- Full answer: x = 4, y = 2
- Full marks = 2

**w25_22 Q14(b):** HCF(N, 360) = 90 [B1; FT answer 30 if their x = 1]

**w25_22 Q14(c):** Smallest k such that kN is a cube number = 180 [B1]

**w25_21 Q15:** x = 2² × 3^n × 7; y = 2³ × 3^(n+1) × 7. HCF in terms of n.
- B1 for 2² or 3^n or 7 in final answer
- Full answer: 2² × 3^n × 7
- Full marks = 2

- Lower bound = value − half of smallest unit

### Key examples from 2025 papers

- Lower bound = 2.45 kg

**w25_21 Q1:** From the list: 52, √169, 27, √8, 12/23, 49

(Note: the PDF strips square root symbols — the list contains √169 and √8, not 169 and 8.)

- (b) Cube number: **27** [B1] — 27 = 3³

## Marking Traps — Number Topic

1. **Percentage**: "Find the interest" vs "find the total amount" — SC1 for the wrong one
2. **Ratio**: "18 more" translates to difference of parts → divide by (8−5) = 3 to get unit value
3. **Compound interest**: Always show A = P(1+r/100)^n clearly — M1 requires formula visible
4. **Standard form**: If answer is 0.034, must write as 3.4 × 10⁻² — leaving as 0.034 loses B1
5. **Bounds**: "Upper bound of a/b" = upper bound of a / lower bound of b (opposite for division)
6. **Recurring decimal**: Must show the algebraic process — answer alone may not earn nfww
7. **LCM/HCF**: Show prime factor trees or factor method explicitly — B marks depend on method being shown
