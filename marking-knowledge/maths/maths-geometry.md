## 2. Angles in Polygons

### Interior and exterior angle rules

- Exterior angle of regular n-gon = 360/n
- Interior angle = 180 − exterior angle = (n−2) × 180 / n

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_21 Q7:** Interior angle of regular decagon (10 sides)
- M1 for (360/10) or (10−2)×180/10
- Full answer: 144°
- Full marks = 2

**w25_22 Q9:** Regular pentagon + regular hexagon joined. Find x.
- M2 for [x =] 360/(360/5 + 360/6) or 360 − (5−2)×180/5 − (6−2)×180/6 oe
- M1 for exterior angle = 360/5 or 360/6 oe; or interior angle of pentagon/hexagon calculated
- Full answer: 132°
- Full marks = 3

**s25_12 Q4:** Diagram with two parallel lines. An isosceles triangle sits between them with base angles 35°. A transversal makes angle 48° with the lower parallel line. Find angle x at the top parallel line.
- B2 for AEB = 110° or BEC = 70° (intermediate angles at the triangle vertex)

- B1 for DCE = 35° or DEC = 110°
- Full answer: x = 22
- Full marks = 4

## 3b. Similar Triangles — Proof (AA Method)

### New question type from 2024: proving triangles are similar

### What the mark scheme rewards
- B2 for two correct pairs of equal angles, each with correct reason
- B1 for one correct pair with reason, OR two correct pairs without reasons
- "Hence similar" must follow from the angle pairs — implied by the mark scheme
- Three pairs not required — AA (two pairs) is sufficient for similarity

### Acceptable proofs
- Vertically opposite angles (for triangles sharing a point)
- Alternate angles (for parallel lines)
- Corresponding angles (for parallel lines)
- Angles in a triangle sum to 180° (to identify the third pair after two are known)

### Key examples from 2024 papers

**s24_21 Q3(b):** Prove triangles EAB and ECD are similar.
- angle AEB = angle CED [vertically opposite angles]
- angle EAB = angle ECD [alternate angles, AB ∥ CD]
- angle EBA = angle EDC [alternate angles, AB ∥ CD] OR [angles in triangle sum to 180°]
- Hence similar
- B2 for two correct pairs with correct reasons
- B1 for one correct pair with reason OR two correct pairs without reasons
- Full marks = 3

**w24_22 Q8(a):** Prove triangle ADE similar to triangle ABC. Three corresponding angles identified:
- angle DAE = angle BAC [same angle / common angle]
- angle ADE = angle ABC [corresponding angles, DE ∥ BC]
- angle AED = angle ACB [corresponding angles, DE ∥ BC]
- B1 for three correct angles in correct order; B1 for line 2 correct; B1 for line 3 correct
- Full marks = 3

## 6. Similar Shapes and Scale Factors

### What the mark scheme rewards
- Identifying correct linear scale factor from given lengths
- Area scale factor = (linear scale factor)²
- Volume scale factor = (linear scale factor)³

### Key examples from 2025 papers

**w25_11 Q17(b):** Two similar triangular prisms. The smaller has volume 180 cm³ and base width 10 cm. The larger has volume 1440 cm³. Find the corresponding base width x of the larger prism.
- Volume ratio 1440/180 = 8; linear scale = ∛8 = 2; base x = 10 × 2 = 20
- M2 for ∛(1440/180) × 10 oe
- M1 for ∛(1440/180) oe seen
- Full answer: x = 20
- Full marks = 3

**w25_12 Q17(a):** Two similar triangles. In the smaller, two sides are QR = 6 and another side = 7. In the larger, the corresponding side to QR is 9. Find the length PR (side corresponding to the 7 in the smaller triangle).
- Scale factor = 9/6; PR = (9/6) × 7
- M1 for 9/6 = PR/7 oe
- Full answer: 21/2 (= 10.5)
- Full marks = 2

**w25_12 Q17(b):** Using the same pair of similar triangles from (a). The area of the smaller triangle is 16 cm². Find the area of the larger triangle.
- Area scale factor = (9/6)² = (3/2)²; area of larger = 16 × (3/2)² = 16 × 9/4 = 36
- B1 for (9/6)² oe soi or (their PR/7)² oe soi
- Full answer: 36 nfww
- Full marks = 2

**s25_22 Q21(c):** A composite solid (hemisphere + cone) has surface area 712 cm². A similar smaller solid has surface area 242 cm². The larger solid has a length of 24 cm. Find the corresponding length of the smaller solid.
- Linear scale = √(242/712); length = √(242/712) × 24
- M2 for √(242/712) × 24
- M1 for √(area ratio) identified as linear scale factor
- Full answer: 14 cm
- Full marks = 3

## 12. Congruence Proof

### New question type from 2023: proving triangles are congruent

### What the mark scheme rewards
- B1 for each correct pair of equal sides or angles, with correct reason
- Must name the congruence condition at the end: SSS, SAS, AAS, or RHS
- Cambridge accepts any valid congruence proof — multiple valid routes exist

### Congruence conditions
| Condition | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| SSS | All three sides equal |
| SAS | Two sides and the included angle equal |
| AAS | Two angles and a corresponding side equal |
| RHS | Right angle, hypotenuse, and one other side equal |

### Key examples from 2023 papers

**w23_21 Q10(a):** Circle with centre O. AC and BD are diameters. Show that triangle OAB is congruent to triangle ODC.
- AO = DO [equal radii]
- BO = CO [equal radii]
- Angle AOB = angle DOC [vertically opposite angles]
- Therefore congruent by SAS
- B2 for two correct pairs of equal sides/angles with correct reasons; B1 for one correct pair with reason OR two correct pairs with no/incorrect reasons
- Note: angles must be described using 3-letter convention (angle AOB, not just "O")
- Full marks = 3

**s23_11 Q19:** O is the centre of a circle. OA and OB are radii. AT and BT are tangents to the circle from external point T. Prove that triangle OAT is congruent to triangle OBT.
- OA = OB [radii of the same circle]
- OAT = OBT = 90° [radius to tangent is perpendicular]
- OT is common [common side]
- Hence triangles OAT and OBT are congruent by RHS (Right angle, Hypotenuse, Side)
- B1 for each of the three correct statements with correct reasons
- Note: also valid via SSS (since AT = BT can be shown from the equal tangent property) or SAS — mark any valid complete set of three
- Full marks = 3

## 13. Circle Theorems — Algebraic Chain (Multiple Theorems in Sequence)

### New pattern from 2023: expressing an angle algebraically through a chain of circle theorem steps

### What the mark scheme rewards
- M1 for each theorem correctly stated with its reason
- B marks for key intermediate angles (expressed algebraically)
- A1 for the final expression in simplest form

### Key examples from 2023 papers

**s23_22 Q3(b)(i):** A, B, C are on a circle with centre O. Angle ABC = x°. Show that angle OAC = (90 − x)°, giving reasons.
- Method 1 (angle at centre): angle AOC = 2x° [angle at centre = twice angle at circumference for same arc AC]
- Triangle OAC is isosceles (OA = OC = radii): angle OAC = (180° − 2x°)/2 = 90° − x°
- B2 for angle AOC = 2x with correct reason (angle at centre is twice angle at circumference)
- B1 dep for angle OAC = (180 − their 2x)/2 with isosceles triangle OAC stated
- Method 2 (alternate): angle AYC = x° (angles in same segment); angle YCA = 90° (angle in semicircle, UY diameter); angle OAC = 90° − x° (angle sum in triangle)
- Full marks = 3

**s23_21 Q3(b):** U, V, W, X, Y are on a circle with centre O. UY is a diameter. ZX is a tangent to the circle at X. Z is on the line UY extended beyond Y. VUX = 35°, XZY = a°, VWY = b°. Find b in terms of a in simplest form.
- Step 1: OXZ = 90° [tangent is perpendicular to radius] [M1]
- Step 2: angle XOY = angle XOZ = 90° − a° [O, Y, Z are collinear with Z beyond Y, so OZ and OY are in the same direction]
- Step 3: Triangle OUX is isosceles (OU = OX = radii): angle XUO = (180° − (90° − a°))/2 = 45° − a/2° [B2]
- Step 4: Since O lies on UY (diameter), ray UO = ray UY, so angle XUY = angle XUO = 45° − a/2°
- Step 5: angle VUY = VUX + XUY = 35° + 45° − a/2° = 80° − a/2°
- Step 6: Arc VY not containing W = 360° − 2(80° − a/2°) = 200° + a°
- Step 7: angle VWY = ½(200° + a°) = 100° + a/2° [inscribed angle = half arc] [M1]
- Full answer: b = 100 + a/2
- Full marks = 4

### Key insight
- When Z is external and on the diameter line extended, angles at O from the tangent-point equal the same angle as viewed from the near diameter end, not the far end
- "Algebraic circle angle" questions require chaining: tangent property → isosceles triangle → inscribed angle theorem. Each must be shown with a reason to earn marks.

## Marking Traps — Geometry Topic

1. **Circle theorems**: The REASON must be stated — value alone is B1, not full marks. Cambridge expects the theorem name.
2. **Alternate segment theorem**: Common error — students call it "angle in alternate segment" instead of "alternate segment theorem". Accept both, but "alternate angles" alone (for parallel lines) is WRONG here.
3. **Similar shapes**: Volume ratio = (linear ratio)³. Forgetting the cube loses the M mark.
4. **Construction**: Arcs must be visible. Erasing arcs = B1 penalty. 
5. **Show that**: Must not work backwards from the given answer. Method must be forward.
6. **Angle reasons**: Don't write vague reasons like "angles on a straight line" when the specific theorem is required.
7. **Cyclic quadrilateral**: When using "opposite angles sum to 180°" — must identify which pair of opposite angles. Just writing the theorem name without applying it correctly loses the dep B mark.
8. **Parallel lines + isosceles triangle questions are NOT circle theorems**: A question with a triangle (C on BD, E on AD, AB ∥ EC, CD = DE) uses alternate angles + isosceles base angles — not circle theorems. Check the diagram carefully. The angles 54° and 28° can appear in both circle and parallel-line contexts; the question type is determined by whether there is a circle in the diagram, not by the angle values alone. (Lesson from w24_21 Q9 — originally misclassified as a circle theorem when there was no circle at all.)
